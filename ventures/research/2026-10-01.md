# Scout report: next business, 2026-10-01

**Context:** Business #1 is an Etsy shop selling AI-assisted SVG cut-file bundles with dark/gothic holiday themes. It is not live yet. It has 5 bundles built and an Etsy API toolchain (`tools/etsy.py`). Designs are generated in code (Python geometry to SVG/PNG/DXF), so the factory already makes clean vectors, DXF files, listing images and listing copy.

**Hard filters applied:** startup cost of $100 or less, no owner-handled inventory, no licence or regulated claims, AI-producible, allowed under the platform's AI policy, and reuse of the factory's assets preferred.

**Confidence note:** fees and policies come mostly from primary or near-primary sources (platform help pages, official blogs, the Authors Guild). Earnings and demand figures are almost all from blogs, forums or seller anecdotes, and I mark them as such. The revenue ranges below are my own assumption-driven estimates, not observed data. **Confidence in revenue figures: low.**

**Why some scores are what they are:**
- **Automation 5 for laser and POD.** Both run on APIs the factory can drive (the Etsy Open API is already wired up in `tools/etsy.py`, and Printify has a public API).
- **Automation 3 for KDP, Creative Market, Silhouette and Payhip.** They have no practical public upload API. Either the owner clicks through the upload pages, or a browser-automation worker uses the owner's session, which is a terms-of-service grey area.
- **Policy risk 2 for both Etsy options.** They put a second business on the same platform as business #1, and Etsy can suspend linked shops together. One enforcement action could take down everything.
- **Owner hours 2 for POD.** Physical orders bring damaged or lost-parcel messages and reprint requests that the owner must answer.
- **Profit scores are relative.** None of these candidates is likely to produce large income in 6 months (see the revenue ranges below).

**Important caveat on Payhip at #3:** it scores well because it has almost no platform risk and full synergy, not because of profit. Without the owner building a Pinterest presence it will sell close to nothing. If the owner's goal is pure profit, **KDP (#4) is effectively tied with it and has the higher 12-month ceiling.**

## 4. Top 3: launch outlines

### #1 Laser-cut file shop (Etsy sister shop): weighted score 3.75

**Why it's #1:** it reuses everything the factory already does, including closed vector paths, DXF export in inches, listing images and the Etsy API uploader. It sells to a different buyer (laser owners) at higher price points than Cricut files. Products can be clearly "substantially different" from the cut-file shop, which Etsy's multi-shop rule requires.

**Products** (dark/gothic and seasonal, original designs only):
- Multi-layer stacked ornaments with 3–5 layers that glue flat. There are no interlocking tabs, so material-thickness and kerf mistakes can't ruin a build.
- Layered shelf sitters and plaques.
- Coaster sets.
- Engrave-plus-cut keychain bundles.

**Formats:** SVG, DXF and PDF, each with a separate cut, score and engrave colour convention, plus a README that gives the layer order and the material thickness used.

**Prices** (assumed, not validated): $4–8 per design and $12–18 per bundle.

**Platform:** a second Etsy shop under its own login and email, listed on both shop profiles and run through the same `etsy.py` toolchain. Optional mirror on Payhip later.

**First 30 days:**
- **Week 1:** a Researcher worker scrapes Etsy market pages for laser best-seller formats. A Designer worker builds a `layered.py` generator that produces offset layers from existing designs, with a 0.5 mm minimum bridge and a 1.5 mm minimum feature at 3 mm plywood.
- **Weeks 1–4:** at most 2 listings per day, starting with Christmas and winter (Q4 window ends about 15 Nov). That gives roughly 40–50 listings by day 30.
- **Week 3:** begin the Valentine and year-round lines (moths, mushrooms, ravens).

**Owner one-time steps (about 1.5–2 h):**
- Create a new email and Etsy shop.
- Complete ID and bank verification and pay the setup fee.
- Run `etsy_auth.py` for the new shop.
- Optionally, pay a local maker or makerspace to test-cut 3 designs for real photos. This is not needed if the listings use rendered mockups and are labelled as such.

**Startup cost:**
- Setup fee $15–29 (blog-sourced).
- Listings 120 × $0.20 = $24.
- **Total about $40–55.** No ads.

**Realistic revenue (assumption-driven, low confidence):**
- **Net per sale:** about $4–7 after Etsy fees, assuming a $5–8 average order.
- **Month 6:** 120 listings, with an average of 0.1–0.5 sales per listing per month. That is 12–60 sales, or about **$50–400 a month**.
- **Month 12:** 200+ listings, the second Q4, and some reviews. That is about **$100–800 a month**.
- The low end is likely if the shop gets no reviews or search traction. The high end assumes a few listings rank for "glowforge christmas ornament"-type terms.
- I have no primary sales-volume data.

**Specific risks:**
- Etsy linkage: a strike on one shop can affect both.
- Designs have not been physically test-cut, which can lead to complaints. The flat-stack design mitigates this.
- Laser-file categories are crowded with cheap mega-bundles.
- Etsy's AI-disclosure enforcement applies, so the shop must use "Designed by" and the AI line in descriptions.

### #2 Gothic POD merch via Printify on Etsy: weighted score 3.35

**Why:** the same designs become stickers, vinyl decals, mugs and tees, and physical goods sell at higher prices to a broader buyer than crafters. Printify's free plan and API make listing fully automatable.

**Products:**
- Die-cut stickers and sticker sheets.
- Kiss-cut vinyl decals.
- Mugs.
- A few tees, limited because of the Amazon AI-merch headwind.

All products use the bat, moth, raven, skull-ornament and spiderweb-snowflake art.

**Platform:** best added to the existing Etsy shop once the gate is met, because the audience and the "Designed by" disclosure fit. Etsy's production-partner disclosure must name Printify. Alternatively, it could live in a third sister shop.

**First 30 days:**
- Connect Printify.
- A worker maps 20 existing designs onto 2–3 product types each.
- Run mockup generation.
- Publish 2 per day.
- Order samples of the 3 best sellers for real photos.

**Owner one-time steps (about 1 h setup):**
- Create the Printify account.
- Connect Etsy.
- Add a payment card for base costs.
- Set up the production-partner entry.

**Ongoing:** about 1–2 h a week on buyer messages about shipping, damaged items and reprints. This is the main owner cost.

**Startup cost:**
- Samples $25–45 (assumption).
- Listing fees about $12.
- **Total about $40–60.**

**Realistic revenue (low confidence):**
- **Net per sale:** stickers about $1.5–3 and mugs or tees about $5–8 (blog-sourced margins).
- **Month 6:** 15–60 orders a month, or **$40–250 a month**.
- **Month 12:** **$80–500 a month**, with a Q4 spike.

**Specific risks:**
- Same Etsy concentration as #1.
- Physical-order complaints hurt the shop's star rating, which also affects the digital listings.
- Generic merch is losing ground to Amazon's shopper-side AI tool.

### #3 Own storefront (Payhip) + Pinterest: weighted score 3.20

**Why:** it is a platform-independent mirror of the whole catalogue (cut files, and laser files later) with near-zero fees: 5% plus processing, and Payhip handles VAT. It can carry higher-value commercial-licence tiers and "everything" mega-bundles, and it builds an email list the factory owns. It hedges the Etsy-concentration risk shared by #1 and #2.

**Products:**
- Every Etsy bundle at the same price.
- Season mega-bundles at $15–25.
- A commercial-use licence upgrade.
- A free 3-design sampler to collect email addresses.

**First 30 days:**
- A worker generates product pages and Pinterest pins (2–3 pin variants per bundle).
- The owner's Pinterest business account gets about 5 pins a day via the Pinterest API, if app access is approved, or a scheduler.
- Link the Etsy shop's About section to the store. Do not divert Etsy buyers, which breaks Etsy rules.

**Owner one-time steps (about 1–1.5 h):**
- Payhip signup and connecting Stripe/PayPal (ID verification).
- Pinterest business account and API app request.

Uploads have no practical public API, so the owner needs either about 5 minutes per product to click through, or a browser-automation worker using their session.

**Startup cost:** $0, or optional paid Pinterest ads of up to $30.

**Realistic revenue (low confidence):**
- **Month 6:** about **$0–100 a month**.
- **Month 12:** **$20–300 a month**. Pinterest traffic typically takes 3–6+ months to build.
- These are pure assumptions. I found no reliable data.

**Specific risks:**
- Traffic is the whole problem.
- Pinterest has its own AI-labelling and spam rules.
- Payment-processor account holds.

## 5. Cross-cutting risks

1. **Platform concentration:** #1 and #2 both put more of the business on Etsy, and Etsy began AI-disclosure enforcement in 2026. Keep disclosure lines, cap uploads at 2 per day per shop, and never duplicate products across shops. Start #3 (Payhip) early as a hedge.
2. **AI-content enforcement is tightening everywhere:** Etsy (Jan 2026), KDP (image books, account-level), TPT (demotion) and Adobe (labelling plus style rules). Quality gates (QA worker) and honest labelling are mandatory, not optional.
3. **Revenue uncertainty:** every earnings figure here is either blog-sourced or my own assumption. Business #1 has no sales data yet. The venture gate exists so that real Etsy conversion data can recalibrate these ranges before #1 launches.
4. **Owner time creep:** POD customer service, KDP and Payhip manual uploads, and ID or payment verification are the real owner costs. Laser files have the fewest owner-touch points after setup.
5. **IP:** no "in the style of", no trademarked holiday characters, and avoid "Creepmas" (already a factory rule).

When #1 launches, also open Payhip (#3) as a low-effort mirror to reduce Etsy dependence. Re-run the Scout after 30 days of real Etsy data to re-rank #2 against KDP.
