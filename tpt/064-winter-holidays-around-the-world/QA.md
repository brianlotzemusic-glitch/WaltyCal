## Christmas (reading page 6)
- Celebrated December 25; for Christians it marks the birth of Jesus. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas
- Some Orthodox churches use the Julian calendar, so their Christmas is January 7 (equals December 25 Julian). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas
- Observed in many countries; many non-Christians also join in customs culturally. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas
- Carol singing is a long-standing tradition (popular since the Middle Ages). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas

## Hanukkah (reading page 8)
- Eight nights, starting on 25 Kislev (the 25th of Kislev) in the Hebrew calendar. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanukkah and https://history.com/news/8-things-you-should-know-about-hanukkah
- Date on the usual calendar changes; falls from November to late December. Source: https://history.com/news/8-things-you-should-know-about-hanukkah
- Commemorates the rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem; story of one day of oil lasting eight days. Sources: same two pages.
- Hanukkiah/menorah has nine branches: eight nights plus the shamash (helper candle) that lights the others; one more candle is lit each night. Sources: same two pages.
- Dreidel is a spinning-top game played during the holiday. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanukkah

## Kwanzaa (reading page 10)
- December 26 to January 1; created in 1966 by Maulana Karenga. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwanzaa and https://www.history.com/articles/kwanzaa-history
- Name from the Swahili "matunda ya kwanza," "first fruits." Source: https://www.history.com/articles/kwanzaa-history
- Seven principles, the Nguzo Saba, one per day, in this order: Umoja (Unity), Kujichagulia (Self-determination), Ujima (Collective work and responsibility), Ujamaa (Cooperative economics), Nia (Purpose), Kuumba (Creativity), Imani (Faith). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwanzaa and https://www.history.com/articles/kwanzaa-history (names and English meanings identical in both). The "In other words" column paraphrases the same two pages.
- Kinara holds seven candles; one is lit each day. Sources: same two pages.
- Karamu is a community feast. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwanzaa

## Las Posadas (reading page 12)
- Nine nights, usually December 16 to 24; mainly Mexico, also Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Hispanic communities in the United States. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Posadas (also Franciscan Media, https://www.franciscanmedia.org/?p=17644, for the nine nights and dates)
- "Posada" means lodging or inn. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Posadas
- Processions from house to house remember Mary and Joseph seeking lodging; songs, piñatas and a shared meal. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Posadas
- Customs vary by place (the page notes regional variations). Source: same page.

## Lunar New Year (reading page 14)
- Follows a calendar based on the moon; falls in late January or February. Sources: https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2024/02/celebrating-lunar-new-year-with-the-library-of-congresss-collections/ ("always falls in late January or in February"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year (new moon between 21 January and 20 February)
- Celebrated in China, Vietnam, Korea and many other places; "Lunar New Year" is the general English name. Sources: same two pages.
- Festival runs to the Lantern Festival on the 15th day (China). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year
- Lion dances are common at Lunar New Year, commonly with drums, gongs and cymbals. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_dance ("usually performed during the Lunar New Year"; "commonly performed to a vigorous drumbeat with gongs and cymbals"); https://asia-archive.si.edu/lion-dancing-for-lunar-new-year (one team: drummer, gong player, two cymbal players); Library of Congress post above (troupes).
