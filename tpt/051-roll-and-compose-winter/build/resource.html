Teacher Notes

Roll and Compose · dice composing in 3 levels · grades 2⁠–⁠5

How it works

  • Students roll a die (1 to 6) and look the number up in a dice table. The table turns each roll into a rhythm or a note.
  • Every bar adds up. In Levels A and B one rhythm roll fills one beat, and in Level C one rhythm roll fills a whole bar, so any sequence of rolls can be finished.
  • Level A rolls rhythm only, on a one-line staff. Level B adds pitch from C, D, E, G and A. Level C uses two dice, 8 bars and a call and response.
  • No die in the room? A classroom die, a paper cube or an online dice roller all work. Any way of getting a number from 1 to 6 is fine.
  • Every composition is different, so there is no single answer. Pages 12⁠–⁠13 show one completed example for each level, with the rolls beside the notation.

Materials

  • A die for each pair of students (or a paper cube, or a device with an online dice roller) and pencils with erasers.
  • Optional: unpitched percussion, and bells or keyboards set to C, D, E, G and A.

Pacing

  • Level A: two lessons of about 20 minutes. Learn the table and compose one piece, then compose a second and share.
  • Level B: two or three lessons of 20 to 30 minutes.
  • Level C: three or four lessons of 25 to 30 minutes, with the last one for sharing.
  • Short on time? Use only the level that fits your grade.

Differentiation

  • Support: start with Level A for everyone, or let students keep the dice table and the student reference on their desks.
  • Challenge: ask students to roll again for any bar they do not like, or to write a second 4-bar piece that answers the first.

Good to know

Model the first bar with the whole class on the board. Skills: reading and writing rhythm, notating pitch on the treble staff, and creating and sharing short pieces. This supports the Creating strand of the National Core Arts Standards for music; please match the skills to your own curriculum. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

The notes you can roll

The notes are C, D, E, G and A, and then high C. Middle C sits on its own short line below the staff, and high C sits in the third space.

Stems and beams

  • Below the middle line, the stem goes up on the right side of the note.
  • On or above the middle line, the stem goes down on the left side.
  • Two eighth notes share a beam.

Three ways to roll

  • A classroom die.
  • A paper cube numbered 1 to 6.
  • An online dice roller.

Roll and ComposeShare and perform
SHARE
Performance ideas
NameDate
Every composition deserves a performance. Pick one idea for each lesson, or let students choose their own way to share.
1Clap and say

Clap the rhythm while saying ta, ti-ti and shh. Count 1 2 3 4 out loud before you start.

2Body percussion

Pat, clap, snap or stomp the rhythm. Use a different sound for each symbol in Level A.

3Classroom percussion

Play the rhythm on drums, wood blocks or shakers. Rests stay silent.

4Bells and keyboards

Set up C, D, E, G and A. On xylophones and bells, lift off the F and B bars.

5Call and response

One player performs the call (bars 1⁠–⁠4). A partner answers with the response (bars 5⁠–⁠8).

6Swap and play

Trade sheets with a partner and perform each other's compositions.

7Gallery walk

Post the compositions. Classmates read them and leave a star and a wish on a sticky note.

8Class piece

Join four 4-bar compositions end to end. Play them in order as a class piece.

9Add an ostinato

A partner plays a short pattern that repeats over and over while you perform your piece.

10Record and listen

Record each performance on a tablet or phone. Listen back and talk about what worked well.

A sharing routine in three steps
1Introduce

Say your title. Tell the class which dice rolls you used.

2Perform

Count in 1 2 3 4. Play or say your piece with a steady beat.

3Respond

Listeners name one star and one wish. Then fill in the rubric on page 14.

Tip for teachers. Let students choose whether to play or say their piece. Keep each performance short, and ask listeners to tap the beat quietly. Celebrate every composition: the dice make each one different.
