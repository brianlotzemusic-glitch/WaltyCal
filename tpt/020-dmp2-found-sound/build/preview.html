The one rule for Class 1

Students record before they edit. By the end of Class 1 every student has at least one clean recording on the Sound log (page 12), so Class 2 is spent building instruments, not hunting for sounds.

Classes 3–5

Working days. Class 5 has no new slide. Circulate and ask: which layer is this, and what sound did it come from? Instruments that never reach the Layer plan are my third struggle.

An invented piece, “Passing Period”, described on paper: no audio is included, so the numbers are easy to check. Students’ pieces will differ.

1 · The five sounds ·

2 · The plan: 45 seconds ·

Intro 0:00–0:05, Build 0:05–0:15, Full 0:15–0:35, Break 0:35–0:40, Outro 0:40–0:45. The plan is 45 seconds: inside my 30–60 second requirement.

3 · Checking the numbers

96 beats per minute is 0.625 s a beat, so a bar of 4/4 is 2.5 s. Sections: 2 + 4 + 8 + 2 + 2 = 18 bars, and 18 × 2.5 s = 45 s. From 0:15 to 0:35 all three layers play at once. Five sounds are recorded and all five are used.

4 · Why it works

Each layer has a job: the hum is the room, the taps and slams are the beat, the thud is the floor. The filter sweep means the texture changes without adding anything. A listener should struggle to guess the sources.

5 · Three Padlet questions (invented) ·

1. Which sound did you guess first, and which one fooled you? 2. If you took one layer out, which one would you miss most? 3. Where does the track change the most, and how did I do it?

Requirements ·

Record or source at least 5 original audio samples
Edit, trim, and normalize sounds in Logic Pro
Import sounds into Quick Sampler to create playable instruments
Design at least 3 distinct sound layers (e.g., percussion, bass, texture)
Apply effects and automation for dynamics and interest
Length: 30–60 seconds

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Collect sounds — Record at least 5 sounds using your phone or the microphone at your station (Sound log: page 12)
  2. 2
    Edit and prep — Trim, normalize, and clean up your recordings in Logic (Logic quick reference: page 9)
  3. 3
    Build instruments — Import sounds into Quick Sampler and experiment with playback modes (Quick Sampler modes: page 10; Instrument builder: page 13)
  4. 4
    Compose — Use your custom instruments to build a short piece with at least 3 layers (Layer plan: page 14)
  5. 5
    Mix — Apply EQ, effects, and automation. Balance levels and add polish.
  6. 6
    Finalize — Bounce your track and prepare your presentation
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.
