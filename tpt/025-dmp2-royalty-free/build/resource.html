What this is ·

A 7-class-period structured project for high school music technology. Each student composes a one-minute piece of royalty-free music in Logic, as if for a school video, guided by three descriptive words they choose from a list. They plan it as a creative brief, give it a build, a peak and a resolve, mix it, write a paragraph on how it reflects their words, and present it with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (type: structured; focus: functional composition), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Set up the Presentation Padlet and decide how you will treat Apple Loops (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 13–24), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–9), Class 3 (slides 10–12), Class 4 (slide 13), Class 5 (slides 14–15), Class 6 (slides 16–17) and Class 7 (slides 18–19). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, video, songs, artists or albums are named or included. Students make every sound in their tracks themselves, from the instruments, kits and Apple Loops in Logic.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: my variations on page 6: 2 words and a structural template, or scoring to a real video clip (both laid out on page 10).
  • Feedback board: my sheets use Padlet for the three questions; any class board works.
  • Word choice: students choose their three words from the list; you could also assign them with a wheel, as a kit suggestion.
  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 21).

Royalty Free · Structured · Functional Composition
Create a 1-minute piece for media use, guided by 3 descriptive words. Compose for a purpose, not just yourself.

What students make ·

One original piece of royalty-free music, about one minute long, built from layers that suit its style and shaped by three descriptive words from the list. It is mixed with EQ and plug-ins and may use Apple Loops as long as the arrangement is the student’s own. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx project and a paragraph explaining how the song reflects their three words, plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then play the piece and present it.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches functional composition — making music for a purpose beyond personal expression. Students learn to work from a creative brief.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Compose music driven by descriptive keywords rather than personal preference
  • Understand what makes music functional for media use
  • Practice efficient composition within a defined length and mood
  • Develop skills in matching musical choices to a visual or emotional context

At a glance ·

Grades9–12: the second-year course, after a first year of Logic Pro basics

Students should already know how to open a project, add software instruments and loops, use the Mixer, and save, bounce and zip in Logic. Nobody needs to read music.

SETUP

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • A place to submit the .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx and the reflection paragraph (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (yours to set up; none is included).
  • The Word list, printed (page 13), or your own copy of the list on your class page.
  • A rule for Apple Loops (requirement 3): loops are allowed but the arrangement must be original. Decide what that means (for example, no loop used untouched for the whole piece) and write it on the board.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 13–24).

Before Class 1 ·

Set up the Presentation Padlet in your class page.
Decide your Apple Loops rule and how you will check it (page 17).
Choose the video you might use for the Harder variation, if any (page 10).
Check that a student computer opens Logic and plays back through headphones.

What to watch (my struggles) ·

Students ignore their three words, tracks feel incomplete at one minute, students lean on Apple Loops, and the reflection paragraph is an afterthought. Pairs well with real district videos. Students love knowing their music might actually get used.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • Ways to use the word list: students pick their own three, or you spin a wheel and let them swap one.
  • Real uses to name on Class 1: a sports highlight, a facilities tour, an announcement, a club promo. Ask what each one needs.
  • Ask while students work: which of your words is this layer for? Where does the peak happen?

Sounds they did not make ·

A kit suggestion

Remind students that “royalty-free” means no copyrighted samples: they use their own sounds, instruments and Apple Loops from Logic, and they hand in only their own track.

Keep the volume moderate

Bright sounds and loud peaks are tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches functional composition — making music for a purpose beyond personal expression. Students learn to work from a creative brief.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students ignore their 3 words and just make what they want.
  • Tracks feel incomplete at 1 minute. Encourage a simple arc: build, peak, resolve.
  • Over-reliance on Apple Loops with minimal original arrangement.
  • Reflection paragraph is an afterthought.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Reduce to 2 descriptive words and provide a structural template.

More Advanced: Require students to score their track to an actual video clip.

  • Incomplete at 1 minute: slide 11 and the Arrangement plan (page 16) show a build, a peak and a resolve.
  • Too many Apple Loops: the layer log on the Layers and mix sheet (page 17) and the worked example (page 12).
  • An afterthought paragraph: the Reflection planner (page 24), with a thin and a useful example.

How to grade ·

  • My rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 21). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Creativity & Originality: ask how the three words shaped the choices, then listen for what is the student’s own. Apple Loops are allowed; a loop left untouched is the default in the 1 column.
  • Technical Skill: open the project. Which layers are the student’s own and which are loops (Layers and mix, page 17)? Are EQ and plug-ins really in use?
  • Composition & Structure: compare the piece with the Arrangement plan (page 16). Is there a build, a peak and a resolve (my second struggle), or one long loop?
  • Sound Design & Mix Quality: close your eyes and listen for each of the three words. Is every layer audible, and does the mix still carry the words?
  • Documentation & Presentation: the Creative brief (page 14), the reflection paragraph (page 24) and the Padlet questions are the evidence.

Royalty FreeLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Royalty Free project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…: Software Instrument tracks for pads, keys, bass and bells, and Drummer or Apple Loops for drums.
Apple LoopsOpen the Loop Browser (O) and drag a loop into a track. Apple Loops are allowed here, but the arrangement must be your own: cut loops, repeat them, drop layers in and out, and add your own parts (my third struggle).
Tempo and lengthSet the tempo in the control bar. Bars × 240 ÷ tempo = seconds; at 120 BPM, 30 bars is 60 seconds. The Arrangement plan has a length helper.
SectionsShow Global Tracks (G) and add arrangement markers named Intro, Build, Peak and Resolve, so the form is visible in the project.
LayersAdd one layer at a time. Mute and unmute tracks, or cut regions, to bring layers in and out of the sections.
AutomationPress A to show track automation. Pick a parameter, such as filter cutoff, volume, pan or reverb send, and draw points so the sound moves.
EQ, pan, plug-insAdd Channel EQ for balance, use each track's Pan knob, and add plug-ins such as a reverb or filter. Press X for the Mixer.
EndingsA resolve usually means layers leave and the last chord settles. Use volume automation or a fade, and check that nothing is cut off at the end.
Scoring to video (Harder)Choose Open Movie from the File menu or the Window menu in your version, and open a clip you have the right to use. Add markers at the moments the music should hit (see the Easier and Harder variations page).
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

My Easier variation: reduce to 2 descriptive words and provide a structural template. The words are your choice, and the template below is the kit’s own (it is not one of my sheets). Copy it onto the board or hand it out.

Easier: a structural template for one minute ·

SectionBars
Introbars 1–4 · 4 bars
Buildbars 5–12 · 8 bars
Peakbars 13–22 · 10 bars
Resolvebars 23–30 · 8 bars

30 bars at 120 BPM = 60 seconds. At another tempo, use the length helper on page 16.

How to use it
  1. Two words instead of three; the brief on page 14 has room for a third.
  2. Fill the sections: the intro has the fewest layers, the peak the most, the resolve thins out.
  3. Students may change the bar counts as long as the piece is about a minute.

Harder: score your track to a real video clip ·

My More Advanced variation. No video is included: choose a clip you have the right to use (a district video, or one your students shot). Students watch it first, write down where something happens on screen, and decide what the music does at that moment.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Open Doors”, described on paper, for a one-minute tour of a renovated school library (an invented video): no audio is included. Students’ projects will differ. 30 bars at 120 BPM, three words, four sections, seven layers (page 1 of 2).

1 · A weak plan

I will make a happy song with a nice beat.

It names no words and nothing says why any choice was made.

2 · A clear plan

My video is a one-minute tour of a renovated school library (an invented video). My three words are Bright, Steady and Uplifting. Bright: bells and plucked keys in a major key, with the highs lifted a little. Steady: 120 BPM that never changes, a kick on every beat and one chord for every two bars. Uplifting: a bell melody that climbs, a new layer every few bars, and an ending that lands on the home chord. It is 30 bars, 60 seconds, in four sections: intro, build, peak and resolve. Two layers are Apple Loops that I cut and rearrange.

What this page is

My sheet asks for a short paragraph explaining how your song reflects your 3 chosen words. Fill in prompts 1 to 3, then write your paragraph on your class page. Prompts 4 and 5 follow my definition: a reflection is a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time; you may talk them through with your teacher instead.

Thin (an invented example)

My song is bright and steady and uplifting. I used bells and drums and it is happy. I think it sounds good and fits my words. I liked making it.

Useful (an invented example)

Bright is the bell melody and the plucked keys in C major, with the highs lifted in the mix. Steady is the 120 BPM pulse with the kick on every beat; I did not change tempo once. Uplifting is the climb: a new layer arrives about every four bars, the bell melody rises to its highest note in the Peak, and the last chord settles on C. Next time I would try a quieter intro.
