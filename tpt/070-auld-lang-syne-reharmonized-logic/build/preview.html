The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a style and a tempo from the tempo table and the first eight bars of the melody entered correctly, so that Class 2 is all chords and groove. Play a few students’ melodies against the chart before they go.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the tempo, the melody and the chords; Class 2 is the groove, the arrangement, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the arrangement and the mix, and Class 4 on presentations and reflection.

Step 3: choose chords. One chord a bar over the 16 bars of the tune. Each row gives the melody note on beat 1 in C major and in G major, the chord in both keys, its Roman numeral, the chord tones, and which part of the chord the beat 1 note is. Every beat 1 note is a chord tone. Passing notes are the other melody notes in the bar that are not in the chord; they move on and are not a problem. Use the pop table or the ballad table, or write your own and check each bar the same way.
