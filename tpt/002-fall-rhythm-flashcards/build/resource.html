Teacher Notes

Fall Rhythm Flashcards & Tracing · grades K–2

Getting the cards ready

  • Print the card pages single-sided on cardstock, cut on the dashed lines, and laminate if you like.
  • Start with the symbol cards, then the pattern cards in order: Set A (quarter and eighth notes), Set B (adds the rest), Set C (adds the half note).
  • Each card comes in standard notation and stick notation. Use stick notation first if your students know it, then move to standard notation.

Ways to use the cards

  • Flash & clap: show a card, count in “1, 2, ready, go,” and the class claps and says it.
  • Pat the leaves: the leaves under each pattern card are the steady beat. Pat on each leaf while you clap the rhythm.
  • Which card did I clap? Lay out 3–4 pattern cards, clap one, and students point to it.
  • Wise owl’s mistake: clap a card with one change; students find the beat that is different.
  • Compose: students line up symbol cards to make their own 4-beat pattern (a half note counts as 2).

Rhythm syllables

Cards print the Kodály syllables (ta, ti-ti, ta-a) only as a small, labelled option. Many teachers use other systems: Takadimi (ta, ta-di), Gordon (du, du-de), counting (1, 1-and) or word chants. Use whatever your students already know. Fall word chant, if you like: leaf (quarter note), pump-kin (two eighths), whoo-oo like an owl (half note), shh (rest).

Tracing & worksheets

  • Tracing pages: trace big, trace small, then draw on the empty lines. The big dot shows where to start each line.
  • Beat-count worksheets have answer keys right after them (pages 26–28).
  • Every page prints well in black and white.

Music notes

All pattern cards are exactly 4 beats in 4/4 time. Notes have stems up, as on a one-line rhythm staff; the two eighth notes are beamed together and share one beat. The quarter rest is one silent beat. The half note lasts two beats.

Standards

Supports reading and performing rhythm patterns in iconic or standard notation (National Core Arts Standards, Music, MU:Pr4.2 in grades 1–2) and steady-beat work in kindergarten.

The steady beat · one leaf for every beat

A pattern card has 4 beats. Each quarter note, pair of eighth notes, or quarter rest fills one leaf. A half note fills two leaves: say it and hold it.

Two eighth notes, a quarter note and a half note: 1 + 1 + 2 = 4 beats.

Remember

  • Stems on these cards go up on the right side of the notehead.
  • A filled notehead is a quarter or eighth note. An open (empty) notehead with a stem is a half note.
  • Two eighth notes are joined by a beam. Together they make one beat.
  • A rest means silence for one beat. Keep the beat going in your head.

Syllables are optional. Your teacher may use different words, such as Takadimi, Gordon or counting.
