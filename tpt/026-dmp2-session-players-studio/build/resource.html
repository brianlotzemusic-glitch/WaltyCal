What this is ·

A 7-class-period structured project for high school music technology. Each student builds a one-minute, live-style band arrangement in Logic with three Session Players: Drummer, Bass Player and Keyboard Player. They choose a chord progression, customize each player’s style, groove and complexity, arrange an intro, groove section and ending, add an original melody, mix it, and present it with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (type: structured; focus: arrangement), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Set up the Presentation Padlet and open Logic to try the three players (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 13–24), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–8), Class 2 (slides 9–10), Class 3 (slides 11–13), Class 4 (slide 14), Class 5 (slide 15), Class 6 (slides 16–17) and Class 7 (slides 18–19). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 21).

OVERVIEW

Session Player’s Studio · Structured · Arrangement
Build a realistic band performance using Logic’s Session Players. Customize groove, complexity, and add your own melody.

What students make ·

One original, live-style production about one minute long. Three Session Players (Drummer, Bass Player and Keyboard Player) play a chord progression the student chooses, each with its own style, groove and complexity. The piece has a clear intro, groove section and ending, with the energy varied between sections, and the student’s own melody on top. It is mixed with EQ and effects. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then present the piece.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches arrangement, harmonic thinking, and how to build a full production with limited manual input. Great for students who struggle with playing instruments.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Learn to use Logic’s Session Players (Drummer, Bass, Keyboard)
  • Adjust style, groove, and complexity to create a realistic performance
  • Arrange a short piece with an intro, groove, and ending
  • Add an original melody and mix for a polished final product

At a glance ·

Grades9–12: the second-year course, after a first year of Logic Pro basics
Length7 class periods: set up and chords, customize, three working days, mix and bounce, present
SoftwareLogic Pro: Session Players (Drummer, Bass Player, Keyboard Player), arrangement, EQ and effects, bounce
You supplyMacs with a current Logic Pro, headphones, a feedback board and a place to submit files
AssessmentMy rubric, 5 categories scored 1–5, /25; final grade = average of the student’s and the teacher’s score (page 21)
Before they start

Students should already know how to open a project, add software instruments, use the Mixer, and save, bounce and zip in Logic. Nobody needs to read music or play an instrument.

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with a current Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Session Players are part of recent versions of Logic Pro, so check yours before Class 1.
  • A place to submit the .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (yours to set up; none is included).
  • The Chord cheat sheet, printed (page 13).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 13–24).

Before Class 1 ·

Set up the Presentation Padlet in your class page.
Open Logic on a student computer and load Drummer, Bass Player and Keyboard Player.
Choose the two YouTube videos you might show (my resources, page 7).
Decide how you will treat the live-recorded element in the Harder variation (page 10).

What to watch (my struggles) ·

Students leave everything on default, the melody is an afterthought, sections sound the same, and chord progressions intimidate some students. This is a great project for students who are less confident with playing but have strong arrangement ideas.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • Ask while students work: what did you change on this player? How is your intro different from your peak?
  • Have students play the same four chords with three different drum settings and say which feels like which section.
  • Give the melody a class of its own (Class 4), as my second struggle suggests.

Original work ·

A kit suggestion

Students may borrow a chord progression (step 2) but not a melody or a recording. They write their own melody and hand in only their own track.

Keep the volume moderate

A full band in headphones can get loud. Tell students to start low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

Session Player’s StudioTeacher notes · the big idea
TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches arrangement, harmonic thinking, and how to build a full production with limited manual input. Great for students who struggle with playing instruments.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students leave everything on default settings. Push them to customize style, groove, and complexity.
  • The melody is an afterthought. Dedicate time specifically to melody writing.
  • Students don’t vary sections — the whole track sounds the same. Show them how to change Drummer patterns and Bass complexity per section.
  • Chord progressions intimidate some students. Provide a cheat sheet of common progressions.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide a chord progression and let students focus on arrangement and melody.

More Advanced: Require an original chord progression, a B-section with a different groove, and at least one live-recorded element.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Default settings: the Players sheet (page 14) asks for a style, groove and complexity for every player.
  • An afterthought melody: Class 4 and the Melody planner (page 16) give it a class of its own.
  • Sections that sound the same: slide 12 and the Arrangement plan (page 15), with the worked example (page 12).
  • Chords that intimidate: the Chord cheat sheet (page 13), in five keys.
Where this kit helps with the variations (kit suggestion)
  • Easier: a given progression and a section plan to fill in (page 10).
  • More advanced: planning pages for an original progression, a B-section and a live-recorded element (page 10).

How to grade ·

  • My rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 21). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Creativity & Originality: are style, groove and complexity set on purpose, or left on default (my first struggle)? The melody is where the student’s own ideas show.
  • Technical Skill: open the project. Are three Session Players loaded with custom settings (Players sheet, page 14), and are EQ and effects really in use?
  • Composition & Structure: compare the piece with the Arrangement plan (page 15). Does the intro feel different from the peak (my third struggle)?
  • Sound Design & Mix Quality: can you hear the melody over the band? Did the student change an instrument (my first tip)?
  • Documentation & Presentation: the Padlet questions, the Reflection page (page 24) and the presentation are the evidence.

Reflection · added by this kit ·

The rubric mentions reflection, but the project sheet has no reflection task, so this kit adds one (page 24): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. It adds no points and no rubric row; use it as evidence when you score Documentation & Presentation.

Notes / observations ·

This is a great project for students who are less confident with playing but have strong arrangement ideas.

Session Player’s StudioLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Session Player’s Studio project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…. Choose Drummer, then add a Bass Player and a Keyboard Player from the same menu.
Session PlayersEach player has its own editor: choose a style, then set how loud or soft and how simple or complex it plays. Drummer also has groove and fill controls. The player creates the notes; you choose the sound.
Change the soundIn the Library, choose a different instrument or kit for a player (my first tip). The part stays; the sound changes.
ChordsShow Global Tracks (G) and use the Chord track to type in the chords, one a bar, so Bass Player and Keyboard Player follow them. Or let the Keyboard Player play from the chords you give it.
Tempo and lengthSet the tempo in the control bar. Bars × 240 ÷ tempo = seconds; at 96 BPM, 24 bars is 60 seconds. The Arrangement plan has a length helper.
SectionsAdd arrangement markers named Intro, Groove, B-section and Ending. In each section, change a player’s complexity or mute it to vary the energy (my third struggle).
Your melodyRecord a Software Instrument with a MIDI keyboard or the on-screen keyboard, or program notes in the Piano Roll. Keep to the chord tones on the strong beats (Melody planner).
Live-recorded element (Harder)Add an Audio track and record a voice or an instrument through a microphone. Wear headphones so the band does not leak into the recording.
EQ, pan, plug-insAdd Channel EQ for balance, use each track's Pan knob, and add effects such as a reverb. Press X for the Mixer.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Porch Light”, described on paper: no audio is included. Students’ projects will differ. 24 bars at 96 BPM, three Session Players, four sections (page 1 of 2).

1 · A weak plan

I will load three players and press play.

It names no chords, no settings and no sections.

2 · A clear plan

My piece is 24 bars at 96 BPM, which is 60 seconds, in the key of C. The chords are C, Am, F and G, and the B-section changes to F, G, C and Am. The Keyboard Player starts alone in the intro. The Bass Player and Drummer join at bar 5 for the groove. In the B-section the Drummer and Bass get busier and the groove changes. My melody comes in at bar 9 and uses chord tones. The ending thins out to a single C.

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Build a band without the band. Use Logic’s Session Players — Drummer, Bass Player, and Keyboard Player — to create a realistic live-style arrangement. Customize their style, groove, and complexity, then add your own melody on top.

Goals ·

  • Learn to use Logic’s Session Players (Drummer, Bass, Keyboard)
  • Adjust style, groove, and complexity to create a realistic performance
  • Arrange a short piece with an intro, groove, and ending
  • Add an original melody and mix for a polished final product

Key Vocabulary ·

Session PlayerA Logic Pro virtual musician that generates realistic performances based on your settings
GrooveThe rhythmic feel of a performance — how tight, loose, or swinging it feels
ComplexityHow busy or simple a session player’s part is
Chord ProgressionA sequence of chords that provides the harmonic foundation of a piece

Requirements ·

Load and customize at least 3 Session Players
Adjust style, groove, and complexity for each player
Arrange a short piece with a clear intro, groove section, and ending
Add your own original melody over the top
Mix and export a 1-minute live-style production

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Set up — Load Drummer, Bass Player, and Keyboard Player into your project (Players sheet: page 14)
  2. 2
    Choose a chord progression — Borrow one from a song you like or create your own (Chord cheat sheet: page 13)
  3. 3
    Customize — Adjust each session player’s style, groove, and complexity settings (Players sheet: page 14)
  4. 4
    Arrange — Build an intro, main groove, and ending. Vary the energy between sections. (Arrangement plan: page 15)
  5. 5
    Add your melody — Record or program an original melody over the top (Melody planner: page 16)
  6. 6
    Mix and finalize — Balance levels, apply EQ and effects. Bounce your track. (Mix sheet: page 17)
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.

Tips for Success ·

  • Don’t be afraid to change the instrument a session player is using. It creates the notes — you choose the sound.
  • Need a chord progression? Ask a search engine or an AI chatbot for the chords to a song you like.
  • Vary the complexity between sections. The intro should feel different from the peak.
  • The melody is what makes this yours. Spend real time on it.

What a reflection is

A reflection is a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Answer all four prompts in your own words. You choose how.

Written. I will write my answers on this page.
Oral. I will talk my answers through with my teacher. My teacher fills in the box at the bottom.

1  What went well? Name something in your project you are proud of, and say why it worked.

2  What did you struggle with? Say what was hard and what you tried to fix it.

3  What would you do differently next time? Be specific: a step, a choice, a sound or a tool.

4  Look at the rubric (page 21). Which category are you proudest of, and which would you work on first next time?
