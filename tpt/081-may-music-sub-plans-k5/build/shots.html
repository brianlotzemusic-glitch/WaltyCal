Instrument Name ClapsDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of instrument and May words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), ko-to (2 claps), Ha-wai-i (3 claps), u-ku-le-le (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Pluck, tap, bow, strum. Hands clap, hearts beat. Look, listen, learn. Thank you, thank you. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant parade
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Clap the Name from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Heritage ReaderDay 1 · Reading about May and heritage · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage Month, five instruments, a thank-you day for a mom or a special grown-up, Memorial Day and the end of the school year, and answer questions.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Opening question
    Say: “May has big ideas: music from Asia and the Pacific Islands, a day to say thank you, and the end of the school year.” Ask: What instruments from around the world do you already know? Take five answers. Any answer is fine.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact? The Memorial Day sentence is optional: you may skip it. Say instrument names the way page 8 shows.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Soft Hands or Echo Me from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 2; keep the reading and the questions.

Heritage ReaderA short reading about May
DAY 1
Grades K–2
NameDate
Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
May is the fifth month of the year. In the United States, May is Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage Month. It is a time to learn about people, art and music from many places in Asia and the Pacific Islands. A koto from Japan has strings that you pluck, and an erhu from China has a bow. A sitar comes from India, a janggu is a Korean drum, and the ukulele comes from Hawaii. Mother’s Day is in May too, a day to thank a mom or a special grown-up. On Memorial Day, the last Monday in May, people remember those who died serving in the military. School is almost over, so it is a good time to share what you learned.

May Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

Pluck, Bow and FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
koto
Stretch your arms wide and pluck pretend strings with your fingers.
erhu
Hold a pretend pole with one hand and draw a slow bow with the other.
sitar
Hold a pretend long neck up high and strum with your other hand.
janggu
Pat your lap with both hands, one hand and then the other.
ukulele
Hold a pretend small guitar against your chest and strum.
flower
Crouch small, then slowly open your arms and bloom.
heart
Put a hand on your heart and tap a gentle steady beat.
sun
Stretch your arms in a big circle, then freeze.
