### 3. Koto: a long Japanese string instrument, most often with 13 strings (17-string versions are also common), plucked with picks worn on three fingers of the right hand, with movable bridges (Day 1, facts page, Day 4 card, Day 5, bingo clues Koto, Pluck, String). The drawing shows 13 strings and 13 bridges on a slant (gen.py --check counts them)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koto_(instrument) (fetched 25 Nov 2026: Japan, national instrument; derives from the Chinese zheng and se; most common version 13 strings, 17 also common; plucked with three fingerpicks (tsume); movable bridges (ji); about 180 cm long). The pages do not say 'national instrument' or give the length.

### 4. Erhu: a Chinese instrument with two strings, played with a bow whose hair passes between the strings; the small sound box is often six-sided (the drawing shows a six-sided box) (Day 1, facts page, Day 4 card, Day 5, bingo clue Erhu)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhu (fetched 25 Nov 2026: 'Chinese two-stringed bowed musical instrument'; bow hair goes between the strings; sound box hexagonal (southern), octagonal (northern) or less commonly round, covered with python skin). The pages do not mention the skin or the shapes other than 'often six-sided'.

### 5. Sitar: an instrument from India used in Hindustani classical music, with a long fretted neck and a large hollow gourd; some of its strings are plucked and others ring along on their own (Day 1, facts page, Day 4 card, Day 5, bingo clue Sitar)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitar (fetched 25 Nov 2026: Indian subcontinent; Hindustani classical music; 18 to 21 strings, six or seven played and the rest sympathetic; curved raised movable frets; calabash gourd resonators; plucked with a metal pick). The origin story (an 18th-century courtier developing it from the setar) is not printed because historians disagree about it.

### 6. Janggu: a Korean drum shaped like an hourglass, with two heads (one deeper, one higher); traditionally one head is struck with the bare hand and the other with a thin bamboo stick (Day 1, facts page, Day 4 card, Day 5, bingo clue Janggu)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janggu (fetched 25 Nov 2026: Korean traditional drum, hourglass-shaped body, heads of different skins for lower and higher tones; traditionally the bamboo yeolchae strikes the higher right head and the bare hand the lower left head; heads secured with rope counter-loops).

### 7. Ukulele: a small instrument with four strings that developed in Hawaii in the 1880s from small guitar-like instruments brought by Portuguese immigrants (Day 1, facts page, Day 4 card, Day 5, bingo clues Ukulele, Hawaii). No maker names, ship or year of arrival is printed because accounts differ
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukulele (fetched 25 Nov 2026: developed in the 1880s in Hawaii from the machete, cavaquinho and rajao brought by Portuguese immigrants from Madeira, the Azores and Cape Verde; standard ukuleles have four nylon strings); https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/501343 and https://www.history.com/articles/ukulele-origins-history (web search 25 Nov 2026: probably introduced in 1879 by Madeiran settlers; details such as the ship and the machete-versus-rajao question are contested).
