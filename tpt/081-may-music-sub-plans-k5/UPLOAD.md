## 2. Description

```
Need sub plans a substitute can teach without any music background, any technology, or any prep from you? These eight May music days are ready to print and hand over. Each day has a step-by-step plan with timed steps and words to say, plus two student sheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5. The theme is May: Asian American and Pacific Islander (AAPI) Heritage Month (five instruments from across Asia and the Pacific, each drawn and described on paper: the Japanese koto, the Chinese erhu, the Indian sitar, the Korean janggu and the ukulele, which developed in Hawaii), a thank-you card for Mother's Day that works for any special grown-up, a short and optional Memorial Day moment, and the end of the school year.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (48 pages, PDF, US Letter)
• Cover and teacher notes with the May note, setup steps and a page guide (2 pages)
• Sub quick start, welcome sheet, days at a glance, quick games, word-rhythm chart, May facts and a class sign (7 pages)
• 8 sub plans, one per day, each 30–45 minutes (8 pages)
• 16 student sheets: a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet for each day (16 pages)
• Day extras: movement picture cards, chant word cards, a chant book page and a read-aloud quiz (4 pages)
• Early finishers (2 pages)
• Exit tickets (1 page)
• Answer keys for Days 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (3 pages)
• Sub report form, folder labels, class star awards, terms of use and a thank-you page (5 pages)

THE EIGHT DAYS
• Day 1, Heritage Reader: K–2 hear a short reading about Asian American and Pacific Islander Heritage Month, five instruments, a thank-you day and the end of the school year and circle answers; 3–5 read a passage of about 200 words and answer five questions
• Day 2, Instrument Name Claps: clap the syllables of instrument and May words and a short original four-line poem; K–2 color a circle for each clap, 3–5 sort words by syllables and build a four-beat chant
• Day 3, Thank-You Card Studio: design a thank-you card for a mom, a special grown-up or anyone who helps you, with an instrument on the front and a four-beat chant inside (paper only, nothing to cut or mail); K–2 draw and circle, 3–5 label parts and write a message
• Day 4, Pluck, Bow and Freeze: move to a tapped beat, try call and response, and freeze, with 8 picture cards (koto, erhu, sitar, janggu, ukulele, flower, heart, sun); K–2 circle how they moved, 3–5 reflect and design a freeze game
• Day 5, Instrument Word Hunt: instrument and place word puzzles; K–2 match words to pictures and trace, 3–5 word search plus a decode-the-message puzzle
• Day 6, Music at Work: five music jobs, from conductor to radio host; K–2 draw a job, 3–5 match jobs and write a job ad or interview questions
• Day 7, Thank-You Chant Book: build four-beat chants from instrument and thank-you word cards, mark the syllables, share with a partner, and keep a chant book page
• Day 8, Heritage Bingo Day: bingo (12 squares for K–2, 16 for 3–5), a read-aloud quiz and Pass the Pencil, with a look back at the year

WHY IT WORKS FOR SUBS
• No tech: nothing needs a computer, projector, speaker or video. Paper, pencils, crayons, hands and voices only
• No music skills: rhythm is taught through speech rhythm, clapping the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. The sub never reads music or sings
• No prep: print, put each day in a folder, and go. Each day works in any order for a mixed K–5 class
• Facts are short, sourced and secular, and the words for saying each instrument's name are on the facts page, so the days work in public schools and for students of every background
```
