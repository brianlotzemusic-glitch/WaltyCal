The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a scene, a tempo and a storm arc on the timeline planner (page 18) whose bars add up to 60 to 90 seconds. If the sums are wrong now, the whole plan is wrong later: use seconds = bars × 240 ÷ BPM.

If you only have three classes

Automate only two lanes (cutoff and volume) and keep reverb and EQ to the basics, so Class 3 covers automation, space, mix and bouncing. Use the last ten minutes of Class 3 for short presentations, and set the reflection page as homework.

Winter SoundscapeSound palette · 1 of 2
STUDENT
Class 1

Every layer in a storm has a job and a zone, the range of pitch it needs (page 17). These cards describe the sounds in words, not presets: use them as recipes and make them your own. Cards 1 to 4 are the body of the storm. Use at least six cards across the two pages; at least two must be made in Alchemy.

1 · Wind bedZone 200 Hz–4 kHz

Its job. The constant body of the storm and the glue of the mix.

Make it. Alchemy with a noise source (pink or white noise). Low-pass or band-pass filter with the cutoff near 800 Hz and little resonance. A slow LFO on the cutoff, about one cycle every 6 to 10 seconds, so the wind breathes.

Automate it. Open the cutoff from the hush to the peak; close it again in the fade.

2 · Gust swellsZone 300 Hz–3 kHz

Its job. Gusts that come and go, so the wind is never flat.

Make it. The same noise as the wind bed on its own track, or one more Alchemy source. Draw volume and cutoff swells of 2 to 4 seconds, each followed by a fall. A longer, softer attack makes a gust sound like it arrives from far away.

Automate it. Draw a Swell curve (page 15) every few bars; make the gusts bigger toward the peak.

3 · Low rumbleZone 30–120 Hz

Its job. Weight under the storm; you feel it more than you hear it.

Make it. Retro Synth or Alchemy: a sine or the sub oscillator on one low note (about 45 Hz) with a trace of noise and a low-pass filter. A slow volume wobble helps. Keep it in the centre of the stereo picture.

Automate it. Bring it in at the middle of the build; take it out in the fade.

4 · Frozen padZone 120 Hz–3 kHz

Its job. A cold, held tone that gives the storm a pitch to sit on. It is a drone, not a tune.

Make it. Alchemy, two or three oscillators slightly detuned, on one low note and its fifth. Attack 3 s or more, release 6 s or more, a gentle low-pass filter. A slow chorus or Step FX filter movement keeps it alive.

Automate it. Automate reverb send: wetter in the hush and fade, drier at the peak.

Class 3

Four lanes are enough to make a storm move. Here are the four lanes of the worked example (pages 9–10) under its four sections. The sections’ bars decide when each curve turns; the values are in the table below.

The four parameters ·

ParameterIt changesWhere it isA storm idea
Filter cutoffHow bright a sound isAlchemy or Auto Filter: the cutoffWind: low in the hush, wide open at the peak
VolumeHow loud a layer isThe channel fader or a Gain plug-inWind: from very quiet to the loudest moment
PanWhere a sound sits, left to rightThe Pan knob on the channel stripIce: tick from left to right and back
Reverb sendHow far away it soundsThe Send knob to a reverb on an auxPad: wet in the hush and fade, dry at the peak

Reading the picture

The cutoff and the volume of the wind both rise through the build and are highest in the peak. The pad’s reverb send does the opposite: it is lowest at the peak and highest at the ends. The ice’s pan swings wider each time. Three curves that move together make the peak feel big; one that moves the other way gives it depth.
