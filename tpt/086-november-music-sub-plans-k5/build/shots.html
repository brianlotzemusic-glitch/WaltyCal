Acorn ClapsDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of November words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), ac-orn (2 claps), No-vem-ber (3 claps), com-mu-ni-ty (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Acorns drop down. Thankful for you. Drums go tap, tap. November’s here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant share
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Thank-You Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

November NotebookDay 1 · Reading about November · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about Veterans Day, Native American Heritage Month and a time for saying thank you, and answer questions.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Opening question
    Say: “November has big ideas: Veterans Day, Native American Heritage Month and a time for saying thank you.” Ask: What is one thing you are glad about? Take five answers. Any answer is fine. Veterans Day, Native American Heritage Month and Thanksgiving: read the facts as printed, never ask who celebrates what or whose family has a veteran, and see page 2.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. The gratitude choice line on the sheet says what to skip. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Soft Rain or Acorn Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 5; keep the reading and the questions.

Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
November is the eleventh month of the year. In the Northern Hemisphere, November is in fall, and in the Southern Hemisphere it is in spring. Veterans Day is on November 11. It honors people who served in the United States military. November is also Native American Heritage Month in the United States. There are many Native nations, and they have many different music traditions. Thanksgiving is on the fourth Thursday of November. Many families use the day to say thank you.

November Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8. Scissors are optional, and only for cutting out the picture cards (Day 4) or the word cards (Day 7)
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

Drop, Drift and FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
acorn
Crouch small like an acorn, then slowly grow tall like an oak tree, then freeze.
leaf
Float down slowly like a falling leaf, then freeze.
wheat
Stand tall and sway side to side like wheat in the wind, then freeze.
basket
Reach down, pick up a pretend heavy basket, carry it two steps and set it down gently.
grapes
Bounce gently in place, light and round like a bunch of grapes, then freeze.
pear
Make a pear shape with your arms, wide at the bottom and narrow at the top, then freeze.
heart
Put one hand on your heart, take a slow breath and freeze with a smile.
star
Stand tall and still like a star in the sky, then freeze.
