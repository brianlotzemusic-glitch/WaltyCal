Teacher Notes

November Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · Gratitude, Veterans Day, Native American Heritage Month

Why November matters

November brings several ideas into a music room. In the United States, Veterans Day on November 11 honors people who have served in the United States military; it was originally called Armistice Day, and in 1954 its name was changed to Veterans Day. November is also Native American Heritage Month: in 1990 President George H. W. Bush approved a joint resolution naming November 1990 National American Indian Heritage Month, and similar proclamations have been issued every year since 1994. Thanksgiving is on the fourth Thursday of November. In the Northern Hemisphere, November is in fall (in the Southern Hemisphere, it is in spring).

Veterans Day: simple and respectful

  • Keep it simple and respectful: Veterans Day honors people who have served in the United States military. There is no war imagery and nothing violent anywhere in the bundle.
  • Some students have a family member who is serving or served, and some have lost someone. Never ask whose family has a veteran. If a student wants to share, listen kindly and thank them.

Native American Heritage Month, with care

  • The days say “many” or “some” Native nations. Native nations are living, different communities today, each with its own history, languages and traditions. No drawing in this bundle is a sacred or ceremonial object, a costume or a symbol of a nation, and nobody is asked to imitate a ceremony or a dance.
  • Please learn about the Native nations local to your school. Start with the official pages of nearby nations. Native Land Digital (native-land.ca) is a nonprofit interactive map of Indigenous territories, treaties and languages; it says that it does not show official or legal boundaries. It is a resource for you before class: nothing in the plans uses it.

Thanksgiving: optional, gratitude first

  • Thanksgiving is optional here. The bundle does not retell the story of the first Thanksgiving, and it shows no costumes. It keeps to gratitude, the fall harvest, family and community. Some families have complicated feelings about the holiday, so every Day 1 sheet has a Gratitude choice line that swaps it for the question “What is one thing you are glad about?”

Why subs can teach it

Each lesson has timed steps, words to say and a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet. Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words. Nobody has to read music or make music; games need only voices, hands and a pencil.

Standards

Supports relating music to varied contexts (MU:Cn11.0) and generating musical ideas (MU:Cr1.1), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Prints well in black and white (facts: page 8). All lessons, chants, games, words and art are original; no lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps every game safe and happy.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Soft Rain.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may know other musicians or celebrate other traditions. Thank them and move on.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Soft Rain to settle a class, Thank-You Claps to wake it up, and Acorn Echo to finish with a smile.

Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
November is the eleventh month, and it brings several observances. In the Northern Hemisphere, November is in fall, and in the Southern Hemisphere it is in spring. Veterans Day is on November 11. It honors people who have served in the United States military. The day was originally called Armistice Day, and in 1954 its name was changed to Veterans Day. November is also Native American Heritage Month. In 1990 President George H. W. Bush approved a joint resolution naming November 1990 National American Indian Heritage Month, and similar proclamations have been issued every year since 1994, under names that include Native American Heritage Month. Thanksgiving is on the fourth Thursday of November, and many families use it to say thank you. Saying thanks is a good habit on any day, and careful listening is where every musician starts.

Acorn ClapsDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of November words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), ac-orn (2 claps), No-vem-ber (3 claps), com-mu-ni-ty (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Acorns drop down. Thankful for you. Drums go tap, tap. November’s here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant share
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Thank-You Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Instrument WorkshopDay 3 · Invent an instrument · no tech, no prep
DAY 3
Sub plan
GoalStudents learn four ways an instrument can make sound (tap, shake, blow, pluck), then invent an instrument of their own, name it and label its parts.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. The four sound words are printed on the worksheets and on page 8.
CopiesK–2: page 17
3–5: page 18
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Four ways to make sound
    Say: “An instrument is a tool for making sound. You can tap it, shake it, blow into it or pluck it.” Point to the four pictures on the sheet and say each sound word with the class. Clap the syllables of each word. Many Native nations have traditional drums, rattles and flutes, and the traditions differ from nation to nation; the 3–5 sheet says so, and nobody needs to say more.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Sound hands
    Say: “Let’s show each way with our hands, with no noise at all.” Students tap one knee for tap, shake both hands for shake, hold a pretend flute near their mouth for blow, and pinch the air with one finger for pluck.
  3. 20 minStep 3
    Invent an instrument
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 17, 3–5 page 18. Say: “Make up an instrument of your own, give it a name, and show how it makes sound.” Read the directions aloud. Students invent something new: they do not need to copy a real instrument.
  4. 5 minStep 4
    Gallery walk
    Students hold up their instruments one table at a time. Walk the room and ask three students: How does your instrument make its sound?
Extra time? Soft Rain or Acorn Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip the gallery walk.

Drop, Drift and FreezeDay 4 · Move like fall and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents move to a steady tap, copy the sub’s moves with their own (call and response), and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them, or cut them out with scissors).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Rules of the freeze
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Practice once: tap a pencil on a desk for five seconds, stop, and wait for stillness.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Call and response
    Say: “Musicians often answer each other: one calls, the other answers.” Make a simple move (a clap, a head nod, a shoulder shrug) and say “Call!” The class copies it and says “Answer!” Do five moves. Then let a few students be the caller.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap. Use at least six cards, and try a slow tap, a fast tap and a bouncy tap (long, short, long, short, like skipping). The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 20, 3–5 page 21. Students sit and fill it in. Read the directions aloud.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Statue patch
    Tap a few beats, stop, and let the class freeze in a favorite pose, such as an oak tree, a star or a leaf. Walk around and name two poses you notice.
Extra time? Oak Tree Freeze or Leaf Pile Stomp from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Use only four cards.
