Every square is something students can do on any instrument or with their voice. "Play" means play or sing. Each board has 24 different challenges and a FREE center. Each of the 48 challenges is on 2 boards.

Differentiation

Grades 4–5: aim for 10 to 15 minutes a day. Start with Boards 1 and 2. Read the challenges aloud, let students use the log as a checklist, and count a single line as a win.

Grades 6–8: aim for 20 to 30 minutes a day. Use all 4 boards, ask for the goal and reflection lines every week, let a student run the caller sheet, and set a blackout as the stretch goal.

How to WinPractice Bingo rules for students
RULES
4 ways to win
Practice Bingo turns practice into a game. Your goal is to mark squares in a line, and every square is a real practice challenge.

How to play in 5 steps

1

Pick a bingo board from pages 11–14. Write your name on it.

2

Do a challenge during your practice. Write your minutes and what you practiced on your weekly log (pages 5–8).

3

Mark the square with a check, a date or a color. Each square counts once.

4

Ask a parent, guardian or teacher to initial the squares you finished.

5

Make a line, then try for a blackout. The FREE square is already marked.

Four ways to win

Row5 squares in a line across.
Column5 squares in a line down.
Diagonal5 squares corner to corner.
BlackoutAll 25 squares marked.

A board has 5 rows + 5 columns + 2 diagonals = 12 possible lines. The FREE square in the center counts for the row, the column and the diagonals that cross it. A blackout needs all 25 squares: 24 challenges and the FREE square.

Keep going

Finish a line, then finish another one. Then try the next board. A square earned with care is worth more than a quick check mark, because every challenge is a way to practice smarter.
