TEACHER
Read first

How it works

  1. You choose the music. Any recording, live performance or class performance works. No pieces are named in this resource, so you can use it with any music you teach.
  2. Set one listening focus (one response page) and say it before you press play.
  3. Play the music one to three times, as the guide on page 3 suggests, and let students respond.
  4. Share answers aloud, then have students stamp the glyphs they used (page 4).

What is inside (19 pages)

  • 10 response pages, pages 6–15, each with two prompt tracks.
  • 4 listening glyphs: Contour, Pulse, Dynamics and Timbre. A key (page 4) and a stamp sheet (page 5).
  • A vocabulary bank of 40 words in 6 groups (pages 16–17).
  • Prompt guide and pacing (page 3), self-check and rubric (page 18).

Two tracks, one page

Every response page has Track A for grades 2–4 and Track B for grades 5–8. The page, the drawing space and the word banks are the same, so a mixed class can listen together. Track A asks for choosing, circling and one short sentence. Track B asks for a reason, evidence from the music and vocabulary words. Grades 4 and 5 can use either track.

Make it reusable

  • Copy a page as often as you like for your own students. The response pages are blank on purpose: write the piece name on the line at the top, not in advance.
  • Slide the pages into plastic sleeves and use dry-erase markers, or print them on the back of scrap paper.
  • Glyphs are quick answers. When time is short, students stamp, draw or circle a glyph instead of writing. Use the stamp sheet (page 5) for cut-out stamps.
  • Build a journal. Staple the vocabulary bank and a stack of response pages behind the listening log (page 14).

Standards and notes

The pages support the Responding processes of the National Core Arts Standards for Music (MU:Re7.1, MU:Re8.1 and MU:Re9.1). Match them to your own curriculum. Tempo and dynamics words follow standard Italian terms. The beat examples on page 11 use unpitched notes on a one-line rhythm staff. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Choosing music

  • Pick a piece, or a one-to-three-minute excerpt, with something clear to listen for.
  • Vary the music across the year: different styles, places, times and ensembles.

Supporting every listener

Younger or newer listeners: use Track A, read prompts aloud and let students stamp glyphs instead of writing. Older or experienced listeners: use Track B and have partners compare reasons.

A 10-minute routine

  1. 1 minute: name the focus and read the prompt together.
  2. 2 to 4 minutes: first listen. Students only listen, or stamp a glyph.
  3. 3 minutes: second listen while students respond on the page.
  4. 2 minutes: partner talk, then two or three students share.
  5. 1 minute: stamp the glyphs that fit and pick a vocabulary word to use next time.

One page a week fits a once-a-week class. Pages 6–13 build skills in order. The log (page 14) can run from week 1.

GLYPHS
4 listening glyphs

A listening glyph is a quick-response picture. Draw it, stamp it or circle it to show what you noticed. The glyphs also appear on page 6, page 7, page 8, page 10, page 11, page 13, page 14 and page 15.

ContourMelody contour

It means: The melody moves: it goes up, goes down, or stays level.

Stamp it when you notice the tune rising, falling or leaping.

Use with the melody shape page (page 10). Add a word: up, down, level, steps or leaps.

PulseRhythm and beat

It means: The steady beat, and the pattern of long and short sounds on top of it.

Stamp it when you can tap the beat or hear a rhythm pattern.

Use with the beat and meter page (page 11). Add a word: steady, speeding up or slowing down.

DynamicsLoud and soft

It means: How loud or soft the music is, and when it gets louder or softer.

Stamp it when the volume changes or stays strong.

Use with the tempo and dynamics page (page 8). Add a letter or word: p, mp, mf, f, crescendo or decrescendo.

TimbreInstrument color

It means: The color of a sound: what makes a flute sound different from a drum.

Stamp it when you notice an instrument or voice and how it sounds.

Use with the instruments page (page 7). Add the instrument or a color word, such as bright or warm.

VOCABULARY
40 words · 1 of 2
WordSay itWhat it means
Beat and rhythm6 words
beat The steady pulse in music, like a heartbeat you can tap.
rhythm The pattern of long and short sounds and silences over the beat.
meter How the beats are grouped into strong and weak, usually in sets of 2, 3 or 4.
measure One group of beats between two bar lines. It is also called a bar.
accent A note played or sung with extra stress so it stands out.
rest A silence that lasts for a certain number of beats.
Tempo8 words
tempo The speed of the beat.
largoLAR-goVery slow and broad.
andanteahn-DAHN-tayA walking speed, easy and a little slow.
moderatomod-er-AH-toeA medium speed.
allegroah-LEG-rowFast and lively.
prestoPRESS-toeVery fast.
accelerandoak-sel-er-AHN-doeGradually getting faster.
ritardandoree-tar-DAHN-doeGradually getting slower.
Melody and pitch6 words
pitch How high or low a sound is.
melody The tune: a line of pitches you could hum or sing.
contour The shape of a melody as it moves up, down or stays level.
step A move from one note to the very next note, up or down.
leap A jump from one note to a note that is farther away.
phrase A musical sentence: an idea in the melody with a beginning and an ending.

Dynamics7 words
dynamics How loud or soft the music is.
pianopee-AH-noSoft. The symbol is p.
mezzo pianoMET-so pee-AH-noMedium soft. The symbol is mp.
mezzo forteMET-so FOR-tayMedium loud. The symbol is mf.
forteFOR-tayLoud. The symbol is f.
crescendokreh-SHEN-doeGradually getting louder. The hairpin opens wider.
decrescendoday-kreh-SHEN-doeGradually getting softer. The hairpin closes.
Instruments and sound7 words
timbreTAM-berThe color or quality of a sound that tells instruments and voices apart.
strings Instruments with strings that are bowed or plucked, such as violin, cello and harp.
woodwinds Instruments played by blowing air across an opening or against a reed, such as flute and clarinet.
brass Metal instruments played by buzzing the lips into a mouthpiece, such as trumpet and tuba.
percussion Instruments played by hitting, shaking or scraping, such as drums and xylophone.
ensembleahn-SAHM-bulA group of musicians who play or sing together.
solo One performer playing or singing alone.
Form and texture6 words
form The plan of a piece: the order of its sections.
section A big part of a piece, often labeled with a letter such as A or B.
repeat Music that comes back again, the same or almost the same.
contrast A change that makes one part sound different from the part before it.
texture How many sounds or layers are heard at once, thin or thick.
accompaniment Music that supports the melody.
