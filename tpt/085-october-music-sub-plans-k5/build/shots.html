Crunch and ClapDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of October words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), pump-kin (2 claps), mar-i-gold (3 claps), cel-e-bra-tion (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Leaves rustle down. Pumpkins grow big. Drums go thump, thump. October’s here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. The harvest choice line on the sheet lets you swap the Halloween words. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant share
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Harvest Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

October LookoutDay 1 · Reading about October · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about the fall harvest, Halloween and Day of the Dead, and answer questions.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Opening question
    Say: “October has big ideas: the fall harvest, Halloween and Day of the Dead.” Ask: What is one thing you like about fall? Take five answers. Any answer is fine. Halloween and Day of the Dead: both are optional, so read the facts as printed, never ask who celebrates what, and see page 2.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. The harvest choice line on the sheet says what to skip. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Calm Leaves or Leaf Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 5; keep the reading and the questions.

Harvest choice: Halloween and Day of the Dead are both optional. To skip Halloween, skip sentence 3 and question 2. To skip Day of the Dead, skip sentences 4–5 and question 4.
Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
October is the tenth month of the year. In the fall, many farms pick apples, pumpkins and corn. Halloween is on October 31, and some families celebrate it and some do not. In Mexico, Day of the Dead is on November 1 and 2. Families remember and honor loved ones who have died, with marigolds and music. Hispanic Heritage Month ends on October 15. In the Northern Hemisphere, many leaves change color in fall. In the Southern Hemisphere, October is in spring.

October Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8. Scissors are optional if you cut out the picture cards (Day 4) or word cards (Day 7)
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

Float, Stomp and FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
Harvest choice: the first six cards are harvest cards. Skip the bat and ghost cards if you like.
pumpkin
Roll your arms in a big circle like a round pumpkin, then freeze.
apple
Reach up high and pick a pretend apple, then put it in a basket.
corn
Stand tall with your arms up and sway like corn in the wind, then freeze.
leaf
Float down slowly like a falling leaf, then freeze.
marigold
Curl up small like a seed, then slowly open your arms like a flower.
drum
Pat a pretend drum with two pretend sticks, one tap for each beat.
bat
Flap your arms slowly like wings, then freeze.
ghost
Float slowly with soft, wavy arms, then freeze. Stay silent.
