Teacher Notes

October Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · Fall harvest, Halloween, Day of the Dead

Why October matters

October brings several ideas into a music room. In the Northern Hemisphere it is harvest time, when many farms pick apples, pumpkins and corn (in the Southern Hemisphere, October is in spring). Halloween is on October 31; many families celebrate it and many do not, so every day offers a harvest choice. Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos) is a Mexican tradition on November 1 and 2, so the pages may say early November: families honor and remember loved ones who have died, with marigolds (cempasúchil) and music, and UNESCO added it to its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2008. Hispanic Heritage Month in the United States runs from September 15 to October 15. The music stays simple: clapping, marching in place and saying words. Facts are kept short and secular.

Halloween: optional and friendly

  • Halloween here is spooky but never scary: a friendly pumpkin, a silly ghost and a bat. There is no gore, no violence and no scary story anywhere in the bundle.
  • Some families do not celebrate Halloween. Every student sheet that mentions it has a Harvest choice line, and the harvest cards, words and scenes always stand on their own. Never ask a student whether they celebrate. The origins of Halloween are not covered.

Teaching Day of the Dead with care

  • Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos) is a Mexican tradition on November 1 and 2 that honors and remembers loved ones who have died. It is a celebration with marigolds and music, it is not Halloween, and it is not a costume.
  • It appears only briefly: in the Day 1 readings and questions, on the facts page, and as the marigold in a few cards and puzzles. Read it as printed, or skip it: the Harvest choice line on the Day 1 sheets says exactly what to leave out.
  • A student may have lost someone. Say “We remember people we love” and move on. Do not ask students to share about a loved one who died; if one wants to, listen kindly and leave a note for the classroom teacher on the sub report.

Why subs can teach it

Eight lessons, each with timed steps, words to say and a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet. Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. Nobody has to read music or make music with an instrument. Games use only voices, hands and a pencil.

Standards

Supports relating music to varied contexts (MU:Cn11.0) and generating musical ideas (MU:Cr1.1), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Prints well in black and white. The facts used in the bundle are on page 8.

All lessons, chants, games, words and art are original. No lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps every game safe and happy.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Calm Leaves.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may know other musicians or celebrate other traditions. Thank them and move on.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Calm Leaves to settle a class, Harvest Claps to wake it up, and Leaf Echo to finish with a smile.

Read, Answer and DrawRead, answer and draw
DAY 1
Grades 3–5
NameDate
Harvest choice: Halloween and Day of the Dead are both optional. To skip Halloween, skip question 1. To skip Day of the Dead, skip questions 3 and 4.
Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
October is a month of falling leaves and many traditions. In the Northern Hemisphere, fall is harvest time: many farms pick apples, pumpkins and corn, and communities gather at markets and festivals. In the Southern Hemisphere, October is in spring. Halloween is on October 31. Many families celebrate it with costumes and carved pumpkins, and many families do not celebrate it. Hispanic Heritage Month in the United States runs from September 15 to October 15. In early November, on November 1 and 2, people in Mexico celebrate Day of the Dead, or Día de Muertos, a tradition that honors and remembers loved ones who have died. It is a celebration with marigolds, called cempasúchil, and with music, and it is separate from Halloween. In 2008 UNESCO added the tradition to its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Music is part of gatherings all through the fall, from harvest festivals to family celebrations, and careful listening is where a musician starts. Rhythm is in the world too: a rake scraping leaves, a bite of an apple and a drum tapped with a stick all make sounds you can count. This month, listen for the rustle, the crunch and the thump of fall.

Crunch and ClapDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of October words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), pump-kin (2 claps), mar-i-gold (3 claps), cel-e-bra-tion (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Leaves rustle down. Pumpkins grow big. Drums go thump, thump. October’s here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. The harvest choice line on the sheet lets you swap the Halloween words. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant share
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Harvest Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Fall Sound ScenesDay 3 · Draw a scene and label its sounds · no tech, no prep
DAY 3
Sub plan
GoalStudents meet five sounds of fall, then draw a fall scene, label the sounds they would hear in it and say how each sound is made.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. The sound words are printed on the worksheets and on page 8.
CopiesK–2: page 17
3–5: page 18
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Meet the sounds
    Say: “Fall is full of sounds: a leaf rustles, an apple crunches, a pumpkin thumps, a drum booms. A silly cartoon ghost says whoo!” Point to the pictures on the sheet and say each sound word with the class. Clap the syllables of each word. The ghost is optional.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Sound hands
    Say: “Let’s show each sound with our hands, with no noise at all.” Students wiggle fingers for rustle, squeeze a fist for crunch, tap one knee for thump and open both hands wide for boom.
  3. 20 minStep 3
    Scene page
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 17, 3–5 page 18. Say: “Pick a scene, draw it big and clear, then label the sounds you would hear.” Read the directions aloud. The scenes are a pumpkin patch, a harvest market, a marigold garden or a silly Halloween night; any one is fine.
  4. 5 minStep 4
    Gallery walk
    Students hold up their scenes one table at a time. Walk the room and ask three students: Which sound would be loudest in your scene?
Extra time? Calm Leaves or Leaf Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip the gallery walk.

Float, Stomp and FreezeDay 4 · Move like fall and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents move to a steady tap, copy the sub’s moves with their own (call and response), and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them or cut them out).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Rules of the freeze
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Practice once: tap a pencil on a desk for five seconds, stop, and wait for stillness.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Call and response
    Say: “Musicians often answer each other: one calls, the other answers.” Make a simple move (a clap, a head nod, a shoulder shrug) and say “Call!” The class copies it and says “Answer!” Do five moves. Then let a few students be the caller.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap. Use at least six cards, and try a slow tap, a fast tap and a bouncy tap (long, short, long, short, like skipping). The first six cards are harvest cards; the bat and ghost cards are optional. The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
