Teacher Notes

Nutcracker Listening Unit · Tchaikovsky, 1892 · grades 2–6

What it is

A short unit that takes students through Tchaikovsky’s ballet The Nutcracker by listening. They read the story, then listen to six famous pieces with a listening map and response page for each, spot instruments, take a quiz and write a review. Every answer comes with a key.

Before you start: choose a recording

No audio is included. Use any recording you have the right to play in class: a school or library music service, your own collection, or an orchestral recording in the public domain. The music is by Tchaikovsky (1840–1893) and is long out of copyright, but many recordings are not, so check the recording you use. Listen to it yourself first, and find the movements by title.

How each listening page works

  1. Read the “About this music” box together.
  2. Play the movement once while students just listen.
  3. Play it again while they mark the map. Say “next” about every 15 seconds so the boxes keep pace with the music.
  4. Play it a third time if you like. Students circle what they heard and write: grades 2–3 finish a sentence, grades 4–6 write three.

Grade levels

Grades 2–3 use the sentence starters; grades 4–6 write full sentences with the toolkit words. Read the quiz aloud to grade 2.

Good to know

  • The story is set at a Christmas Eve party, as in the original. The unit is about music and ballet, with no religious teaching, and works in any winter program.
  • Productions differ; the story here is one common version. Prints well in black and white.

The Nutcracker StoryMeet the ballet · the story in six scenes
READ
Story summary
The composerPyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840–1893) was a Russian composer. He also wrote the ballets Swan Lake and The Sleeping Beauty.
The balletThe Nutcracker was first performed on December 18, 1892, at the Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg, Russia. The choreography was by Lev Ivanov, from plans by Marius Petipa.
The storyIt comes from a tale by E. T. A. Hoffmann (1816), retold by Alexandre Dumas in 1844.
A balletA ballet tells a story with dance and music, but no talking. The orchestra plays all the music. Tchaikovsky also made a concert suite of eight pieces from it that is played without dancers.
Hearing itYou will hear six pieces (movements). Each one matches a part of the story.
1
Act I · The party
On Christmas Eve, Clara and her brother Fritz welcome guests. Her godfather Drosselmeyer, a toymaker, brings gifts, including a nutcracker doll shaped like a soldier. (Movement 1, March)
2
Act I · Midnight
Fritz breaks the nutcracker, and Clara tucks it in. At midnight the Christmas tree grows tall and mice creep in. The Nutcracker leads the toy soldiers against the Mouse King. Clara throws her slipper, and the Nutcracker wins and turns into a prince.
3
Act I · The snowy forest
The Prince takes Clara through a forest where snowflakes dance. (Movement 2, Waltz of the Snowflakes)
4
Act II · The Kingdom of Sweets
They arrive in a land of sweets ruled by the Sugar Plum Fairy. She welcomes them and orders dances to be shown for Clara. (Movement 3, Trepak; Movement 4, Reed Flutes)
5
Act II · The flowers and the Fairy
The flowers dance a waltz. The Sugar Plum Fairy and the Prince dance. (Movement 5, Waltz of the Flowers; Movement 6, Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy)
6
Act II · Goodbye
Clara says goodbye to her new friends. In many versions she wakes up at home, wondering if it was a dream.
Every company tells the story a little differently: names, scenes and endings change from one production to the next. This is one common version.

Listening ToolkitWords for describing music
LEARN
Listening toolkit
These are the words you will use to describe music. Keep this page next to you while you listen.
WordWhat it meansTry saying it
TempoHow fast or slow the beat is. adagio = slow, moderato = medium, allegro = fast.“The tempo is fast.”
DynamicsHow loud or soft. piano (p) = soft, forte (f) = loud. Crescendo = gets louder, decrescendo = gets softer.“It starts soft and gets louder.”
BeatThe steady pulse you can tap.“I can tap the beat.”
MeterHow the beats group: in 2s (march or gallop), in 3s (waltz) or in 4s.“It feels like ONE-two-three.”
PitchHow high or low a sound is.“The tune goes up high.”
TimbreThe color of a sound: which instrument or voice you hear.“It sounds like tiny bells.”
MoodThe feeling the music gives you.“It sounds magical.”
The four families of the orchestra
FamilyHow the sound is madeExamples in this unit
StringsBowed or plucked stringsviolin, harp
WoodwindsBlowing air across or through a pipe or reedflute, bass clarinet
BrassBuzzing lips into a metal tubetrumpet, French horn
PercussionHitting, shaking or scraping. A celesta has keys that strike metal bars.celesta
How to listen: three times
1Just listen. Close your eyes. Do not write yet.
2Listen with your listening map. Mark as you go.
3Circle what you heard and tell about it.
Be a polite audience
  • Quiet voices and bodies so everyone can hear.
  • There are no wrong feelings about music. Say why you feel that way.

About this music
  • A trepak is a fast folk dance from Russia. Dancers leap and kick.
  • It is in groups of 2 beats (2/4), like a gallop: ONE-two, ONE-two.
  • It starts fast and keeps speeding up to a big finish.

Instrument gallery
Meet the eight sounds you will hunt for. Next, use the spotting chart on page 13.
Violin
Strings
Bowed strings. The violin section is the heart of the orchestra.
Harp
Strings
Plucked strings. Sweeping glides are called glissandos.
Flute
Woodwinds
A high woodwind you blow across. Light and bird-like.
Bass clarinet
Woodwinds
A very low woodwind with a curved metal bell.
Trumpet
Brass
Bright, bold brass. Often plays calls and fanfares.
French horn
Brass
A round, warm brass sound from a coiled tube.
Celesta
Keyboard percussion
A keyboard that strikes metal bars. Sounds like tiny bells.
Children's voices
Voices
Singers. In the ballet, children sing without words.
Did you know? Tchaikovsky heard the celesta in Paris and brought one to Russia for The Nutcracker. Most listeners had never heard it before 1892. It is played with a keyboard, but a hammer strikes a metal bar inside, so it sounds like a tiny bell.

Listening maps (answers vary)
Trepak: the needle moves toward FAST by the end. Reed Flutes: three flutes. Sugar Plum Fairy: the celesta is the sparkly high sound, the bass clarinet the low one. Flowers: the harp plays first; the tune begins after it. March: mark loud and soft as you hear them, and accept anything the class can support. Snowflakes: voices join the orchestra partway through. Maps and responses are about careful listening, so give credit for reasons.
