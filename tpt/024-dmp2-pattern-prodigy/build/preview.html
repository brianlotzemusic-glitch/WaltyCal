The one thing to show on Class 1

Put a hi-hat on a grid and give a step a 50% chance (my third struggle). Loop it for a few bars so the class hears it change. Do the same on a bass row so it is not only for drums.

Classes 3–5

Working days; Class 5 has no new slide. Circulate with one question: which feature changed the sound you just heard?

Pattern ProdigyWorked example · an invented groove
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Night Shift”, described on paper: no audio is included. Students’ projects will differ. 20 bars at 96 BPM, four sections, five layers, five Step Sequencer features (page 1 of 2).

1 · A weak plan

I will make a beat with drums and a bass.

It names no features and nothing says what will change.

2 · A clear plan

I am making “Night Shift”, a 50-second groove at 96 BPM. It opens with kick and snare, and the hi-hat row is unmuted at bar 3. A bass pattern arrives at bar 5 and a synth arp at bar 9. I will use five Step Sequencer features: probability on the hat’s even steps, velocity accents, a cutoff on every step of the arp, mute and unmute, and an alternate drum pattern for the Build. Four automation lanes keep it moving, and in the last four bars it thins out.

3 · One bar of the groove: 16 steps ·

The bass plays C, Eb, G and Bb (C minor 7) on steps 1, 4, 7, 11 and 14, and the synth arp climbs C, Eb, G, Bb, C and comes back down on the odd steps. Every pitch is a chord tone, so the layers agree. Hat velocity: 100 on steps 1, 5, 9, 13 and 70 on the other odd steps; the even steps are ghosts at 25% probability.

4 · The five features it uses ·

Probabilitythe hi-hat’s even steps play 25% of the time; raised by hand to 60% in the Build
Velocityhat accents on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 (100) over softer ones (70); kick and snare at about 90 to 110
Step automationthe synth arp’s filter cutoff has its own value on every step, rising from 15 to 97
Mute / unmutethe hat row is unmuted at bar 3; the snare row is muted at bar 19
Pattern alternatea second drum pattern, B, plays in bars 13–16 and then the first returns

STUDENT

NameDate

Requirements ·

Length: 30–60 seconds
Use at least 3 different Step Sequencer features
Include at least one drum kit, one bass or synth, and one additional element
Demonstrate balance, clarity, and creative use of space in your mix

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Explore — Open the Step Sequencer and experiment with basic drum patterns (Pattern grids: page 13)
  2. 2
    Build rhythms — Create your core drum and percussion patterns
  3. 3
    Add melodic layers — Program bass and synth patterns that complement your groove
  4. 4
    Automate and vary — Use probability, velocity, and automation to make patterns evolve (Feature tracker: page 14)
  5. 5
    Mix and refine — Apply EQ, panning, and plug-ins. Arrange into a cohesive piece. (Arrangement plan: page 15; Listen-back sheet: page 16)
  6. 6
    Finalize — Bounce your track and prepare your presentation
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.
