What this is ·

A 7-class-period structured project for high school music technology. Each student builds a 30–60 second groove in Logic with the Step Sequencer and Pattern Regions: drums, a bass or synth and one more element, with probability, velocity and automation so the patterns evolve. They bounce it, reflect and present it with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (type: structured; focus: Step Sequencer), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Set up the Presentation Padlet, practise the hi-hat demo and decide which features count (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 13–23), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–10), Class 3 (slides 11–13), Class 4 (slides 14–15), Class 6 (slides 16–18) and Class 7 (slides 19–20). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

Pattern Prodigy · Structured · Step Sequencer
Master Logic’s Step Sequencer with automation, probability, and velocity to build evolving grooves.

What students make ·

One original loop-based groove, 30–60 seconds long, built from Pattern Regions in the Step Sequencer: at least one drum kit, one bass or synth and one additional element. It uses at least three different Step Sequencer features, such as probability, velocity and step automation, and a mix with balance, clarity and space. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then play the piece and present it.

What this project reinforces ·

Introduces the Step Sequencer workflow. Builds rhythm programming, automation, and arrangement skills.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Learn to create and manipulate Pattern Regions for drums, bass, and synths
  • Use probability, velocity, and step automation to add organic variation
  • Apply EQ, panning, and plug-ins for a balanced, polished sound
  • Explore texture and form through pattern layering and evolving loops

At a glance ·

Grades9–12: the second-year course, after a first year of Logic Pro basics
Length7 class periods: explore and build drums, add melody, three working days, mix and bounce, present

Before they start

Students should already know how to open a project, add and play software instruments, use the Mixer, and save, bounce and zip in Logic. The Step Sequencer is new: the quick reference (page 9) shows where its controls live.

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • A place to submit the .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (yours to set up; none is included).
  • A hi-hat demo ready for Class 1 or 2 (my third struggle): a hi-hat row with a few steps at 25–50% probability, looped.
  • A rule for “at least 3 features” (requirement 2): which features count. Write it on the board and on page 14.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 13–23).

Before Class 1 ·

Practise opening a Pattern Region and the Step Sequencer on a classroom Mac.
Set up the Presentation Padlet in your class page.
Decide which features count, and whether to offer the pre-built pattern (page 10).
Check that a student computer opens Logic and plays back through headphones.

What to watch (my struggles) ·

Students use the Step Sequencer only for drums, patterns sound static, probability is hard to see, and the short length makes students rush. Works well for students drawn to electronic, lo-fi, or beat-driven music.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • Features that count: probability, velocity, step automation, mute and unmute, and pattern alternates are the ones my notes and tips name. Add others if you like.
  • Ask while students work: which feature changed what you just heard? Which step would you change next?
  • Plan the bars first. Thirty seconds is only about 15 bars at 120 BPM, so a short piece goes fast.

Sounds they did not make ·

A kit suggestion

Tell students to build their grooves from the instruments and kits in Logic, and to hand in only their own track.

Keep the volume moderate

Loud kicks and bass are easy to overdo in headphones. Tell students to start low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

Pattern ProdigyLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Pattern Prodigy project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…: a Software Instrument track for drum kits, bass and synths.
Drum kit and soundsChoose a drum kit from the Library (Y) on a Software Instrument track. Kits that show a grid of pads, such as Drum Machine Designer, are the easiest to read in a sequencer.
Pattern Region and Step SequencerCreate a Pattern Region on the track (look for the Pattern Region option when you make or draw a region) and double-click it to open the Step Sequencer in the editor area. Click a step to turn it on; click again to turn it off.
Bass and synth rowsA melodic track can use the Step Sequencer too: each row is a pitch, so the steps you click make a bass line or a synth arp. This answers my first struggle.
Velocity and probabilityUse the controls along the bottom or side of the Step Sequencer to choose which value you edit for each step. Velocity is how hard a step hits; probability is its chance of playing. Try it on a hi-hat.
Step automationPick a parameter, such as a filter cutoff, and set a value for each step. The values repeat every time the pattern loops.
Mute and unmuteEach row has a mute control. Muting and unmuting rows as the piece plays is the simplest way to change the arrangement (my fourth tip).
AlternatesFor my Harder variation, put a second Pattern Region with a different pattern after the first. Check whether your version also offers pattern sets inside one region.
Live recordingYou can also play a pattern in live and let Logic fill the steps. Quantize or edit the steps afterwards.
Automation, EQ, pan, plug-insPress A to show track automation. Add Channel EQ for balance, use each track's Pan knob, and add plug-ins such as a reverb or filter. Press X for the Mixer.
Length check and bounceBars × 240 ÷ tempo = seconds. File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Pattern ProdigyWorked example · an invented groove
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Night Shift”, described on paper: no audio is included. Students’ projects will differ. 20 bars at 96 BPM, four sections, five layers, five Step Sequencer features (page 1 of 2).

1 · A weak plan

I will make a beat with drums and a bass.

It names no features and nothing says what will change.

2 · A clear plan

I am making “Night Shift”, a 50-second groove at 96 BPM. It opens with kick and snare, and the hi-hat row is unmuted at bar 3. A bass pattern arrives at bar 5 and a synth arp at bar 9. I will use five Step Sequencer features: probability on the hat’s even steps, velocity accents, a cutoff on every step of the arp, mute and unmute, and an alternate drum pattern for the Build. Four automation lanes keep it moving, and in the last four bars it thins out.

3 · One bar of the groove: 16 steps ·

The bass plays C, Eb, G and Bb (C minor 7) on steps 1, 4, 7, 11 and 14, and the synth arp climbs C, Eb, G, Bb, C and comes back down on the odd steps. Every pitch is a chord tone, so the layers agree. Hat velocity: 100 on steps 1, 5, 9, 13 and 70 on the other odd steps; the even steps are ghosts at 25% probability.

4 · The five features it uses ·

Probabilitythe hi-hat’s even steps play 25% of the time; raised by hand to 60% in the Build
Velocityhat accents on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 (100) over softer ones (70); kick and snare at about 90 to 110
Step automationthe synth arp’s filter cutoff has its own value on every step, rising from 15 to 97
Mute / unmutethe hat row is unmuted at bar 3; the snare row is muted at bar 19
Pattern alternatea second drum pattern, B, plays in bars 13–16 and then the first returns

Pattern Prodigy

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Become a pattern master. Use Logic’s Step Sequencer and Pattern Regions to craft an original groove-driven composition. Create a 30–60 second piece that evolves over time through layering, automation, and creative use of sequencer features like probability and velocity.

Goals ·

  • Learn to create and manipulate Pattern Regions for drums, bass, and synths
  • Use probability, velocity, and step automation to add organic variation
  • Apply EQ, panning, and plug-ins for a balanced, polished sound
  • Explore texture and form through pattern layering and evolving loops

Key Vocabulary ·

Step SequencerA Logic tool that lets you program rhythms and melodies by toggling steps on a grid
Pattern RegionA region in Logic that contains step sequencer data
ProbabilityA feature that gives each step a chance of playing or not, adding randomness
VelocityHow hard a note is played — higher velocity = louder and often brighter
AutomationChanging a parameter (volume, filter, pan, etc.) over time to create movement

Tips for Success ·

  • Start with your drums. Once you have a groove that feels good, everything else falls into place.
  • Probability is your friend — even a small percentage of randomness makes patterns feel more human.
  • Don’t just stack patterns. Think about how your piece builds and evolves.
  • Use mute/unmute in the Step Sequencer to create arrangement changes.
