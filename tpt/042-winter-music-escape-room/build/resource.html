Teacher Guide

Winter Music Escape Room · grades 3–6 · 5 puzzles · about 45 minutes

The game

The snow-globe workshop is locked! Teams solve five music puzzles. Each puzzle gives an answer. A team finds that answer on an answer-check card; the card tells it which lock wheel to fill and with which digit. When all five wheels are filled, the digits are turned into letters with the lock chart. If the letters make a word, the lock opens. A wrong answer matches no card, or a wrong card, and the final word comes out as nonsense, so teams find their own mistakes.

Puzzles can be done in any order, so a team that is stuck can move on. Each puzzle has exactly one correct answer, and the five wheel digits depend on all five.

Setup (10 minutes)

  1. Print pages 4–10 for each team (one set of 7 pages per team of 2–4 students). Print page 12 for the hint card box.
  2. Print page 11 once, cut along the dashed lines and put each row of three cards into an envelope marked Puzzle 1 to Puzzle 5. One set serves a whole class if teams share the envelopes at a table.
  3. Cut the hint cards (page 12) and keep them at the front, with the optional workshop manual (page 13).
  4. Optional: set a 5-digit combination lock to the code on the answer key (page 15) for a real click at the end.

You need

  • Pencils and scissors
  • 5 envelopes (or 5 paper clips)
  • A timer to project
  • Optional: a 5-digit combination lock

No cutting?

Leave page 11 uncut and give each team a copy of one row at a time when they report a finished puzzle.

Running the Room

Timing, story, hints and differentiation

Timing for a 45-minute class

MinutesWhat happens
0–5Read the story, form teams, hand out the mission page
5–35Teams solve the five puzzles and fetch cards
35–40Final lock: digits to letters
40–45Debrief: which puzzle was trickiest, and why?

For a 30-minute class use puzzles 1, 3, 4 and 5 with the answers for 2 given (see the key); for a double period add a performance of any tune the class knows.

Read-aloud story

“High in the hills sits a snow-globe workshop where the music boxes are made. Last night a gust of wind blew the door shut and the lock froze. The lock listens only for music: five wheels, five puzzles. Solve a puzzle to earn a digit. When the wheels are full, the snow-globe will tell you the secret word.”

Hints

Teams may take one hint card per puzzle at any time. Each card on page 12 is labelled with its puzzle and a level: 1 nudges, 2 gives a strong start. Hints cost nothing; ask teams to say where they are stuck first.

If the lock says nonsense

One digit is wrong. Ask: “Which puzzle’s answer did you check against the cards least carefully?” The letter at each wheel should look like part of a word you know. Have the team re-solve one puzzle at a time.

Differentiation

SupportChallenge
Hand out the workshop manual (page 13) at the start.No manual and no hint cards: teams earn a bonus if they finish in 30 minutes.
Teams of 3–4, each student responsible for one puzzle, then shared.Ask teams to write one new rhythm-math bar of their own with a single right answer.
Give puzzle 2 or 4 as a class warm-up beforehand.Ask them to explain why a wrong card could not open the lock.

Grade guide

  • Grades 3–4: puzzles 1, 3, 4 and 5 with the manual; puzzle 2 together.
  • Grades 5–6: all five puzzles, manual closed.

Standards (modest fit)

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music, Performing, MU:Pr4.2 (demonstrate understanding of the elements of music and notation), in grades 3–6.

About the music

Every puzzle is built from one rule and has one correct answer. Staff positions, bar totals and the beat code are computed and re-checked before release. The workshop story is original and non-denominational; no songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

The snow-globe workshop is locked. Wind slammed the door and the frost froze the lock. It has five wheels, and each wheel listens for a digit. You are the team that can open it. Every puzzle you solve earns a digit.

How it works

  1. Solve a puzzle. Each puzzle page ends with an answer line. There is exactly one right answer.
  2. Find your answer card. Open the envelope with the puzzle’s number. Take the card that shows your answer. The other cards are traps.
  3. Read the card. It names a wheel and a digit. Write the digit on the lock page (page 10).
  4. Open the lock. When all five wheels are filled, use the lock chart to change digits into letters.

Team rules

  • Everyone does their own work first, then compares answers.
  • If two answers disagree, find the staff note or symbol you do not agree about.
  • Stuck? Ask for a hint card. Hints are allowed.
  • No card for your answer? Your answer is not right yet. Look again.

CUT-OUTS
Teacher prep
Cut along the dashed lines and keep the cards at the front of the room. Teams may take one hint card per puzzle: hint 1 nudges, hint 2 gives a strong start.
Puzzle 1 · hint 1Look at the clef at the start. A treble clef curls around the second line from the bottom: that line is G.
Puzzle 1 · hint 2Lines from the bottom: E G B D F. Spaces from the bottom: F A C E.
Puzzle 2 · hint 1This clef has two dots around the second line from the top: that line is F. The notes are not the same as in the treble clef.
Puzzle 2 · hint 2Lines from the bottom: G B D F A. Spaces from the bottom: A C E G.
Puzzle 3 · hint 1Add up the beats that are already in the bar. Subtract from the number on top of the time signature.
Puzzle 3 · hint 2A quarter note is 1 beat, a half note 2, a dotted half note 3 (a dot adds half), a whole note 4. Two beamed eighths make 1 beat.
Puzzle 4 · hint 1Start with the symbols you know for sure, then cross out their meanings. The two hairpins open or close.
Puzzle 4 · hint 2Hairpin opening to the right = louder. Closing = softer. A dot over a note = short. A curved bird’s-eye over a note = hold it.
Puzzle 5 · hint 1Say the name of each symbol aloud. A rest hangs below or sits on top of the line: the whole rest hangs, the half rest sits.
Puzzle 5 · hint 2A dot after a note makes it longer, so a dotted half note is different from a half note.

Bass clef

The music alphabet has only seven letters: A B C D E F G, then it starts again. Going up from a line to the next space means the next letter.

Note and rest values · when a quarter note gets the beat

whole note4 beats
dotted half note3 beats
half note2 beats
dotted quarter note1½ beats
quarter note1 beat
eighth note½ beat
whole rest4 beats of silence
half rest2 beats of silence
quarter rest1 beat of silence

A dot after a note makes it half as long again: a dotted half note is 2 + 1 = 3 beats; a dotted quarter note is 1 + ½ = 1½ beats.
