## 2. Description

```
Drum programming for high school music technology: students program a holiday percussion groove with no melody in Logic Pro. They build a Drum Machine Designer kit of sleigh bells, shaker, tambourine, wood block, kick, snare and hi-hat, program a 4-bar groove in the Step Sequencer on a 16-step grid, then add swing and compare straight with swung feel in words and in milliseconds. This Sleigh Bell Groove project takes 3 class periods (the pacing guide also shows two and four) and is an original Hudson Beat project; the steps are written for Logic Pro, while the ideas (a 16-step grid, layers, swing percent) carry over to GarageBand, Ableton Live and BandLab.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (ZIP: 24-page PDF = 1 cover + 9 teacher + 13 student + 1 terms page, plus 14-slide intro deck as .pptx and PDF)
• Teacher pages: how to use, project overview, setup, a 3-class pacing guide with the deck slides for each class, two pages of teacher notes (struggles, variations, resources, grading), a Logic quick reference and a two-page worked example (an invented plan on paper: an 8-bar groove at 100 BPM = 19.2 seconds, with seven layers, a map and the swing numbers) (9 pages)
• Student pages: eight step-grid pattern cards on two pages (sleigh bells, shaker, tambourine, wood block, kick, snare, hi-hat; each shows its hits on the 16 grid steps with the count 1 e & a and the hits swing moves), the straight vs swing timing table (swing percent to milliseconds, all 16 steps), My groove plan, a Layer map with a groove-length helper, a listening and compare worksheet, the 3-page project sheet (overview, goals, vocabulary, requirements, steps, tips, submission checklist), the /25 rubric, a vocabulary page, a feedback planner and a reflection page (13 pages)
• Teacher-presented intro deck, 14 slides with speaker notes on every slide: a think-and-share hook, what the project is, the 16-step grid, building the kit in Logic, the seven layers, how swing moves the second note, where swing lives in Logic, a worked-example map, what to turn in and the rubric

THE THREE CLASS PERIODS
• Class 1: hook; the 16-step grid; build the kit; program the straight groove
• Class 2: finish the straight groove; swing it at 50, 60 and 66 percent; the listening worksheet
• Class 3: grow to 8 bars with a variation and a fill; mix, bounce, present and reflect

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN
• Building a Drum Machine Designer kit and programming patterns in Logic Pro's Step Sequencer
• Reading a 16-step grid: four beats, four sixteenth notes in each, counted 1 e & a
• Straight versus swing feel, and swing as a percent and as milliseconds (at 120 BPM a 16th note lasts 125 ms)
• Balancing layers with volume, Channel EQ and a Compressor
• Planning a groove in bars and seconds (bars × 240 ÷ BPM), then explaining their choices in a reflection

GOOD TO KNOW
• There is no audio in this resource and no student work. No melody or song is printed or notated; students make every sound themselves in Logic Pro
• Requires Logic Pro on a Mac. A MIDI keyboard is not needed; nobody has to read music. Where a control may differ between versions, the pages say "if your version has it"
• Rubric: 5 categories scored 1-5, /25; the final grade is the average of the student's and the teacher's score
```

- [ ] **Timing numbers (all recomputed in `gen.py --check`).** 16 grid steps = 4 beats x 4 sixteenths, counted 1 e & a. At 120 BPM a 16th lasts 60000 / 120 / 4 = 125 ms, a pair of 16ths 250 ms, a pair of 8ths 500 ms. Swing percent = where the second note of a pair starts: 50% = 125 ms (straight), 60% = 150 ms (25 ms late), 66% = 165 ms (40 ms late; a true triplet is 166.7 ms), 75% = 187.5 ms; for 8th pairs 60% = 300 ms and 66% = 330 ms. A 16th-note swing moves grid steps 2, 4, ... 16; an 8th-note swing moves 3, 7, 11 and 15. Worked example: 8 bars at 100 BPM = 8 x 2.4 = 19.2 s; a 16th is 150 ms; 60% swing starts the 2nd note at 180 ms (30 ms late), 66% at 198 ms (48 ms late). Groove-length helper: bars x 240 / BPM at 90 to 120 BPM.
