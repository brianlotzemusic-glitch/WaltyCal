The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a tempo from 90 to 120 BPM, a Drum Machine Designer track with sounds on at least three pads and a first layer programmed in the Step Sequencer. If the tempo is only a guess, tap a steady beat for ten seconds and count the taps.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the kit and the straight groove; Class 2 is swing, 8 bars, mixing, bounce and short presentations. Four classes: keep Class 1 and Class 2, then spend Class 3 on 8 bars and the mix and Class 4 on bouncing, presentations and reflection.

Sleigh Bell GrooveStep-grid cards 1: bells, shaker, tambourine
STUDENT
Classes 1–2
NameDate

A bar of 4/4 has 4 beats, and each beat holds 4 sixteenth notes, so one bar is 16 grid steps. Count every beat 1 e & a. Each card shows one layer’s hits on that grid: the numbers 1 to 16 are the grid steps, the counts sit under them, and the shaded steps are the beats. A black square is a hit that stays put when a 16th-note swing is on; a blue circle is a hit on an “e” or an “a”, and it moves later. Each card also lists its hits in words, so it works in black and white. Step 3 of the project sheet: program a straight groove from these cards.

Sleigh bells · Quarter jingle

One jingle on every beat. It keeps time and leaves room for everything else.

Counts: 1 · 2 · 3 · 4

Grid steps: 1, 5, 9, 13

16th swing moves: none

8th swing moves: none

Sleigh bells · Gallop

A long-short-short rhythm in every beat: an eighth note, then two sixteenths. It sounds like a trotting horse.

Counts: 1 · 1 & · 1 a · 2 · 2 & · 2 a · 3 · 3 & · 3 a · 4 · 4 & · 4 a

Grid steps: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16

16th swing moves: 4, 8, 12, 16

8th swing moves: 3, 7, 11, 15

Shaker · Sixteenth shake

A shake on every grid step, so a 16th-note swing moves half of them. It makes swing easy to hear.

Counts: every step, 1 e & a in each beat

Grid steps: all 16

16th swing moves: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16

8th swing moves: 3, 7, 11, 15

Tambourine · Backbeat shake

A shake on beats 2 and 4, each followed by the “a” just before the next beat.

Counts: 2 · 2 a · 4 · 4 a

Grid steps: 5, 8, 13, 16

16th swing moves: 8, 16

8th swing moves: none

Step 4: swing it. One bar is 16 grid steps, so at 120 BPM a 16th note lasts 60000 ÷ 120 ÷ 4 = 125 ms. Swing takes each pair of notes and moves the second one later: the swing percent is how far through the pair the second note starts. 50% is straight. Bold numbers below are the steps a 16th-note swing moves.

Where swing lives in Logic

Look for a Swing control in the Step Sequencer, and for Q-Swing in a MIDI region’s Quantize settings, if your version has them (page 8). Scales differ: find the setting that sounds even first and call it straight, then test 60 and 66.
