Teacher Notes

Music Sub Plans K–5 · 5 no-prep emergency days

What it is

Five complete music lessons a substitute can teach with no tech, no instruments and no music background. Each day has a step-by-step plan with words to say, timed steps, a closing game, and two worksheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5.

The five days

  1. Sound Detectives: listening and describing sounds.
  2. Rhythm Readers: steady beat and reading rhythm.
  3. Loud & Soft, Fast & Slow: dynamics and tempo.
  4. Rhythm Composers: composing and performing rhythms.
  5. Music Word Games: music vocabulary.

Use them in any order. The welcome sheet has a “Day #” column so the sub knows which day each class gets next.

Set up the sub tub (about 15 minutes, once)

  1. Fill in the welcome sheet (page 3).
  2. Print the plans, the rhythm chart and the sub report; copy each worksheet for your largest class.
  3. Put each day in its own folder with its worksheets, and add the answer keys.

Why subs can teach it

Every rhythm has its syllables (ta, ti-ti, shh, ta-a) printed under the notes, so the sub can lead echo clapping without reading music. Games use only voices, hands and an eraser.

About the notation

Rhythms are in 4/4 and use quarter notes, paired eighth notes, quarter rests, half notes and whole notes, engraved with a professional music font. Every rhythm was read back by computer and checked for 4 beats per bar, except the bars on Day 2 that students must spot as wrong; every key comes from the same checked data.

Standards

Supports reading rhythm notation (MU:Pr4.2), creating rhythms (MU:Cr2.1) and describing what students hear (MU:Re7.2), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Good to know

  • Prints well in black and white.
  • K–2 sheets have read-aloud parts; the plans tell the sub which ones.
  • Rhythm syllables follow the common ta / ti-ti system. If your students use counting or other syllables, add a note on the welcome sheet.

All lessons, chants, games, words and art are original. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Sound DetectivesDay 1 · Listening and describing sounds · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents listen closely and describe sounds as loud or soft, high or low, long or short.
You needPencils, the worksheets, and a few classroom objects (pencil, paper, a book, keys).
CopiesK–2: page 7
3–5: page 8
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Echo me
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I’m your guest teacher today. Let’s warm up. Echo me!” Say each line in a clear voice and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s make music! Do it again once quietly and once in a strong voice (strong, not shouting).
  2. 5 minStep 2
    One-minute listen
    Everyone closes their eyes (or looks down) and stays silent for one minute. Say: “Count how many different sounds you hear.” Then ask 3 or 4 students to share one sound each.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Guest teacher sound show
    Students close their eyes while you make a sound; they guess what it was. Try: tap a pencil on a desk, crumple paper, knock on the door, clap once, rattle keys, zip a bag, flip book pages, whisper “hello”. For each one ask: Loud or soft? High or low? Long or short?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Listening log
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 My Listening Log (page 7), 3–5 Listening Log (page 8). Read the directions aloud. Make the sounds again, one at a time, while students fill in their sheets.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Copy my sound
    One student makes a short body-percussion sound (pat, clap, snap, stomp). The class copies it. The leader picks the next leader.
Extra time? Play Hot and Cold Dynamics or Freeze Beat from the game bank (page 4). Short class? Skip step 2 and keep the worksheet.

Rhythm ReadersDay 2 · Steady beat and reading rhythm · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents keep a steady beat and read and clap simple rhythms with rhythm syllables.
You needPencils, the worksheets, the rhythm chart (page 5). No music reading needed: the syllables are printed under every note.
CopiesK–2: page 10
3–5: page 11
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Copy the beat
    Pat a slow, steady beat on your legs. Say: “Copy me!” Switch to clapping, then tapping shoulders, at the same speed. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Rhythm chart echoes
    Show the rhythm chart (page 5) or copy one line at a time on the board. Clap and say a line; the class echoes. ta = one clap, ti-ti = two quick claps in one beat, shh = a silent beat (open hands), ta-a = one clap held for 2 beats. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  3. 15 minStep 3
    Worksheet
    K–2 Rhythm Readers (page 10): read Part B aloud. 3–5 Rhythm Math (page 11). Answer keys are on page 22.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Poison Rhythm
    Pick one chart line as the poison rhythm and show it. Clap other lines; the class echoes. When you clap the poison rhythm, everyone stays silent. Anyone who claps it becomes a judge. Change the poison line each round.
Extra time? Let students take turns as the leader for Poison Rhythm. Short class? Shorten step 2 to lines 1–4 and keep the worksheet.

Loud & Soft, Fast & SlowDay 3 · Dynamics and tempo · no tech, no prep
DAY 3
Sub plan
GoalStudents show and name dynamics (how loud or soft) and tempo (how fast or slow).
You needPencils, the worksheets, one small object to hide (an eraser).
CopiesK–2: page 13
3–5: page 14
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Voice dynamics
    Say: “Dynamics means how loud or soft the music is. Piano means soft. Forte means loud.” Say Music is fun!; the class echoes it in a piano voice, then a forte voice (strong, never shouting).
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Hot and Cold Dynamics
    One student covers their eyes while another hides the eraser in the room. The seeker looks for it while the class claps a steady beat: soft when the seeker is far away, louder as they get closer. Play 3 or 4 rounds.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Tempo walk
    Say: “Tempo means how fast or slow.” Clap a beat while students walk in place (or pat their legs). Try very slow (largo), walking speed (andante), fast (allegro), very fast (presto). When you stop, they freeze.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 Loud or Soft? (page 13): read each sound in Parts B and C aloud. 3–5 Dynamics and Tempo (page 14). Answer keys are on page 23.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Silent Conductor
    A student conducts the class’s clapping: hands far apart = loud, close together = soft, fast or slow moves = tempo, fists closed = stop. Take turns.
Extra time? Echo short lines from the rhythm chart (page 5) at different dynamics and tempos. Short class? Skip step 3 and keep the worksheet.

Rhythm ComposersDay 4 · Composing and performing rhythms · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents create their own 4-beat rhythms, check the beats, and perform them.
You needPencils, the worksheets, the rhythm chart (page 5).
CopiesK–2: page 16
3–5: page 17
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Chart review
    Clap and say lines 1–4 of the rhythm chart (page 5); the class echoes. 3–5 classes add lines 5–8.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Word rhythms
    Say a word and clap its syllables: cat = ta (one clap), piz-za = ti-ti (two quick claps), shh = a silent beat. Students suggest more words: one syllable = ta, two syllables = ti-ti.
  3. 15 minStep 3
    Compose
    K–2 Word Rhythm Composer (page 16). 3–5 Rhythm Composer (page 17). Walk around and ask: “Does each box (or bar) have the right number of beats?”
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Composer concert
    Volunteers clap and say their rhythm while the class pats a steady beat, then the class echoes it. Clap for every composer!
Extra time? Partners trade sheets and clap each other’s rhythms. Short class? Skip step 2 and show the example on the worksheet instead.

Music Word GamesDay 5 · Music vocabulary · no tech, no prep
DAY 5
Sub plan
GoalStudents review music words: beat, rhythm, dynamics, tempo, pitch and more.
You needPencils and the worksheets.
CopiesK–2: page 19
3–5: page 20
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Show me
    Call out a word; students show it. High: hands up and a high “ooo”. Low: hands down and a low “ooo”. Loud / soft: big or small claps. Fast / slow: pat fast or slow.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Rhythm Telephone
    Students sit in a circle or line. Clap a short rhythm from the chart (page 5) to the first student, who claps it to the next, and so on. Does it reach the end the same? Start a new rhythm each round.
  3. 15 minStep 3
    Worksheet
    K–2 Music Opposites (page 19). 3–5 Music Word Search (page 20). Answer keys are on page 23.
  4. 5 minStep 4
    Freeze Beat
    Pat a steady beat while students move in place (march, sway, tiptoe). When you stop, everyone freezes. Anyone who moves helps you keep the beat.
Extra time? Early finishers: the drawing and rhythm page (page 21). Short class? Skip step 2 and keep the worksheet.
