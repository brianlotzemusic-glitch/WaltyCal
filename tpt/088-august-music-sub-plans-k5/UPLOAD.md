## 2. Description

```
Need sub plans a substitute can teach without any music background, any technology, or any prep from you? These eight August music days are ready to print and hand over. Each day has a step-by-step plan with timed steps and words to say, plus two student sheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5. The theme is back to school: music class habits (listen, take turns, take care of instruments), the four instrument families (strings, woodwinds, brass and percussion), getting to know you (clap the syllables of your own name), calm breathing for first-day jitters, and the sounds of a school. Nothing assumes a student has a backpack, particular supplies or a particular family, and school starts at different times in different places, so the days work any time in the first weeks.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (48 pages, PDF, US Letter)
• Cover and teacher notes with the August note, class habits, names and jitters and instrument family teaching notes, setup steps and a page guide (2 pages)
• Sub quick start, welcome sheet, days at a glance, quick games, word-rhythm chart, August facts and a class sign (7 pages)
• 8 sub plans, one per day, each 30–45 minutes (8 pages)
• 16 student sheets: a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet for each day (16 pages)
• Day extras: movement picture cards, chant word cards, a chant book page and a read-aloud quiz (4 pages)
• Early finishers (2 pages)
• Exit tickets (1 page)
• Answer keys for Days 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8; Day 3 is open-ended and its entry says what to look for (3 pages)
• Sub report form, folder labels, class star awards, terms of use and a thank-you page (5 pages)

THE EIGHT DAYS
• Day 1, Welcome to Music Class: K–2 hear a short reading about the four instrument families and three music class habits, and circle answers; 3–5 read a passage of about 200 words and answer six questions
• Day 2, Name Rhythms: clap the syllables of school words, their own names and a short original four-line poem; K–2 color a circle for each clap, 3–5 sort words by syllables and build a four-beat chant
• Day 3, Sound Detectives: a found-sound hunt on paper: four everyday sound makers (bell, pencil, shoes, book), then draw and label a school scene full of sounds; K–2 label two sounds, 3–5 complete a sound hunt table and write two sentences
• Day 4, Calm and Freeze: slow calm breathing, a silent counting game, and move and freeze to a tapped beat with 8 picture cards (bell, pencil, book, ruler, shoe, clock, star, heart); K–2 circle how they moved, 3–5 reflect and design a calm-down routine
• Day 5, Family Word Trail: instrument family puzzles; K–2 match words to pictures and trace, 3–5 word search, a decode-the-message puzzle and a family sort
• Day 6, Meet the Music Workers: music jobs around a school: five music jobs, from conductor to radio host; K–2 draw a job, 3–5 match jobs and write a job ad or interview questions
• Day 7, Hello Chant Book: build four-beat class-habit chants from music class word cards, mark the syllables, share with a partner, and keep a chant book page
• Day 8, Hello Bingo: bingo (12 squares for K–2, 16 for 3–5), a read-aloud quiz and Pass the Pencil, with an about-me reflection

WHY IT WORKS FOR SUBS
• No tech: nothing needs a computer, projector, speaker or video. Paper, pencils, crayons, hands and voices only
• No music skills: rhythm is taught through speech rhythm, clapping the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. The sub never reads music or sings
• No prep: print, put each day in a folder, and go. Each day works in any order for a mixed K–5 class
• Facts are short, sourced and secular, and the facts page answers common questions, so the days work in public schools and for students of every background
```
