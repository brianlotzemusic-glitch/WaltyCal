### 1. The four instrument families and their short definitions: strings make sound with vibrating strings that a player plucks, strums or bows (violin, cello, guitar); woodwinds are blown, into or across the instrument (flute, clarinet, recorder), and a woodwind does not have to be made of wood; brass instruments make sound when the player buzzes their lips into a mouthpiece (trumpet, trombone, tuba); percussion instruments are hit, shaken or scraped (drums, xylophones, triangles) (Day 1 readings and key, Day 5, bingo clues, facts page, teacher notes)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_instrument (fetched 27 Nov 2026: string instruments 'produce sound from vibrating strings'; players set them vibrating by plucking, striking or bowing; violins and cellos are bowed, guitars and harps typically plucked); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodwind_instrument (fetched 27 Nov 2026: flutes direct air across a sharp edge, reed instruments use reeds; flute, clarinet, oboe, bassoon and saxophone are common examples; recorders are listed; 'a woodwind may be made of any material, not just wood'); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_instrument (fetched 27 Nov 2026: sound comes from the player's lips vibrating; trumpet has three piston valves (usually; some models four), trombone has a slide, tuba and French horn have valves); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_instrument (fetched 27 Nov 2026: 'makes sound when it is hit, scraped, or rubbed'; examples drums, xylophone, triangle, cymbals, tambourine; rattles count as beaters). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument_classification (fetched 27 Nov 2026: the standard Western grouping is strings, winds, percussion and electronic; families do not always fit cleanly; keyboard instruments are often treated as a separate category). Second source (QA, 27 Nov 2026, fetched): https://www.yourclassical.org/story/2015/09/14/learning-to-listen-the-instruments-of-the-orchestra (strings: bows drawn along the string or fingers plucking it; woodwinds: sound made when air is blown inside, across an edge or with reeds; brass: the player buzzes into a mouthpiece; percussion: hit, shaken, rattled, rubbed or scratched). It agrees with the wording on the pages. dsokids.com returned 404 and britannica.com, kennedy-center.org and carnegiehall.org returned 403. The pages never say how many instruments or families an orchestra has.

### 2. The recorder is a woodwind instrument (Day 1 K-2 reading and key, bingo clue Recorder, Day 5 match, facts page)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorder_(musical_instrument) (fetched 27 Nov 2026: 'The recorder is a family of woodwind musical instruments'; the player blows through a windway; finger holes). Second source for the recorder: a Britannica search result (britannica.com/art/recorder-musical-instrument, snippet only, page returned 403) calls it a wind instrument of the fipple or whistle flute class, and several school-instrument pages describe it as a woodwind with a block mouthpiece and finger holes. The page says a recorder is blown 'into', the pages say 'blow into or across' for woodwinds generally.

### 5. In the Northern Hemisphere, August is a summer month; in the Southern Hemisphere, it is a winter month (Day 1 3-5 reading and key, facts page, teacher notes)
Source: https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/news/meteorological-versus-astronomical-seasons (fetched 27 Nov 2026: 'meteorological summer includes June, July, and August' in the Northern Hemisphere); https://www.timeanddate.com/calendar/aboutseasons.html (fetched 27 Nov 2026: Northern Hemisphere summer 'runs from June 1 to August 31'; in the Southern Hemisphere, where seasons are reversed, winter 'starts June 1 and ends August 31'). ### 6. School starts at different times in different places, and in many US schools a new school year begins in August or September (Day 1 readings, facts page, teacher notes)
