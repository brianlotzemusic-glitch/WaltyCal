Teacher Notes

August Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · Back to school, instrument families, getting to know you

Why August matters

August is the eighth month of the year. School starts at different times in different places, and in many US schools a new school year begins in August or September. In the Northern Hemisphere, August is a summer month; in the Southern Hemisphere, it is a winter month. This is the one month of the year with no holiday or heritage observance, so the days are about the start of a school year: how a music class works, the four instrument families, getting to know each other, and the sounds around a school.

Music class habits and inclusive wording

  • Listen, take turns and take care of instruments are three simple music class habits written for this bundle. If you have your own rules, list them on the welcome sheet and the sub can use those first. No instruments are needed on any day, so the habit of caring for instruments is practiced with pretend instruments and drawings.
  • Nothing in the days depends on backpacks, particular supplies or a particular family. Every day needs only pencils and paper, with crayons on some days, and the sub never asks students to bring anything.

Names and first-day jitters

  • Day 2 has students clap the syllables of their own names. Use the name a student likes to be called, never guess how a name is said (ask), and let any student pass or clap a favorite word instead.
  • The first days of a school year can bring excitement and nerves. Day 4 offers slow breathing and a silent counting game. Slow breathing can help many people feel calmer, but it is a simple habit and not a medical treatment, and a student may always sit quietly instead.

Instrument families: simple and accurate

  • The four families (strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion) are kept to short, simple definitions. A woodwind does not have to be made of wood, and the recorder is a woodwind. Some instruments are hard to sort (keyboard instruments, for example, are often treated as a separate category), so the days only ask students to sort instruments that clearly belong to one of the four families.

Why subs can teach it

Each lesson has timed steps, words to say and a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet. Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words. Nobody has to read music or make music; games need only voices, hands and a pencil.

GameHow to do itDay
Bell EchoSay a short phrase, like “bell, bell, clap, shh.” The class answers with the same phrase, then once softly.any

Tick-Tock ArmsTap a pencil slowly. Students swing their arms like the hands of a clock, slowly with a slow tap, quicker with a quicker tap, and stop when you stop.4

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Quiet Counting.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may know more than the page says, or may feel nervous. Thank them kindly.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Quiet Counting to settle a class, Name Claps to wake it up, and Bell Echo to finish with a smile.

Read, Answer and DrawRead, answer and draw
DAY 1
Grades 3–5
NameDate
Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
August is the eighth month of the year. In the Northern Hemisphere it is a summer month, and in the Southern Hemisphere it is a winter month. School starts at different times in different places, and in many US schools a new school year begins in August or September. A new year in music class starts with a few simple habits: listen carefully, take turns, and take care of instruments. Musicians also sort instruments into four families, based on how they make sound. String instruments, such as the violin and the guitar, make sound with vibrating strings that a player plucks, strums or bows. Woodwinds, such as the flute, the clarinet and the recorder, make sound when a player blows air into or across them. A woodwind does not have to be made of wood. Brass instruments, such as the trumpet and the tuba, make sound when the player buzzes their lips into a mouthpiece. Percussion instruments, such as drums, xylophones and triangles, make sound when they are hit, shaken or scraped. Every musician, from a beginner to a conductor, starts by listening.

You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14

about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: bell (1 clap), pen-cil (2 claps), in-stru-ment (3 claps), kin-der-gar-ten (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Name claps
    Say: “Every name has a rhythm. Let’s clap yours.” Go around the room: each student says their name, or the name they like to be called, and the class claps its syllables together. A student who would rather not may pass or clap a favorite word instead.
  4. 5 minStep 4
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Bells ring hello. Pencils go tap. Music starts now. August is here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  5. 10 minStep 5
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
Extra time? Name Claps from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Do only Part A and Part B; skip the poem.

Sound DetectivesDay 3 · A found-sound hunt on paper · no tech, no prep
DAY 3
Sub plan
GoalStudents listen for the everyday sounds of a school, name how each sound is made, and draw and label a school scene full of sounds.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else is needed: students listen and tap quietly with a fingertip.
CopiesK–2: page 17
3–5: page 18
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Listening minute
    Say: “Detectives, close your eyes or look down. Listen for one minute. Count the different sounds you hear on your fingers, with no talking.” Wait one minute. Ask: What sounds did you find? Take four or five answers (a door, a chair, a cough, a pencil, a clock, a hallway).
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Four sound makers
    Say: “Everyday things make sound. A bell can ring, a pencil can tap, shoes can step and a book can flip.” Point to the four pictures on the sheet and say each sound word with the class. Clap the syllables of each word.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Quiet tap hunt
    Say: “Now be a sound detective at your own desk. Tap softly with one fingertip.” Students find three different sounds on their desk, a book or a pencil, all very quietly. Ask three students: How did you make that sound?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Sound scene
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 17, 3–5 page 18. Say: “Draw a place in a school and show what makes sound there.” Read the directions aloud. Students may draw a hallway, a library, a lunchroom or a music room, or any place they like. There are no wrong answers.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Gallery walk
    Students hold up their scenes one table at a time. Ask three students: Which sound in your picture is the loudest, and which is the softest?
Extra time? Quiet Counting or Bell Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip the gallery walk.

Calm and FreezeDay 4 · Calm breathing and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents practice slow breathing, try a silent counting game, move to a steady tap and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them, or cut them out with scissors).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Calm breaths
    Say: “Many people feel excited or nervous at the start of a school year. Slow breathing can help us feel calmer. Let’s try it.” Everyone sits comfortably. Show four fingers. Breathe in slowly while you raise one finger at a time to four, then breathe out slowly while you lower them. Do it three times. A student may sit quietly instead.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Quiet Counting
    Do Quiet Counting (page 6): raise your fingers slowly from one to ten while the class counts silently in their heads. Do it twice. Then say: “Our class made ten calm seconds together.”
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap a pencil on a desk. Use at least six cards, and try a slow tap, a fast tap and a bouncy tap (long, short, long, short, like skipping). The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 20, 3–5 page 21. Students sit and fill it in. Read the directions aloud. Answers are on page 43.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Statue patch
    Tap a few beats, stop, and let the class freeze in a favorite pose, such as a star, a ruler or a bell. Walk around and name two poses you notice.
Extra time? Hallway Freeze or Tick-Tock Arms from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Use only four cards.
