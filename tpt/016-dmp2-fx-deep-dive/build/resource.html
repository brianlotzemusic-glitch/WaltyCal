What this is ·

A 7-class-period mixing project for high school music technology. Each student takes an existing track, either one of their own earlier projects or a multitrack from the Cambridge-MT library, and mixes it in Logic Pro with at least five different audio effects: EQ and panning on every track, plus effects such as compression, reverb, delay or modulation. It is a structured project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (focus: mixing and effects), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Decide where source tracks come from and pre-select sessions (page 4): no audio is included.
  3. Print the student pages (pages 13–22), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–11), Class 3 (slide 12), Class 4 (slide 13), Class 6 (slides 14–16) and Class 7 (slides 17–18). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student (a current version), and headphones. Speakers help for the headphones-and-speakers check in step 5.
  • A source track for every student: their own earlier project, or a multitrack they download. Nothing is included.
  • A place to submit an .mp3 or .wav and a zipped .logicx project (your class page).
  • A feedback board for the three questions (my sheets use Padlet).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 13–22).

Before Class 1 ·

Pre-select 5–10 multitrack sessions with sensible track counts (8–16), as my notes suggest, or decide to let students choose.
Read the Cambridge-MT library’s terms of use so you can tell students what they may do with a download.
Mix a short stretch of your own track twice, dry and with effects, for slide 2.
Print the student pages; set up the submission spot and the feedback board.

Choosing a source ·

My project gives two choices. Page 13 is the student log for either one.

  • Remix your own project. No download is needed, and the student already knows the track. Check that it has at least five separate tracks, so there is something to EQ and pan.
  • A multitrack from Cambridge-MT. The library’s sessions are separate audio files for each instrument. Students download one from the library’s own site and import it into Logic (page 9). This kit does not include or redistribute any of them.
Pre-selecting sessions
  • Aim for 8–16 tracks; a 40-track session is a different project.
  • Download each one yourself and open it in Logic before students do.
  • Note the genre of each, so students can pick one whose style they can describe.
  • Keep a short list on the board, with the track count beside each.
Terms and credit

Libraries set their own rules for what you may do with their files, such as using them to practice mixing but not sharing them. Check before students post a mix publicly, and credit the source if the library asks.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

This is the primary mixing skills project. It shifts focus entirely to post-production. The EQ Cheatsheet PDF and Plug-in Links doc are key companion resources.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students apply FX randomly without purpose — push them to articulate WHY they’re using each effect.
  • EQ feels invisible to beginners. Do a live demo early with dramatic cuts and boosts on a soloed track.
  • Cambridge-MT tracks can be overwhelming. Pre-select 5–10 sessions with reasonable track counts.
  • Students skip panning and leave everything center. Play a professional mix and point out the width.
  • Some students treat this as a composition project. Redirect early.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Reduce the FX requirement to 3 and provide a pre-mixed reference with notes on which effects were used.

More Advanced: Require 7+ FX including at least one send/bus effect. Have them write a paragraph justifying each FX choice.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • FX without purpose: the FX log (page 16) asks why, and slide 6 gives students four questions.
  • EQ feels invisible: the EQ guide (page 10) is a demo script, and slide 8 is the demo.
  • Overwhelming sessions: page 4 helps you pre-select, and the Source log (page 13) has students count tracks.
  • Everything panned center: slide 9 and the Track map (page 15).
  • Treated as composition: slide 6 and the goals on page 17 keep the focus on FX.

How to grade ·

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Technical Skill: ask the student to open Channel EQ on one track and say which frequency they moved, whether they boosted or cut it, and why.
  • Sound Design & Mix Quality: have the student bypass one effect live and describe what changed (an A/B test). Listen on headphones and on the main speakers.

FX Deep DiveLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Everything students need in Logic for this project, in one table. Demonstrate EQ (slide 8) and sends (slide 10), then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions.

Import a multitrackFile › Import › Audio File (Shift-Command-I), or drag the audio files onto the Tracks area. Set the project tempo if the session came with one.
MixerPress X to show or hide the Mixer. Each track has a channel strip with an EQ display, Audio FX slots, Sends, a Pan knob and a fader.
Add an effectClick an empty Audio FX slot on a channel strip and choose a plug-in from the menu. Plug-ins are grouped by kind: EQ, Dynamics, Reverb, Delay, Distortion, Modulation.
EQDouble-click the EQ display on the channel strip to open Channel EQ. Drag a band up to boost or down to cut; the Q setting sets how wide the move is.
CompressionAudio FX slot › Dynamics › Compressor. Lower the Threshold until the meter shows gain reduction, then set the Ratio.
Reverb and delayReverb › ChromaVerb (or Space Designer). Delay › Echo or Stereo Delay. Try a delay time that matches the tempo, such as 1/8 note.
Chorus, phaser, flangerModulation › Chorus, Phaser or Flanger. These move the sound over time; use a small amount.
DistortionDistortion › Distortion II or Overdrive. A little adds warmth; a lot is an effect on its own.
Send and busClick a Send slot and choose Bus 1; an Aux channel appears. Put a reverb or delay on the Aux, then turn each track’s send knob up to taste.
PanDrag the Pan knob under the fader; Option-click it to return to center.
A/B testClick the plug-in’s Bypass button in its window title bar to hear the track without it, then switch it back on. Use Solo (S) to hear one track alone.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

FX Deep DiveEQ guide and live demo plan
EQ GUIDE
Teacher + student

My notes say EQ feels invisible to beginners, so do a live demo early. This page is a one-page guide you can project or print, and the plan for a five-minute demo (slide 8). The bands and fixes are rules of thumb, not laws: tell students to trust their ears.

Common problems and a first try ·

It sounds…Try
Muddy or boomyCut a little around 200–400 Hz
Boxy, closed inCut a little around 400–800 Hz
Harsh or sharpCut a little around 2–5 kHz
Thin or weakBoost a little around 100–250 Hz
Dull, no sparkleBoost the highs, 6 kHz and up
Rumble you cannot hearHigh-pass below 30–80 Hz

The five-minute demo ·

  1. Pick a drum or bass track from your own project and solo it.
  2. Open Channel EQ. Make a narrow band and boost 12 dB. Sweep it slowly from low to high.
  3. Stop on a spot that sounds bad. Cut it by 6 dB.
  4. Bypass the EQ and back: that is A/B.
  5. Un-solo. Ask: did the mix get clearer?

Four kinds of EQ band ·

BandWhat it does
BellBoosts or cuts around one frequency. The Q setting makes it narrow or wide.
ShelfBoosts or cuts everything above (high shelf) or below (low shelf) a frequency.
High-passRemoves everything below a frequency. Good for clearing rumble from tracks that do not need low end.
Low-passRemoves everything above a frequency. Good for making a sound darker.
EQ rules for students

Cut before you boost. Make small moves. Listen to the whole mix, not only the solo.

Panning

Keep the kick, snare, bass and lead vocal near the center. Spread other parts and balance the two sides (page 12).

EXAMPLE

An invented eight-track session, described on paper: no audio is included, so the numbers are easy to check. Students’ sessions will differ.

1 · Place the band ·

Left tracks: keys 40 + shaker 30 = 70. Right tracks: hi-hat 25 + guitar 45 = 70. The sides balance.

2 · The plan for each track ·

TrackPanEQEffects
KickcenterBoost 60 Hz +3 dB; cut 300 Hz −4 dBCompressor
SnarecenterCut 400 Hz −3 dB; boost 4 kHz +3 dBReverb (short, on a send)
Hi-hatright 25High-pass at 300 HzNone
BasscenterCut 250 Hz −3 dB; boost 80 Hz +2 dBCompressor
Keysleft 40High-pass at 150 Hz; cut 300 Hz −3 dBChorus
Guitarright 45Cut 250 Hz −3 dB; boost 3 kHz +2 dBDelay (1/8 note)
Lead vocalcenterHigh-pass at 100 Hz; boost 3 kHz +3 dBCompressor, reverb, delay (dotted 1/8)
Shakerleft 30High-pass at 500 HzNone

Five different effects are used: EQ, compressor, reverb, delay and chorus.

3 · Delay times at 100 BPM
NoteDelay time
Quarter note600 ms
Eighth note300 ms
Dotted eighth450 ms
Sixteenth note150 ms

One beat = 60 000 ÷ 100 = 600 ms.

4 · Compression, by the numbers

Threshold −18 dB, ratio 4:1. A peak at −6 dB is 12 dB over the threshold. 12 ÷ 4 = 3 dB over, so it comes out at −18 + 3 = −15 dB. Gain reduction = 9 dB (slide 11).

FX Deep Dive

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Time to level up your mixing skills. Take an existing track — either one of your own previous projects or a song from the Cambridge-MT Multitrack Library — and enhance it using at least 5 different audio effects. Your goal is to use FX creatively and intentionally to shape tone, depth, and space in a professional-sounding mix.

Goals ·

  • Learn and apply at least 5 different audio effects in a real mix
  • Develop EQ and panning skills across all tracks in a session
  • Understand how FX choices relate to genre and style
  • Build a polished, balanced mix with clarity and depth

Key Vocabulary ·

EQ (Equalization)A tool that adjusts the volume of specific frequencies to shape tone and clarity
CompressionReduces the dynamic range — making quiet parts louder and loud parts quieter
ReverbSimulates the natural reflections of a space, adding depth and ambience
DelayRepeats a sound after a set time, creating echo or rhythmic effects
PanningPlacing sounds left or right in the stereo field to create width and separation
ModulationEffects like chorus, phaser, and flanger that add movement by altering a sound over time

  4. 4

  5. 5

  7. 7

Tips for Success ·

  • Less is more. A well-placed reverb or delay can transform a track. Piling on effects usually makes it worse.
  • Always A/B test your effects — bypass the plug-in and listen with and without it.
  • EQ is your most powerful tool. Get your EQ right before adding other effects.
  • If using Cambridge-MT, pick a song with 8–16 tracks. Don’t pick a 40-track session.
