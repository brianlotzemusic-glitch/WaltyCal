What this is ·

A 7-class-period open-ended project for high school music technology. Each student builds a 60–90 second ambient soundscape in Logic: a drone or pad, more layers, and automation on filters, reverb, volume and panning so the sound is always moving. There is no beat and no song form. They bounce it, reflect and present it with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (type: open-ended; focus: automation and texture), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Choose your listening examples, set up the Presentation Padlet and decide your minimum number of automation lanes (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–22), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–10), Class 3 (slides 11–13), Class 4 (slides 14–15), Class 6 (slides 16–18) and Class 7 (slides 19–20). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

Infinite Horizon · Open-Ended · Automation & Texture
Create an evolving ambient soundscape through automation and modulation. Focus on motion, texture, and space.

What students make ·

One evolving ambient soundscape, 60–90 seconds long, with no traditional rhythm or song structure. It is built from layered textures and drones using synths, samples and FX, with automation of parameters such as filters, reverb and volume, and with gradual transitions. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then play the piece and present it.

What this project reinforces ·

Deep dive into automation, modulation, and effects processing. Challenges students to think about music as atmosphere rather than structure.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Use automation and modulation to create evolving, non-repeating textures
  • Layer ambient sounds, drones, synths, and effects for depth
  • Explore how sound can create mood and atmosphere without traditional structure
  • Build a 60–90 second soundscape that sustains listener interest

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • Ambient examples of your choice to play on Class 1 and as your “compelling examples” (my fourth struggle). None is included.
  • A place to submit the .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (yours to set up; none is included).
  • A minimum number of automation lanes (my second struggle). Write it on the board and on page 14.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–22).

Before Class 1 ·

Choose two or three ambient examples to play, and decide what students listen for.
Set up the Presentation Padlet in your class page.
Decide your minimum number of automation lanes and your rule for the starter template (page 10).
Check that a student computer opens Logic and plays back through headphones.

What to watch (my fourth struggle and closing note) ·

Some students find ambient music boring, and many add a beat. This project stretches students’ definition of what music can be. Pair with a brief discussion of ambient and experimental music history.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • A minimum of three automation lanes works for most classes; use five for my Harder variation. The number is yours to set.
  • While students work, ask: what is moving in your piece right now? What was the last change you could hear?
  • Short, quiet listening works best with eyes closed and the lights low. Keep it to a minute or two.

Music they did not make ·

A kit suggestion

The sheet allows samples. Tell students to use only audio they made or have the right to use, and to hand in only their own soundscape. Examples you play for listening stay out of their projects.

Keep the volume moderate

Slow, low drones can hide how loud a piece is. Tell students to start with the headphones low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

What this project reinforces ·

Deep dive into automation, modulation, and effects processing. Challenges students to think about music as atmosphere rather than structure.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students default to adding beats. Remind them this is about texture and motion, not rhythm.
  • Soundscapes feel static because students don’t automate enough parameters. Require a minimum number of automation lanes.
  • Students struggle without traditional structure to guide them. Suggest thinking in phases: opening, evolution, climax, fade.
  • Some students find ambient music boring. Play compelling examples.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide a starter template with one drone layer and basic automation. Students add layers on top.

More Advanced: Extend to 2+ minutes. Require at least 5 automation lanes and use of Alchemy for custom ambient patches.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Adding beats: slide 9 and the Listen-back sheet’s “no beat crept in” check (page 15).
  • Too little automation: the Automation plan (page 14), with a box for the minimum you set.
  • No structure to guide them: the four phases on slide 8 and the Phase plan (page 13).
  • Ambient seems boring: the Listening log (page 12) asks what changes, which gives listening a job.
Where this kit helps with the variations (kit suggestion)
  • Easier: the starter-template recipe, to build on paper’s instructions in a few minutes (page 10).

Teacher + student

Where to find the tools an Infinite Horizon project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 3 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; check the labels on yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…: a Software Instrument track for synths and pads, an Audio track for samples and recordings.
A drone or padOn a Software Instrument track open the Library (Y) and browse the pad and synth sounds. Draw one long region with a held note or chord and loop it, so the sound never stops.
Synths and samplesAlchemy (my Harder variation) and the other software instruments make drones and textures. Drop audio you own onto an Audio track, or load it in Quick Sampler, to build a texture.
AutomationPress A to show automation, choose a parameter such as a filter cutoff, volume, pan or a reverb send, and click to add points on the lane. Drag points to shape the curve.
ModulationMany synth sounds have LFOs and envelopes that move a parameter for you. In Alchemy, look at the modulation section. Automation is you drawing the change; modulation is a signal doing it.
Filters and EQAdd Channel EQ or use a synth’s own filter. Automating a filter cutoff slowly opens or closes the sound.
Reverb and delayInsert Space Designer or ChromaVerb, or send tracks to a bus with a reverb or delay on it. Automate the send level to change how much space a sound has.
Mixer: volume and panningPress X to open the Mixer. Move each track’s fader for volume and its Pan knob to place it, then automate both.
Save versionsUse File › Save As… with a numbered name before each big change.
Length checkLook at the position display: a 60–90 second piece ends between 1:00 and 1:30 at the project’s tempo.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Infinite HorizonStarter template and my Harder variation
TEACHER
Easier + Harder

My Easier variation: provide a starter template with one drone layer and basic automation; students add layers on top. A Logic project is not included in this kit, so here is a recipe for it. Build it yourself in a few minutes, with sounds you have the right to use.

Starter template: one drone layer, basic automation ·

  1. New Software Instrument track; open the Library (Y) and pick a pad or drone sound.
  2. Draw one long region (8 to 16 bars) with a held note or chord, and cycle it so the sound never stops.
  3. Add a filter: the synth’s own cutoff or Channel EQ. Press A and choose its cutoff on the lane.
  4. Draw a slow curve: closed at the start, open by the middle, partly closed at the end. This is the “basic automation”.
  5. Add a reverb send, set it moderate, and automate nothing else yet. Save as “Starter”.
  6. Open it for each student (or share it on your class page). They add layers on top, as my Easier variation says.

My Harder variation, laid out ·

My optionHow to use it
Extend to 2+ minutesThe worked example (page 11) is 75 seconds, so at 2 minutes each phase is about a third longer. Students replan the four phases on page 13 for 120 seconds or more.
At least 5 automation lanesThe worked example has five lanes. Students list them on page 14 and you count them.
Alchemy for custom ambient patchesStudents build or edit a drone or pad patch in Alchemy rather than picking one from the Library. Show the source and filter sections and the modulation section (page 9).
How to use this page

Offer the starter to students who freeze at the empty project; nobody has to take it. Offer the Harder variation to students who finish early. Both are your choice, as in my notes. The 60–90 second length is the rule for everyone else (requirement 1).

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Slow Tide”, described on paper: no audio is included. Students’ projects will differ.

1 · A weak plan

I will make a calm ambient track with a soft beat.

It has a beat, and nothing says what will change.

2 · A clear plan

I am making “Slow Tide”, a 75-second soundscape about the sea coming in. It has no beat. It opens with a low drone, a pad joins at 8 seconds, and noise and bell notes arrive in the evolution. I will automate the drone’s filter, the pad’s reverb, the texture’s volume and panning and the chimes’ delay: five lanes, so nothing stays the same. It climbs to a swell at 45 seconds and fades to the drone alone.

3 · The map: 1:15, four phases, five layers, five lanes ·

4 · Phases
Opening0:00–0:12the drone alone, filter nearly closed; the pad joins at 0:08
Evolution0:12–0:45texture and chimes arrive; the filter and reverb open slowly; the texture pans left and right
Climax0:45–1:03the sweep rises, everything is at its fullest and widest, the chimes echo the most
Fade1:03–1:15texture and sweep leave, the pad fades, the filter closes on the drone
5 · Layers (in to out)
Droneone low, sustained synth chord that never stops0:00–1:15
Pada soft, wide synth pad that slowly joins0:08–1:10
Texturefiltered noise, like wind far away0:16–1:03
Chimesa few soft bell notes, no pulse, long reverb0:28–1:06
Sweepa noise swell that rises into the climax0:42–1:03
6 · Automation lanes
Drone · filter cutoff0:00–1:15
Pad · reverb send0:08–1:10
Texture · volume0:16–1:03
Texture · panning0:16–1:03
Chimes · delay feedback0:28–1:06
7 · Checking the numbers

12 + 33 + 18 + 12 = 75 s = 1:15, inside my 60–90 second range. Five lanes, my Harder variation’s minimum, and every one moves a lot. Three Padlet questions (invented): 1. Where did you first notice something change? 2. Which layer could you hear moving, and which was still? 3. Did your eyes-closed picture match the title?

Goals ·

Tips for Success ·

  • This project is about patience. Let sounds evolve slowly — don’t rush changes.
  • Reverb and delay are your best friends here. Use them to create a sense of space.
  • Automate everything. If a parameter stays the same the whole time, your soundscape will feel flat.
  • Close your eyes while listening to your piece. Does it take you somewhere? That’s the goal.

What a reflection is

A reflection is a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Answer all four prompts in your own words. You choose how.
