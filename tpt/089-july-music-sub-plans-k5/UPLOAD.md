## 2. Description

```
Need sub plans a substitute can teach without any music background, any technology, or any prep from you? These eight July music days are ready to print and hand over. Each day has a step-by-step plan with timed steps and words to say, plus two student sheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5. The theme is summer: the sounds of summer (waves, crickets and fireworks as sound words on paper), marching bands and parades, music at the beach and the park, and Independence Day as a short, optional, secular fact. Every Day 1 sheet has a Summer choice line that swaps Independence Day for a question about summer sounds, because some families do not celebrate it. No lyrics are printed, nobody has to perform anything, and the patriotic music facts (the national anthem, a famous march) are plain facts about when they were written.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (48 pages, PDF, US Letter)
• Cover and teacher notes with the July note, Independence Day and inclusive wording, summer sound and movement notes, marching band instrument notes, setup steps and a page guide (2 pages)
• Sub quick start, welcome sheet, days at a glance, quick games, word-rhythm chart, July facts and a class sign (7 pages)
• 8 sub plans, one per day, each 30–45 minutes (8 pages)
• 16 student sheets: a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet for each day (16 pages)
• Day extras: movement picture cards, chant word cards, a chant book page and a read-aloud quiz (4 pages)
• Early finishers (2 pages)
• Exit tickets (1 page)
• Answer keys for Days 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8; Day 3 is open-ended and its entry says what to look for (3 pages)
• Sub report form, folder labels, class star awards, terms of use and a thank-you page (5 pages)

THE EIGHT DAYS
• Day 1, July Sounds and Stories: K–2 hear a short reading about summer sounds, marching bands and parades, and circle answers; 3–5 read a passage of about 200 words and answer six questions (Independence Day is optional: a Summer choice line on each Day 1 sheet skips it)
• Day 2, Summer Syllables: clap the syllables of summer words and a short original four-line poem; K–2 color a circle for each clap, 3–5 sort words by syllables and build a four-beat chant
• Day 3, Summer Sound Scene: think about the sounds of summer: four sound makers (ocean wave, cricket, fireworks, flip-flops) as pictures and sound words, then draw and label a summer scene; K–2 label two sounds, 3–5 complete a sound planner and write two sentences
• Day 4, March and Freeze: move to a steady pencil tap and freeze, with 8 picture cards (wave, kite, sun, cricket, firework, popsicle, drum, star); K–2 circle how they moved, 3–5 reflect and design a quiet parade
• Day 5, Summer Word Trail: summer and marching band puzzles; K–2 match words to pictures and trace, 3–5 word search, a decode-the-message puzzle and a marching band sort
• Day 6, Summer Music Workers: music jobs at parades and concerts: five music jobs, from drum major to radio host; K–2 draw a job, 3–5 match jobs and write a job ad or interview questions
• Day 7, Summer Chant Book: build four-beat chants from summer word cards, mark the syllables, share with a partner, and keep a chant book page
• Day 8, Summer Bingo: bingo (12 squares for K–2, 16 for 3–5), a read-aloud quiz and Pass the Pencil, with a favorite-summer-sound reflection

WHY IT WORKS FOR SUBS
• No tech: nothing needs a computer, projector, speaker or video. Paper, pencils, crayons, hands and voices only
• No music skills: rhythm is taught through speech rhythm, clapping the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. The sub never reads music or sings
• No prep: print, put each day in a folder, and go. Each day works in any order for a mixed K–5 class
• Facts are short, sourced and secular, and the facts page answers common questions, so the days work in public schools and for students of every background
```
