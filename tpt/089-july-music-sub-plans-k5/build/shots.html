Summer SyllablesDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of summer words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 35 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: sun (1 clap), sum-mer (2 claps), sou-sa-phone (3 claps), wa-ter-mel-on (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Summer word claps
    Say: “Every word has a rhythm. Who has a summer word to clap?” Go around the room: each student says a summer word they like (beach, popsicle, swimming, anything), and the class claps its syllables together. A student who would rather not may pass.
  4. 5 minStep 4
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Waves roll in slow. Crickets chirp soft. Parade drums boom. Summer is here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  5. 10 minStep 5
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
Extra time? Word Claps from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Do only Part A and Part B; skip the poem.

July Sounds and StoriesDay 1 · Reading about July and summer music · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about summer sounds, marching bands and parades, and answer questions.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Summer is here. Ears are open. Hands are calm. Music class begins! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Summer sounds
    Say: “Summer has its own sounds. Close your eyes and think of one: waves, a cricket, a drum in a parade.” Ask: What is one summer sound you like? Take five answers. Any answer is fine. A student may pass.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact? The facts are also on page 8. Independence Day is optional: use the Summer choice line printed at the top of each Day 1 sheet to skip it (see also page 2).
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Cricket Quiet or Parade Echo from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 5; keep the reading and the questions.

July Sounds and StoriesA short reading about July and summer music
DAY 1
Grades K–2
NameDate
Summer choice: Independence Day is optional. To skip it, skip sentence 7 and question 5, and ask the class instead: What is a summer sound you like?
Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
July is the seventh month of the year. In the Northern Hemisphere, where the United States is, July is a summer month. Summer has its own sounds, like waves at the beach and crickets chirping. Many parks and towns have a parade or an outdoor concert in the summer. A marching band is a group of musicians who march while they make music, and it often marches in parades. A marching band has drums and brass instruments, like a trumpet. In the United States, July 4 is Independence Day, and many communities have a parade or a concert that day.

July Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8. Scissors are optional, and only for cutting out the picture cards (Day 4) or the word cards (Day 7)
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

March and FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
wave
Sway your arms side to side like a wave rolling in, then freeze with your arms still.
kite
Stand tall and tilt slowly from side to side like a kite in the wind, then freeze.
sun
Make a big circle with your arms like the sun, then a small circle, then freeze.
cricket
Take five tiny hops in place like a cricket, as quietly as you can, then freeze.
firework
Crouch low, then rise slowly and open your fingers wide, with no sound, then freeze.
popsicle
Stand tall and stiff like a popsicle, then melt slowly toward the floor, then freeze.
drum
March in place with high knees, quietly, like a band in a parade, then freeze.
star
Stand like a star with your arms and legs out wide, then freeze with a smile.
