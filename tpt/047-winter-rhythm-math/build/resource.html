Teacher Notes

Winter Rhythm Math · 15 worksheets in 3 levels · grades 2–5

How to use

  • Each worksheet has 8 snowy items. Students add the beats they see, compare them, or find the beats that are missing.
  • Beats are counted in quarter-note beats: a quarter note is 1, a half note is 2 and a whole note is 4.
  • Rests count exactly like notes, because a rest is a silent beat.
  • Two beamed eighth notes are 1 beat together. Single eighth notes (½ beat) appear only in Level C.
  • Rhythms use one line of a five-line staff, so the work is counting, not pitch.
  • Print single-sided. Each worksheet footer names its answer key page.

Pacing

  • One worksheet takes about 10 to 15 minutes. Use one as a warm-up or an exit ticket.
  • Plan for 5 class sessions per level, or one worksheet a week from December to March.
  • Before a worksheet, clap or tap two or three of its rows and count the beats aloud together.

Level A adds beats. Level B compares totals with >, < and =, and finds missing beats in 4/4 and 2/4. Level C adds dotted notes, ties, rests and 3/4. 6/8 is not included. Page 3 is a student reference. Pages 19–21 are the answer keys.

What students practice

Adding whole numbers and halves, comparing quantities with >, < and =, finding a missing addend, and reading note and rest values. No standards codes are listed here: please match the skills to your own curriculum.

Good to know

Every sum, comparison and missing-beat answer was recomputed in code from the drawn notes. Each item has exactly one answer. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Using the sheets in 3 steps

1Get ready

Print the worksheet single-sided. Students can keep the page 3 reference beside them.

2Count together

Clap or tap two or three rows and count the beats aloud before students start.

3Check

Students work on their own, then check with the answer key on the page named in the footer.

Student Reference

Keep this page beside you while you add and compare.

How long is each note and rest?

quarter note1 beat
half note2 beats
whole note4 beats
two eighth notes½ + ½ = 1 beat
dotted half note3 beats
dotted quarter note1½ beats
eighth note½ beat
tied notes1 + 1 = 2 beats
quarter rest1 beat of silence
half rest2 beats of silence
whole rest4 beats of silence
eighth rest½ beat of silence

A dot makes a note half as long again. A dotted half note is 2 + 1 = 3 beats. A dotted quarter note is 1 + ½ = 1½ beats. A tie joins two notes into one long sound, so you add their beats.

Comparing beats

SymbolNameMeansExample
>greater thanthe left side is longer4 > 3
<less thanthe left side is shorter2 < 3
=equal toboth sides are the same length2 = 1 + 1

Missing beats

A measure is full when its beats add up to the top number of the time signature: 4 beats in 4/4, 3 beats in 3/4 and 2 beats in 2/4. To find the missing beats, add what you can see and subtract the total from the top number.

Snow tip: build the answer one beat at a time, like stacking snowballs. 1 beat, then 2 beats, then 3, until the row is finished.
