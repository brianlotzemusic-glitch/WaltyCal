Teacher Notes

September Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · Hispanic Heritage Month, Labor Day, fall

Why September matters

September brings several ideas into a music room. Hispanic Heritage Month runs from September 15 to October 15 in the United States and honors the cultures and contributions of Hispanic and Latino Americans; Congress created a week for it in 1968 and extended it to a full month in 1988. This bundle meets five instruments, the guitar, maracas, güiro, marimba and cajón, and describes each one with the place or region that sources give. Labor Day, the first Monday in September, honors the achievements of workers, and Day 6 looks at jobs in music. Fall begins in the Northern Hemisphere about September 22 or 23, on the equinox (spring begins in the Southern Hemisphere), and many students go back to school. The music stays simple: clapping, marching in place and saying words. Facts are kept short and secular.

Teaching Hispanic Heritage Month with care

  • Read the facts as printed. Hispanic Heritage Month honors the cultures and contributions of Hispanic and Latino Americans.
  • Each instrument is tied to the place or region the sources give. Do not guess where a student’s family is from, and do not treat Spanish-speaking regions as one place.
  • Students come from many backgrounds. Invite them to share about their own families if they wish, and never ask one student to speak for a group.
  • Say the instrument names as printed (güiro: GWEE-roh, cajón: kah-HOHN). If you are unsure, try it together; a student may help.
  • If a student asks a hard question, say “That is a good question. Let’s ask your classroom teacher.” You may leave a note on the sub report.
  • No speeches or lyrics are quoted, and no piece of music is named.

The eight days

  1. September Snapshots: reading about September.
  2. Say It and Clap It: speech rhythm: clap the syllables.
  3. Instrument Sketchbook: design and label an instrument.
  4. Strum, Shake and Freeze: move like an instrument and freeze.

  7. My Fall Chant Book: compose a four-beat chant.
  8. Fall Roundup Bingo: review game day.

Use them in any order. The welcome sheet has a “Day #” column so the sub knows which day each class gets next.

Why subs can teach it

Eight lessons, each with timed steps, words to say and a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet. Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. Nobody has to read music or make music with an instrument. Games use only voices, hands and a pencil.

Standards

Supports relating music to varied contexts (MU:Cn11.0) and generating musical ideas (MU:Cr1.1), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps every game safe and happy.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Quiet Hands.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may know other musicians or celebrate other traditions. Thank them and move on.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Quiet Hands to settle a class, Name Claps to wake it up, and Echo Claps to finish with a smile.

TopicWhenA few facts
Hispanic Heritage MonthSeptember 15 to October 15A US observance that honors the cultures and contributions of Hispanic and Latino Americans. In 1968 Congress created Hispanic Heritage Week, and in 1988 Congress extended it to a full month. It begins on September 15, a date tied to the independence anniversaries of several Latin American countries. Students may be from any background: let them describe their own families in their own words.
Labor DayFirst Monday in September (September 6 in 2027)A US holiday that honors the social and economic achievements of American workers. Congress made it a national holiday in 1894.
Fall begins (the September equinox)About September 22 or 23 (September 23 in 2027)At the equinox, day and night are nearly equal in length. It begins fall in the Northern Hemisphere and spring in the Southern Hemisphere.
Back to schoolLate summer or early fallMany US schools start in late summer or early fall; dates vary.
The five instrumentsDays 3, 4, 5 and 8Guitar (gi-TAR): A stringed instrument with a hollow body, a neck and a round sound hole. A player strums or plucks the strings. The classical guitar is also called the Spanish guitar. Maracas (mah-RAH-kahs): Shaken instruments, usually used as a pair. Maracas have pre-Columbian roots in the Caribbean and South America, and early ones were made from gourds. Güiro (GWEE-roh): A hollow gourd with notches cut in one side. A player scrapes a stick across the notches. It is used in music from Cuba, Puerto Rico and other Latin American places. Marimba (mah-RIM-bah): Wooden bars of different lengths, each with a tube below it. A player strikes the bars with mallets. The marimba is popular throughout Central America. Cajón (kah-HOHN): A box-shaped drum that comes from Peru. The player sits on the box and strikes the front with the hands. A sound hole is cut in the back.

Radio host: talks with listeners and may interview guests. Music teacher: helps students learn about music.

Read, Answer and DrawRead, answer and draw
DAY 1
Grades 3–5
NameDate
Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
September is a month of beginnings and celebrations. On the first Monday in September, the United States observes Labor Day, a holiday that honors the contributions of workers. Hispanic Heritage Month runs from September 15 to October 15. In 1968 Congress created a week for this celebration, and in 1988 Congress extended it to a full month. The month celebrates the cultures and contributions of Hispanic and Latino Americans. Music is one way to learn about cultures, and learning can start with careful listening and good questions. Musicians also work together, listening closely to one another. Instruments such as the guitar, maracas, güiro, marimba and cajón are used in music from different Spanish-speaking regions, and each one has its own story. September is also the month when fall begins in the Northern Hemisphere, about September 22 or 23, on a day called the equinox, when day and night are nearly equal in length. In the Southern Hemisphere, spring begins then. Many students go back to school in late summer or early fall, so September is a good time to start the year by listening carefully, working together and learning something new.

DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of September words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), ap-ple (2 claps), ma-rim-ba (3 claps), cel-e-bra-tion (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Autumn leaves fall. Workers, we cheer. Shake, strum, scrape, tap. September’s here. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant share
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Name Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Strum, Shake and FreezeDay 4 · Move like an instrument and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents move to a steady tap, copy the sub’s moves with their own (call and response), and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them or cut them out).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Rules of the freeze
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Practice once: tap a pencil on a desk for five seconds, stop, and wait for stillness.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Call and response
    Say: “Musicians often answer each other: one calls, the other answers.” Make a simple move (a clap, a head nod, a shoulder shrug) and say “Call!” The class copies it and says “Answer!” Do five moves. Then let a few students be the caller.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap. Use at least six cards, and try a slow tap, a fast tap and a bouncy tap (long, short, long, short, like skipping). The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 20, 3–5 page 21. Students sit and fill it in. Read the directions aloud.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Statue garden
    Tap a few beats, stop, and let the class freeze in a favorite pose. Walk around and name two poses you notice.
Extra time? Statue Stop or Step in Place from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Use only four cards.

DAY 6

NameDate
On Labor Day we honor the work that people do, and music is work too. Every concert, school show and radio program depends on many workers, and not all of them are on the stage. A conductor leads a group of musicians during a performance. An instrument maker builds or repairs instruments, such as guitars, so that musicians have something to use. A sound engineer sets up and runs the equipment that carries sound to the audience at a concert. A radio host talks with listeners and may interview guests. A music teacher helps students learn how music works and introduces them to many kinds of music. Most of these jobs need careful listening, and many need teamwork.
