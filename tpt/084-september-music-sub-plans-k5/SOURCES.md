### 2. Origin: Congress created Hispanic Heritage Week in 1968 and extended it to a full month in 1988 (Day 1 3-5 reading and its question, facts page, teacher notes). Public law numbers, the sponsors' names and the presidents who signed are NOT printed
Source: https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2017/09/25/origins-of-national-hispanic-heritage-month/ and https://history.house.gov/historicalhighlight/detail/15032398402 (QA fetched 26 Nov 2026: week law signed 17 Sep 1968; month law signed 17 Aug 1988; Sept 15 is the 1821 independence anniversary of Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua and Costa Rica, and the period includes Mexico's 16 Sep). Also https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_Heritage_Month (fetched 26 Nov 2026: National Hispanic Heritage Week established by legislation in 1968, signed by President Lyndon Johnson; expanded to the month of September 15 to October 15 by legislation in 1988 signed by President Ronald Reagan). The owner's brief gives the same: a week in 1968, a month in 1988.

### 6. Guitar: a stringed instrument, usually with six strings, strummed or plucked, with a neck, headstock, sound hole and strings; the classical guitar is also called the Spanish guitar (facts page, Day 3, bingo clue Guitar). No claim is made about who invented the guitar or where
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar (fetched 26 Nov 2026: a stringed instrument played by plucking or strumming; parts neck, headstock, sound hole, strings; 'Classical guitars are also known as Spanish guitars'; 'The origins of the modern guitar are not known', so no origin is printed).

### 7. Maracas: shaken, usually as a pair; pre-Columbian roots in the Caribbean and South America; early ones were made from gourds (facts page, Day 3, bingo clue Maracas)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maraca (fetched 26 Nov 2026: 'of pre-Columbian origin', credited to the Arawak and Taino peoples; used by Indigenous groups in parts of South America and the Caribbean; early rattles made from gourds; 'usually played as part of a pair'). The pages say 'Caribbean and South America' and name no single people.

### 8. Guiro: a hollow gourd with parallel notches cut in one side, scraped with a stick; used in music from Cuba, Puerto Rico and other Latin American places; pronounced GWEE-roh (facts page, Day 3, bingo clue Guiro)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCiro (fetched 26 Nov 2026: 'a Latin American percussion instrument ... an open-ended, hollow gourd with parallel notches cut in one side'; a stick is rubbed across the notches; appears in Cuban, Puerto Rican and other Latin American music; Spanish pronunciation [gwiro]). Origin credits are not printed.

### 9. Marimba: wooden bars of different lengths, each with a tube (resonator) below, struck with mallets; popular throughout Central America (facts page, Day 3, bingo clue Marimba). No claim is made about its origin or about a national instrument
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marimba (fetched 26 Nov 2026: bars usually wood, a resonator tube below each bar; struck with mallets; 'popular throughout Central America'; accounts of its origins differ, so none is printed).

### 10. Cajon: a box-shaped drum that comes from Peru; the player sits on it and strikes the front face with the hands; a sound hole is cut in the back; pronounced kah-HOHN (facts page, Day 3, bingo clue Cajon). The drawing shows the plain front face with a hand patting it and no hole (the sound hole is in the back, which is not shown)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caj%C3%B3n (fetched 26 Nov 2026: 'originally from Peru'; the player sits on the box and strikes the front face between the knees; the sound hole is cut on the back side; the striking surface is the tapa). The Afro-Peruvian history is NOT printed.
