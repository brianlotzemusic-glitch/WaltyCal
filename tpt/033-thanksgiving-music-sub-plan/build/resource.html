Thanksgiving Music Sub Plan K–5 · 3 no-prep lessons

What it is

Three complete music lessons for Thanksgiving week that a substitute can teach with no tech, no instruments and no music background (Lesson 3 needs a way to play one piece of music you choose). Each has a step-by-step plan with words to say, timed steps, and two worksheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5.

The three lessons

  1. Turkey Rhythm Reading: reading and clapping rhythms.
  2. Gratitude Lyrics: writing a gratitude chant or verse.
  3. Thankful Listening: listening and responding to music.

Use them in any order; each lesson takes 30 to 45 minutes. The welcome sheet has a “Lesson #” column so the sub knows which one each class gets next.

Set up the sub tub (about 15 minutes, once)

  1. Fill in the welcome sheet (page 3), including the listening music for Lesson 3.
  2. Print the plans, the rhythm chart and the sub report; copy each worksheet for your largest class.
  3. Put each lesson in its own folder with its worksheets, and add the answer key.

Lesson 3 listening music

No audio is included. Choose a calm instrumental piece you already have the right to play in class, about 2 to 3 minutes long, and write how to start it on the welcome sheet. If your sub has no audio, the plan gives a no-tech sound show instead.

About the notation

Rhythms are in 4/4 and use quarter notes, paired eighth notes, quarter rests, half notes and whole notes, engraved with a professional music font. Every rhythm is read back by computer and checked for 4 beats per bar, except the bars in Lesson 1 part B that students must spot as wrong; the key comes from the same checked data. Each example lyric line is counted by computer too.

Standards

Supports reading rhythm notation (MU:Pr4.2), creating original lyrics and rhythms (MU:Cr2.1) and describing what students hear (MU:Re7.2), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Good to know

  • Prints well in black and white.
  • Not every family celebrates Thanksgiving. The lessons are about food, gratitude and music, with no history lesson and no required beliefs, and every student can pass or choose their own thankful word.
  • Rhythm syllables follow the common ta / ti-ti system.

All lessons, chants, verses, games, words and art are original. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap a short rhythm and have the class clap it back.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps the loud parts happy and safe.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or Feast Freeze.
  • Students may have very different holiday plans. Keep the talk on gratitude, food and music, and let anyone pass.

Turkey Rhythm ReadingLesson 1 · Reading and clapping rhythms · no tech, no prep
LESSON 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap, say and read rhythms with ta, ti-ti and shh (grades 3–5 add ta-a and ta-a-a-a).
You needPencils, the rhythm chart (page 5) shown or copied on the board, and the worksheets.
CopiesK–2: page 7
3–5: page 8
about 45 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Turkey trot echo
    Say: “Happy November, musicians! I’m your guest teacher today. Let’s warm up. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s make music! Then pat a slow, steady beat on your knees and have the class pat along.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Learn the rhythm words
    Show the rhythm chart (page 5). Point to the key at the bottom: ta is one clap, ti-ti is two quick claps, shh is a silent beat (open hands, no sound). Say line 1 aloud, then clap it. The class echoes. Do lines 1 to 4 with every class. Grades 3–5 also try lines 5 to 8, which add ta-a (a long sound, 2 beats) and ta-a-a-a (4 beats).
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Gobble and freeze
    Pick one chart line as the gobble line. Clap lines in any order. The class echoes every line, but when the gobble line comes they stay silent and gobble quietly with their hands over their mouths. Let a student leader choose the lines for the last round.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheets
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 Feather Rhythms (page 7), 3–5 Turkey Rhythm Math (page 8). Read the directions aloud. Clap each rhythm out loud with the class first if students ask. Answers are on page 16.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Pass the pie
    Sit in a circle or stay in rows. Clap a short rhythm to the first student, who claps it to the next, and so on. Does it arrive the same? Ask: Which rhythm was the hardest to pass?
Extra time? Students make up a 4-beat rhythm of their own with pie, corn, tur-key and shh, then clap it for a neighbor. Or use the early finishers page (page 15). Short class? Do steps 1 to 3 and send the worksheet home to finish.

Gratitude LyricsLesson 2 · Writing a gratitude chant or verse · no tech, no prep
LESSON 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents write original thankful lyrics to a steady beat: a one-beat word chant (K–2) or a four-line rhyming verse (3–5).
You needPencils, the worksheets, and the board for the class chant. No instruments needed.
CopiesK–2: page 10
3–5: page 11
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Thankful circle
    Everyone pats a slow, steady beat. Go around the room: each student says one thing they are thankful for in a single beat, such as pie, pup or sun. Anyone can pass. Tip: thankful can be anything small, a pet, a sunny day, a good book or a hot meal. Not every family celebrates Thanksgiving, and that is fine; this lesson is about noticing good things.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Write a class chant
    On the board, write Thank you for ______. Say it on a steady beat (Thank, you, for and then a word of your own on beat 4). Take word ideas from the class and chant each line together. A one-syllable word is one clap (ta); a two-syllable word, like turkey, is two quick claps (ti-ti).
  3. 15 minStep 3
    Worksheets
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 My Thankful Chant (page 10), 3–5 Gratitude Verse (page 11). Grades 3–5: read the example verse aloud on a steady beat, and show students how to count syllables on their fingers. A line should have exactly 7 syllables and then a silent beat. This lesson has no answer key because every answer is the student’s own.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Share and chant
    Invite volunteers to read or chant their lines while the class pats the beat. Praise one specific thing: a funny word, a great rhyme or a line that fits the beat perfectly. Finish with the whole class chanting Thank you for ______, each student adding their own word.
Extra time? Add a second verse, or turn a favorite line into a poster with a drawing. Or use the early finishers page (page 15). Short class? Do steps 1 and 2, then let students start the worksheet.

Thankful ListeningLesson 3 · Listening and responding to music · no tech, no prep
LESSON 3
Sub plan
GoalStudents listen carefully to one piece of music and describe its speed, loudness, instruments and mood.
You needThe listening music your teacher chose (see the welcome sheet, page 3), a way to play it, pencils and the worksheets. No audio is included in this resource.
CopiesK–2: page 13
3–5: page 14
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Listening ears
    Say: “A good listener has quiet hands, a quiet voice and open ears. Today we listen the way a musician does.” Ask: What sounds can you hear in the room right now? Take 3 or 4 answers.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Music words
    Read the words on the student sheet aloud. K–2: loud, soft, fast, slow, happy, calm. 3–5: tempo, dynamics, instrument families, mood. Act them out: clap loud and soft, walk fast and slow.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    First listen: just listen
    Play the music your teacher chose (about 2 to 3 minutes). Students close their eyes or look down and only listen. No audio today? Do the sound show instead: clap, stomp and whisper in a pattern of soft, loud, soft, fast, slow, and let students describe what changed.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Second listen: fill in the sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 My Listening Page (page 13), 3–5 Listening Response (page 14). Play the music again and let students fill in the sheet as it plays. Play it a third time if there is time. There are no wrong answers, because each student describes what they heard.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Turn and talk
    Partners share one thing they heard and one thing they imagined. Then ask: Who heard something different from their partner?
Extra time? Students draw a second picture for a different scene, or write 3 sentences about the music. Or use the early finishers page (page 15). Short class? Do steps 1, 3 and 4 only.
