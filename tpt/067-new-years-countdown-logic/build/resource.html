What this is ·

A 3-class-period build-up and drop project for high school music technology. Every student arranges a track of 40 to 48 bars in Logic Pro: an intro, a build-up with risers, a snare roll and a filter sweep, a 10-second countdown, the drop and an outro. Students choose a tempo, do the timing math, build and mix the parts, present the result and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Look at the tempo table, the section map, the snare ladder and the cue sheet (pages 11–14) and the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–22), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–9) and Class 3 (slides 10–14). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, recordings, lyrics or melodies are included, and no songs, artists or albums are named. Students make every sound themselves, from the instruments, samples and loops in Logic and from their own voices or synth beeps.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: see the variations on page 6.
  • Pace: Class 1 is the timing math and the plan, Class 2 the intro and the build-up, Class 3 the countdown, the drop, the mix and the presentations (page 5); the pacing page also shows two and four periods.

  • Grading: the rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 19).

OVERVIEW
Build-up and drop project
The project in one line

New Year’s Countdown · Build-up and drop · Logic Pro
Five sections, ten seconds of numbers, one big drop.

What students make ·

A track of 40 to 48 bars in five sections, built in Logic Pro: an 8-bar intro, an 8-bar build-up, a countdown of 4, 5 or 6 bars, a 16-bar drop and an 8-bar outro. The build-up has a riser, a snare roll that speeds up from quarter to eighth, sixteenth and thirty-second notes, a filter sweep and a pitch-up. The countdown is the numbers 10 to 1, one on each second, and the drop lands on the first beat after 1. Students hand in a bounced file and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the track and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a build-up and drop is made. Students learn what risers, snare rolls, filter sweeps and pitch-ups do to tension, how to turn a tempo into bars and seconds with simple arithmetic, why 10 seconds is a whole number of bars only at some tempos, and how to place ten spoken cues on exact beats, all in Logic Pro.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Arrange a 40 to 48 bar track in five sections: intro, build-up, a 10-second countdown, drop and outro
  • Use one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds and tempo = 24 × bars to choose a tempo at which 10 seconds is a whole number of bars, and to place every section
  • Build tension in Logic Pro with a riser, a snare roll that speeds up through four note values, a filter sweep and a pitch-up, all shaped with automation
  • Place the numbers 10 to 1 one second apart and land the drop on the first beat after 1, then mix, bounce and reflect on the choices

About the theme ·

Many communities around the world count down the last seconds of the old year together and cheer when the new one begins. This project borrows only that idea: a count to a big moment. It is a music-technology project that suits any class. Students may choose a different countdown, such as a launch, a game start or the final seconds of a race.

The build-up and drop is a structure found in many styles of electronic music: the energy climbs for a while, a short count or a moment of near-silence leads in, and then everything arrives at once.

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav files, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The tempo table, section map, snare ladder and cue sheet, printed (pages 11–14), or shown on the projector.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–22).
  • A microphone is optional: the countdown can be beeps made with a synth. A MIDI keyboard is optional too.

Before Class 1 ·

Read the tempo table (page 11) so you can answer ‘where does the drop land?’ at 96, 120 and 144 BPM.
Open Logic Pro on a student computer and check which tools your version has: Alchemy, Quick Sampler, Drum Machine Designer, Step Sequencer, Channel EQ (see page 8).
Check that you can add a marker and draw filter automation.
Read the worked example (pages 9–10).

What to watch ·

Watch for numbers that drift off the second, a roll that does not speed up, a build-up that is already loud before it starts, and a drop that is not louder than the countdown. A good listening day: play each student’s track and ask the class to call out the moment the tension is highest, and then to count aloud with the numbers. If the drop lands a little before or after the room says it should, check the cue sheet together. This kit names no songs or artists.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: what is rising right now? What could you take away before the drop?
  • Say the theme plainly: the countdown is an inspiration for the structure, and students may count down to anything.
  • A student who is stuck can build the riser, the countdown and the drop only: those three already sound like a build-up.

The voice (optional) ·

The countdown can be students’ own voices, a classmate’s voice with permission, or beeps and tones made with a synth, so no one has to record themselves. Use only sounds students made or have the right to use.

Keep the volume moderate

Rising sounds, fast drum rolls and a big drop can be tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a build-up and drop is made. Students learn what risers, snare rolls, filter sweeps and pitch-ups do to tension, how to turn a tempo into bars and seconds with simple arithmetic, why 10 seconds is a whole number of bars only at some tempos, and how to place ten spoken cues on exact beats, all in Logic Pro.

Common student struggles ·

  • The drop does not feel big: take things away in the countdown (kick, bass and hi-hat stop) and bring everything back on the drop bar (page 15).
  • The arithmetic looks hard: work one cue aloud with the formula beats = second × BPM ÷ 60, then bar = 17 + whole bars and beat = 1 + the leftover (page 14).
  • The numbers drift: check that the first number starts exactly on bar 17 beat 1, and that the drop is on the first beat of bar 17 plus the countdown bars (page 11).
  • The snare roll does not feel faster: make each rung of the ladder two full bars, and raise the velocity a little on every rung (page 13).

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Keep the tempo at 120 BPM, where 10 seconds is exactly 5 bars and every number lands on a beat (page 14); skip the pitch-up, and use only the 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16 rungs of the snare ladder.

More Advanced: Build two versions at two tempos, such as 96 and 144 BPM, and compare where the drop lands (page 11). Or add a false drop: a second countdown of 3, 2, 1 inside the drop section that ends in silence before the full drop.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • A drop that does not hit: the section map (page 12) shows what leaves in the countdown, and the mix checklist (page 15) covers the drop.
  • The arithmetic: the tempo table (page 11) gives every section start at three tempos, and the worked example (pages 9–10) shows each step.
  • Numbers that drift: the cue sheet (page 14) lists the bar and beat of every number at three tempos.
  • A roll that does not speed up: the ladder (page 13) lists hits per beat and the gap in milliseconds for each rung.

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 19). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Build-Up & Tension (rubric row 1): listen for the roll speeding up, the riser climbing and the filter opening; open the automation lanes (page 13).
  • Countdown & Drop (rubric row 2): play bars 17 onward and count the numbers against the cue sheet; the drop must be on the first beat after 1 (page 14).
  • Tempo & Timing Math (rubric row 3): recompute one section start with the student: bars gone by × (240 ÷ BPM) (page 11).
  • Sound & Mix (rubric row 4): listen for the voice over the riser, check the meter for clipping and compare the loudness of the countdown and the drop (page 15).
  • Plan & Reflection (rubric row 5): the section map, the cue sheet, the feedback questions and the Reflection page (page 22) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 22): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Plan & Reflection row of the rubric.

Notes / observations ·

A good listening day: play each student’s track and ask the class to call out the moment the tension is highest, and then to count aloud with the numbers. If the drop lands a little before or after the room says it should, check the cue sheet together. This kit names no songs or artists.

New Year’s CountdownLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a New Year’s Countdown track is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

Project and tempoFile › New…, then a Software Instrument track to start. Set the project tempo in the control bar to 96, 120 or 144 BPM and keep the time signature at 4/4. The timing arithmetic only works at that tempo, so set it first. Set a cycle from bar 1 to the bar after your last outro bar to hear the whole track.
Sections with markersShow the Marker global track (the Global Tracks controls or the View menu). Add a marker with the plus (+) button in that track, or with a Create Marker key command if your version has one, then drag or type it to the bars on your section map. Name them Intro, Build-up, Countdown, Drop and Outro.
Risers and reverse effectsMake a riser in Alchemy or Retro Synth, for example a noise or saw sound that rises, or record a cymbal or crash into an audio track or Quick Sampler and play it backward. Look for a Reverse option in the audio region inspector or in Quick Sampler or Sample Alchemy, if your version has it. Place the riser so it ends on the drop.
Snare rollUse a snare on a Drum Machine Designer pad or in Quick Sampler. In the Piano Roll, set the grid division to 1/4, then 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32 as the roll speeds up, and click the hits in, or draw them with the Step Sequencer if your version has it. Raise the velocity a little on each rung so the roll swells.
Filter sweepOpen the filter of Alchemy or Retro Synth and automate its cutoff, or put a Channel EQ (its high cut) or an Auto Filter, if your version has it, on the riser or the whole mix. Press A to show automation, choose the cutoff or the filter frequency, and click points to draw it rising across the build-up.
Pitch-up and tensionAutomate the pitch or tuning control of the riser instrument, if it has one, so it climbs across the build-up. Twelve semitones is one octave. Also automate volume, so the riser swells, and add reverb that grows toward the countdown if you like.
Countdown voiceRecord each number into an audio track with a microphone, or make beeps with a synth. Place each one at the bar and beat on your cue sheet. Drag the region with the snap set to a fine value, or nudge it, and read its position, then play it against the clock. Leave a short rest after the number 1 so the drop hits.
Drop, impact and outroAt the first beat after 1, bring in the kick, bass, lead and an impact or crash, and stop the riser. Fill the drop with the loop you built in the intro, only bigger. In the outro, take layers away and let the filter close if you like.
Mixer, automation and bouncePress X for the Mixer and set levels and pan. Keep the countdown voice above the riser, and keep the master out of the red. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV, for the whole track. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

The same track, page 2 of 2: the map of the whole track, the tension tools, then the numbers.

5 · The map: 45 bars, 5 sections, 10 elements ·

6 · The filter sweep and pitch-up ·

7 · Checking the numbers

The snare ladder: 1/4 on bars 9–10 (500 ms apart); 1/8 on bars 11–12 (250 ms apart); 1/16 on bars 13–14 (125 ms apart); 1/32 on bars 15–16 (62.5 ms apart); that is 120 hits in 8 bars. The sweep: 200 Hz at bar 9, 500 Hz at bar 11, 1,500 Hz at bar 13, 5,000 Hz at bar 15, 12,000 Hz at bar 17. The pitch climbs 12 semitones, one octave, so its frequency doubles. At 120 BPM one bar is 2 s, so 45 bars = 90 s, and 8 + 8 + 5 + 16 bars = 37 bars gone by before the outro, which starts at 74 s.

8 · The automation

The riser fades in over bars 9 and 10 and swells to bar 17; the filter cutoff and the pitch rise together; the kick, bass and hi-hat stop at bar 17 and return on bar 22; the numbers are 3 dB above the riser.

9 · Three feedback questions (invented)

1. Where did you feel the tension was highest, and what caused it? 2. Was the drop big enough? What would make it bigger? 3. Did the countdown feel steady? Which number felt off, if any?

10 · Moments to point out in class
  • The kick, bass and hi-hat stop at bar 17, so the countdown is the emptiest moment, and that makes the drop on bar 22 feel biggest.
  • Both the cutoff and the pitch reach their top at bar 17 and hold there; at 120 BPM the numbers fall on beats 1 and 3, so the class can count along.

Step 1: choose tempo and map. In 4/4 there are 4 beats in a bar. These formulas turn a tempo into seconds and bars. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas (4/4)

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM

Seconds per bar = 240 ÷ BPM

Bars in 10 seconds = 10 ÷ (240 ÷ BPM) = BPM ÷ 24

Tempo = 24 × bars, when 10 seconds is a whole number of bars

A section starts at (bars gone by) × 240 ÷ BPM seconds

Example: 120 BPM: 240 ÷ 120 = 2 s per bar, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 bars, and 120 ÷ 24 = 5 too.

Rounding rules

Seconds per beat and per bar to 3 decimals, bars in 10 seconds to 4, and seconds for sections and totals to 2; a 5 rounds up. So 240 ÷ 144 = 1.667 seconds per bar, and the 16 bars before the countdown at 144 BPM last 26.67 seconds (26.666… rounds up). Every start time is rounded from its exact value.

Step 5: countdown and drop. The number 10 is said on second 0 of the countdown, which starts on bar 17 beat 1, and the number 1 on second 9. The drop is on second 10, the first beat of the drop section. The table gives the bar and beat of every cue.

How to find a cue at your tempo

Beats from the start of the countdown = second × BPM ÷ 60. Bar = 17 + the whole bars in (beats ÷ 4). Beat = 1 + the beats left over.

Example: 96 BPM, second 3: 3 × 96 ÷ 60 = 4.8 beats, which is 1 whole bar and 0.8 beat, so bar 18, beat 1.8. A beat of 1.8 is 80% of the way from beat 1 to beat 2.

Step 6: mix and bounce. A mix makes the build-up grow, the countdown clear and the drop big. Work through the boxes with headphones, then check each one off.

Build-up (bars 9 to 16)
The riser starts quiet and swells; it is never louder than the drums.
The snare roll grows louder as it speeds up.
The filter opens and the pitch climbs without a sudden jump.
Countdown (bars 17 on)
I can hear every number clearly over the riser.
The kick, bass and hi-hat have stopped, so the sound feels empty.
The numbers sit on the cue sheet’s bars and beats.

New Year’s Countdown

Music Technology | Build-Up and Drop Project | 3 class periods

Overview ·

Arrange a countdown track in Logic Pro: 40 to 48 bars in five sections. An intro sets the mood. A build-up raises the tension with a riser, a snare roll that speeds up from quarter notes to eighth, sixteenth and thirty-second notes, a filter sweep that opens a low-pass filter, and a pitch that climbs. Then comes a 10-second countdown, the numbers 10 to 1 with one number on each second, and the drop lands on the first beat after 1. A drop section and an outro finish the track. Ten seconds is a whole number of bars only at some tempos: tempo = 24 × bars, so 96, 120 and 144 BPM give 4, 5 and 6 bars. Nobody needs to read music.

Goals ·

  • Arrange a 40 to 48 bar track in five sections: intro, build-up, a 10-second countdown, drop and outro
  • Use one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds and tempo = 24 × bars to choose a tempo at which 10 seconds is a whole number of bars, and to place every section
  • Build tension in Logic Pro with a riser, a snare roll that speeds up through four note values, a filter sweep and a pitch-up, all shaped with automation
  • Place the numbers 10 to 1 one second apart and land the drop on the first beat after 1, then mix, bounce and reflect on the choices

Key Vocabulary ·

RiserA sound that climbs in pitch, brightness or volume to build tension, such as a rising synth or noise sweep
Snare rollA fast run of snare hits that gets quicker in steps, from quarter notes (1 hit per beat) to eighths (2), sixteenths (4) and thirty-seconds (8)
Filter sweepMoving the cutoff of a low-pass filter from low to high so that a muffled sound slowly opens up and brightens
AutomationDrawing a change over time in Logic, such as a filter cutoff, a pitch or a volume, so a control moves by itself
DropThe moment after a build-up when the full beat and bass arrive together; here it lands on the first beat after the number 1

Tips for Success ·

  • Get the intro and the drop sounding good before you build the tension. The build-up needs something to lead to.
  • Make the drop feel bigger by taking things away in the countdown: fewer sounds there make the drop seem louder.
  • Check your countdown against the clock on your cue sheet: the numbers should be exactly one second apart, not just roughly even.
  • Save a new version of your project at the end of each class, so you can always go back.
