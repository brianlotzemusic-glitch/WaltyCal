The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with 96, 120 or 144 BPM and a section map with the right bar numbers, so that Class 2 is all building. Check each tempo against the tempo table before they go.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the plan, the set-up and the intro; Class 2 is the build-up, the countdown, the drop, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the countdown, the drop and the outro, and Class 4 on the mix, presentations and reflection.

Riser checklist
I made a riser from a rising synth or noise sound (Alchemy or Retro Synth), or a reversed cymbal (a Reverse effect, if my version has it).
It starts quiet at bar 9 and gets louder and brighter.
It reaches its top by bar 17, holds through the countdown and stops on the drop.
Automation checklist
The low-pass cutoff rises from low to open across bars 9 to 17 (instrument filter, Channel EQ or Auto Filter, if my version has it).
The riser’s pitch climbs, for example 12 semitones, by bar 17, if its instrument has a pitch control.
Volume swells, and the filter stays open on the drop.

Step 1: choose tempo and map. In 4/4 there are 4 beats in a bar. These formulas turn a tempo into seconds and bars. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas (4/4)

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM

Seconds per bar = 240 ÷ BPM

Bars in 10 seconds = 10 ÷ (240 ÷ BPM) = BPM ÷ 24

Tempo = 24 × bars, when 10 seconds is a whole number of bars

A section starts at (bars gone by) × 240 ÷ BPM seconds

Example: 120 BPM: 240 ÷ 120 = 2 s per bar, 10 ÷ 2 = 5 bars, and 120 ÷ 24 = 5 too.

Rounding rules

Seconds per beat and per bar to 3 decimals, bars in 10 seconds to 4, and seconds for sections and totals to 2; a 5 rounds up. So 240 ÷ 144 = 1.667 seconds per bar, and the 16 bars before the countdown at 144 BPM last 26.67 seconds (26.666… rounds up). Every start time is rounded from its exact value.
