Teacher Notes

Band Rhythm Counting Worksheets · 20 worksheets · grades 5–8

How to use

  • Students look at the time signature, then write the count in the box under every note and every rest.
  • Write the count where each note or rest begins. A half note on beat 1 gets a 1; the 2 is held, so nothing is written for it.
  • Rests are counted exactly like notes. They are silent beats, not skipped ones.
  • In 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4 the beat is a quarter note: count 1 2 3 4, and write & for the half beat between (the “and” of a beat).
  • In 6/8 the eighth note gets one count: count 1 2 3 4 5 6. Counts 1 and 4 are the strong pulses. If you count 6/8 in two (1-la-li 2-la-li), write the numbers 1–6 anyway.
  • Check with the answer key, or clap, tap or play on one note with the class: say the counts aloud, then play.
  • Print single-sided. Every worksheet has 6 lines of two or three measures.

Great for

  • Band, orchestra and general music, grades 5–8
  • Warm-ups, homework, sub plans and rhythm quizzes
  • Practice before sight-reading or a new method-book page

Standards

Practices reading, counting and understanding rhythm and meter. Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music, Performing (MU:Pr4.2, understanding the elements of music), in grades 5–8.

Good to know

The rhythms are written on a one-line staff so students focus on counting, not pitch. Every line is generated and checked so that its measures add up to the time signature; the keys are built from the same data as the worksheets. Notes of the same line are beamed by beat. No ties, no sixteenth notes and no songs are used. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Student Reference

Keep this page nearby while you count.

Note and rest values · (in 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4)

whole note4 beats
half note2 beats
quarter note1 beat
eighth note½ beat
dotted half note3 beats
dotted quarter note1½ beats
whole rest4 beats of silence
half rest2 beats of silence
quarter rest1 beat of silence
eighth rest½ beat of silence

A dot after a note makes it half as long again: a half note is 2 beats, a dotted half note is 2 + 1 = 3 beats; a quarter note is 1 beat, a dotted quarter note is 1 + ½ = 1½ beats.

Counting in 4/4 and 6/8

In 6/8 a quarter note is 2 counts, a dotted quarter note is 3 counts and a dotted half note is 6 counts. Write the count under the place where each note begins.
