What this is ·

A 3-class-period house remix project for high school music technology. Every student remixes one traditional winter song at 128 BPM: they work out the timing, build a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid, add a four-chord loop and the tune, set up sidechain compression so the bass, pad and tune duck on each kick, and arrange and mix 64 bars with a build and a drop. Students plan with simple arithmetic, present the result and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Look at the timing page, the song page, the step grid, the chord page and the sidechain pages (pages 11–16) and the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–25), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–8), Class 2 (slides 9–14) and Class 3 (slides 15–20). The slide about the three classes carries the badge for all three. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

House Remix · Four on the floor, sidechain and a 64-bar arrangement · Logic Pro
One traditional winter song, rebuilt as a house track at 128 BPM.

What students make ·

A 64-bar house remix of one traditional winter song, built in Logic Pro. A 4-bar pattern puts a kick on every beat, an open hat and a bass on the off-beats and a clap on beats 2 and 4. A four-chord loop and the tune sit in one key. A compressor with a side chain turns the bass, pad and tune down each time the kick hits, and the layers become an arrangement with an intro, a build, a drop, a break, a second drop and an outro. Students hand in a bounced file and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the remix and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a house track is built and why it sounds the way it does. Students work out a tempo with arithmetic, place a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid, add a bass and chords in one key, use sidechain compression to make room for the kick, and arrange a build, a drop and a break, all in Logic Pro.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Plan the track with timing math: one beat = 60 ÷ BPM seconds (468.75 ms at 128 BPM), one 16th-note step = 15 ÷ BPM seconds, one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds (1.875 s)
  • Build a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid: kick on grid steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, open hat on the off-beats, clap on 2 and 4, and bass between the kicks
  • Explain sidechain compression and set it up so the bass, pad and tune duck when the kick hits, with a release timed to the beat
  • Add a I-IV-V-vi chord loop and the tune in one key, then arrange a 64-bar track with a build, a drop and a break, and mix it

About the song and the theme ·

The song is “Deck the Halls”, a traditional Welsh melody that dates back to the sixteenth century and was in print by the 1790s under the name “Nos Galan”. It is far older than any copyright, so the tune is in the public domain. This kit uses the tune only: no words, no recordings and no existing remix.

A remix keeps a song’s tune and changes everything around it. Here a traditional winter song is rebuilt as house music: a kick on every beat, hats between the beats, claps on beats 2 and 4, a bass that gets out of the kick’s way, and a build and a drop that make a room want to move. Students make every sound themselves, so there is no existing remix anywhere in the project.

House RemixSetup and what to watch
SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the listening day, because the kick and the bass are low sounds.
  • Drum and synth sounds: the ones that come with Logic Pro are enough (a kick, a clap, hats, a bass and a pad or lead sound). A keyboard or pad controller helps but is not required.
  • A score of the tune in the public domain, from the school or town library or a free score site, if students prefer not to play it by ear. This kit prints no notes.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav files, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–25).

Before Class 1 ·

Read the timing page (page 11) so you can answer ‘how long is one beat at 128 BPM?’ and ‘how long are 64 bars?’
Open Logic Pro on a student computer, set the tempo to 128 BPM and the time signature to 4/4, and play a kick on every beat (page 13).
Check which tools your version has: Drum Machine Designer, the Step Sequencer, the Compressor’s Side Chain menu, Retro Synth or Alchemy (see page 8).
Read the worked example (pages 9–10).

What to watch ·

Watch for a kick that is not on every beat, a bass that plays on the same step as the kick, a sidechain whose source is not set to the kick track, a release so long that the bass never comes back, and a mix so loud that it clips. A good listening day: play each remix and ask the class two questions. Where did the build start, and what changed at the drop? Can you hear the bass dip on each kick? If a layer is hard to hear, find it on the arrangement map together. This kit includes no recordings and names no existing remixes.

Key and meter

Key and meter are choices for the remix, not facts about the song: this kit writes its examples in F major and in 4/4, and any key a student can play the tune in works. If your version of the tune is written in another meter, it can still be remixed in 4/4 by counting the same beats again.

Their own work only ·

Students make their OWN remix. They do not sample recordings, videos or games, and they do not copy anyone else’s remix. The only borrowed material is the public-domain tune, which students enter themselves.

Listening

House music is built on low sounds played loudly. Keep headphone volume at a comfortable level, and take a break from loud kicks and bass.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a house track is built and why it sounds the way it does. Students work out a tempo with arithmetic, place a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid, add a bass and chords in one key, use sidechain compression to make room for the kick, and arrange a build, a drop and a break, all in Logic Pro.

Common student struggles ·

  • The beat feels off or the loop drifts: check that the project tempo is 128 BPM and the time signature is 4/4, then count the steps on the step grid (pages 11 and 13).
  • The bass and the kick fight and the low end is muddy: the bass should play between the kicks, on the off-beats, and the sidechain should turn it down on each kick (pages 14 and 15).
  • The sidechain does nothing: the compressor’s side chain must be set to listen to the kick track, and the threshold low enough that the kick pushes the level over it (pages 15 and 16).
  • The tune clashes with the chords: check each bar’s first tune note against its chord, and try another chord from the table (page 14).

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Keep one key (F major), use the chord loop on page 14 as written, build only the first 32 bars of the arrangement (intro, build and the first drop) and use a ready-made drum kit.

More Advanced: Compare two release times by ear and write which sounds better (page 15). Or add a second chord loop for the second drop, or move the whole remix to another key from the chord table.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • A beat that drifts: the timing page (page 11) gives the length of a beat, a step and a bar at six tempos, and the step grid (page 13) shows every hit.
  • A muddy low end: the chord page (page 14) puts the bass between the kicks, and the sidechain page (page 15) shows the dip.
  • A sidechain that does nothing: the layer table (page 16) shows who triggers and who ducks, with a signal-chain drawing.
  • A tune that clashes: the chord table checks each bar, and the worked example (pages 9–10) shows a full set of choices.

SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a House Remix is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

Project and tempoFile › New…, then a Software Instrument track. Set the project tempo in the control bar to 128 BPM and keep the time signature at 4/4. Set the snap or grid value to 16th notes (if your version shows one) so that every hit lands on a step.
DrumsAdd a Drum Machine Designer track (if your version has it) and load a kick, a clap, a closed hat and an open hat, or use a ready-made kit. The Step Sequencer (if your version has it) shows one row for each sound and a column for each step: 16 steps to a bar. Or draw notes in the Piano Roll with the grid set to 16th notes.
Bass and chordsAdd a Software Instrument track with a bass sound and another with a soft pad sound; Retro Synth or Alchemy (each if your version has it) are good places to look. Draw one chord in each bar on the pad, and the chord’s root on the off-beats for the bass, in the Piano Roll with the grid set to 16th notes.
Enter the tunePlay the tune from a keyboard or draw it in the Piano Roll, using a public-domain score or your ear. Logic’s middle C is named C3 by default, so check the octave against the score. Look for a transpose option on the region (if your version has one) to move the tune to your key.
Sidechain compressionInsert a Compressor plug-in on the bass, pad and tune tracks and look for a Side Chain menu in the plug-in window (if your version has it); choose the kick’s track as the side chain source. Set the ratio, lower the threshold until the gain-reduction meter dips on each kick, make the attack as fast as it will go and set the release by ear, starting near one beat (468.75 ms at 128 BPM). The control names and scales differ between versions, so listen.
Timing the releaseMany compressors show release in milliseconds. Work out the beat and the step from the timing page (page 11) and type the number in, or turn the control until the meter is back at zero just before the next kick. Treat every number as a starting point and decide by ear.
Build and dropArrange your regions along the timeline in sections of 8 or 16 bars. Look for the Marker track (if your version has one) to name the sections. Mute and unmute tracks, or use region automation, to bring layers in at the build and out at the break.
Mixer and bounceOpen the Mixer (the key command is X by default, if your version has it) and set levels and pan. Keep the master peak below 0 dBFS with room to spare. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV, for the whole remix. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Step 1: plan the tempo. In 4/4 there are 4 beats in a bar, and each beat holds four 16th-note steps, so a bar has 16 steps. House music is typically 115 to 130 BPM, and 120 to 130 BPM in its most typical form, so 128 BPM fits. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas (4/4)

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM (× 1000 for milliseconds)

Seconds per 16th-note step = 15 ÷ BPM

Seconds per bar = 240 ÷ BPM (16 steps, 4 beats)

A hit on grid step s of bar b is at ((b − 1) × 16 + (s − 1)) × 15 ÷ BPM seconds

A piece of b bars lasts b × 240 ÷ BPM seconds

At 128 BPM: 60 ÷ 128 = 0.46875 s = 468.75 ms per beat; 15 ÷ 128 = 0.1171875 s per step; 240 ÷ 128 = 1.875 s per bar.

Rounding rules

Milliseconds per beat and per step to 2 decimals and seconds to 3; a 5 rounds up. So 15 ÷ 128 = 0.1171875 s = 117.1875 ms prints as 117.19 ms. The 128 BPM beat (468.75 ms) and bar (1.875 s) are exact.

Reading the grid

The kick plays on grid steps 1, 5, 9 and 13: one on each beat. The open hat and the bass play on the off-beats, grid steps 3, 7, 11 and 15, halfway between kicks. The clap plays on 5 and 13, which are beats 2 and 4.

A hit on grid step s of bar b starts at ((b − 1) × 16 + (s − 1)) × 15 ÷ BPM seconds. At 128 BPM, bar 2, grid step 5 (a kick on beat 2) is at (16 + 4) × 0.1171875 = 2.34375 s.

Step 5: set up the sidechain. A compressor with a side chain input controls the gain of one signal from the level of another. In dance music it is often used on basslines, controlled by the kick drum, so the two do not conflict and the bass pulses with the beat. The kick plays; the bass, pad and tune are turned down for a moment each time it hits.

Gain reduction = (level − threshold) × (1 − 1 ÷ ratio) = (level + 20) × 3/4. With a ratio of 4:1, 4 dB over the threshold is turned down by 3 dB. These are the kit’s example settings, not Logic’s defaults.

Starting points to set by ear

Attack: as fast as the control allows, so the bass is down by the time the kick sounds. Release: start near one beat, 468.75 ms, so the level is back as the next kick hits; shorten it if the bass sounds too quiet. Ratio and threshold: start at 4:1 and lower the threshold until the meter dips on each kick and returns to zero between them.

There is no industry standard for what attack and release numbers mean, so the same number can sound different in two compressors. Listen, then decide.

House Remix

Music Technology | House Remix at 128 BPM | 3 class periods

Overview ·

Remix one traditional winter song as a house track in Logic Pro. Students work out a tempo of 128 BPM with timing math, build a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid (kick on every beat, open hat on the off-beats, clap on beats 2 and 4, bass between the kicks), add a four-chord loop, enter the tune, set up sidechain compression so the bass, pad and tune duck when the kick hits, and arrange a 64-bar track with an intro, a build, a drop, a break, a second drop and an outro. Nobody needs to read music: the tune can be entered from a public-domain score or by ear.

Goals ·

  • Plan the track with timing math: one beat = 60 ÷ BPM seconds (468.75 ms at 128 BPM), one 16th-note step = 15 ÷ BPM seconds, one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds (1.875 s)
  • Build a four-on-the-floor pattern on a 16-step grid: kick on grid steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, open hat on the off-beats, clap on 2 and 4, and bass between the kicks
  • Explain sidechain compression and set it up so the bass, pad and tune duck when the kick hits, with a release timed to the beat
  • Add a I-IV-V-vi chord loop and the tune in one key, then arrange a 64-bar track with a build, a drop and a break, and mix it

Key Vocabulary ·

Four on the floorA steady 4/4 rhythm in which the kick drum is hit on every beat: 1, 2, 3 and 4
SidechainA compressor input that lets one sound, such as the kick, control how much another sound is turned down
KickThe low drum hit that plays on every beat in a house track; its job is the pulse
Off-beatThe halfway point between two beats, the “and” of each beat: grid steps 3, 7, 11 and 15
BuildA section that raises the tension, often with more layers, before the drop
DropThe point where the full beat and bass arrive after a build; usually the loudest part of the track
BreakdownA quieter section in which the beat drops out and the tune or chords are heard alone
ReleaseHow long a compressor takes to let the level rise again after it has turned a sound down

Tips for Success ·

  • Work out the tempo first: every later step, from the grid to the release time, comes from 128 BPM.
  • Solo the kick and the bass together while you set the sidechain: you should hear the bass dip on every kick.
  • Keep the kick, clap and hats at a sensible level before you add bass and chords, and leave headroom on the master.
  • Save a new version of your project at the end of each class, so you can always go back.
