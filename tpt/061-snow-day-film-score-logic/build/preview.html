The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a scene (their own clip or a storyboard), one word for its mood and at least two hit points with times in seconds. Tap the seconds on a phone timer while the scene plays, so the times are real and not guesses.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the scene, the mood and the spotting sheet; Class 2 is the tempo, the layers, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the percussion hits, the foley accents and the mix, and Class 4 on bouncing, presentations and reflection.

Step 3: pick a tempo. In 4/4 there are 4 beats in a bar. These formulas turn a time in seconds into a bar and a beat. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas (4/4)

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM

Seconds per bar = 240 ÷ BPM

Bar number = 1 + t ÷ (240 ÷ BPM)

Beats from the start = t × BPM ÷ 60

Example: t = 12.5 s at 96 BPM: 1 + 12.5 ÷ 2.5 = 6.00, so bar 6, beat 1.

Rounding rules

Seconds to 3 decimals, bar numbers to 2, a 5 rounds up. For a marker, round to the nearest sixteenth note: sixteenths = t × BPM ÷ 60 × 4, rounded to a whole number. Then bar = sixteenths ÷ 16 (whole part) + 1, and beat = 1 + (sixteenths left over after the bars) ÷ 4. A bar number of 6.25 is bar 6 and a quarter, which is beat 2.
