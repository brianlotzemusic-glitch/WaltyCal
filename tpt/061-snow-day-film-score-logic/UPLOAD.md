## 2. Description

```
Film scoring for high school music technology: students score a 20-second winter scene in Logic Pro, filmed on a phone or taken from one of three printable storyboards. They name the mood and the emotional arc, mark hit points on a timeline, choose a tempo so the hits land on beats or bars, and build the score from a pad, a motif of their own, percussion hits and foley accents. This Snow Day Film Score project takes 3 class periods (the pacing guide also shows two and four) and is an original Hudson Beat project; the steps are written for Logic Pro, while the ideas (hit points, tempo math, layers) carry over to GarageBand.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (ZIP: 25-page PDF = 1 cover + 9 teacher + 14 student + 1 terms page, plus 14-slide intro deck as .pptx and PDF)
• Teacher pages: how to use, project overview, setup, a 3-class pacing guide with the deck slides for each class, two pages of teacher notes (struggles, variations, resources, grading), a Logic quick reference and a two-page worked example (an invented 20-second ice-skating scene with 4 hit points at 96 BPM = 8 bars, a score map and every number worked out) (9 pages)
• Student pages: three ready-made storyboards to print (a snow-day morning, sledding and snowman building, five 4-second frames each, with a timeline for marking hit points), a mood and emotional-arc page with a word bank, a spotting sheet, the hit-point calculator (bar numbers and bar · beat for 12 times at 80, 96, 100, 120 and 128 BPM), a layer plan, the 3-page project sheet (overview, goals, vocabulary, requirements, steps, tips, submission checklist), the /25 rubric, a vocabulary page, a feedback planner and a reflection page (14 pages)
• Teacher-presented intro deck, 14 slides with speaker notes on every slide: a think-and-share hook, what the project is, mood and arc, spotting, seconds to bars, the four layers, building it in Logic, a worked-example map, what to turn in and the rubric

THE THREE CLASS PERIODS
• Class 1: hook; choose the scene; name its mood and arc; spot the hit points
• Class 2: turn times into bars and pick a tempo; set up Logic with markers; start the pad and the motif
• Class 3: percussion hits and foley accents; automation, mix and bounce; present and reflect

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN
• Spotting a scene: finding its mood, its emotional arc and its hit points
• Timing math: seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM, seconds per bar in 4/4 = 240 ÷ BPM, and bar number = 1 + t ÷ (240 ÷ BPM) (at 96 BPM a 20-second scene is exactly 8 bars)
• Scoring to picture in Logic Pro: importing a movie with File > Open Movie, markers on hit points, a pad in Alchemy or Retro Synth, Quick Sampler, Drum Machine Designer, automation and bounce
• Layering a pad, a motif they invent, percussion hits and foley, and explaining their choices in a reflection

GOOD TO KNOW
• There is no audio in this resource and no student work. No melody, film music or song is printed or notated; students make every sound themselves in Logic Pro
• Requires Logic Pro on a Mac. A MIDI keyboard is not needed; nobody has to read music. Students who cannot film use a storyboard. Where a control may differ between versions, the pages say "if your version has it"
• Rubric: 5 categories scored 1-5, /25; the final grade is the average of the student's and the teacher's score
• Grades 9-12; music technology or music production classes
• The deck uses the free Oswald and Source Sans 3 fonts (built into Google Slides); install them for PowerPoint or Keynote, or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look
• Every student page prints well in black and white

Logic Pro is a product of Apple Inc., named only to describe compatibility. This resource is not affiliated with Apple.

Created with the help of digital and AI tools. Notation and answer keys are verified before release.
```
