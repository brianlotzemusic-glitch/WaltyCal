Teacher Notes

January Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · goals, winter and Martin Luther King Jr. Day

Why January matters

January begins with a fresh start: on January 1, many people set goals for the new year. It is also a winter month in the Northern Hemisphere, and fresh snow can make the world sound quieter. On the third Monday of January the United States observes a federal holiday for Martin Luther King Jr., who was born on January 15, 1929, in Atlanta, Georgia. He was a Baptist minister and a leader of the civil rights movement, and the holiday was first observed in 1986. This bundle shares short, plain facts about each and keeps the music simple: clapping, patting and saying words. Nothing asks a student to take part in a religious practice.

What it is

Eight complete music lessons a substitute can teach with no tech, no instruments and no music background. Each day has a step-by-step plan with words to say and timed steps, and two worksheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5.

The eight days

  1. Fresh Start Reader: reading about January.
  2. Snow Sound Syllables: speech rhythm: clap the syllables.
  3. Music Resolution Poster: draw and design a goal poster.
  4. Snow Day Statues: move to a tapped beat and freeze.
  5. Snow Word Puzzles: January vocabulary puzzles.
  6. Music Job Goals: music jobs.
  7. Resolution Chant Book: compose a four-beat chant.
  8. Fresh Start Bingo: review game day.

Use them in any order. The welcome sheet has a “Day #” column so the sub knows which day each class gets next.

Set up the sub tub (about 15 minutes, once)

  1. Fill in the welcome sheet (page 3).
  2. Print the plans, the quick start, the word-rhythm chart, the facts page and the sub report; copy each worksheet for your largest class.
  3. Cut out the folder labels (page 45), put each day in its own folder, and add the answer keys.

Why subs can teach it

Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. Nobody has to read music or make music with an instrument. Games use only voices, hands and a pencil.

Standards

Supports relating music to varied contexts (MU:Cn11.0) and generating musical ideas (MU:Cr1.1), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps every game safe and happy.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Whisper Countdown.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may share different goals and family traditions. Thank them and move on.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Whisper Countdown to settle a class, Clap It Out to wake it up, and Goal Round to finish with something positive.

Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2

    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact?

Read, Answer and DrawRead, answer and draw
DAY 1
Grades 3–5
NameDate
Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
January is the first month of the Gregorian calendar, and January 1 is New Year’s Day. Many people use the start of a year to set goals, sometimes called resolutions. A good goal is small and clear, such as practicing a little every day. January is also a winter month in the Northern Hemisphere. After a fresh snowfall the world often seems quiet, because snow is full of tiny air pockets that soak up sound instead of bouncing it back. Martin Luther King Jr. was born on January 15, 1929, in Atlanta, Georgia. He was a Baptist minister and a leader of the civil rights movement. The United States honors him with a federal holiday on the third Monday of January, first observed in 1986. Songs and singing were part of the civil rights movement: they helped groups of people feel united at meetings and marches. Goals, winter and history all meet in this one month, and each of them gives us something to think about. You might choose a small goal of your own, listen for the quiet after a snowfall, or learn one more fact about the people who worked for fairness.

2. Why does fresh snow make the world seem quiet?
3. On which day is the federal holiday for Martin Luther King Jr.?
4. In what year was the holiday first observed?
5. What did songs and singing help people do in the civil rights movement?

Snow Sound SyllablesDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of January words and use them to build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like soft snowfall. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: snow (1 clap), mit-ten (2 claps), cal-en-dar (3 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Chart echoes
    Show page 7. Read a line out loud, one box per beat, while clapping the dots. One dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant parade
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Clap It Out from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Snow Day StatuesDay 4 · Move to a tapped beat and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents move to a steady tap, change speed with the tap, and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them or cut them out).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Rules of the freeze
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a snow statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Practice once: tap a pencil on a desk for five seconds, stop, and wait for stillness.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Snowfall
    Show the snowflake card on page 22. Tap slowly: students drift like snowflakes. Tap fast: a snow squall. Stop: freeze. Repeat with three different speeds.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap. Use at least six cards. The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 20, 3–5 page 21. Students sit and fill it in. Read the directions aloud.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Statue gallery
    Tap a few beats, stop, and let the class freeze in a favorite pose. Walk around and name two poses you notice.
Extra time? Shadow Moves from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Use only four cards.

Jobs and Their GoalsRead, match and write
DAY 6
Grades 3–5
NameDate
A new year is a good time to think about goals, and every job in music has one. A conductor, also called a music director, leads an orchestra or another group of musicians so that everyone stays together. An instrument maker builds or repairs instruments. A luthier, for example, makes and restores stringed instruments such as violins and guitars. A sound engineer balances and adjusts sounds so that a performance can be heard clearly. A radio host broadcasts music, news and weather, interviews guests, and often specializes in one kind of music. A music teacher helps students learn how music works and gives them chances to try it. All of these jobs need careful listening, and most of them need teamwork. Imagine that you chose one of these jobs for your first year of work. What goal would you set, and what small steps would help you reach it?
