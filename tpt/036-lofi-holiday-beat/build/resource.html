What this is ·

A 4-class-period beat-making project for high school music technology. Each student chooses one of three public-domain carols, enters its melody and chords into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, and turns it into a lo-fi hip-hop beat: a slow, swung groove with tape wobble, a Bitcrusher, a low-pass filter and vinyl noise. The beat is 60 to 90 seconds. Students plan it, build and mix it, present it and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Decide which carols to offer (page 9) and check the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–26), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–9), Class 3 (slides 10–11) and Class 4 (slides 12–15). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: see the variations on page 6: one looped section on Good King Wenceslas, or a counter-melody.
  • Carol choice: two winter or Christmas carols and a winter tune about an evergreen (page 9). Offer the ones that suit your school.
  • Feedback: students write three questions and answer classmates’ on a class board or on paper.
  • Grading: the rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 23).

Lo-Fi Holiday BeatProject overview
OVERVIEW
Beat-making project
The project in one line

Lo-Fi Holiday Beat · Beat making · Logic Pro
Turn a carol everyone knows into a lo-fi hip-hop beat.

What students make ·

One original beat, 60 to 90 seconds long, built on a public-domain carol. The melody is entered from a piano-roll chart and stays recognizable; the tempo is 70 to 90 BPM, the drums are swung, and the sounds are softened, wobbled and crushed with a layer of vinyl noise. It has at least five layers and an intro, beat sections and an outro, is mixed with volume, pan and EQ, and uses at least four of the five lo-fi moves. Students hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the beat and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a style is built from small choices. Students learn that a slow tempo, swing, softened and crushed sounds and a layer of noise, not only the notes, make a beat sound lo-fi, and they practice entering notes and chords in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody and chords into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Program a swung lo-fi drum pattern and a bass line at 70 to 90 BPM
  • Use swing, tape, bitcrushing, a low-pass filter and vinyl noise on purpose, and explain what each one does
  • Plan, build and mix a 60 to 90 second beat with an intro, beat sections, a break and an outro

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The three charts, printed (pages 12, 13 and 14), or shown on the projector.
  • The drum patterns and effect cards, printed (pages 15–17).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–26).
  • A MIDI keyboard is optional: every melody can be drawn with the mouse.

Before Class 1 ·

Decide which carols you will offer (page 9).
Open Logic Pro on a student computer: can you make a track, open the Piano Roll and hear playback through headphones?
Check that the plug-ins on page 16 and page 17 are installed (Bitcrusher, Channel EQ, Tape Delay) and that your Logic has a noise or crackle loop or a synthesizer with a noise oscillator.
Read the worked example (pages 10–11).

What to watch ·

Students enter the notes but get a wrong one, the drums stay stiff, the melody is buried in noise, or the effects are on but nothing sounds lo-fi. Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished beats without saying the title and let the class name the carol. A tune that people name quickly is a beat that kept the melody.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: is it lazy yet? Can you hear the carol? Which lo-fi move is this?
  • On Class 1, tap 70, 80 and 90 BPM on the desk so students feel how slow lo-fi is.
  • A student who is stuck on drums can start from a Drummer track in a hip-hop style and swing it.

Choosing carols ·

A respectful choice

Good King Wenceslas is a winter carol about snow and kindness, God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen is a Christmas carol, and O Tannenbaum is a winter tune about an evergreen tree. Offer the ones that fit your school and let students choose. If a student prefers another melody, a public-domain one you approve (first published in the United States in 1930 or earlier) works with the same plan, and the blank piano roll on page 18 is there for their own chart.

Keep the volume moderate

Bass-heavy beats and loud peaks are tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a style is built from small choices. Students learn that a slow tempo, swing, softened and crushed sounds and a layer of noise, not only the notes, make a beat sound lo-fi, and they practice entering notes and chords in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Common student struggles ·

  • The notes go in but the tune sounds wrong: check the grid setting and the note lengths against the chart, bar by bar (Logic quick reference, page 8).
  • The drums sound stiff, not lazy: check that swing is on and that the melody sits a little behind the beat (page 16).
  • The beat is muddy or buried in noise: turn the effects and the vinyl noise down until the carol is clear (page 17).
  • Effects are on but nothing sounds lo-fi: compare with the cards on pages 16–17: a low-pass filter, a little wobble, grit on one sound and quiet noise under everything.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Use Good King Wenceslas, loop one four-bar section for the whole beat, and start from pattern A on the drum patterns page or a Drummer track in a hip-hop style.

More Advanced: Write a second four-bar section with a different chord order, add a counter-melody or a chopped copy of the melody in Quick Sampler, and automate the filter across the whole piece. O Tannenbaum is the hardest chart: it is in 3/4, so it needs the waltz pattern.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Wrong notes: the chart pages (pages 12, 13 and 14) list every note by bar, and the Logic quick reference (page 8) shows the grid.
  • Stiff drums: the swing card (page 16) and slide 8 show what swing does.
  • A muddy or noisy mix: the effect cards (page 17), the mix checks on the Arrangement plan (page 19) and slide 11.
  • Effects with no lo-fi feel: the effect cards (pages 16–17) and the worked example (pages 10–11).

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 23). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Melody & Chords (rubric row 1): open the project and compare the Piano Roll with the chart, bar by bar. Check that the chords match the plan.
  • Lo-Fi Beat and Lo-Fi Sound (rubric rows 2 and 3): check the tempo is 70 to 90 BPM, that swing is on, and that at least four of the five lo-fi moves can be heard and named.
  • Arrangement and Mix (rubric row 4): compare the project with the Arrangement plan (page 19). Is there an intro, beat sections, a break and an outro, and can you hear the melody over the noise?
  • Plan and Reflection (rubric row 5): the Beat plan (page 18), the questions on the feedback planner and the Reflection page (page 26) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 26): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Plan & Reflection row of the rubric.

Notes / observations ·

Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished beats without saying the title and let the class name the carol. A tune that people name quickly is a beat that kept the melody.

Lo-Fi Holiday BeatLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Lo-Fi Holiday Beat is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add a Software Instrument track with Track › New Tracks…. Use one track for the melody and more for chords, bass and drums.
Tempo and time signatureType the tempo in the control bar (70 to 90 BPM). O Tannenbaum is in 3/4: set the time signature before you enter notes (the control bar, or the Signature track in Global Tracks).
Make a region, open the Piano RollWith the Pencil tool, click on the track where bar 1 starts to make an empty MIDI region, then double-click it to open the Piano Roll editor.
Draw notes and chordsPick a grid value in the Piano Roll’s Snap menu (for example, 1/16 note) so notes land on the chart’s sixteenth-note grid, click with the Pencil to add a note and drag its right edge to change its length. Draw a chord as notes stacked in one column. A MIDI keyboard is optional.
Fix wrong notesSelect a note and drag it up or down a row, or press delete. To play a melody an octave lower, select the region and change Transpose in the Region inspector by 12 steps.
SwingSelect the MIDI region. In the Region inspector, set Quantize to a 1/16-note value, then raise Q-Swing so the off-beat notes land late. The slider is Logic’s own scale, so compare with the diagram on page 16 by ear.
DrumsMake a Drummer track and pick a hip-hop style, or make a Drum Machine Designer track and open the Step Sequencer: click the 16 steps for each drum, as on the patterns on page 15.
Bass, keys and soundsBrowse the Library (press Y) for instruments, or open Alchemy, Retro Synth or Vintage Electric Piano. A Session Player (Bass Player, Keyboard Player) can follow a Chord Track in Global Tracks.
Tape and bitcrushAdd plug-ins in a channel strip’s Audio FX slots. For wobble try Scanner Vibrato or a light Chorus; for echo try Tape Delay with low feedback; for warmth try ChromaGlow if your version has it, or a little Overdrive. For grit use Bitcrusher.
Low-pass filterIn Channel EQ, switch on the High Cut filter (a low-pass filter) and slide it down until the highs soften. Draw its frequency with automation to open and close it.
Vinyl noiseSearch the Loop Browser (press O) for a noise or crackle loop, or make quiet noise from a synthesizer’s noise oscillator (Alchemy has one) and filter it with Channel EQ. Keep it very low in the mix.
Automation, mixing and bouncePress A to show automation, pick a parameter such as a filter cutoff or volume, and draw points. Press X for the Mixer. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Where the tunes come from ·

  • Good King Wenceslas: A 13th-century spring carol, first published in the Finnish collection Piae Cantiones in 1582. The English words (1853) are not used.
  • God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen: An English traditional melody in print in the early 1800s (an 1815 piano rondo, a parody in the 1820s). The words are not used.
  • O Tannenbaum (O Christmas Tree): A traditional German tune, printed in August Zarnack’s school songbook in 1820. The words (1824) are not used.

The public-domain rule ·

In 2026 a melody is in the public domain in the United States when it was first published in 1930 or earlier (1931 from 1 January 2027). If a student suggests another carol or winter song, check its first publication date before you approve it, and never use a recording, a film or pop version, or its lyrics unless they are public domain too.

Picking a tune for a student ·

  • First time in the Piano Roll: Good King Wenceslas. Mostly quarter notes, and a loop that repeats cleanly.
  • Wants a moody sound: God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen. A minor key, so the seventh chords sound dark and warm.
  • Wants a challenge, or a waltz: O Tannenbaum, in 3/4 with the waltz pattern.
Reading the charts

Pitch names run down the left; middle C is C4. Bold lines are bars, grey lines are beats. A note’s length is the name beside it: 16th, eighth, dotted eighth, quarter, dotted quarter, half or dotted half. Chords are one idea that fits, not the only one.

Why a four-bar loop

Lo-fi beats are built on a short loop that repeats. Each chart ends so that it leads back into bar 1; where a tune starts with a lead-in note, that note sits at the end of bar 4.

1 · A weak plan

I will make Good King Wenceslas into a chill lo-fi beat.

2 · A clear plan

My carol is Good King Wenceslas, the four-bar melody from the chart in C major. My beat is lo-fi hip-hop at 80 BPM. The loop is 4 bars with the chords Cmaj7 | Cmaj7 Em7 | Am7 | Fmaj7 Cmaj7. I use all five lo-fi moves: swing on the hi-hats, tape wobble and echo on the keys, a Bitcrusher on the melody, a low-pass filter on the keys in the intro and the break, and vinyl noise under everything. Lo-Fi Holiday BeatLo-fi drum patterns
STUDENT
Class 2

Lo-fi drums are simple and soft. Each card gives a one-bar pattern (16 steps, 4 to a beat; shaded steps are the beats; the waltz has 12), a bass idea and sounds to try. Pick one for your beat at 70 to 90 BPM, copy it into the Step Sequencer, or use a Drummer track, then swing it (page 16). Patterns A to C are for the 4/4 carols; pattern D is for O Tannenbaum.

Pattern A · Lazy kick · relaxed, with space

Drums. Kick on beat 1 and an eighth note after beat 3; snare on beats 2 and 4; hats on every eighth note.

Bass. Soft sub: one long note on each chord root.

Sounds. A dusty kit with a soft kick, a short snare and quiet, swung hats.

Try. Turn the hats down so they are felt, not heard.

Pattern B · Boom-bap · nodding, a little busier

Drums. Kick on beat 1, the “and” of 2 and the “and” of 3; snare on beats 2 and 4; hats on every eighth note.

Bass. Follow the kick: short notes on the root, a little behind the beat.

Sounds. A punchy but soft kick, a snare with a short tail, a touch of room reverb.

Try. Nudge the snare a hair late. Lo-fi drummers rarely sit dead on the grid.

Pattern C · Ghost notes · rolling, with little taps

Drums. Kick on beats 1 and 3 and an eighth note after beat 3; snare on 2 and 4; very quiet ghost snare hits on the last sixteenth of beat 2 and the “and” of beat 4; hats on every eighth.

Bass. Long roots, with a short walk-up note before each chord change.

Sounds. The same kit as A, with the ghost snare played at very low velocity.

Try. In the Piano Roll or Step Sequencer, set the ghost notes’ velocity low, about a quarter of full.

Pattern D · Waltz (3/4) · slow, rocking, for O Tannenbaum

Drums. Kick on beat 1 and the “and” of 2; rim or snare on beats 2 and 3; hats on every eighth note. One bar is 12 steps.

Bass. One long root per bar.

Sounds. Soft kick, a rim click, quiet hats with swing.

Try. Count 1-2-3 out loud while you click the steps. Keep the swing light.

Class 3

Five small moves make a beat sound lo-fi: swing, tape, a Bitcrusher, a low-pass filter and vinyl noise. Use at least four. This page has swing and tape; page 17 has the other three. Every move should be small enough that the carol is still easy to hear.

Swing · make the beat bounce

What it does. Moves the off-beat 16th notes a little later, so a steady pattern rolls and bounces instead of marching. More swing, more bounce.

In Logic. Select the MIDI region. In the Region inspector, set Quantize to a 1/16-note value, then raise Q-Swing. Try the hats first.

Try. Light swing on the hats, a little less on the kick and snare. Q-Swing is Logic’s own scale, so use the diagram as a guide and decide by ear.

Listen for. The pattern leans back instead of marching. If it sounds loose or sloppy, you have too much.

Tape · wobble, warmth and echo

What it does. Old tape drifts a little in pitch, softens the highs and adds warm distortion and echo. A touch of all of that makes a digital sound feel played from a tape.

In Logic. Put Scanner Vibrato or a light Chorus on the keys for a slow, small wobble. Add Tape Delay with low feedback for a soft echo. For warmth use ChromaGlow if your version has it; if not, turn Overdrive down to a tiny amount.

Try. Keep the wobble depth low: the pitch should drift, not warble. Set the delay to an eighth note and the mix low.

Listen for. A slightly seasick warmth. If the chords sound out of tune, back the depth off.

Bitcrusher · grit

What it does. Throws away detail: it lowers the bit depth and the sample rate, so a sound gets gritty and fuzzy, like an old sampler.

In Logic. Insert Bitcrusher in an Audio FX slot. Lower Resolution and Downsampling, then pull Mix back so some of the clean sound stays.

Try. Use it on one sound, such as the melody or the drums. Around 12 bits is subtle; around 8 bits is crunchy. Start with Mix near half.

Listen for. Grit you can hear on the tail of a note or on the hats. If the melody turns to mush, raise the bits.

Vinyl noise · the bed under everything

What it does. A soft bed of hiss and crackle, as if the beat were playing from a record. It glues the sounds together and says “lo-fi” at once.

In Logic. Search the Loop Browser (press O) for a noise or crackle loop, or make quiet noise with a synthesizer’s noise oscillator and filter it with Channel EQ. Put it on its own track and loop it across the whole beat.

Try. Turn it up until it is loud, then bring it down until you only notice it when it stops. Raise it a little in the break.

Listen for. Warmth under everything, not a wall of noise. The carol must stay clear.

Low-pass filter · muffled and far away

What it does. A low-pass filter lets the low sounds through and cuts the highs, so keys and drums sound muffled, like a radio in another room.

In Logic. In Channel EQ, switch on the High Cut filter and slide it down. Automate its frequency to close it in the intro and the break and open it when the drums come in.

Try. A cutoff of about 3 to 6 kHz muffles keys; higher is a lighter touch.

Listen for. The highs soften and the sound sits back. If the keys go dull and lifeless, open the filter a little.

Lo-Fi Holiday Beat

Music Technology | Beat-Making Project | 4 class periods

Overview ·

Turn a carol into a lo-fi hip-hop beat. Choose one of three public-domain melodies, enter its four-bar melody and chords into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, build a slow, swung drum pattern, and make everything sound warm and a little old with tape wobble, a Bitcrusher, a low-pass filter and vinyl noise. Your finished beat is 60 to 90 seconds long at 70 to 90 BPM, and people should still be able to name the carol.

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody and chords into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Program a swung lo-fi drum pattern and a bass line at 70 to 90 BPM
  • Use swing, tape, bitcrushing, a low-pass filter and vinyl noise on purpose, and explain what each one does
  • Plan, build and mix a 60 to 90 second beat with an intro, beat sections, a break and an outro

Tips for Success ·

  • Lo-fi is about restraint. Keep every effect small enough that the carol is still easy to hear.
  • Play the melody softly and a little behind the beat. Lo-fi feels relaxed, never rushed.
  • Add one effect at a time and listen before you add the next. If you cannot hear what an effect does, turn it up and then back down.
  • Save a new version of your project before each big experiment, so you can always go back.
