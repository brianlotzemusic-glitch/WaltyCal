Before a concert

A week or so beforeTeach the 23-minute mini-lesson (page 3). It ends with the exit ticket.
Before the dayPost the 6 posters (pages 7–12) in the rehearsal room and near the entrance.
The day of the concertHand out the audience checklist (page 13) to students who will be listening and the performer checklist (page 14) to students who will be performing.
After the concertStudents tick the boxes and write one thing that went well. Talk about it together the next time you meet.

Good to know

Concert customs vary by venue, style and culture. At many school concerts the audience claps when a piece has ended. At a jazz club, a drum circle or a pop show, people may clap or cheer during the music. Teach students to watch and listen for cues, and tell them what your own concert expects.

Differentiation

Grades 3–5: read each poster aloud and have students repeat the habit. Do the role-plays as a whole class with volunteers, use two or three discussion questions, and have students tick the checklist with a partner.

Grades 6–8: let students lead the discussion and the role-plays, and use all 5 discussion questions. Ask them to explain why each habit helps performers, and to compare customs at different kinds of events.

Including everyone

Some students need to move, use a quiet fidget, wear ear protection or step out for a moment. Treat these as part of respectful listening, and plan a quiet way to do each. Applause can also be silent, such as waving open hands.

Extension ideas

  • Students write a short note for the concert program that welcomes the audience and explains one habit.
  • Students design their own poster for a habit that is not covered here.
  • Students ask a family member where they have been in an audience and what the customs were there, then share what they learned.

No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used. Standards: none cited. Match the activities to your own curriculum.

Students will

  • name the 6 habits of a respectful audience and of focused, kind performers;
  • explain why each habit helps the performers and the people around them;
  • show how to handle a tricky moment at a concert without instruments.

Get ready

  • Print the 6 posters (pages 7–12) and tape them up or show them on a screen.
  • Print the think-pair-share sheet (page 6) and the exit ticket (page 15), 2 tickets to a page.

The 6 habits

  1. Arrive early and quietly
  2. Silence phones
  3. Clap at the right time
  4. Stay seated until the music ends
  5. Be a respectful audience
  6. Performers: ready, focused, kind

A note on customs

Concert customs differ from venue to venue and culture to culture. This lesson teaches the usual habits for a school concert and invites students to notice where customs change.

Words to know

audiencethe people who watch and listen to a performance
performera person who sings, plays or speaks for an audience
applauseclapping or cheering to show thanks and enjoyment
cuea signal, such as a nod from the director, that tells everyone what happens next
venuethe place where a concert happens, such as a gym, a hall or a park
etiquettethe customs for polite, respectful behavior in a certain situation

Teacher Script

Steps 1 to 3 · say the shaded lines in your own words
1
Step 1: Hook
3 min
SayThink about a time you watched someone perform, in person or at school. Raise your hand if you have ever been in an audience. Now keep your hand up if you have ever been a performer.
SayPerformers practice for weeks. Today we will learn what the audience can do to help them shine, and what performers can do to be their best on stage.
2
Step 2: The six habits
6 min
DoShow the 6 posters one at a time, about a minute each. Read each heading aloud and have students repeat it. Then ask why the habit matters.
  1. Arrive early and quietly. Find your seat before the music starts. (Poster 1, page 7)
  2. Silence phones. Turn off the sound and the buzz, then put your phone away. (Poster 2, page 8)
  3. Clap at the right time. Listen for the end of the piece, then clap when the music has stopped. (Poster 3, page 9)
  4. Stay seated until the music ends. Stay in your seat until the performance is over. (Poster 4, page 10)
  5. Be a respectful audience. Listen with your eyes and ears, keep your voice low, and cheer kindly for every group. (Poster 5, page 11)
  6. Performers: ready, focused, kind. Ready: warm up and be on time. (Poster 6, page 12)
3
Step 3: Discuss
4 min
AskTake as many of these 5 discussion questions as time allows. Call on a few students for each one.
  1. Why does a performer do their best work in a quiet room?
  2. What can happen to the music when a phone buzzes or someone whispers?
  3. How might it feel to look out and see an audience that is listening closely?
  4. In some places, people clap or cheer during the music. Why might customs be different from place to place?
  5. What can you do if you need to cough, sneeze or step out during a concert?
NoteListen for answers about focus, safety, respect and kindness. If students say that customs differ, agree: different places have different customs, so we watch and listen for cues.

Teacher Script

Steps 4 to 6 · say the shaded lines in your own words
4
Step 4: Think-pair-share
3 min
SayYour friend’s group is playing a soft, slow piece. In the audience, your phone buzzes in your pocket. What do you do, and why does it matter to the performers?
DoHand out the think-pair-share sheet (page 6). Students think on their own for 1 minute, talk with a partner for 1 minute, then share with the class for 1 minute.
Listen forSilence it quickly, put it away, and wait until after the piece. Accept any calm, considerate answer that keeps the performers in mind.
5
Step 5: Show me
5 min
SayNow you will show me. We will use no instruments, only our bodies and our manners. I need volunteers to act out 4 role-play scenarios.
A

You arrive just as the concert is starting. Show how to enter quietly and find a seat, or wait at the back for a pause between pieces.

B

Your phone buzzes during a soft piece. Show how to silence it quickly and put it away without a fuss.

C

A piece ends, and you are not sure whether to clap. Show how to watch the director and the rest of the audience for a cue.

D

You are a performer, and the last note has just ended. Show how to stay focused, smile or bow, and wait for your director's cue.

DoAsk 1 or 2 volunteers to act out each scenario in silence, then replay it. After each one, ask the class: “What did you see that helped the performers?” Give a thumbs-up for each habit spotted.
6
Step 6: Exit ticket
2 min
SayLast, answer 3 short questions on the exit ticket. Do it on your own. This tells me what stuck.
DoHand out the exit tickets (page 15), 2 to a page, cut apart. Collect them at the door. The answer key is on page 16. Question 1 has one right answer. Questions 2 and 3 have many good answers.

Exit Ticket Answer Key3 exit ticket questions · only Question 1 has one right answer
ANSWER KEY
for page 15
Question 1 · Answer: B

When is it usual to clap at a school concert?

A.Wrong. A quiet moment in the middle of a piece is when the audience listens most closely, so clapping there can cover the music.
B.Correct. At most school concerts the audience claps after a piece has ended and the music has stopped. Some pieces have several parts, so watch your director or the program for cues.
C.Wrong. Clapping is welcome at concerts. The habit is to clap at the right time, which is after a piece ends.

Customs vary. At a jazz club, a pop show or a drum circle, people may clap during the music. If a student chooses A for that reason, ask where they have seen that, then explain that at most school concerts we wait for the end of a piece.

Question 2 · Many answers are right

Name two ways an audience member can help the performers do their best.

Look for any two of these: arrive early and quietly; silence and put away a phone; listen quietly; clap at the end of a piece; stay seated until the music ends; cheer kindly for every group; keep voices low; thank the performers.

Question 3 · Many answers are right

You are performing and you make a mistake. What is one calm, kind thing you can do next?

Look for one calm, kind action, such as: take a breath and keep going with the group; watch the director for the next cue; stay still and quiet until the piece ends; smile and move on; encourage a fellow performer afterward. Be careful with answers that blame someone else.

Scoring idea: give 1 point for Question 1, and 1 point each for Questions 2 and 3 when the answer is on topic and respectful. That makes 3 points in all.
