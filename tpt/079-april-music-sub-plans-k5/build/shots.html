Poem Word ClapsDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of jazz, poetry and spring words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: jazz (1 clap), trum-pet (2 claps), pi-an-o (3 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable: Soft rain on the leaves (5 claps), a green seed wakes up and grows (7 claps), April hums with sound (5 claps). Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant parade
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Word Claps from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Jazz Month ReaderDay 1 · Reading about jazz, poems and Earth Day · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about Jazz Appreciation Month, two jazz musicians, Poetry Month, Earth Day and spring, and answer questions about them.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Opening question
    Say: “April is full of big ideas: jazz, poems, and a day for our planet.” Ask: Which musicians, poems or jazz words do you already know? Take five answers. Any answer is fine.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Quiet Rain or Call and Reply from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 2; keep the reading and the questions.

Jazz Month ReaderA short reading about April
DAY 1
Grades K–2
NameDate
Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
April is the fourth month of the year. April is Jazz Appreciation Month, a time to enjoy jazz music. Jazz musicians often make up music on the spot, which is called improvising. Dizzy Gillespie was a trumpeter whose trumpet had a bell that pointed up. Mary Lou Williams was a pianist who wrote music for big bands. April is also Poetry Month, when we enjoy poems and the rhythm of words. Earth Day is on April 22, a day to care for our planet. In the Northern Hemisphere, April is spring, and flowers and leaves start to grow.

April Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

Swing and FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
seed
Crouch small, then slowly grow tall like a sprout.
rain
Wiggle your fingers and tap softly on your legs, like light rain.
butterfly
Flap your arms slowly like wings and tiptoe around.
trumpet
Hold a pretend horn up high and sway side to side.
piano
Tap pretend keys on your desk, one finger at a time.
bucket drum
Tap a steady beat on your legs, like a bucket drum.
tree
Stand tall and sway your arms like branches in the wind.
earth
Turn slowly in a circle, then freeze.
