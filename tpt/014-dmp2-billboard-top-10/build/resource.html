What this is ·

A 7-class-period project for high school music technology. Students study the current Billboard Top 10, pick one song and recreate it in Logic Pro: the same tempo, the exact song form, drums they program themselves, instruments that fit the genre, and a mix with EQ and plug-ins. It is a structured project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (focus: song form and drums), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Check this week’s Top 10 on billboard.com and preview the songs (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–19), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–10), Class 3 (slide 11), Class 4 (slide 12), Class 6 (slides 13–15) and Class 7 (slides 16–17). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

Make it fit your class ·

  • The chart changes every week. Use the Top 10 of the week you start; students keep their song even if it drops.

OVERVIEW

Billboard Top 10 · Structured · Song Form & Drums
Analyze and recreate a current Billboard hit. Study song form, production techniques, and drum programming.

What students make ·

One Logic Pro project: their own version of a song from the current Billboard Top 10, at the original tempo and in the original song form, with Arrangement Markers for every section, drum patterns they programmed themselves, instruments and layers that fit the genre, and EQ, panning and plug-ins. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx project, the Top 10 analysis, and a Padlet post with three feedback questions.

What this project reinforces ·

This builds directly on song form, drum programming, and arrangement skills. The emulation approach forces precision with tempo, form, and instrumentation while still requiring creative production choices. It teaches students to listen analytically rather than passively.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): analyzing how musical works are structured (MU:Re7.2), developing and refining work (MU:Cr2.1, MU:Cr3.1) and presenting it (MU:Pr6.1).

Goals ·

  • Analyze song form, structure, and arrangement in commercial music
  • Develop drum programming skills by recreating real-world patterns
  • Practice matching tempo, key, and instrumentation to a reference track
  • Strengthen mixing and production techniques through style emulation

At a glance ·

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student (a current version: the Step Sequencer and pattern regions came in Logic Pro X 10.5), and headphones.
  • The current chart: the Billboard Hot 100 on billboard.com, top 10 songs.
  • A way to play the songs: YouTube or Spotify through your school’s own access. Nothing is included.
  • A place to submit an .mp3 or .wav and a zipped .logicx project (your class page).
  • A feedback board for the three questions (my sheets use Padlet).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–19).

A MIDI keyboard? ·

Helpful for bass, chords and melodies, not required: notes can be drawn in the Piano Roll.

Before Class 1 ·

Open this week’s Hot 100 and list the top 10 songs.
Preview all ten. Note clean versions, or make an approved list for your school.
Note which songs have clear, easy-to-hear sections (my first common struggle).
Test the class speakers; cue up the No. 1 for slide 2.
Print the student pages; set up the submission spot and the feedback board.

Before Class 2 ·

Skim every Song Form map: sections, bar counts, start times.
Have the worked example (page 10) ready for the tempo and drums demos.
Songs that work well
  • Clear, identifiable sections: you can hear where the chorus starts
  • A steady beat you can count, and drums you can pick out
  • A clean version your school is comfortable with
Songs that will be hard
  • Heavily layered pop or hip-hop with 50+ tracks (my teacher notes)
  • Tempo changes, or long sections with no beat
  • If a student insists: the easier variation, the first 1–2 minutes

Billboard Top 10Teacher notes · the big idea
TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

This builds directly on song form, drum programming, and arrangement skills. The emulation approach forces precision with tempo, form, and instrumentation while still requiring creative production choices. It teaches students to listen analytically rather than passively.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students pick songs that are too complex (heavily layered pop or hip-hop with 50+ tracks). Guide them toward songs with clear, identifiable sections.
  • Song form analysis is rushed or inaccurate — students skip counting bars. Require the Song Form map (page 12) before they open Logic.
  • Drum programming feels robotic. Encourage velocity variation and subtle timing adjustments to add human feel.
  • Students copy the arrangement but ignore the mix — the EQ and panning step often gets skipped.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Allow students to recreate only the first 1–2 minutes of the song, or provide a pre-built drum pattern they can modify.

More Advanced: Require students to add an original 8-bar section (bridge, outro, or remix) that fits the style but is entirely their own.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Songs that are too complex: “Songs that work well” on page 4, and the “Clear sections?” column of the Top 10 analysis (page 11).
  • Skipped bar counts: the Song Form map (page 12) has a bar column and a length check.
  • Robotic drums: the velocity lane in the worked example (page 10) and slide 9.
  • A skipped mix: slide 13 puts EQ, pan, levels and plug-ins on the screen in Class 6.

How to grade ·

  • My rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 17). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Composition & Structure: hold the student’s Song Form map next to their Arrangement Markers. Do the sections, bar counts and tempo match the original?
  • Technical Skill: are the drums programmed by the student (percussion loops only as accents), with some velocity variation?
  • Sound Design & Mix: solo a few tracks. Is there EQ on every track, panning for width, and nothing clipping on the loudest section?

Notes / observations ·

This project works well early when students need structure. The Top 10 analysis and the song form and drum pattern pages front-load the analytical work.

Billboard Top 10Logic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Everything students need in Logic for this project, in one table. Demonstrate the tempo and markers (slide 8) and the drums (slide 10), then let students work.

TempoCount the song’s beats for 15 seconds and multiply by 4. Double-click the tempo in the control bar and type it in. Turn on the metronome (K) and play the song alongside it: if the click drifts, adjust by 1 BPM.
Arrangement MarkersTurn on Global Tracks (G). In the Arrangement track, click + to add a marker, drag its edges to fit the section’s bars, and rename it (Intro, Verse 1, Chorus…).
Drum trackTrack › New Software Instrument Track; in the Library (Y) choose a kit under Electronic Drum Kit. This loads Drum Machine Designer.
Pattern regionControl-click in the drum track at a marker and choose Create Pattern Region. Double-click it to open the Step Sequencer; click steps to turn hits on.
VelocityIn the Step Sequencer, switch the edit mode to Velocity and drag hits up or down; in the Piano Roll, select notes and use the Velocity slider.
Copy a sectionOption-drag a region to copy it, or select it and press Command-R to repeat it.
EQ and panOpen the Mixer (X). Double-click the EQ display at the top of a channel strip to open Channel EQ; turn the Pan knob to place the track left or right.
Percussion accentsLoop Browser (O): percussion Apple Loops are allowed as accents only; the main drum patterns are programmed by the student.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx project and choose Compress.

An original practice map, not a real song, so you can model each step with numbers that are easy to check. Students’ maps come from their own song.

1 · Tempo

Count the beats for 15 seconds: 25 beats × 4 = 100 BPM. One bar of 4/4 lasts 240 ÷ 100 = 2.4 seconds.

Billboard Top 10

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Your job is to study the current Billboard Top 10, pick one song, and recreate it in Logic Pro. You’re not just copying — you’re reverse-engineering what makes a hit song work. You’ll analyze song form, drum patterns, instrumentation, and production techniques, then build your own version from scratch.

Goals ·

  • Analyze song form, structure, and arrangement in commercial music
  • Develop drum programming skills by recreating real-world patterns
  • Practice matching tempo, key, and instrumentation to a reference track
  • Strengthen mixing and production techniques through style emulation

Key Vocabulary ·

Song FormThe structure of a song — how sections like intro, verse, chorus, and bridge are organized
TempoThe speed of a song, measured in BPM (beats per minute)
Drum PatternA repeating rhythmic figure played by the kick, snare, and hi-hat that defines the groove
EmulationRecreating the style and sound of an existing track as closely as possible
Arrangement MarkersLogic’s tool for labeling and organizing song sections on the timeline

Requirements ·

Choose one song from the current Billboard Top 10
Match the tempo of your chosen song exactly
Recreate the exact song form (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, etc.)
Drum patterns must be self-created — percussion loops are okay as accents
Use appropriate instruments and layers for the genre
Apply EQ and plug-ins to shape your sounds authentically

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Explore the Billboard Top 10 — listen to all 10 songs and complete the Top 10 analysis (page 11)
  2. 2
    Study song form — map out the exact structure of your chosen song using the Song Form map (page 12)
  3. 3
    Analyze and program drum patterns — break down the kick, snare, and hi-hat and build them in Logic (page 13). Set up Arrangement Markers.
  4. 4
    Build your foundation — lay down bass, chords, and core instrumentation to match the original’s energy
  5. 5
    Add layers — fill in melodies, pads, FX, and transitions to match the fullness of the original
  6. 6
    Mix and refine — EQ every track, pan for width, adjust levels, and add plug-ins for authenticity
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.

Tips for Success ·

  • Listen to your reference track with headphones and really pay attention to what’s happening in each section. Count the bars.
  • Don’t try to perfectly match every single sound — focus on capturing the vibe, structure, and energy.
  • Your drums are the backbone. If the beat doesn’t feel right, the whole track will feel off.
  • Use Logic’s Arrangement Markers early to block out your form — it keeps you organized and saves time.
