Teacher Notes

How to use this scavenger hunt

Students hunt through six scenes for hidden music symbols. They find each symbol, circle it in a color, count it and write the number. Then they review tempo terms and dynamics. Every scene has an answer key that circles each symbol, and every review has a key too.

Quick start

1. Copy the symbol key (page 3) and vocabulary (page 4) for each student.

2. Hand out a scene page and crayons in several colors.

3. Students color a circle beside each symbol, then circle the symbols in the scene with that crayon.

4. They count and write the numbers, then check with the key.

What is inside (21 pages)

Page 3: symbol key. Page 4: vocabulary.

Pages 5–10: six scenes with 130 hidden symbols (scene 1 (21), scene 2 (21), scene 3 (21), scene 4 (23), scene 5 (22), scene 6 (22)).

Pages 11–12: tempo and dynamics review. Page 13: scoring.

Pages 14–19: scene answer keys. Page 20: review keys. Page 21: terms of use.

Pacing

Lesson 3 (20 min)Scenes 3 and 4: accidentals and time signatures, then dynamics and hairpins.7–8
Lesson 4 (20 min)Scenes 5 and 6, the challenge scenes with ten kinds of symbols each.9–10
Lesson 5 (20 min)Tempo and dynamics review, then scoring (page 13).11–13

Tips

  • The scenes get harder. Scenes 1 and 2 have seven kinds of symbols each and scenes 5 and 6 have ten, so you can match the scene to the student.
  • The hidden symbols are printed in black and are drawn without a staff. Some notes have stems down and some have stems up. The symbol key shows each symbol on a staff.
  • Black-and-white printing works: every color is also written as a word. Students can circle with a pencil and write the color name instead.
  • If a count is off by one, ask the student to look again near the edges of the picture and behind the line art.

Standards and ideas

The hunt supports reading and naming notation, tempo terms and dynamics, as described in the National Core Arts Standards for Music. Match the wording to your own curriculum. For extra practice, students can clap or play a short pattern in the tempo or dynamic that a symbol shows. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Treble clefMarks the higher notes. It curls around the G line.
Bass clefMarks the lower notes. Its two dots sit around the F line.
Quarter noteLasts 1 beat in 4/4.
Half noteLasts 2 beats in 4/4.
Whole noteLasts 4 beats in 4/4.
Quarter restSilence for 1 beat.
Half restSilence for 2 beats. It sits on the line.
Repeat signGo back and play the music again.
FermataHold the note longer than usual.
SharpRaises a note by a half step.
FlatLowers a note by a half step.
NaturalCancels a sharp or a flat.
Forte (f)Play loudly.
Piano (p)Play softly.
Mezzo forte (mf)Play medium loud.
Mezzo piano (mp)Play medium soft.
CrescendoGet gradually louder.
DecrescendoGet gradually softer.
Time signature 4/4Four beats in a bar. A quarter note gets one beat.
Double barlineMarks the end of a section.

In the scenes the symbols hide without a staff. Stems go up on the right of the head below the middle line, and down on the left on or above it.

Italian terms and music words

Tempo terms

TermSay itMeaningSpeed
largoLAR-govery slowabout 40–60
andanteahn-DAHN-taya walking speedabout 76–108
moderatomod-er-AH-toha medium speedabout 108–120
allegroah-LEG-rohfast and livelyabout 120–156
prestoPRES-tohvery fastabout 168–200

BPM means beats per minute. These are common ranges. Books, teachers and conductors use slightly different numbers.

Dynamics

SymbolTermSay itMeaning
pianopee-AH-nohsoft
mezzo pianoMET-soh pee-AH-nohmedium soft
mezzo forteMET-soh FOR-taymedium loud
forteFOR-tayloud
crescendokreh-SHEN-dohgradually getting louder
decrescendoday-kreh-SHEN-dohgradually getting softer

The letter m in mp and mf stands for mezzo, which means “medium.” The words are Italian.

Music words

WordWhat it means
TempoHow fast or slow the music goes.
DynamicsHow loud or soft the music is.
ClefA symbol at the start of the staff that tells you the names of the lines and spaces.
RestA symbol for silence. It has a length, just like a note.
AccidentalA sharp, flat or natural sign written before a note.
Time signatureTwo numbers at the start of the music. In 4/4 there are four beats in a bar, and a quarter note gets one beat.
BarlineA vertical line that divides the music into bars. A double barline marks the end of a section.

How to score

On each scene, a student earns 1 point for every kind of symbol that is counted correctly. The score for a scene is the number of kinds of symbols on its hunt list. The tempo review is worth 15 points and the dynamics review is worth 18 points. Use the answer keys on pages 14–20.
