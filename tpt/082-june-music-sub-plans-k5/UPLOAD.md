## 2. Description

```
Need sub plans a substitute can teach without any music background, any technology, or any prep from you? These eight June music days are ready to print and hand over. Each day has a step-by-step plan with timed steps and words to say, plus two student sheets: one for grades K–2 and one for grades 3–5. The theme is June: Juneteenth (short, true, age-appropriate facts about the day, the order read in Galveston, Texas, in 1865 and the parades, songs and singing of its celebrations, with a teacher note on teaching it with care), the start of summer, Father's Day with a special grown-up alternative, and the end of the school year.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (48 pages, PDF, US Letter)
• Cover and teacher notes with the June note, a Juneteenth teaching note, setup steps and a page guide (2 pages)
• Sub quick start, welcome sheet, days at a glance, quick games, word-rhythm chart, June facts and a class sign (7 pages)
• 8 sub plans, one per day, each 30–45 minutes (8 pages)
• 16 student sheets: a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet for each day (16 pages)
• Day extras: movement picture cards, chant word cards, a chant book page and a read-aloud quiz (4 pages)
• Early finishers (2 pages)
• Exit tickets (1 page)
• Answer keys for Days 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (3 pages)
• Sub report form, folder labels, class star awards, terms of use and a thank-you page (5 pages)

THE EIGHT DAYS
• Day 1, June Reader: K–2 hear a short reading about Juneteenth, the start of summer, Father's Day (with a special grown-up alternative) and the end of the school year and circle answers; 3–5 read a passage of about 200 words and answer five questions
• Day 2, Summer Sound Claps: clap the syllables of summer and celebration words and a short original four-line poem; K–2 color a circle for each clap, 3–5 sort words by syllables and build a four-beat chant
• Day 3, Parade Planner: plan a parade for a celebration you choose, with a banner, one instrument and a four-beat chant (paper only, nothing to cut or mail); K–2 draw and circle, 3–5 label parts and write two sentences
• Day 4, March and Freeze: march and freeze to a tapped beat, try call and response, with 8 picture cards (sun, drum, tambourine, maracas, triangle, kite, wave, banner); K–2 circle how they moved, 3–5 reflect and design a freeze game
• Day 5, Summer Word Hunt: summer, instrument and place word puzzles; K–2 match words to pictures and trace, 3–5 word search plus a decode-the-message puzzle
• Day 6, Who Makes the Music?: five music jobs, from conductor to radio host, with summer concerts and parades as the setting; K–2 draw a job, 3–5 match jobs and write a job ad or interview questions
• Day 7, Cheer Chant Book: build four-beat cheer chants from summer and celebration word cards (including a cheer for a dad or any special grown-up), mark the syllables, share with a partner, and keep a chant book page
• Day 8, Summer Send-Off Bingo: bingo (12 squares for K–2, 16 for 3–5), a read-aloud quiz and Pass the Pencil, with a look back at the year

WHY IT WORKS FOR SUBS
• No tech: nothing needs a computer, projector, speaker or video. Paper, pencils, crayons, hands and voices only
• No music skills: rhythm is taught through speech rhythm, clapping the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. The sub never reads music or sings
• No prep: print, put each day in a folder, and go. Each day works in any order for a mixed K–5 class
• Facts are short, sourced and secular, and the facts page answers common questions, so the days work in public schools and for students of every background
```
