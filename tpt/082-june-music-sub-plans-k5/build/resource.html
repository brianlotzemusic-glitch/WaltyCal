Teacher Notes

June Music Sub Plans K–5 · 8 no-prep, no-tech days · Juneteenth, summer, cheers, end of year

Why June matters

June brings several ideas into a music room. June 19 is Juneteenth, which remembers June 19, 1865, when Major General Gordon Granger arrived in Galveston, Texas, and an order was read that said all enslaved people in Texas were free. Freed people in Galveston organized a celebration the next year. Celebrations have included parades, picnics, prayer, readings, songs and singing. Texas made it a state holiday starting in 1980, and in 2021 it became a federal holiday. This bundle teaches it as short, true facts and as a celebration, with no re-enacting, no lyrics, no speeches and nothing graphic. June is also the start of summer (the June solstice, on June 20 or 21, starts summer in the Northern Hemisphere and winter in the Southern Hemisphere), Father’s Day (the third Sunday in June, offered as a cheer for a dad or any special grown-up so every student can take part) and the end of the school year, which Day 8 looks back on. The music stays simple: clapping, marching in place and saying words. Facts are kept short and secular.

Teaching Juneteenth with care

  • Teach it as freedom and celebration. Read the facts as printed, calmly and plainly, and use the words “enslaved people.”
  • Do not ask students to act out history, pretend to be enslaved or use accents. The Day 4 moves are about instruments, summer and parades only.
  • Some students and families have personal ties to Juneteenth, and some students will know more than the page says. Thank them. Never ask one student to speak for a group.
  • If a student asks a hard question, say “That is an important question. Let’s ask your classroom teacher.” You may leave a note for the teacher on the sub report.
  • No speeches or lyrics are quoted, and no piece of music is named.

The eight days

  1. June Reader: reading about June and its celebrations.
  2. Summer Sound Claps: speech rhythm: clap the syllables.
  3. Parade Planner: design a parade.
  4. March and Freeze: move like an instrument and freeze.
  5. Summer Word Hunt: summer word puzzles.
  6. Who Makes the Music?: music jobs.
  7. Cheer Chant Book: compose a four-beat chant.
  8. Summer Send-Off Bingo: review game day.

Use them in any order. The welcome sheet has a “Day #” column so the sub knows which day each class gets next.

Why subs can teach it

Eight lessons, each with timed steps, words to say and a K–2 and a 3–5 sheet. Rhythm work is speech rhythm: clap the syllables of words, with the claps printed as dots. Nobody has to read music or make music with an instrument. Games use only voices, hands and a pencil.

Standards

Supports relating music to varied contexts (MU:Cn11.0) and generating musical ideas (MU:Cr1.1), National Core Arts Standards, Music, grades K–5.

Prints well in black and white. The facts used in the bundle are on page 8.

Tips for a calm class ·
  • Use the teacher’s attention signal from the welcome sheet (page 3), or clap two times and have the class clap back two.
  • “Strong voice, not shouting” keeps every game safe and happy.
  • If a game gets too loud, switch to a worksheet or to Soft Hands.
  • Let students take turns as the leader. Keep turns short.
  • Students may know other musicians or celebrate other traditions. Thank them and move on.
Not sure which game to pick? Use Soft Hands to settle a class, Clap the Name to wake it up, and Echo Me to finish with a smile.

Read, Answer and DrawRead, answer and draw
DAY 1
Grades 3–5
NameDate
Read the passage. Underline one fact that was new to you. Then answer the questions in complete sentences.
June is a month with many reasons to celebrate. June 19 is Juneteenth, a United States holiday about freedom. On June 19, 1865, Major General Gordon Granger arrived in Galveston, Texas, and an order was read that said all enslaved people in Texas were free. On June 19, 1866, freed people in Galveston organized a celebration, and people have gathered to celebrate ever since. Celebrations have included parades, picnics, prayer, readings, songs and singing. Texas made Juneteenth a state holiday starting in 1980, and in 2021 Congress passed a law, signed by President Joe Biden, that made Juneteenth National Independence Day a federal holiday. June is also when summer begins in the Northern Hemisphere, on June 20 or 21; in the Southern Hemisphere, winter begins then. Father’s Day is on the third Sunday in June, a day when many students thank a dad or another special grown-up. By the end of June, many schools have finished their year, so it is a good time to look back at what you learned. Music can be part of every one of these days, from a drum in a parade to a cheer for someone you want to thank.

Summer Sound ClapsDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of summer and celebration words, hear how a short poem has a rhythm, and build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: drum (1 clap), sum-mer (2 claps), Gal-ves-ton (3 claps), wa-ter-mel-on (4 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Poem and chart echoes
    Read this original poem line by line, clapping each syllable (every line has 4 claps): Sun, drum, wave, cheer. Parade, come here. Summer is here. Freedom, we cheer. Then read a line from page 7, one box per beat, clapping the dots: one dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant parade
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Clap the Name from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

March and FreezeDay 4 · Move like an instrument and freeze · no tech, no prep
DAY 4
Sub plan
GoalStudents move to a steady tap, copy the sub’s moves with their own (call and response), and freeze when it stops.
You needPencils, the worksheets, a clear space to move, and the picture cards on page 22 (point at them or cut them out).
CopiesK–2: page 20
3–5: page 21
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Rules of the freeze
    Say: “When I tap, you move. When I stop, you freeze like a statue. Keep your hands to yourself.” Practice once: tap a pencil on a desk for five seconds, stop, and wait for stillness.
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Call and response
    Say: “Musicians often answer each other: one calls, the other answers.” Make a simple move (a clap, a head nod, a shoulder shrug) and say “Call!” The class copies it and says “Answer!” Do five moves. Then let a few students be the caller.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Picture cards
    Hold up (or point at) one card at a time. Students move as the card says while you tap. Use at least six cards, and try a slow tap, a fast tap and a bouncy tap (long, short, long, short, like skipping). The words under each card tell you what to say.
  4. 10 minStep 4
    Reflection sheet
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 20, 3–5 page 21. Students sit and fill it in. Read the directions aloud.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Statue garden
    Tap a few beats, stop, and let the class freeze in a favorite pose. Walk around and name two poses you notice.
Extra time? Freeze Frame or March in Place from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Use only four cards.
