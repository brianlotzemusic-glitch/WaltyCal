What this is ·

A 7-class-period sound design project for high school music technology. Each student explores Logic’s Alchemy synthesizer, designs at least three original patches from oscillators, filters, envelopes and modulation, and composes a 45–60 second track that features them. It is a structured project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (focus: synthesis), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Check that Alchemy opens on every computer (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–22), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–11), Class 3 (slide 12), Class 4 (slide 13), Class 6 (slides 14–16) and Class 7 (slides 17–18). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, video, songs or presets are included. Students make every sound themselves in Logic’s Alchemy, so everything on their tracks is original. For the hook, you choose a passage of electronic music yourself and find it on a service your school allows.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: my variations on page 6: two patches and a class walkthrough, or five patches and a 90-second track.
  • Feedback board: my sheets use Padlet for the three questions; any class board works.
  • Sound library: my notes suggest students name their patches and share them as a class library.
  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 20).

Alchemy Architect · Structured · Synthesis
Design original sounds from scratch using Logic’s Alchemy synthesizer. Oscillators, filters, modulation, and patches.

What students make ·

At least three original Alchemy patches, designed from scratch, and one 45–60 second track that features all of them, with effects and automation. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx project and a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then play the track and talk through their sounds.

What this project reinforces ·

Introduces sound synthesis fundamentals. Students learn that every sound in electronic music was designed by someone, and they can be that person.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): refining original work (MU:Cr3.1), presenting it (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Learn the foundations of sound synthesis using Alchemy
  • Design original patches using oscillators, filters, and modulation
  • Apply effects and automation for expressivity
  • Compose a short track featuring your custom sounds

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student (a current version, which includes Alchemy), and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • Logic’s sound library downloaded, so Alchemy’s presets show up.
  • A place to submit an .mp3 or .wav and a zipped .logicx project (your class page).
  • A feedback board for the three questions (my sheets use Padlet).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–22).

Before Class 1 ·

Open Alchemy on a student computer. Check that presets load and that the sound plays through headphones.
Decide how students will save patches: in the project, plus a numbered name for each version.
Choose a short passage of electronic music with a bold synth sound for slide 2, and find it yourself.
Print the student pages; set up the submission spot and the feedback board.

Keeping Alchemy small ·

My notes say Alchemy is overwhelming at first. A plan that works:

  • Class 1. Simple view only. Presets, the big knobs and the Explore sheet (page 12).
  • Class 2. Open Advanced for one sound: a source, a filter, an envelope and one LFO, built together on slide 11.
  • Classes 3 to 5. Students build Patches 2 and 3 their own way, then compose.
A save-as-you-go routine
  • Name every patch the moment it makes a sound you like.
  • Use a number for each version: Tide 1, Tide 2.
  • Save again before trying anything new.
  • Log each version on the Patch card (pages 13–14).
Why thin, and what to do

A single oscillator sounds thin next to a preset, which layers many. Turn on a second source, detune it a little or put it an octave up, add a touch of chorus or reverb, and open the filter a bit. Page 11 shows how.

Alchemy ArchitectTeacher notes · the big idea
TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Introduces sound synthesis fundamentals. Students learn that every sound in electronic music was designed by someone, and they can be that person.

Common student struggles ·

  • Alchemy is overwhelming at first. Start with the Simple view before opening Advanced.
  • Students tweak endlessly without saving patches. Enforce a save-as-you-go habit.
  • Custom patches sound thin compared to presets. Show them how layering and effects can thicken a patch.
  • Students struggle to compose with unfamiliar sounds. Encourage them to design first, compose second.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide a guided walkthrough for building one patch together as a class. Reduce to 2 custom patches.

More Advanced: Require 5 patches with at least 2 using advanced modulation routing. Extend track to 90 seconds.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Alchemy is overwhelming: the Explore sheet (page 12) and the Alchemy quick reference (page 9) keep Class 1 small.
  • Not saving: slide 6 and the Patch cards (pages 13–14), which have a version log.
  • Thin patches: layering and effects on the Patch card, and the worked example on page 11.
  • Composing with unfamiliar sounds: slide 13 and the Track plan (page 15) give each patch a job.

How to grade ·

  • My rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 20). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Technical Skill: ask the student to open one patch and point to the oscillator, the filter, an envelope and one modulation (an LFO, say), and say what each one does to the sound.
  • Sound Design & Mix Quality: play each of the three patches alone, then in the track. Are they clearly different from each other, and does each have a job (bass, pad, lead) in the mix?
  • Composition & Structure: does the piece change over its 45–60 seconds, with patches entering and leaving? The Track plan (page 15) shows the intended shape.
  • Documentation & Presentation: the Explore sheet (page 12), the Patch cards (pages 13–14) and the Mix check (page 16) are the evidence, along with the Reflection page (page 23).

Reflection · added by this kit ·

The rubric mentions reflection, but the project sheet has no reflection task, so this kit adds one (page 23): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. It adds no points and no rubric row; use it as evidence when you score Documentation & Presentation.

Notes / observations ·

Consider having students name their patches creatively and share them with the class as a sound library.

Alchemy ArchitectLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where each Alchemy control lives, in one table. Demonstrate the building blocks (slides 7 to 11) and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; check the labels on yours.

Add AlchemyAdd a Software Instrument track, click the instrument slot on the channel strip and choose Alchemy. It opens in its own window, with presets on the left.
Simple firstAlchemy opens with a simplified set of big controls. Explore with those first, as my notes say. Open the Advanced view only when you want to build a sound from the ground up.
A basic startChoose a plain, basic starting patch if your version lists one, or take a simple preset and strip it back. Then change one setting at a time.
OscillatorIn Advanced, Source A is the sound source. A Virtual Analog source gives the classic waveforms: sine, triangle, saw and square. Play a held note and switch between them.
FilterEach source feeds a filter. Choose a low-pass type, hold a note and move the Cutoff. A little Resonance adds a peak at the cutoff.
Envelope (ADSR)The amplitude envelope sets how a note's volume changes: Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release. A short attack is a pluck; a long attack is a pad.
ModulationAssign an LFO or an envelope to a parameter such as the filter cutoff to make it move. Start with a slow rate and a small amount.
Layer sourcesTurn on a second source and tune it a little sharp, or an octave up, to thicken a thin patch.
EffectsAlchemy has its own effects section (reverb, delay, chorus and more). Logic’s channel-strip effects work too.
Save your patchUse Save As in the plug-in window header, give the patch a numbered name, and save after every change you like.
AutomationPress A to show automation, choose a parameter such as volume or a macro, and draw changes over time.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Alchemy ArchitectSynth guide · the four building blocks
SYNTH GUIDE
Teacher + student

My notes say to teach oscillators, filters and envelopes first. This page is the explanation to project or print. The numbers are for a note at 110 Hz (the A two octaves below middle A) and a tempo of 120 BPM.

Harmonics of each wave ·

Sineonly the 1st
Triangleodd: 1st, 3rd, 5th…, each much quieter (1/k²)
Sawall of them, each 1/k as loud
Squareodd: 1st, 3rd, 5th…, each 1/k as loud

Harmonic k of a note at 110 Hz is k × 110 Hz. An octave up doubles the frequency: 55, 110, 220, 440 Hz.

Where to start with an envelope ·

SoundADSR
Pluck0.01 s0.3 s20%0.2 s
Pad1 s0.5 s80%1.5 s
Bass0.005 s0.2 s70%0.1 s
Bell0.01 s1.2 s0%1.5 s

What a low-pass filter keeps ·

CutoffSaw harmonics keptSquare harmonics kept
500 Hz42
1000 Hz95
2000 Hz189

At 110 Hz, counting harmonics at or below the cutoff. A real filter fades gradually, so the numbers are a guide.

LFO speed and the bar ·

RateOne cycleCycles per bar
0.25 Hz4 s0.5
0.5 Hz2 s1
1 Hz1 s2
2 Hz0.5 s4
4 Hz0.25 s8

At 120 BPM a bar is 2 s, so cycles per bar = rate × 2. Rules of thumb, not laws.

2 · The track plan: 26 bars at 120 BPM ·

Intro bars 1–4, Build bars 5–12, Lift bars 13–20, Outro bars 21–26. One bar is 2 s, so 26 bars is 52 s: inside my 45–60 second requirement.

3 · Checking the numbers

Slow Tide plays a note at 110 Hz. With the cutoff at 1000 Hz, 1000 ÷ 110 = 9.09, so harmonics 1 to 9 pass. Its LFO at 0.5 Hz takes 2 s, which is one bar. Sub Pulse at 55 Hz with a 400 Hz cutoff keeps the odd harmonics 1, 3, 5 and 7 (55, 165, 275, 385 Hz). Glass Bell’s second sine is 440 Hz when the first is 220 Hz. A quarter-note delay at 120 BPM is 500 ms.

4 · The shape of the track

The pad opens alone and carries the piece. The bass joins at bar 5 (0:08) to start the Build. The bell enters at bar 13 (0:24) for the Lift, the bass drops out at bar 21 (0:40), and the pad and bell fade in the Outro.

Alchemy Architect

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Design your own sounds from scratch. Using Logic’s Alchemy synthesizer, you’ll explore oscillators, filters, envelopes, and modulation to create at least 3 custom patches. Then use those sounds to compose a short original piece.

Goals ·

  • Learn the foundations of sound synthesis using Alchemy
  • Design original patches using oscillators, filters, and modulation
  • Apply effects and automation for expressivity
  • Compose a short track featuring your custom sounds

Key Vocabulary ·

SynthesizerAn instrument that generates sound electronically using oscillators, filters, and modulation
OscillatorThe sound source in a synth — generates the raw waveform (sine, saw, square, etc.)
FilterShapes the tone of a sound by cutting or boosting certain frequencies
Envelope (ADSR)Controls how a sound changes over time: Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release
ModulationUsing one signal (like an LFO) to control another parameter for movement and variation
PatchA saved set of synth settings that creates a specific sound

Tips for Success ·

  • Start with a simple waveform and shape it. Don’t jump into the advanced features right away.
  • Small changes in filter and envelope settings make a huge difference. Experiment with subtle tweaks.
  • Save your patches as you go — it’s easy to lose a sound you liked if you keep tweaking.
  • Listen to electronic music producers for inspiration on what’s possible with synthesis.
