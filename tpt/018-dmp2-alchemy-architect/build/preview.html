The one rule for Class 1

From my common struggles: start with the Simple view before opening Advanced. Let students play and listen on Class 1; the Explore sheet (page 12) keeps them moving without turning it into a lecture. My two tutorial links (page 7) make a good take-home watch after Class 1.

Classes 3–5

Working days. Slides 12 and 13 open Classes 3 and 4; Class 5 has no new slide. Circulate and ask: what is this patch for, and have you saved it?

EXAMPLE
Teacher

Three invented patches and a 26-bar plan, described on paper: no audio is included, so the numbers are easy to check. Students’ sounds will differ.

Melody		Two sine waves, the second one octave upOpen: a sine has nothing above its pitch to cutA 0.01 s · D 1.2 s · S 0% · R 1.5 sNone: the long decay is the movementReverb, quarter-note delay

2 · The track plan: 26 bars at 120 BPM ·

Intro bars 1–4, Build bars 5–12, Lift bars 13–20, Outro bars 21–26. One bar is 2 s, so 26 bars is 52 s: inside my 45–60 second requirement.

3 · Checking the numbers

Slow Tide plays a note at 110 Hz. With the cutoff at 1000 Hz, 1000 ÷ 110 = 9.09, so harmonics 1 to 9 pass. Its LFO at 0.5 Hz takes 2 s, which is one bar. Sub Pulse at 55 Hz with a 400 Hz cutoff keeps the odd harmonics 1, 3, 5 and 7 (55, 165, 275, 385 Hz). Glass Bell’s second sine is 440 Hz when the first is 220 Hz. A quarter-note delay at 120 BPM is 500 ms.

4 · The shape of the track

The pad opens alone and carries the piece. The bass joins at bar 5 (0:08) to start the Build. The bell enters at bar 13 (0:24) for the Lift, the bass drops out at bar 21 (0:40), and the pad and bell fade in the Outro.

Alchemy ArchitectProject sheet · requirements and steps
STUDENT
2 of 3
NameDate

Requirements ·

Design at least 3 original patches in Alchemy
Use oscillators, filters, and modulation in your sound design
Compose a 45–60 second track featuring all of your custom sounds
Apply effects and automation for expressivity
Mix and bounce your track for presentation

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Explore — Open Alchemy and experiment with the interface, sound sources, and presets (Explore sheet: page 12)
  2. 2
    Learn the building blocks — Understand oscillators, filters, envelopes, and modulation routing (Synth guide: page 10)
  3. 3
    Design Patch 1 — Create your first original sound from scratch. Save it. (Patch card: page 13)
  4. 4
    Design Patches 2 & 3 — Build two more distinct sounds. Aim for variety. (Patch cards: page 14)
  5. 5
    Compose — Use your 3 patches to build a 45–60 second track (Track plan: page 15)
  6. 6
    Mix and finalize — Apply effects, automation, EQ, and panning. Bounce your track. (Mix check: page 16)
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.
