How to use
  1. Choose the concert pages that match your ensemble: 5 for band, 6 for choir, 7 for orchestra. Print one copy of the pages you need for each student.
  2. Students fill in My Concert (page 4) first, then the checklist, the etiquette page and one Looking Back page.
  3. On the checklist, a student ticks every statement that is true of their own concert, counts the ticks in each area and turns the count into a level.
  4. Optional: score the same six areas yourself with the rubric (page 8) and compare.
  5. Finish with the Goal-Setting Sheet (page 12). Record scores on the Class Summary (page 13).
Student time

About 45 minutes in all: My Concert 8, checklist 10, etiquette 7, Looking Back 10 and goal sheet 10. Use one long period, or split it over two.

Scoring the checklist

Each ensemble page has 6 skill areas with 3 statements each, so 18 tick boxes per page. Students turn the ticks in an area into a level:

Ticks0123
Level1234

Level = ticks + 1, so every area scores from 1 to 4. The 6 levels add up to a total from 6 to 24.

Worked example
AreaTicksLevel
Tone34
Rhythm23
Pitch and Intonation23
Dynamics34
Teamwork12
Concert Etiquette23
Total1319

4 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 3 = 19 out of 24, which is in the Proficient band.

Ensemble versions
  • Band (page 5): wind, brass and percussion.
  • Choir (page 6): singers in any voice part.
  • Orchestra (page 7): string players. Wind, brass and percussion players in orchestra use the Band page.

The six skill areas, the levels and the rubric are the same for every ensemble, so a class summary can mix all three.

Conferencing and feedback
  • Start with what the student is proud of (page 4), then look at their highest area.
  • If your rubric level and the student's level are 2 or more apart in an area, ask the student for evidence from the concert.
  • Connect the lowest area to the goal sheet. One small goal is better than three large ones.
  • Keep the wording neutral. These pages say "concert", "performance" and "winter"; they use no holiday or religious references, so they fit any school.
Standards

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music: MU:Pr5.1 (rehearse, evaluate and refine) and MU:Re9.1 (evaluate performances). Modest claim: a structured reflection routine, not a full assessment program.

Printing

US Letter. The student pages print in black and white; print them single-sided.

No songs, lyrics, notation or copyrighted characters are used.

Concert EtiquetteHow did I act on stage and in the audience?
Student
Page 9
Name Date
Directions: Tick each statement that is true. There are 10 statements: 5 for performers and 5 for audience members. Leave a box empty if it was not true this time.
As a performer
I waited quietly before walking on stage.
I walked on in order and found my spot without talking.
I kept my body still and quiet between pieces.
I stayed focused on the conductor during the music.
I bowed or stood with the group and waited for the cue to leave.
As an audience member
I turned off my phone or put it away before the concert.
I stayed quiet while music was playing.
I waited until the end of each piece to clap.
I stayed in my seat until the performance ended.
I showed respect to every group, including the ones I was not in.

Looking BackGrades 7–12: use evidence from the concert.
Grades 7–12
Page 11
Name Date
Directions: Answer each prompt in a short paragraph. Use your checklist and rubric scores as evidence.
1. Choose your highest-scoring skill area. Give two pieces of evidence from the concert, such as a section, a phrase or a moment, that show it.
2. Choose your lowest-scoring skill area. What caused it, and what did you do in rehearsal that did or did not help?
3. Compare your self-assessment total with the teacher rubric total, if you have both. Where do they differ, and why might that be?
4. How did your part fit with the other parts or sections? Describe one moment when listening to others changed how you played or sang.
5. What would you change about how we prepared for this concert, and what would you keep?
