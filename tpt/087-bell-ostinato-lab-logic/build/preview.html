The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a tempo and a one-bar motif that has been checked note by note against the note chart and repeats for 4 bars without a gap, so that Class 2 is all layers and the answer.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the plan, the motif, the ostinato and the first layers; Class 2 is the answer, the automation, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the automation and the mix, and Class 4 on presentations and reflection.

Step 2: learn the motif. The motif is four notes long and fills one bar of 3/4: a quarter note, two eighth notes and a quarter note, which add up to 3 beats. It uses three different pitches and sits in G minor (two flats, Bb and Eb). Read the picture as a piano roll: each row is one semitone and each column is one eighth note. Bb means B-flat and F# means F-sharp. The letters are written this way throughout the kit.

The lengths add up to 3 beats, one bar of 3/4. MIDI note 60 is middle C (C4) and A4 is note 69 at 440 Hz; a note’s frequency is 440 × 2^((MIDI − 69) ÷ 12). Software may number octaves differently, so compare the letters and the semitone distances.

−1 + 1 + (−3) = −3: the last note is 3 semitones below the first, a minor third. The highest and lowest notes are Bb4 and G4, so the whole motif spans a minor third.

Moving the motif to a new key

New MIDI number = old number + shift. Every note in the second key is 3 semitones lower, so the intervals do not change: −1, +1 and −3.

Step 4: layer it. A layer is one part of the piece on its own track, with its own sound and its own bars. This form is 16 bars, four sections of 4 bars, and it has 5 layers. A new idea joins or changes in each section, so the listener hears the piece grow.

Step 6: shape the sound. Dynamics are how loud or soft the music is. A crescendo is a gradual increase in loudness. In Logic Pro you can draw one as a volume automation line: points on a track’s volume that you place and drag (if your version has automation). The plan is an example on a 0 to 127 scale, like MIDI velocity, not a rule.

Reading the line

Between two points the level changes by the same amount each bar: (end level − start level) ÷ number of bars. Bars 13 to 16: (112 − 80) ÷ 4 = 8 levels per bar, so the line is at 80 at bar 13 and 104 at bar 16.

Velocity sets how hard each note is played; volume automation sets the level of the whole track. Try both and listen to the difference.
