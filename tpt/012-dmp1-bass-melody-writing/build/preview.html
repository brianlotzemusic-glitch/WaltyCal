Bass & Melody WritingFour ways to write a melody
STUDENT
Day 1
NameDate

There is more than one way to write a melody. Try at least two of these today. Whichever you use, the rules are the same: 2 or 4 measures, notes from your scale, start or end on the root.

1
Play it in

Loop 2 measures with Cycle (C). Press R, play on the white keys, stop. Select the notes and press Q to quantize.

Good when you can already hear an idea in your head.

2
Draw a rhythm first

With the Pencil, click notes in a rhythm you like, all on the root. Play it back. Then drag some notes up or down to other scale notes.

Good when you have a groove but no tune yet.

3
Click one note, move it

Click in one note. Listen. Drag it up or down until it fits the chord. Then click the next one.

Good when you want to build slowly, one decision at a time.

4
Use the step sequencer

Control-click the track lane and choose Create Pattern Region. Turn steps on to place notes; each row is one pitch.

Good when you want a short, repeating, rhythmic hook.

Stuck?

Use only 3 notes. C major: C, E, G · A minor: A, C, E. Go up, then come back down. That is a melody.

Remember

Simple and repetitive will always beat complicated and forgettable.

Open the Logic file your teacher provides. A bassline is already written. Before you do anything else, answer the questions below.

Look at the bassline in the piano roll. Answer these questions ·

What key is this bassline in?
What chord is playing in the first section?
What note does the bassline play during that chord?
Is that note the root of the chord?
What happens to the bass note when the chord changes?
