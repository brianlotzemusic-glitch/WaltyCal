## 2. Description

```
Melody writing for high school music technology: students use a scale to write a short, singable melody over a chord progression, discover the one rule behind every bassline, then write their own bassline over four chords they choose, all in Logic Pro. This 4-day unit is Unit 4 of my Digital Music Production 1 course; the steps are written for Logic Pro, while the music ideas (scales, root-note basslines, Roman numerals, frequency separation) carry over to GarageBand, Ableton Live and BandLab.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (ZIP: 30-page PDF + 22-slide intro deck as .pptx and PDF)
• How to use, unit overview, setup page with the chords-and-bassline Logic file (continue from Unit 3, or a 10-minute recipe), and a one-page Logic quick reference (4 pages)
• 4-day pacing guide and minute-by-minute day plans, with the deck slides for each block (3 pages)
• Teacher notes: the melody, the bassline reveal moment, the presentation question that matters, eight common student struggles, variations, the frequency separation mini-lesson and grading tips (4 pages)
• Student pages: two classroom posts, the Scary Stuff packet Part 2 (scales) and Part 4 (bassline analysis and planning table), Reference Cards for C major and A minor, Four Ways to Write a Melody, two project sheets with requirements, tips and submission checklists, two /15 rubrics, and vocabulary (14 pages)
• Answer keys, a worked melody in both keys and a worked bassline with its plan, as piano-roll diagrams (3 pages)
• Teacher-presented intro deck, 22 slides with speaker notes on every slide: a pop-culture hook, a plain-English definition of a melody, guided listening questions, worked examples in the piano roll, the steps, deliverables and rubrics for both projects

THE FOUR DAYS
• Day 1, Scales and melody: bright or dark, the scale as an ingredient list, four ways to write a melody, start a 2- or 4-measure melody
• Day 2, Finish the melody and meet the bass rule: frequency separation with EQ, submit, then look hard at a bassline and find the rule
• Day 3, Plan and build the bassline: four chords with Roman numerals and root notes, roots first, then rhythm
• Day 4, Present: what key, what Roman numerals, what did you want it to feel like?

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN
• A scale is the ingredient list for a key; simple and repetitive beats complicated and forgettable
• Play-in with quantize, click-in, and the step sequencer
• When the chord changes, the bass note changes to the root of the new chord
• Basslines are monophonic and need rhythm; frequency separation gives bass and melody their own space

GOOD TO KNOW
• Requires Logic Pro on a Mac. A MIDI keyboard helps (Musical Typing works too). Logic files are not included; students continue in their project from Unit 3, Chords by Ear (sold separately), or use your file, made from the 10-minute recipe on the setup page
• Built for about 53-minute periods, grades 9-12; C major or A minor, white keys only; no music reading needed
• The deck uses the free Oswald and Source Sans 3 fonts (built into Google Slides); install them for PowerPoint or Keynote, or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look
• Songs are named by title only, with the artist for the two listening songs (Seven Nation Army by The White Stripes, Stand by Me by Ben E. King). Play them from your own YouTube or Spotify access. No audio, video, lyrics or album art are included
• Every student page prints well in black and white

Designed with the help of digital and AI tools, and checked by hand.
```
