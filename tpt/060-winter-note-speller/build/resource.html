Teacher Notes

Winter Note Speller: spell-the-word worksheets, riddles and answer keys

What students do

Students read a row of notes, name each note by its letter and write the word the notes spell. Every word uses only the seven music letters A, B, C, D, E, F and G, so a student who reads every note correctly can spell every word.

What is inside

  • Reference sheet: page 3. Lines, spaces and notes outside the staff in both clefs.
  • Treble clef: pages 4–7, with Level A on pages 4–5 and Level B on pages 6–7.
  • Bass clef: pages 8–11, with Level A on pages 8–9 and Level B on pages 10–11.
  • Winter riddles: pages 12–14, 12 riddles in three sets.
  • Write your own word: page 15. Spell it with notes: page 16.
  • Answer keys: pages 17–19. Terms of use: page 20.

Each worksheet page has eight words, so the 8 worksheet pages hold 64 words in all. Words run from three letters, such as BAD, to seven letters in the riddles.

The two levels

Level A: every note sits on a line or in a space inside the staff.

Level B: some notes sit above or below the staff, and every word has at least one note that needs a ledger line. In treble clef those are A and B below the staff, middle C and high A. In bass clef they are D and E below the staff, and middle C and D above it.

The riddles

Each riddle has a clue, a row of notes and a line for the answer. The notes spell the answer, so students can check their reading against the clue. If the word does not fit the clue, the notes need a second look. Sets 1 and 2 use Level A notes, and set 3 uses Level B notes.

Suggested path

  1. Read the reference sheet together and practice the sayings for the lines and spaces.
  2. Do treble clef Level A, then bass clef Level A.
  3. Move to Level B when students can name notes inside the staff without the reference sheet.
  4. Use the riddles as a challenge or an early-finisher task.

Write your own and spell it

On page 15 students choose their own words and draw the notes. On page 16 the word is given and students draw the notes. Quarter notes are used on every page, so the notes show pitch only. Stems point up for notes below the middle line and down for notes on or above it. Students can trade papers and read each other's words. Answers on these two pages will vary, and page 19 shows one correct set for page 16.

Grades 3 to 4

  • Start with Level A in one clef and keep the reference sheet on the desk.
  • Work in pairs: one student reads the notes aloud and the other writes the word.
  • Read the riddle clues aloud to the class.

Grades 5 to 6

  • Use Level B and both clefs as fluency practice, without the reference sheet.
  • Time a page for a friendly challenge and ask students to beat their own time.
  • Have students write riddles of their own with answers spelled from A to G.

Standards and printing

These pages practice reading notes in standard notation, which supports the performing and reading goals in the National Core Arts Standards for music. The pages print well in black and white. The blue is one accent color. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Seven letters

Music uses only the letters A, B, C, D, E, F and G. After G the letters start again at A, so every word in this book is spelled with just those seven letters.

Ledger lines

A ledger line is a short line added above or below the staff for a note that does not fit. Middle C sits on a ledger line below the treble staff and on a ledger line above the bass staff.
