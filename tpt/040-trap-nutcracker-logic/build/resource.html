What this is ·

A 4-class-period beat-making project for high school music technology. Each student chooses one of two public-domain themes from Tchaikovsky’s Nutcracker (1892), enters its melody and chords into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, and flips it into a trap beat: a half-time snare, rolling hi-hats, a gliding 808 sub bass and a chopped, moved or reversed melody. The beat is 60 to 90 seconds. Students plan it, build and mix it, present it and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Decide which themes to offer (page 9) and check the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–26), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–9), Class 3 (slides 10–11) and Class 4 (slides 12–15). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, recordings, lyrics, artists or albums are named or included. The two themes are public domain and appear only as note charts. Students make every sound in their beats themselves, from the instruments, drum kits and plug-ins in Logic.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: see the variations on page 6.
  • Theme choice: Trepak is the easier chart, Sugar Plum Fairy the harder (page 9). They are in related keys (G major and E minor), so strong students can combine them.
  • Feedback: students write three questions and answer classmates’ on a class board or on paper.
  • Grading: the rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 23).

Trap NutcrackerProject overview
OVERVIEW
Beat-making project
The project in one line

Trap Nutcracker · Beat making · Logic Pro
Flip a Tchaikovsky theme into a trap beat.

What students make ·

One original beat, 60 to 90 seconds long, built on a public-domain theme. The melody is entered from a piano-roll chart and stays recognizable; the tempo is 130 to 150 BPM, the snare is half-time, the hats roll, and an 808 plays the chord roots. It has at least five layers and an intro, beat sections, a break and an outro, is mixed with volume, pan and EQ, and uses at least four of the five trap moves. Students hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the beat and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a style is built from small choices. Students learn that a half-time snare, an 808, rolling hi-hats and a flipped melody, not only the notes, make a beat sound like trap, and they practice entering notes and chords in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody and chords into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Program a half-time trap drum pattern with hi-hat rolls, and an 808 sub bass on the chord roots, at 130 to 150 BPM
  • Flip a melody on purpose (chop it, move it up or down an octave, reverse it) and explain what each flip does
  • Plan, build and mix a 60 to 90 second beat with an intro, beat sections, a break, a drop and an outro

Trap NutcrackerSetup and what to watch
SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen, because an 808 needs good low end.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The two charts, printed (pages 12 and 13), or shown on the projector.
  • The flips, drum patterns and effect cards, printed (pages 14–17).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–26).
  • A MIDI keyboard is optional: every melody can be drawn with the mouse.

Before Class 1 ·

Decide which themes you will offer (page 9).
Open Logic Pro on a student computer: can you make a track, open the Piano Roll and hear playback through headphones?
Check that the tools on page 16 and page 17 are there (Alchemy or Retro Synth, Compressor, a Distortion or Overdrive plug-in, ChromaVerb or Space Designer) and that you know where your Library keeps sub bass sounds.
Read the worked example (pages 10–11).

What to watch ·

Students enter the notes but get a wrong one, the beat is busy instead of heavy, the 808 turns to mud, or the tune disappears. Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished beats without saying the title and let the class name the Tchaikovsky theme. A tune that people name quickly is a beat that kept the melody.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: where is the snare? Can you hear the theme? Which trap move is this?
  • On Class 1, tap 130, 140 and 150 BPM on the desk, then clap only on every second backbeat, so students feel half-time.
  • A student who is stuck on drums can start from a Drummer track in a hip-hop style and move the snare to beat 3.

Choosing themes ·

A respectful choice

Both themes come from a Christmas-season ballet, but neither is a carol and neither has words. Trepak is a lively Russian dance and the Sugar Plum Fairy is a dance for a fairy; students of any background can use them. If a student prefers another melody, a public-domain one you approve (first published in the United States in 1930 or earlier) works with the same plan, and the blank piano roll on page 18 is there for their own chart.

Keep the volume moderate

Bass-heavy beats and loud peaks are tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a style is built from small choices. Students learn that a half-time snare, an 808, rolling hi-hats and a flipped melody, not only the notes, make a beat sound like trap, and they practice entering notes and chords in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Common student struggles ·

  • The notes go in but the tune sounds wrong: check the grid setting and the note lengths against the chart, bar by bar (Logic quick reference, page 8). Remember that two bars of the score are written as one 4/4 bar.
  • The beat sounds busy, not heavy: check that the snare lands on beat 3 only and that the hi-hats are quieter than the snare (page 15).
  • The 808 is muddy or vanishes: shorten its notes, sidechain it to the kick or add a little distortion so small speakers can hear it (page 16).
  • The tune disappears: compare with the flips on page 14 and the cards on pages 16–17. A flip changes the rhythm or register, never the order of the pitches, unless it is flip 3 on purpose.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Use Trepak, loop one four-bar section for the whole beat, and start from pattern A on the drum patterns page or a Drummer track in a hip-hop style, then make it half-time.

More Advanced: Write a second four-bar section with a different chord order, play the melody twice, once chopped and once an octave up (see the flips page), and automate the filter and the 808’s distortion across the whole piece. Sugar Plum Fairy is the harder chart: it has sharps and a pick-up.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Wrong notes: the chart pages (pages 12 and 13) list every note by bar, and the Logic quick reference (page 8) shows the grid.
  • A busy, weak beat: the drum patterns (page 15) and slide 8 show where the snare and the rolls go.
  • A muddy 808: the 808 card (page 16), the mix checks on the Arrangement plan (page 19) and slide 11.
  • A tune that disappears: the five flips (page 14) and the worked example (pages 10–11).

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 23). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Melody & Chords (rubric row 1): open the project and compare the Piano Roll with the chart, bar by bar. Check that the chords match the plan.
  • Trap Beat and 808 & Sound (rubric rows 2 and 3): check the tempo is 130 to 150 BPM, that the snare is half-time, that there is a hi-hat roll, and that at least four of the five trap moves can be heard and named.
  • Arrangement and Mix (rubric row 4): compare the project with the Arrangement plan (page 19). Is there an intro, beat sections, a break with a drop and an outro, and can you hear the melody over the 808?
  • Plan and Reflection (rubric row 5): the Beat plan (page 18), the questions on the feedback planner and the Reflection page (page 26) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 26): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Plan & Reflection row of the rubric.

Notes / observations ·

Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished beats without saying the title and let the class name the Tchaikovsky theme. A tune that people name quickly is a beat that kept the melody.

Trap NutcrackerLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Trap Nutcracker beat is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add a Software Instrument track with Track › New Tracks…. Use one track for the melody and more for chords, the 808 and drums.
Tempo and time signatureType the tempo in the control bar (130 to 150 BPM) and keep 4/4. In the score both themes are in 2/4; the charts write two score bars as one 4/4 bar, so nobody needs to change the time signature.
Make a region, open the Piano RollWith the Pencil tool, click on the track where bar 1 starts to make an empty MIDI region, then double-click it to open the Piano Roll editor.
Draw notes and chordsPick a grid value in the Piano Roll’s Snap menu (for example, 1/16 note) so notes land on the chart’s sixteenth-note grid, click with the Pencil to add a note and drag its right edge to change its length. Draw a chord as notes stacked in one column. Logic names middle C “C3” by default; the charts call it C4, so subtract one from the chart’s octave number when you read the roll.
Fix wrong notesSelect a note and drag it up or down a row, or press delete. To play a melody an octave lower, select the region and change Transpose in the Region inspector by 12 steps.
DrumsMake a Drum Machine Designer track and open the Step Sequencer: click the 16 steps for each drum, as on the patterns on page 15. Or make a Drummer track and, if your version has a hip-hop or trap style, start there and make it half-time.
808 sub bassMake a Software Instrument track with Alchemy or Retro Synth and browse the Library (Y) for a sub bass or 808 sound, or build one: a sine oscillator, mono with Glide on, a long release. Play low notes (E1 to D2). Add a little Distortion or Overdrive so small speakers can hear it.
Hi-hat rollsPut the closed hat on its own pad. In the Piano Roll draw the roll on a fine grid (1/16 triplet or 1/32), or in the Step Sequencer set the row’s rate to a faster value. A Note Repeater MIDI plug-in on the track will repeat a held note. Ramp the velocities up with MIDI Draw or the velocity controls so the roll swells.
Sidechain, reverb and EQOn the 808 track add a Compressor and set its Side Chain to the kick’s track, so the 808 ducks when the kick hits. For space use ChromaVerb or Space Designer on the clap and the pad. Use Channel EQ to cut mud and to close a low-pass filter (High Cut) in the intro.
Chop and reverseSelect regions and press Command-T to split at the playhead, or bounce a part to audio and open it in Quick Sampler, which can play slices from the keyboard and has a Reverse button.
Automation, mixing and bouncePress A to show automation, pick a parameter such as a filter cutoff or distortion amount, and draw points. Press X for the Mixer. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Where the themes come from ·

  • Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy: The celesta tune from Act II of Tchaikovsky’s Nutcracker ballet (Variation II of the Pas de deux, No. 3 of the Op. 71a suite), first performed in 1892. This chart is score bars 5 to 12, with the pitches moved down an octave.
  • Trepak (Russian Dance): The main theme of the Russian Dance from Act II of Tchaikovsky’s Nutcracker ballet (No. 4 of the Op. 71a suite), first performed in 1892. This chart is score bars 1 to 8, at score pitch.
  • The transcription: the notes were taken from free transcriptions of the score, checked against each other, and written here in the kit’s own layout. Both dances are in 2/4 in the score; this kit writes two score bars as one 4/4 bar. If you want to check a note, the score of Op. 71 (and the suite, Op. 71a) is free on IMSLP.

The public-domain rule ·

In 2026 a melody is in the public domain in the United States when it was first published in 1930 or earlier (1931 from 1 January 2027). Tchaikovsky’s Nutcracker was first performed and published in 1892. Recordings, film and pop versions, and their arrangements are separate works, so students must not use them. If a student suggests another tune, check its first publication date before you approve it.

Picking a theme for a student ·

  • First time in the Piano Roll: Trepak. Short notes, mostly G, C and D, and a loop that repeats cleanly.
  • Wants a dark, bell-like sound: Sugar Plum Fairy. E minor, with sharps and seventh chords.
  • Wants a challenge: use both: Trepak (G major) for Beat A and Sugar Plum Fairy (E minor, the relative minor) for Beat B.
Reading the charts

Pitch names run down the left; middle C is C4 (Logic’s piano roll calls it C3). Bold lines are bars, grey lines are beats. A note’s length is the name beside it: 16th, eighth, quarter or half. Black-key notes are marked with a sharp (F#). Chords are one idea that fits, not the only one.

Why a four-bar loop

Trap beats are built on a short loop that repeats. Each chart ends so that it leads back into bar 1: the Sugar Plum loop ends on B7 and returns to Em, and the Trepak loop ends on D and returns to G.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented plan on paper: the Sugar Plum Fairy, the four-bar loop, as a trap beat at 150 BPM. No audio is included. Student projects will differ. Page 1 of 2: the plan and the trap moves.

1 · A weak plan

I will make the Sugar Plum Fairy into a trap beat.

It names no tempo, no moves and nothing that would sound like trap.

2 · A clear plan

My theme is the Sugar Plum Fairy celesta tune, the four-bar chart in E minor. My beat is trap at 150 BPM, in half-time. The loop is 4 bars with the chords Em B7 | C#dim7 Cdim7 Em | Em Cmaj7 | B7. I use all five trap moves: hi-hat rolls in the last beat of every fourth bar, an 808 that glides on the chord roots, a clap on beat 3 only, the melody chopped and then an octave higher, and a riser before each drop. The beat is 40 bars, 64 seconds: intro, beat, break, beat with bells, outro.

3 · Five trap moves, and what stays ·

Hi-hat rolls16th-note hats, with a 32nd roll in the last beat of bars 4, 8, 12 and so on; the roll’s velocity ramps up (page 16)
808E1, B1, C#2 and C2 on the chord roots, Glide on, a little Distortion, sidechained to the kick
Half-time snareone clap layered with a snare on beat 3 only; the kick has just three hits a bar
Flipchopped in Beat A (flip 2), an octave higher on bells in Beat B (flip 4); see page 14
Riser and dropa noise riser in bars 3–4 and 19–20, then one beat of silence before the 808 returns
StaysEvery pitch and every length of the chart before it is flipped: the theme keeps its tune, so people still know it.

9 · Checking the numbers

Trap NutcrackerFive flips
STUDENT
Class 2

A flip remakes a melody without losing it. Here is bar 1 of Trepak (page 13) in five flips. Every picture is computed from the same nine notes: nothing is drawn by hand. Try one flip per section of your beat, and keep the original for the part where people should name the tune.

FlipWhat changesIn Logic
1 · StretchEvery start and length doubles, so one bar becomes two. The tune is the same, but it breathes like half-time.Draw the notes again twice as long: double every start and every length in the table on page 13.
2 · ChopEvery note is cut to one step. The pitches and starts stay, so the rhythm of the tune is still there, but it ticks like a hi-hat.Shorten the notes to one grid step, or draw them one step long, on a 1/16 grid.
3 · ReverseThe bar played backwards: the same notes and lengths, in the opposite order. Use it for a break or a fill.Bounce the region to audio and use Reverse in Quick Sampler, or enter the notes from the end of the table up.
4 · Octave jumpEvery second note moves up an octave, so the line leaps. It sounds bright and bell-like over a heavy 808.Select every second note and drag it up 12 rows (an octave) in the Piano Roll.
5 · DropThe whole line moves down an octave. Use it to double the 808, or to hide the tune under the next section.Select the region and set Transpose in the Region inspector to −12.
The rule of the flip

Change the rhythm, the register or the sound, but keep the pitches in order (except flip 3, on purpose). Play your flip next to the original: if a listener cannot name the theme, bring back more of the original.

Class 2

Trap drums are simple and heavy. Each card gives a one-bar pattern (16 steps, 4 to a beat; shaded steps are the beats), an 808 idea and sounds to try. Every pattern is half-time: the clap or snare lands on beat 3 only. Pick one for your beat at 130 to 150 BPM and copy it into the Step Sequencer, or use a Drummer track, then add rolls (page 16). Use pattern C only as the last bar of a phrase and pattern D for intros and breaks.

Pattern A · Half-time basic · slow and heavy, with space

Drums. Kick on beat 1 and an eighth note after beat 3; clap or snare on beat 3 only; hats on every eighth note.

808. one long note on each chord root, sliding at the chord change.

Sounds. A deep kick, a layered clap and snare, tight closed hats.

Try. Turn the hats down until the snare and 808 own the beat.

Pattern B · Bounce · rolling, a little busier

Drums. Kick on beat 1, the last sixteenth of beat 1 and the “and” of beat 3; clap or snare on beat 3 only; hats on every sixteenth; open hat on the last eighth of the bar.

808. follow the kick, with short notes on the root and a long one on beat 3.

Sounds. The same kit as A, with the hats at lower velocity and an open hat for lift.

Try. Vary the hat velocities: accents on the beat, softer in between.

Pattern C · Roll fill · a fill, for the end of a phrase

Drums. Kick on beat 1, the “and” of 2 and the “and” of 3; clap or snare on beat 3; hats on eighth notes, then four sixteenths in the last beat (a roll).

808. long roots, with a short note before the change.

Sounds. The same kit as A; ramp the roll’s velocity up from soft to loud.

Try. Use this bar only as the fourth bar of a phrase, then go back to A or B.

Pattern D · Sparse · for intros, breaks and outros

Drums. Kick on beat 1 only; clap or snare on beat 3; hats only on beats 2 and 4; open hat on the last eighth.

808. one long note per bar, or leave it out for a break.

Sounds. Soft and spacious: more reverb on the clap, hats very quiet.

Try. Use it for the intro and the break, then bring in pattern A or B for the drop.

Class 3

Five moves make a beat sound like trap: hi-hat rolls, an 808, a half-time snare, a flipped melody and a riser and drop. Use at least four. This page has the rolls and the 808; page 17 has the other three. Every move should leave room for the theme.

Hi-hat rolls · the trap signature

What it does. A roll is a burst of very fast hi-hat notes. Trap hats are steady eighths or sixteenths, with a roll of triplets or 32nd notes at the end of a bar or a phrase.

In Logic. Put the closed hat on its own pad. In the Piano Roll draw the hats on a fine grid (1/16, 1/16 triplet or 1/32), or in the Step Sequencer set the hat row’s rate to a faster value. A Note Repeater MIDI plug-in will repeat a held note. Ramp the velocities up (MIDI Draw or the velocity controls) so the roll swells.

Try. Roll only in the last beat of the bar, and only every fourth bar. Keep the hats much quieter than the snare.

Listen for. The hats speed up and lean into the next bar. If the whole beat is rolling, nothing stands out.

808 · the sub bass that is also a drum

What it does. A long, deep bass note that plays the chord roots and can glide from one to the next. It is felt more than heard.

In Logic. Make a Software Instrument track with Alchemy or Retro Synth and look in the Library (Y) for a sub bass or 808 sound, or build one: a sine oscillator, mono with Glide on, a long release. Play low notes (E1 to D2). Add a little Distortion or Overdrive so small speakers can hear it, and put a Compressor on it with its Side Chain set to the kick.

Try. One long root per chord. Cut each note a hair before the next kick. Glide only on the notes that change chord.

Listen for. Weight under everything. If it vanishes on a laptop, add distortion; if it is muddy, make the notes shorter.

Half-time snare · the heavy backbeat

What it does. In half-time the clap or snare lands on beat 3 only, not on 2 and 4, so the beat feels half as fast even though the hats are busy.

In Logic. Put a clap and a snare on their own pads (Drum Machine Designer) and click both on step 9 of the Step Sequencer, which is beat 3. Layer them, and put a long reverb on the clap. A Drummer track can do the same if you move its snare to beat 3.

Try. Add a very quiet ghost hit before beat 3, or leave a bar with no snare before a drop. Keep the kick to three hits a bar or fewer.

Listen for. The beat feels slow and heavy while the hats still move. If it sounds like a march, check that the snare is only on beat 3.

Flip the melody · same tune, new job

What it does. A flip changes a melody’s rhythm, register or sound while keeping its notes. See the five flips on page 14.

In Logic. In the Piano Roll shorten the notes to chop them, drag notes up 12 rows to jump an octave, or set Transpose in the Region inspector. To play slices from the keyboard, bounce the melody to audio and drop it into Quick Sampler. For a new sound, try a bell, a pluck or a mallet instrument from the Library.

Try. Play the original in the intro, a chopped version in Beat A and an octave higher in Beat B. Move the flipped tune away from the 808’s register.

Listen for. A listener should still name the theme. If they cannot, bring back more of the original rhythm.

Riser and drop · tension and release

What it does. A riser is a sound that climbs before a drop. The drop is the moment the 808 and the kick come back. A beat of silence just before it makes it hit harder.

In Logic. Use a synthesizer’s noise oscillator and automate its filter cutoff or pitch upward (press A for automation), or search the Loop Browser (O) for a riser or sweep. To reverse a cymbal or a hit, bounce it to audio and use Reverse in Quick Sampler. Put a ChromaVerb or Space Designer on the last note.

Try. One riser of two bars before each drop. Cut every sound for one beat, then bring the 808 back on beat 1.

Listen for. The room fills up, then empties, then the bass lands. If the drop does not hit, make the silence longer.

Trap Nutcracker

Music Technology | Beat-Making Project | 4 class periods

Overview ·

Flip Tchaikovsky into a trap beat. Choose one of two public-domain themes from The Nutcracker (1892), enter its four-bar melody and chords into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, then build a half-time trap beat around it: a heavy snare, rolling hi-hats, a gliding 808 sub bass and a flipped, chopped version of the tune. Your finished beat is 60 to 90 seconds long at 130 to 150 BPM, and people should still be able to name the theme.

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody and chords into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Program a half-time trap drum pattern with hi-hat rolls, and an 808 sub bass on the chord roots, at 130 to 150 BPM
  • Flip a melody on purpose (chop it, move it up or down an octave, reverse it) and explain what each flip does
  • Plan, build and mix a 60 to 90 second beat with an intro, beat sections, a break, a drop and an outro

Key Vocabulary ·

TrapA style of hip-hop from the southern United States with a half-time feel, a heavy 808 sub bass, snappy snares and fast, rolling hi-hats
808A deep, long, pitched bass sound that is part drum and part bass note; it plays the chord roots and can glide between notes
Hi-hat rollA burst of very fast hi-hat notes, often triplets or 32nd notes, that speeds up or swells in volume
FlipTo remake an existing melody into something new by changing its rhythm, register or sound, while keeping it recognizable

Tips for Success ·

  • Trap is about space. Leave room for the 808 and the snare, and let the tune be the star.
  • Keep the melody in a different register from the 808: if they overlap, one of them disappears.
  • Add one layer at a time and listen before you add the next. Hi-hat rolls work best when they are rare.
  • Save a new version of your project before each big experiment, so you can always go back.
