The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a theme, a tempo from 130 to 150 BPM and the chords of their loop written on the Beat plan. If the tempo is only a guess, tap it: tap a steady beat for ten seconds and count the taps.

If you only have three classes

Merge Classes 2 and 3: build the beat and add the 808, rolls and riser on Class 2, then arrange and mix on Class 3 and use Class 4 for bouncing, presentations and reflection.

About this chart

The celesta tune from Act II of Tchaikovsky’s Nutcracker ballet (Variation II of the Pas de deux, No. 3 of the Op. 71a suite), first performed in 1892. This chart is score bars 5 to 12, with the pitches moved down an octave. Bars 1 and 3 start with an eighth rest (the celesta comes in on the “and”). The last chord, B7, leads straight back to Em, so the loop turns around. Chords shown are one idea that fits; try others by ear. Drums: pattern A, B or C (page 15).

Trap NutcrackerTrap effect cards · 1 of 2
STUDENT
Class 3

Five moves make a beat sound like trap: hi-hat rolls, an 808, a half-time snare, a flipped melody and a riser and drop. Use at least four. This page has the rolls and the 808; page 17 has the other three. Every move should leave room for the theme.

Hi-hat rolls · the trap signature

What it does. A roll is a burst of very fast hi-hat notes. Trap hats are steady eighths or sixteenths, with a roll of triplets or 32nd notes at the end of a bar or a phrase.

In Logic. Put the closed hat on its own pad. In the Piano Roll draw the hats on a fine grid (1/16, 1/16 triplet or 1/32), or in the Step Sequencer set the hat row’s rate to a faster value. A Note Repeater MIDI plug-in will repeat a held note. Ramp the velocities up (MIDI Draw or the velocity controls) so the roll swells.

Try. Roll only in the last beat of the bar, and only every fourth bar. Keep the hats much quieter than the snare.

Listen for. The hats speed up and lean into the next bar. If the whole beat is rolling, nothing stands out.

808 · the sub bass that is also a drum

What it does. A long, deep bass note that plays the chord roots and can glide from one to the next. It is felt more than heard.

In Logic. Make a Software Instrument track with Alchemy or Retro Synth and look in the Library (Y) for a sub bass or 808 sound, or build one: a sine oscillator, mono with Glide on, a long release. Play low notes (E1 to D2). Add a little Distortion or Overdrive so small speakers can hear it, and put a Compressor on it with its Side Chain set to the kick.

Try. One long root per chord. Cut each note a hair before the next kick. Glide only on the notes that change chord.

Listen for. Weight under everything. If it vanishes on a laptop, add distortion; if it is muddy, make the notes shorter.
