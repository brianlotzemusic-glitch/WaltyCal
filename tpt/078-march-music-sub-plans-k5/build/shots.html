Clap the NamesDay 2 · Speech rhythm: clap the syllables · no tech, no prep
DAY 2
Sub plan
GoalStudents clap the syllables of composers’ names and spring words and use them to build a four-beat chant.
You needPencils, crayons, the worksheets and the word-rhythm chart (page 7). No music reading needed: every word shows its claps.
CopiesK–2: page 14
3–5: page 15
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Steady pat
    Pat your legs slowly and evenly, like a clock. Say: “Copy me!” Students pat with you. Say: “This steady pulse is called the beat.”
  2. 10 minStep 2
    Clap the words
    Say a word, then say it again while clapping once for each syllable: spring (1 clap), Cla-ra (2 claps), Hil-de-gard (3 claps). Students echo. Try five or six words from page 7.
  3. 5 minStep 3
    Chart echoes
    Show page 7. Read a line out loud, one box per beat, while clapping the dots. One dot = ta, two dots in one box = ti-ti, shh = a silent beat (open hands). Students echo. K–2 use lines 1–4; 3–5 use all 8.
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Worksheet
    K–2 page 14, 3–5 page 15. Read Part A aloud and clap each word with the class before they color or write. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Chant parade
    Volunteers read their chant aloud while the class pats a steady beat. Everyone claps at the end.
Extra time? Name Clap from the quick games (page 6), with the names of students. Short class? Do only Part A and Part B.

Composer Spotlight ReaderDay 1 · Reading about March music and women composers · no tech, no prep
DAY 1
Sub plan
GoalStudents hear or read short facts about Music In Our Schools Month, three women composers, Irish music and spring, and answer questions about them.
You needPencils, crayons and the worksheets. Nothing else.
CopiesK–2: page 11
3–5: page 12
about 40 min
Step by step ·Italic lines are words you can say.
  1. 5 minStep 1
    Welcome and echo
    Say: “Good morning, musicians! I am your guest teacher today. Echo me!” Say each line and let the class repeat it: Ears are on. Eyes are up. Hands are still. Let’s start! Then say it once in a whisper.
  2. 5 minStep 2
    Opening question
    Say: “March is the month when schools celebrate music. It is also Women’s History Month, when we learn about women who changed the world.” Ask: Which composers or musicians do you already know? Take five answers. Any answer is fine.
  3. 10 minStep 3
    Read aloud
    K–2: read the short reading on page 11 aloud, slowly, once. Read it a second time and let students point to the words. 3–5: students read page 12 silently, then you ask: What was one new fact?
  4. 15 minStep 4
    Questions and drawing
    Hand out the worksheet: K–2 page 11, 3–5 page 12. Read the directions aloud. Walk around and help students find answers in the reading. Answers are on page 41.
  5. 5 minStep 5
    Share a picture
    Ask 3 or 4 volunteers to hold up their drawing and say one sentence about it. Clap for each artist.
Extra time? Try Whisper Countdown or Echo Beat from the quick games (page 6). Short class? Skip step 2; keep the reading and the questions.

Composer SpotlightA short reading about March
DAY 1
Grades K–2
NameDate
Your teacher reads the reading. Follow along with your finger. Then answer the questions.
March is the third month of the year. Every March, schools celebrate Music In Our Schools Month. March is also Women’s History Month, a time to learn about women who changed history. Hildegard of Bingen lived in Germany about 900 years ago, wrote music and led a community of nuns. Clara Schumann was a famous pianist who gave her first big concert when she was nine. Fanny Mendelssohn Hensel wrote more than 450 pieces of music, and she held concerts in her home. On March 17, many people celebrate St. Patrick’s Day, and Irish music often uses the harp, the fiddle and a drum called the bodhrán. Spring starts in March too, and the world gets a little louder with rain, birds and frogs.

March Music Sub PlansThe first 5 minutes
FOR THE SUB
Quick start
Short on time? These three things get you started in the first 5 minutes. Everything else is in the plan.
1
Pick the day
Look at the welcome sheet (page 3) for the day number for this class. Turn to that day’s plan page (they are listed on page 5). Each class gets a day it has not had yet.
2
Set up the room
Write the day’s title on the board. Hold up the class sign (page 9) if you like. Have pencils ready, and crayons for Days 1, 2, 3 and 6 (optional on Days 5 and 7). Count out one worksheet per student: K–2 or 3–5 as the plan says.
3
Say the first words
Stand where everyone can see you. Use the attention signal from the welcome sheet. Then say the first line of step 1 exactly as printed. Every step has words you can read aloud.
You do not need to ·
  • use any technology or electronics
  • know anything about music: you will clap, pat and say words
  • worry about supplies: pencils and crayons are nearly all the days need, plus a clear space to move for Day 4 and scrap paper to cover squares (optional) for Day 8
If something goes wrong: skip to the worksheet. Every day still works with only the worksheet and the words in step 1. Use “Short class?” at the bottom of each plan.

Spring FreezePicture cards · cut out or point
DAY 4
Move cards
Cut out the cards, or just point at them. Tap a pencil while students move; stop tapping and they freeze.
harp
Pluck pretend strings in the air, slow and gentle.
bodhrán
Tap a steady beat on your legs, like a bodhrán.
whistle
Hold a pretend whistle and wiggle your fingers.
fiddle
Hold a pretend fiddle and sweep your arm back and forth.
tulip
Crouch small, then slowly grow tall like a tulip.
raindrop
Wiggle your fingers and tap softly, like light rain.
bird
Flap your arms like wings and tiptoe around.
shamrock
Take three slow steps, then freeze.
