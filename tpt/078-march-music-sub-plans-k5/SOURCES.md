### 1. Source: https://www.womenshistory.org/womens-history/womens-history-month, https://vanderbilt.edu/innervu/?p=18967 and https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/why-the-us-celebrates-womens-history-month-every-march (web search 24 Nov 2026: Santa Rosa 1978; Congress backed a week from 1981; Public Law 100-9 in 1987 designated March; sources differ on the details of the campaign, so the product prints only 1978 for the local start and 1987 for Congress).

### 3. Hildegard of Bingen: born about 1098 near Bingen in what is now Germany, died September 17, 1179 in Bingen; a Benedictine abbess who led her community, composer of chants (about 69 of her musical works survive, printed as about seventy), and the author of books on nature and medicine (Day 1, Day 2, Day 5, Day 6, Day 8, facts page, bingo clue Hildegard)
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_of_Bingen (fetched 24 Nov 2026: born c. 1098, Bermersheim vor der Höhe; died 17 September 1179, Bingen am Rhein; Benedictine abbess and polymath; community elected her magistra in 1136; liturgical chants; about 69 musical works survive; medical texts Physica and Causae et Curae). ### 5. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Mendelssohn (fetched 24 Nov 2026: born 14 November 1805, Hamburg; died 14 May 1847, Berlin; over 450 compositions; Sunday concerts at the family home in Berlin, run by Fanny after 1831; most works unpublished in her lifetime; brother Felix born four years after her).

### 7. Irish traditional instruments: the harp is Ireland's emblem and appears on its coins; the fiddle looks like a violin; the tin whistle is a small pipe with finger holes that is blown (traditionally metal, today also plastic and wood, so the pages do not say 'metal'); the bodhrán is a single-headed frame drum covered with stretched skin, commonly struck with a stick called a tipper; the uilleann pipes are bagpipes named for the elbow-pumped bellows; the shamrock is a small three-leaf clover often linked with Ireland, and both the harp and the shamrock are listed as official State emblems by Ireland's Department of Enterprise (the pages call the harp the official emblem, which is true) (Day 1, Day 3, Day 4, Day 5, Day 8, facts page, bingo clues Harp, Bodhran, Whistle, Fiddle, Shamrock)
Source: https://www.ireland.com/en-nz/magazine/music/traditional-irish-music-instruments/ (fetched 24 Nov 2026: bodhrán is a large drum covered in stretched animal skin and struck with a stick; uilleann pipes are 'pipes of the elbow' with pump-operated bellows; Celtic harp is plucked; fiddle looks identical to a violin; the harp appears on Ireland's euro coins); https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/coins/html/ie.en.html and https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-the-taoiseach/publications/the-harp (web search 24 Nov 2026: the harp is the national emblem and is on Irish coins); https://scalar.usc.edu/works/music-in-global-america/traditional-music-and-folk-music-of-ireland (tin whistle, fiddle and uilleann pipes among the traditional instruments); https://time.com/5550579/shamrock-history/ (shamrock is a type of clover used as a symbol of Ireland; Ireland's national emblem is the harp).
