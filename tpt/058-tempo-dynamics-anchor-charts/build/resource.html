Teacher Notes

Dynamics & Tempo anchor charts: 12 posters, a reference sheet and a quiz

What is inside

Pages 3–14 hold 12 posters. Posters 1 to 6 each show one dynamic from pianissimo to fortissimo. Poster 7 is crescendo, poster 8 is decrescendo and diminuendo, and posters 9 to 12 show the tempo words in pairs: largo and adagio, andante and moderato, allegro and presto, accelerando and ritardando. The reference sheet is on page 15, the 10-question quiz on pages 16–17 and the key on page 18.

How to display

  • Print each poster on one US Letter page. For smaller charts, print two to a sheet from your printer settings.
  • Hang the dynamics posters 1 to 6 in a row from softest to loudest, with the crescendo and decrescendo posters under them.
  • Hang the tempo posters on another wall, slowest to fastest, so the order is part of the picture.
  • Prints well in black and white. The blue is one accent color.

Rotating word wall

Post two or three posters at a time. Each week add the next poster, move the oldest to a folder, and start class by asking students to point to the word that fits the day's song. Keep the reference sheet in student folders so every term stays in reach.

About the BPM ranges

BPM means beats per minute. The tempo posters give common ranges, such as largo about 40–60 and presto about 168–200. Books, apps and teachers use slightly different numbers, and a conductor may choose a speed inside or outside a range, so treat them as guides, not rules.

Games

  • Point and play. Point to a dynamics poster. The class says a short rhyme at that level.
  • Human crescendo. The class starts at pp and, row by row, grows to ff as you raise your hand.
  • Tempo walk. Set a metronome app to about 60, 90, 120 and 170 BPM. Students walk to the beat and name the tempo word.
  • Mystery poster. Cover a poster's heading. Students guess the term from the meaning.
  • Order race. Teams line up cards pp to ff, or largo to presto, as fast as they can.

Grades 2 to 4

  • Start with the six dynamics and the four words largo, andante, allegro and presto.
  • Use the posters orally: point, say, echo and move.
  • Give the quiz aloud or in small groups, and read each question to the class.

Grades 5 to 8

  • Add the symbols, abbreviations and all eight tempo words, plus the BPM ranges.
  • Students find three dynamic or tempo markings in their own band, choir or orchestra music and explain what they ask the player to do.
  • Give the quiz on its own as an assessment. Use the key on page 18 to score it out of 20 points.

A note on meanings

Mezzo means medium or half. Piano means soft and forte means loud, so mezzo piano is a little louder than piano and mezzo forte is a little softer than forte.
