Rhythm Warm-UpsWarm-up 1: ta and ti-ti
WARM-UP 1
Clap and say
Name Date

Teacher: lead the class to say the words, then clap and say ta and ti-ti.

Ta is one beat (a quarter note): snow, sled, hat, boot, ice, cold, frost. Ti-ti is two sounds in one beat (two eighth notes joined by a beam): sled-ding, co-coa, mit-tens, pen-guin, snow-man.

Rhythm Warm-UpsWarm-up 2: quarter rests
WARM-UP 2
Clap, say and shh
Name Date

Teacher: on a rest, say shh, keep your hands apart and tap the beat on your knees.

A quarter rest is one beat of silence. Say shh and do not clap. Every bar still has four beats, so count them all: 1 2 3 4.

Rhythm Warm-UpsWarm-up 3: half and whole notes
WARM-UP 3
Clap and hold
Name Date

Teacher: clap once on a half or whole note, then hold the sound and count the beats.

A half note lasts two beats: say ta-a. A whole note lasts four beats: say ta-a-a-a. Stretch the word to fill the beats: sno-ow, sno-o-o-ow.

Rhythm Warm-UpsWarm-up 4: rhythm echo chain
WARM-UP 4
Clap and echo
Name Date

Teacher: clap one bar, let the class echo it, then clap the whole line together.

Each chain mixes ta, ti-ti, shh, ta-a and ta-a-a-a. Say the syllables as you clap.

How to use the warm-ups

Use one page a day as a five-minute warm-up, or all four in one lesson. Say the words first, then the syllables, then clap. On the My turn staves, children draw their own two or four bars on the middle line, then clap them for a partner. These warm-ups do not cite standards; match them to your own music goals.
