What this is ·

A 7-class-period structured project for high school music technology. Each student picks a genre they do not already make, researches what defines it, studies 2–3 reference tracks, sketches a loop, composes a full track in that genre, mixes it to fit, reflects, and presents it with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (type: structured; focus: research and style), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Set up your Genre Research Google Form, the Presentation Padlet and the submission spot (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–22), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–10), Class 3 (slides 11–12), Class 4 (slides 13–14), Class 6 (slides 15–17) and Class 7 (slides 18–19). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, video, songs, artists or genres are named or included. Students choose their own genre and their own reference tracks, and make every sound in their projects themselves. For the Class 1 hook, you only ask questions aloud.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: my variations on page 6: a curated list of 5–8 genres (a blank for it is on page 10), or 2+ minutes with a full arrangement and 2 genre-specific mixing techniques.
  • Feedback board: my sheets use Padlet for the three questions; any class board works.
  • Any genre: the pages never name one, so students and you fill in your own.
  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 19).
In one sentence

Students study a genre they do not know until they can make a track that sounds like it, and then say what they learned.

Genre Exploration · Structured · Research & Style
Step outside your comfort zone and produce a track in an unfamiliar genre. Research, compose, and reflect.

What students make ·

One finished track in a genre the student does not already make, following the genre’s typical song form and conventions, with instruments, synths or samples that belong to it and genre-appropriate mixing and production. They complete the Genre Research Google Form first, and hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx project and a written reflection (one paragraph minimum), plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then present.

What this project reinforces ·

Builds analytical listening, research skills, and genre awareness. Forces students out of their default sound palette.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Research and identify the defining characteristics of an unfamiliar genre
  • Compose a track that authentically reflects the genre’s conventions
  • Apply genre-appropriate instrumentation, production techniques, and mixing
  • Reflect on the creative process and what you learned

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • The Genre Research Google Form. It is your own form: share the link on your class page. It is not included.
  • A way to stream reference tracks on student devices (my resource names Spotify, YouTube and Apple Music). The kit includes no track or link.
  • A place to submit the bounce, the zipped .logicx and the written reflection (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (yours to set up; none is included).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–22).

Before Class 1 ·

Share the Genre Research Google Form on your class page.
Set up the Presentation Padlet and, if you like, a shared class playlist (my resource).
Decide how students choose: free choice, or a short list from page 10 (my Easier variation).
Check that a student computer opens Logic and plays back through headphones.

What to watch (my closing note) ·

Students often surprise themselves by enjoying genres they thought they’d hate. Check in with students who expected to dislike their genre.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • Approve each genre on Class 1. If it is close to what the student already makes, ask what they would not normally reach for.
  • While students work, ask: is that the instrument, or the production style? Which reference sounds most like yours?
  • The Work plan (page 15) shows time management in the students’ own words.

Music they did not make ·

A kit suggestion

Reference tracks are for studying and comparing. Tell students to keep each reference on its own muted track in Logic, delete it before they bounce, and hand in only their own track. No one else’s audio goes into the project they submit.

Keep the volume moderate

Tell students to start with the headphones low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

What this project reinforces ·

Builds analytical listening, research skills, and genre awareness. Forces students out of their default sound palette.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students pick genres too close to what they already make.
  • Shallow research — require specific reference tracks and concrete elements.
  • Students nail the instruments but miss the production style.
  • Reflections are thin. Give specific prompts.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide a curated list of 5–8 genres with starter reference tracks and cheat sheets.

More Advanced: Require 2+ minutes with a full arrangement. Add a requirement for 2 genre-specific mixing techniques.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Genres too close to home: the genre check on the Genre research sheet (page 12) and your approval on Class 1.
  • Shallow research: the Reference track log (page 13) asks for tempo, form in bars, instruments and production.
  • Instruments right, production wrong: slides 8 and 9, and the Work plan’s mix-technique lines (page 15).
  • Thin reflections: the Reflection prompts (page 22), with a thin and a useful example.

  • Easier: a blank Genre shortlist for your 5–8 genres, starter tracks and cheat sheets (page 10).

Genre ExplorationLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Genre Exploration project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 3 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; check the labels on yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…: a Software Instrument track for MIDI sounds, an Audio track for recordings, a drum track for beats.
Set the tempoClick the tempo in the control bar and type the BPM you measured from your references. Tap Tempo (the tempo display’s menu) helps if you are not sure.
Bring in a referenceDrag an audio file you own or have the right to use onto its own Audio track, name it “REF” and mute it when you are not comparing. Delete it before you bounce. Only your own track is handed in.
Loop and arrangementBuild the 8–16 bar loop, then copy and paste regions to build sections. Name and color each section so the form is easy to see.
Software instrumentsOpen the Library (Y) to browse sounds by category and try instruments until the sound fits the genre. Drum Kit Designer and Drum Machine Designer give genre-specific drum kits.
Mixer: volume and panningPress X to open the Mixer. Move each track’s fader for volume and its Pan knob to place it.
EQ and filtersAdd Channel EQ to a channel strip. A high-cut or low-cut filter changes how bright or heavy a part sounds; cut what you do not need before you boost anything.
Reverb and effectsInsert Space Designer or ChromaVerb to give a part space; Delay and Compressor are also on the channel strip. Keep effects subtle until the rest of the mix is balanced.
AutomationPress A to show automation, choose a parameter such as volume or a filter, and draw changes over time.
Save versionsUse File › Save As… with a numbered name before each big change.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Genre ExplorationWorked example · an invented genre
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented genre, “Harbor Drift”, with three invented reference tracks, described on paper: no audio is included and no real genre is named. Students’ projects will differ.

1 · A genre card ·

Tempoabout 76–84 beats per minute
Rhythmsoft kick on beats 1 and 3, light brushed hi-hat in eighth notes, no heavy snare
Instruments and soundswide warm pads, a plucked bass, a bell-like lead; no distorted guitars
Song formintro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, a short bridge, chorus, outro
Production stylelots of space: reverb on the pads, the drums’ highs rolled off, the bass quiet and centered

2 · Three reference tracks, broken down ·

TrackTempoFormNotes
Reference Aabout 80 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 40 barsstarts with the pads alone
Reference Babout 84 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 48 barsdrums wait until the first verse
Reference Cabout 76 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 44 barsa short bridge before the last chorus

What they share: slow, spacious, the same section order, a short intro and a short bridge.

3 · The arrangement map: 2:36 ·

4 · Checking the numbers

80 beats per minute is 0.75 s a beat, so a bar of 4/4 is 3 s. Sections: 4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 = 52 bars, and 52 × 3 s = 156 s = 2:36. It follows the form the three references share, and each chorus brings back the lead.

5 · Instruments right, production wrong?

Weak: I used pads, a bass and a bell. It is Harbor Drift.

Strong: The instruments are only half of Harbor Drift. The drums’ highs are rolled off so they stay soft, the pads sit in a wide reverb while the bass stays dry and in the center, and the whole track is quiet and spacious like Reference A, B and C. I checked this by playing Reference A next to my track in Logic and matching the space.

6 · Two genre-specific mixing techniques (my Harder variation)
Roll off the drums’ highsA gentle low-pass filter on the drums keeps the hi-hat soft, as in all three references.
Wide reverb on the pads onlyA long reverb on the pads gives the space; the bass stays dry and centered so it is not washed out.

Three Padlet questions (invented): 1. Where did you first hear the track open up? 2. Which part would you turn up or down, and why? 3. Does it sound like the genre I described?

Genre Exploration

Digital Music Production | Structured Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

Step outside your comfort zone. Pick a musical genre you’re unfamiliar with, research what defines it, and produce a track that captures its signature sound. This project pushes you to listen deeper, experiment with new sounds, and grow as a producer.

Goals ·

  • Research and identify the defining characteristics of an unfamiliar genre
  • Compose a track that authentically reflects the genre’s conventions
  • Apply genre-appropriate instrumentation, production techniques, and mixing
  • Reflect on the creative process and what you learned

Key Vocabulary ·

GenreA category of music defined by shared characteristics like rhythm, instrumentation, and structure
Reference TrackA professionally produced song you study and use as a guide for your own production
InstrumentationThe specific instruments and sounds used in a piece of music
Song FormHow a song is structured — verse, chorus, bridge, etc.

  7. 7

Tips for Success ·

  • Commit to the genre fully. Don’t just add one instrument that “sounds kinda like it.”
  • Use reference tracks constantly. Pull them into Logic and compare your mix.
  • Don’t be afraid to sound weird to yourself — that’s the point.
  • Your Google Form research will save you time later.

What this page is

My sheet asks for a short written reflection, 1 paragraph minimum, on what you learned. A reflection is a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Answer the prompts below, then write your paragraph on your class page. Your teacher may take it orally instead.

Thin (an invented example)

I learned a lot. It was fun and I liked the genre more than I thought.

Useful (an invented example)

I thought I would hate slow, spacious music, but I liked it. The hard part was the mix: my first bounce had every part loud and dry, so it sounded like a different genre even with the right instruments. Comparing to Reference A showed me the drums needed their highs rolled off. Next time I would start with the pads and the space first, and I would check my references in the first class instead of the fourth. My strongest rubric category is Composition and Structure; my weakest is Mix.

1  What went well? Name something in your project you are proud of, and say why it worked.

2  What did you struggle with? Say what was hard and what you tried to fix it.

3  What would you do differently next time? Be specific: a step, a choice, a sound or a tool.

4  Look at the rubric (page 19). Which category are you proudest of, and which would you work on first next time?

5  What did you learn about the genre? Say what surprised you, and whether your opinion of it changed.
