Genre ExplorationWorked example · an invented genre
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented genre, “Harbor Drift”, with three invented reference tracks, described on paper: no audio is included and no real genre is named. Students’ projects will differ.

1 · A genre card ·

Tempoabout 76–84 beats per minute
Rhythmsoft kick on beats 1 and 3, light brushed hi-hat in eighth notes, no heavy snare
Instruments and soundswide warm pads, a plucked bass, a bell-like lead; no distorted guitars
Song formintro, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, a short bridge, chorus, outro
Production stylelots of space: reverb on the pads, the drums’ highs rolled off, the bass quiet and centered

2 · Three reference tracks, broken down ·

TrackTempoFormNotes
Reference Aabout 80 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 40 barsstarts with the pads alone
Reference Babout 84 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 48 barsdrums wait until the first verse
Reference Cabout 76 BPM4 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 8 + 4 = 44 barsa short bridge before the last chorus

What they share: slow, spacious, the same section order, a short intro and a short bridge.

3 · The arrangement map: 2:36 ·

4 · Checking the numbers

80 beats per minute is 0.75 s a beat, so a bar of 4/4 is 3 s. Sections: 4 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 = 52 bars, and 52 × 3 s = 156 s = 2:36. It follows the form the three references share, and each chorus brings back the lead.

5 · Instruments right, production wrong?

Weak: I used pads, a bass and a bell. It is Harbor Drift.

Strong: The instruments are only half of Harbor Drift. The drums’ highs are rolled off so they stay soft, the pads sit in a wide reverb while the bass stays dry and in the center, and the whole track is quiet and spacious like Reference A, B and C. I checked this by playing Reference A next to my track in Logic and matching the space.

6 · Two genre-specific mixing techniques (my Harder variation)
Roll off the drums’ highsA gentle low-pass filter on the drums keeps the hi-hat soft, as in all three references.
Wide reverb on the pads onlyA long reverb on the pads gives the space; the bass stays dry and centered so it is not washed out.

Three Padlet questions (invented): 1. Where did you first hear the track open up? 2. Which part would you turn up or down, and why? 3. Does it sound like the genre I described?

Requirements ·

Choose a genre you are NOT already comfortable producing in
Complete the Genre Research Google Form before composing
Your track should follow typical song forms and conventions of the genre
Use instruments, synths, or samples authentic to the genre
Apply genre-appropriate mixing and production techniques
Write a short reflection (1 paragraph minimum) on what you learned

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Research — Select your genre and complete the Google Form analyzing its key characteristics (Genre research sheet: page 12)
  2. 2
    Listen — Study 2–3 reference tracks and break down their elements (Reference track log: page 13)
  3. 3
    Sketch — Build an 8–16 bar loop that captures the genre’s essence (Loop and form sketch: page 14)
  4. 4
    Compose — Develop the full song structure. Layer instrumentation and refine. (Work plan: page 15)
  5. 5
    Mix — Apply genre-appropriate mixing techniques. Polish the track.
  6. 6
    Write your reflection and finalize your bounce (Reflection: page 22)
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.
