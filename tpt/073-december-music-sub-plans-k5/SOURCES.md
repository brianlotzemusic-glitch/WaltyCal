### 1. Winter solstice: December 21 or 22 in the Northern Hemisphere; least daylight, longest night; caused by Earth's tilt; opposite season in the Southern Hemisphere
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_solstice (fetched 23 Nov 2026: "21 or 22 December", "shortest period of daylight and longest night", tilt, six months later in the other hemisphere); NASA Watch the Skies post https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/watch-the-skies/2021/12/20/december-solstice-brings-winter-summer-seasons/ (tilt, shortest day in the northern half); 2026 date of Dec 21: https://www.space.com/winter-solstice (via search, 23 Nov 2026)

### 2. New Year's Eve: December 31, last day of the Gregorian year; many people count down to midnight; time zones, Pacific islands (Line Islands, Samoa, Tonga) among the first to greet the year; customs vary
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year%27s_Eve (fetched 23 Nov 2026)

### 3. Hanukkah: eight nights, starts 25 Kislev (Hebrew calendar), date on the usual calendar changes; rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem; nine-branch hanukkiah with a helper candle (shamash), one more candle each night; dreidel is a spinning-top game
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanukkah and https://history.com/news/8-things-you-should-know-about-hanukkah (verified for tpt/064, QA.md, 20 Nov 2026; re-fetched in QA 23 Nov 2026: eight nights, 25 Kislev, Maccabees recovered Jerusalem and the Second Temple, nine-branched candelabrum with a shamash). Dreidel as a four-sided spinning top played at Hanukkah: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreidel (fetched in QA 23 Nov 2026)

### 4. Christmas: December 25; Christians celebrate the birth of Jesus; many non-Christians take part in some customs; some Orthodox churches on the Julian calendar celebrate on January 7
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas (verified for tpt/064, QA.md, 20 Nov 2026; re-fetched in QA 23 Nov 2026: Dec 25, birth of Jesus Christ, many non-Christians celebrate culturally, Julian-calendar churches on Jan 7)

### 5. Kwanzaa: December 26 to January 1 (seven days); created in 1966 by Maulana Karenga; name from Swahili "matunda ya kwanza", first fruits; seven principles (Umoja, Kujichagulia, Ujima, Ujamaa, Nia, Kuumba, Imani) with their English meanings; kinara holds seven candles, one lit each day; karamu is a community feast
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwanzaa and https://www.history.com/articles/kwanzaa-history (verified for tpt/064, QA.md, 20 Nov 2026; Wikipedia page re-fetched in QA 23 Nov 2026: Dec 26 to Jan 1, Karenga 1966, matunda ya kwanza = first fruits, Nguzo Saba names and meanings as printed, kinara with seven candles one lit each day, karamu communal feast)

### 6. Las Posadas: nine nights, usually December 16 to 24; mainly Mexico and other Spanish-speaking communities; "posada" means lodging or inn; house-to-house processions, piñatas, a shared meal; customs vary
