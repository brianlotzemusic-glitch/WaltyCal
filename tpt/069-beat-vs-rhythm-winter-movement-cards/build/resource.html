Teacher Notes

Teacher notes: the big idea

Young children feel the steady beat in their bodies long before they can read a note. These cards connect the two ideas that every musician needs: the beat and the rhythm. Each card gives a winter movement, such as marching like a penguin or rolling a snowball, and asks children to move in one of two ways.

Beat

The beat is the steady pulse of the music, like a heartbeat. It stays the same speed. You can pat it, step it or tap it. Steady beat cards show a row of four dots: move on every dot.

Rhythm

Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds that fits on top of the beat. The words of a song have a rhythm, and so does your name. Rhythm cards show the pattern in notes: move to the words.

Beat and rhythm together

The dots are the beat. The notes sit on top of it. Count the dots, then say the words: ta, ta, ti-ti, ta.

Try this first (two minutes)

  • Put a hand on your chest and feel your heartbeat. Pat it gently on your knees. That is the beat.
  • Keep patting the beat and say “hot co-coa”. Clap only the words. That is the rhythm. “Hot” is one long sound (ta). “Co-coa” is two quick sounds (ti-ti).
  • Ask: “Which one stayed steady, the pat or the words?” The pat did. The words moved on top of it.

Same movement, two ways

Each of the twelve movements appears twice. First children feel it on every beat (cards 1–12). Later they move to a rhythm (cards 13–24). Clap a rhythm while the class pats the beat, so they hear the rhythm sit on top of the beat.

Teacher Notes

Teacher notes: games and pacing

Three games

1. Beat vs rhythm sorting game

Spread out all 24 cards. Cover the colored label at the top of each card with a sticky note. Children look at the bottom of the card: a row of dots means steady beat, and notes or rests mean rhythm. They place each card on the matching side of the sort mat (page 11), then lift the sticky note to check. Play in pairs or at a center.

2. Echo game

Keep a steady beat on your knees or a drum. Show one echo strip (pages 9–10). First say the syllables together (ta, ti-ti, rest). Then clap the strip while the class keeps the beat softly. Then clap it once and let the class echo you. Later a child can be the leader. Each strip has four beats, so the echo always fits in one bar.

3. Freeze game

Play a slow, steady beat. Children move the way a card says, one move on every beat. When the beat stops, everyone freezes like an icicle. With rhythm cards, children move once for each sound and freeze on the quarter rest, which is one beat of silence.

Pacing

LessonPlanPages
Lesson 1 (10–15 min)Feel the beat. Do the heartbeat activity from page 2, then try steady beat cards 1–6 and play the freeze game.4, 5
Lesson 2 (10–15 min)More beat movements. Steady beat cards 7–12. Let children take turns choosing a card.6
Lesson 3 (10–15 min)Meet rhythm. Read the guide together and say ta, ti-ti and rest. Try rhythm cards 13–18, then echo strips 1–6.4, 7, 9
Lesson 4 (10–15 min)Beat or rhythm? Rhythm cards 19–24 and echo strips 7–12. Play the sorting game, then use the checklist and the I can sheet.8, 10, 11–13

Tips

  • Start slowly, at about the speed of a calm walk. A steady beat matters more than a fast one.
  • For kindergarten, use steady beat cards 1–12 and rhythm cards 13–15, and echo only strips 1–4. For second grade, let children lead the games and write their own rhythm on the I can sheet.

Beat and rhythm, step by step

Beat = steady pulse

The beat is like a heartbeat. It is steady, like a clock. Move on every dot.

Rhythm = long and short sounds on top of the beat

Rhythm is the pattern of sounds. Say the words and move once for each sound. The beat keeps going underneath.

The three symbols on the cards

ta

quarter note

1 beat

ti-ti

two eighth notes, joined by a beam

1 beat, two quick sounds

rest

quarter rest

1 beat of silence

4/4: four beats in each bar

Every rhythm card and echo strip is one bar of four beats. A ta fills one beat. A ti-ti fills one beat. A rest fills one beat too. The numbers 1 2 3 4 under each strip count the beats.
