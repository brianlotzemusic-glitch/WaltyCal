HOLIDAY RHYTHM
I HAVE, WHO HAS
Two 24-card loop games · quarter, eighth and sixteenth notes
Grades 2–5 · 48 cards · 13 pages

Teacher Notes

Holiday Rhythm: I Have, Who Has · two games of 24 cards · grades 2–5

What this is. Two complete "I have, who has" loop games for the music room. Each card shows a one-bar rhythm in 4/4 and asks for another card's rhythm by its rhythm syllables. Students read notation, say the syllables and clap. Level 1 uses quarter notes, quarter rests, pairs of eighth notes and half notes. Level 2 adds sixteenth-note patterns. The holiday art is non-denominational: snowflakes, stars, ornaments and trees.

Getting ready. Print the cards on card stock and cut on the dashed lines. Level 1 cards are on pages 5–7 and Level 2 cards are on pages 9–11. Each level has its own teacher key with the loop order: page 8 for Level 1 and page 12 for Level 2. The card numbers are only for sorting. Card 1 is marked START and the card that closes the loop is marked END. The rules of play are on page 3.

Differentiation

  • Grades 2–3. Start with Level 1. Do the rhythm echo first, keep the beat for the class, and let students keep the syllables guide (page 4) beside them. Give a student a partner or a second look at the card before they answer.
  • Grades 4–5. Play Level 1 quickly as a warm-up, then move to Level 2. Remove the teacher beat so the class keeps its own, and challenge groups to close the loop with no mistakes. Ask students to explain how ti-ti-ka differs from ti-ka-ti when they are written and when they are clapped.

Assessment idea

While a loop is played, listen to each student clap and say their own "I have" rhythm. Mark a class list with a check for a steady beat, a check for syllables that match the notation, and a check for reading the "Who has" prompt correctly. For a quick written follow-up, say one prompt aloud and ask students to draw the rhythm in one bar of 4/4.

Standards

The games support the Performing and Responding processes of the National Core Arts Standards. Please match them to your own curriculum.

How to Play

Whole class, small groups, partners and the rhythm echo

Every card has an I have rhythm and a Who has prompt. The prompt spells out another card's rhythm in rhythm syllables, such as "ta ti-ti ta shh", so exactly one card matches it. Because the prompts form one loop, the class visits all 24 cards once and comes back to the start.

Before you play

Print the cards, cut on the dashed lines and keep each level in its own envelope. Level 1 cards are on pages 5–7 and Level 2 cards are on pages 9–11. Review the syllables on page 4 first. The loop order for each level is on the teacher keys, page 8 and page 12.

Whole class

1

Deal. Give each student one card from the same level. With fewer than 24 students, give some students two or three cards and have them answer for each. With more than 24, let students share a card as partners.

2

Start. The student holding card 1, the START card, reads the Who has prompt aloud in rhythm syllables, keeping a steady beat.

3

Answer. Everyone checks the I have rhythm on their own card. The student whose rhythm matches stands, claps it while saying the syllables, and then reads their own Who has prompt.

4

Finish. Play continues until the END card reads its prompt, which names the START card. The START student then claps their own I have rhythm, and the loop is closed after all 24 cards.

Small groups

Use groups of 4 to 6 students and one set of 24 cards per group. Deal every card, so each student holds several. Play exactly like the whole class game. Groups can race to close their loop with no mistakes, or try for the steadiest beat.

Partner version

Partners share one set. Spread all 24 cards face up in any order. Partner A reads the Who has prompt on the START card. Partner B finds the card with that I have rhythm, claps and says it, and lays it next in the row, then reads its prompt. Switch roles after each card. The chain is done when the END card is placed, and the partners check their row against the teacher key.

Rhythm echo variation

Before a student reads their Who has prompt, they clap their I have rhythm one time without speaking. The whole class echoes it by clapping the same rhythm, and then everyone says the syllables together. Then the student reads the prompt and play moves on. The echo works well with body percussion too: pat, snap or stamp instead of clapping. A slow steady beat of about 60 beats per minute works well at first.

Rhythm Syllables Guide

How to say and clap every rhythm in the games
NotationNameLengthSayLevel
Quarter note1 beattaLevel 1
Quarter rest (silence)1 beatshhLevel 1
Two eighth notes, joined by a beam1 beat togetherti-tiLevel 1
Half note2 beatsta-aLevel 1
Four sixteenth notes, joined by two beams1 beat togetherti-ka-ti-kaLevel 2
Eighth note then two sixteenth notes1 beat togetherti-ti-kaLevel 2
Two sixteenth notes then an eighth note1 beat togetherti-ka-tiLevel 2

Clapping and speaking

  • Steady beat first. In 4/4 each bar has four beats and a quarter note gets one beat. Count "1 2 3 4" or pat the beat while you say the syllables.
  • Clap every note. Clap when a note starts. A rest is silence: say "shh" with your hands apart. Clap a half note once and hold your hands together for two beats while you say "ta-a".
  • Beams. Eighth notes joined by a beam share one beat, so each is half a beat. Sixteenth notes have two beams and four of them share one beat, so each is a quarter of a beat.
  • Reading the card. The notes sit on a one-line rhythm staff and every stem points up. The mark at the start is the neutral clef, used for rhythm without pitches, and 4/4 means four quarter-note beats in each bar.
