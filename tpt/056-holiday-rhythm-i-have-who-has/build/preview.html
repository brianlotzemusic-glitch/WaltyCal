How to Play

Whole class, small groups, partners and the rhythm echo

Every card has an I have rhythm and a Who has prompt. The prompt spells out another card's rhythm in rhythm syllables, such as "ta ti-ti ta shh", so exactly one card matches it. Because the prompts form one loop, the class visits all 24 cards once and comes back to the start.

Before you play

Print the cards, cut on the dashed lines and keep each level in its own envelope. Level 1 cards are on pages 5–7 and Level 2 cards are on pages 9–11. Review the syllables on page 4 first. The loop order for each level is on the teacher keys, page 8 and page 12.

Whole class

1

Deal. Give each student one card from the same level. With fewer than 24 students, give some students two or three cards and have them answer for each. With more than 24, let students share a card as partners.

2

Start. The student holding card 1, the START card, reads the Who has prompt aloud in rhythm syllables, keeping a steady beat.

3

Answer. Everyone checks the I have rhythm on their own card. The student whose rhythm matches stands, claps it while saying the syllables, and then reads their own Who has prompt.

4

Finish. Play continues until the END card reads its prompt, which names the START card. The START student then claps their own I have rhythm, and the loop is closed after all 24 cards.

Small groups

Use groups of 4 to 6 students and one set of 24 cards per group. Deal every card, so each student holds several. Play exactly like the whole class game. Groups can race to close their loop with no mistakes, or try for the steadiest beat.

Partner version

Partners share one set. Spread all 24 cards face up in any order. Partner A reads the Who has prompt on the START card. Partner B finds the card with that I have rhythm, claps and says it, and lays it next in the row, then reads its prompt. Switch roles after each card. The chain is done when the END card is placed, and the partners check their row against the teacher key.

Rhythm echo variation

Before a student reads their Who has prompt, they clap their I have rhythm one time without speaking. The whole class echoes it by clapping the same rhythm, and then everyone says the syllables together. Then the student reads the prompt and play moves on. The echo works well with body percussion too: pat, snap or stamp instead of clapping. A slow steady beat of about 60 beats per minute works well at first.
