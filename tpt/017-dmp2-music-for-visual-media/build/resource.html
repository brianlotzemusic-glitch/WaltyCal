What this is ·

A 7-class-period scoring project for high school music technology. Each student chooses a 2–3 minute video clip and composes an original soundtrack for it in Logic Pro: music that matches the mood, pacing and emotional arc of the picture, with at least some elements timed to specific visual moments, called hit points. It is an open-ended project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (focus: scoring and sync), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Decide how students will find a clip they may use (page 4): no video is included.
  3. Print the student pages (pages 13–23), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–11), Class 3 (slide 12), Class 4 (slide 13), Class 6 (slides 14–16) and Class 7 (slides 17–18). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No video or audio included

No video, audio, films, games or songs are included. Students use their own clip, or one they may use from a site such as YouTube or Vimeo, and choose it themselves (page 4), so you know exactly where every picture came from.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Clips: students may film their own, so the project also works with no downloads at all.
  • Easier or harder: my variations on page 6.
  • Feedback board: my sheets use Padlet for the three questions; any class board works.
  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 21).

What students make ·

One Logic Pro score for a 2–3 minute video clip, with at least some musical elements timed to the picture and, if they like, sound effects. They hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx project, and a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then show the video with their music and talk through their choices.

What this project reinforces ·

This connects music production to real-world application in film, TV, games, and advertising. Builds synchronization skills, emotional storytelling through music, and arrangement for narrative purposes.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): refining original work (MU:Cr3.1), presenting it (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Compose music that serves a visual narrative
  • Develop synchronization skills — timing music to visual cues
  • Explore mood, atmosphere, and pacing through musical choices
  • Optionally incorporate sound effects to enhance the audio-visual experience

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student (a current version), and headphones. Speakers help for the final check that the music and the picture feel right together.
  • A video clip for every student: their own footage, or one they may use. Nothing is included.
  • A place to submit an .mp3 or .wav and a zipped .logicx project (your class page).
  • A feedback board for the three questions (my sheets use Padlet).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 13–23).

Before Class 1 ·

Decide how clips will be chosen: your own footage, ones students find, or a short list you provide (my easier variation).
Read the terms of the site students will use, so you can tell them what they may do with a clip.
Pick one short scene you may show, for slide 2: first with the sound off, then with its score.
Print the student pages; set up the submission spot and the feedback board.

Choosing a clip ·

My project says: your own, or from YouTube or Vimeo. Page 13 is the student log for either.

  • Your own footage. A phone video, a school project, or screen capture of a game the student plays. No source questions, and the student already knows the story.
  • A clip you give them. Provide 3–5 clips you may use and let students choose, as my easier variation says.
  • A clip they find. Look for sites and licenses that allow reuse, and have the student write the source on the Clip log.
Five checks for any clip
  • Does it run 2–3 minutes, with 1–2 of them worth scoring?
  • Does it have a clear mood a student can name?
  • Does it have a few obvious moments to hit?
  • Is it simple enough (one place, a few cuts)?
  • Does the student know where it came from?
Rights and credit

Clips belong to their makers. Scoring a clip for a class exercise is different from posting the finished video online. Check the clip site’s terms before anyone posts publicly, and credit the maker. This kit gives no legal advice and includes or links to no clip.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

This connects music production to real-world application in film, TV, games, and advertising. Builds synchronization skills, emotional storytelling through music, and arrangement for narrative purposes.

Common student struggles ·

  • Students pick clips that are too long or too complex. Encourage 1–2 minutes of actual scored content within the 2–3 minute window.
  • Students compose music they like rather than music that fits the video. Keep redirecting to the visuals.
  • Synchronization is hard. Show them how to use markers in Logic and count frames.
  • Students forget about dynamic range — everything is the same volume the whole time.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide 3–5 pre-selected video clips to choose from. Shorter clips (60–90 seconds) reduce overwhelm.

More Advanced: Require at least 3 specific sync points. Add a requirement for original sound effects alongside the score.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Clips too long or complex: slide 6, the clip checks on page 4 and the Clip log (page 13).
  • Music they like, not music that fits: the Watch notes (page 14) and the Score plan (page 15) start from the picture.
  • Sync is hard: the markers demo (slide 8), the sync guide (page 10) and the Cue sheet (page 16).
  • Same volume the whole time: slide 11 and the dynamics row of the Score plan (page 15).

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Technical Skill: ask the student to point to three markers in the Marker track and say which moment each one marks.

Music for Visual MediaLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Everything students need in Logic for this project, in one table. Demonstrate markers (slide 8) and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions.

Open the clipFile › Movie › Open Movie, then choose the video file. The picture shows in a movie window; the clip’s own sound arrives as a track you can mute.
Frame rateProject Settings › Synchronization › Frame Rate. Match the video’s rate (often 24, 25 or 30) so markers and timecode line up.
Read the timeSwitch the LCD to show SMPTE time (Control-click the display for the choices): hours:minutes:seconds:frames.
MarkersView › Show Global Tracks, then use the Marker track. Stop on a key moment, add a marker at the playhead and give it a name, such as “pop” or “cut”.
Set the tempoClick the tempo in the LCD and type a number, or draw changes on the Tempo global track. A bar is 4 × 60 ÷ tempo seconds (page 10).
Snap and gridSet Snap to Bar, Beat or Division, so regions land on the grid. Turn Snap off to nudge by smaller steps.
Software instrumentsClick an empty track, choose Software Instrument, and pick a sound in the Library. Start with one pad or bass as the backbone, then layer in detail.
Volume automationPress A to show automation, choose Volume and draw swells and dips. This is how a score gets quiet before a big moment.
Sound effectsRecord your own sounds, or build effects with a software instrument. Place each one at a marker.
Mute the clip’s audioMute or delete the video’s own audio track, so the original soundtrack does not play under yours.
BounceFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. File › Export › Audio to Movie puts the music on the video, or you can play the video and the bounce together.
Zip the projectIn Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

SYNC GUIDE
Teacher + student

My notes say sync is hard: show students how to use markers and count frames. This page is the explanation to project or print. The numbers use an invented scene at 120 BPM in 4/4.

Seconds and bars ·

BPMOne beatOne barBars/min
80750 ms3.00 s20.0
90667 ms2.67 s22.5
100600 ms2.40 s25.0

One bar in 4/4 = 4 × 60 ÷ BPM seconds. A hit at time t is in bar t ÷ (bar length) + 1.

Counting frames ·

VideoOne frame
24 frames per second41.7 ms
25 frames per second40.0 ms
30 frames per second33.3 ms

Timecode is hours:minutes:seconds:frames. At 24 fps, 0:17.5 is 00:00:17:12, or frame 420. Match the project’s frame rate to the video’s.

Four ways to hit a moment ·

On the beatThe hit lands exactly on a beat or the start of a bar. Easiest to feel; use it for the big moments.
Just before itA short run-up or pickup leads into the moment, so the music arrives as the picture does.
A stingerOne short sound on the exact frame, such as a bell, a hit or a rise, over a quieter bed of music.
SilenceThe music stops, so the picture speaks alone. Often the strongest hit of all.

Mood: first ideas ·

Tenseslow pulse, low register, sparse, notes held and unresolved
Joyfulmajor key, brighter register, steady and light
Mysteriousquiet, lots of space, unusual sounds, reverb
Sadslow, minor key, soft, simple melody
Excitedfast, rhythmic, rising lines, getting louder

Rules of thumb, not laws. Trust your ears and the picture.

Music for Visual MediaComposing to picture
TEACHER + STUDENT
Classes 1-2

From the teacher’s own materials: the advice I give my own students when they score a picture for the first time. Project it, or hand it out on Class 1 or 2, next to the Watch notes (page 14) and the Cue sheet (page 16).

Composing to picture ·

Watch before you buildPlay the clip twice without touching Logic. What moments stand out? Where does the energy change? What would a sound do to that moment? Write notes before you open a single plug-in.
Mark your momentsAdd markers in Logic at the timestamps where you want something to happen. A footstep. A sudden silence. A swell. Having the map in advance stops you from improvising past the moments that matter.
Silence is a toolIf nothing is playing for a moment and then a sound enters, the entry feels enormous. Silence is not dead air; it is tension. Use it deliberately.
Let the picture breatheYou do not need to fill every second with sound. Sparse music over a quiet visual can be more unsettling than a wall of noise. Less is often more here.
Match the energyIf the picture gets more intense, your music should respond. Build toward moments of tension and release from them.
Try this in Class 1

Play a scene you may show to the class with no comment. Then ask: what would you expect to hear if this were a film? Take answers aloud before anyone opens Logic.

Overview ·

Score a video. Choose a 2–3 minute video clip and compose an original soundtrack in Logic Pro that complements the visuals. Your music should match the mood, pacing, and emotional arc of the video — and at least some elements should be timed to specific visual moments.

Goals ·

  • Compose music that serves a visual narrative
  • Develop synchronization skills — timing music to visual cues
  • Explore mood, atmosphere, and pacing through musical choices
  • Optionally incorporate sound effects to enhance the audio-visual experience

Key Vocabulary ·

ScoringWriting music designed to accompany and enhance visual media
Synchronization (Sync)Aligning musical events with specific visual moments on screen
MoodThe emotional tone that music creates — tense, joyful, mysterious, etc.
Sound DesignCreating or selecting sound effects that enhance the visual experience
Hit PointA specific moment in a video where the music emphasizes a visual event

Requirements ·

Choose a 2–3 minute video clip (your own or from YouTube/Vimeo)
Compose a fully formed piece of music that complements the visuals

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Choose your video — Find a 2–3 minute clip that inspires you. Import it into Logic. (Clip log: page 13)
  2. 2
    Study the video — Watch it multiple times. Note the mood, pacing, and key visual moments. (Watch notes: page 14)
  3. 3
    Plan your score — Decide where the music builds, drops, shifts, and hits visual cues. (Score plan: page 15; cue sheet: page 16)
  4. 4
    Compose — Build your score in Logic. Start with the backbone and layer in detail.
  5. 5
    Sync and refine — Fine-tune timing to the visuals. Add sound effects if desired. (Sync log: page 17)
  6. 6
    Finalize — Bounce your audio and prepare the video with your music for presentation
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.

Tips for Success ·

  • Watch the video at least 5 times before you start composing. You need to know it inside and out.
