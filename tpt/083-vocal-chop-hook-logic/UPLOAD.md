## 2. Description

```
Vocal chop project for high school music technology: students record ONE short hummed phrase of their own voice (2 to 4 seconds, no words, no existing melody) and chop it into a hook in Quick Sampler (if their version has it). They slice the hum into 8 or 16 pieces, assign the slices to keys, play a 4-bar chop pattern on a 16-step grid, pitch pieces by semitone, try a reversed slice, add a simple drum groove and bass, and build an 8- or 16-bar arrangement with an intro, a hook, a drop and an outro. Timing math runs through every step: one bar of 4/4 = 240 ÷ BPM seconds, one 16th-note step = 15 ÷ BPM seconds (0.125 seconds at 120 BPM), and a 3-second hum cut into 8 equal slices gives slices of 0.375 seconds. Pitch is arithmetic too: the ratio for n semitones is 2^(n/12), so +12 is × 2.000, +7 is × 1.498 and −5 is × 0.749. The theme is a winter morning (wind at the window, boots on snow, a kettle starting to sing) as the hum's inspiration. This Winter Vocal Chop project takes 3 class periods (the pacing guide also shows two and four) and is an original Hudson Beat project; the steps are written for Logic Pro, while the ideas (slices, step grids, semitones, arrangement) carry over to GarageBand and other music software.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (ZIP: 26-page PDF = 1 cover + 9 teacher + 15 student + 1 terms page, plus 17-slide intro deck as .pptx and PDF)
• Teacher pages: how to use, project overview, setup, a 3-class pacing guide with the deck slides for each class, two pages of teacher notes (struggles, variations, resources, grading), a Logic quick reference and a two-page worked example (an invented hook at 120 BPM: a 3-second hum, 8 slices of 0.375 seconds, a 4-bar pattern with 26 hits, three pitch choices and a 16-bar arrangement of 32 seconds) (9 pages)
• Student pages: a timing page (seconds per beat, step and bar at six tempos, 4-bar and 16-bar lengths, and slice lengths for hums of 2 to 4 seconds), a recording checklist with a copyright note and take log, a slice map (a waveform with slice lines, a keyboard map and a slice table), a 4-bar chop pattern step grid with a blank grid to fill in, a pitch table (semitone offsets with ratios), a drum groove and bass page, an arrangement map with 5 layers, a mix checklist, the 3-page project sheet (overview, goals, vocabulary, requirements, steps, tips, submission checklist), the /25 rubric, a vocabulary page, a feedback planner and a reflection page (15 pages)
• Teacher-presented intro deck, 17 slides with speaker notes on every slide: a think-and-share hook, what the project is, the slices, the timing math, the keyboard map, the recording checklist, chopping in Quick Sampler, the step grid, pitch by semitone, groove and bass, the arrangement, what to turn in and the rubric

THE THREE CLASS PERIODS
• Class 1: hook; the timing math and a tempo; recording the hum; slicing it and mapping the slices
• Class 2: finish the slices; play a 4-bar chop pattern on the step grid; pitch slices by semitone and try a reversed slice
• Class 3: add a drum groove and bass, arrange 8 or 16 bars, mix, bounce, present and reflect

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN
• Timing math: seconds per bar in 4/4 = 240 ÷ BPM; seconds per step = 15 ÷ BPM; 16 steps make a bar; slice length = hum length ÷ number of slices
• Sampling: recording a clean source in their own voice, cutting it into slices (transient, equal or manual, if your version has them), playing slices from keys and reading a pattern as a step grid
• Pitch: 12 semitones make an octave; ratio = 2^(n/12); intervals add and ratios multiply
• Arranging: intro, hook, drop and outro, layers that build, and a mix that keeps the chops clear

GOOD TO KNOW
• There is no audio in this resource and no student work. No melody, song or lyrics are printed. Students record their OWN voice; the kit tells them not to sample commercial recordings. They make every sound themselves in Logic Pro
• Requires Logic Pro on a Mac with Quick Sampler. A microphone is recommended, and a classmate can hum with permission; nobody has to read music. Where a control may differ between versions, the pages say "if your version has it" and describe controls generically
• All recording levels and mixing numbers are starting points to set by ear, not rules
• Rubric: 5 categories scored 1-5, /25; the final grade is the average of the student's and the teacher's score
• Grades 9-12; music technology or music production classes
```

## 9. Sources and credits

All sources were fetched on 25 Nov 2026 unless a line says "could not". Recording and mixing numbers are starting points, and the product says so.

- **Semitone, octave and the ratio 2^(1/12).** English Wikipedia, "Semitone" (page read): the equal-tempered semitone is 2^(1/12), about 1.05946 (100 cents), "one twelfth of an octave"; 12 semitones per octave; an octave is a 2:1 ratio. The page does not give the fifth's decimal: 2^(7/12) = 1.49831 and every other ratio in the table are arithmetic, computed in `gen.py --check` by repeated multiplication of 2^(1/12) and again with 12 x log2(ratio) = n. Pitch table: −12 = x0.500; −7 = x0.667; −5 = x0.749; 0 = x1.000; +3 = x1.189; +4 = x1.260; +5 = x1.335; +7 = x1.498; +12 = x2.000. The interval names (octave, fifth, fourth, thirds) are standard music-theory vocabulary, not sourced here.
- **A 16th note and steps per bar.** English Wikipedia, "Sixteenth note" (page read): a sixteenth note is 1/16 of a whole note, half an eighth note. The page does not state the counts per beat or per bar; 4 per quarter-note beat and 16 per bar of 4/4 follow from the definition. Seconds per beat = 60 / BPM, per step = 15 / BPM and per bar = 240 / BPM, and every table, are arithmetic computed in `gen.py --check`, exactly with fractions and with an independent float method. At 120 BPM a step is 0.125 s.
- **Sampler, slicing and keys.** English Wikipedia, "Sampler (musical instrument)" (page read): a sampler is an electronic musical instrument that records and plays back samples; samples can include speech and other sounds; keyboard tracking shifts a sample's pitch in semitones relative to its center pitch. The page does not describe slicing by name.
- **Quick Sampler's Slice mode (transient, equal divisions, beat divisions, manual).** Apple, "Quick Sampler Slice mode in Logic Pro for Mac" (support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/slice-mode-lgcp18515788/mac): **could not read the page body** (the fetch returned only the User Guide's navigation); the three marker modes and the sensitivity and slice-count controls were seen only in a search summary. Apple's "Quick Sampler" overview page could not be read either. The kit therefore says "if your version has it", names the modes generically (transient, equal, manual), invents no menu paths and promises no key layout, and tells students to play each key and write down which slice it plays. Logic's command that converts an audio region to a sampler instrument was seen only as a table-of-contents entry in the User Guide; the kit hedges it too.
- **Voice peaks around −12 to −6 dBFS.** musicstudioinsights.com, "Best dB Level for Recording Vocals: Peaks & Headroom" (page read): loud vocal peaks between −12 and −6 dBFS when recording at 24-bit, as "a practical starting point, not a strict limit"; a take peaking at −12 dBFS can be raised later; a clipped take cannot be repaired by turning it down; 0 dBFS is the digital ceiling. musosoup.com, "How to Record Vocals at Home" (page read): average around −18 dBFS with peaks near −10 dBFS, so the sources differ; the kit calls −12 to −6 a starting point and says a quieter recording is safe.
- **Mic distance, pop filter, quiet room.** musosoup.com (page read): the microphone 10 to 30 cm from your mouth, a pop filter about 2 to 3 inches (5 to 8 cm) in front of the microphone, soft furnishings in a larger room. The kit says "about 10 to 30 cm is this kit’s starting range" and "try two distances and listen", mentions a pop filter only as "if there is one", and gives no pop filter distance. A "hand's width" rule of thumb is not used because no page read states it. The Shure "vocal miking tips" page was fetched and returned no content about distance, so Shure is not cited.
- **Example values (not from any source).** The 3-second hum, the 120 BPM tempo, the model chop pattern (6, 6, 6 and 8 hits), which slices are pitched and by how much, the 200 Hz example hum, the drum groove (kick 3, snare 2, hat 8), the bass offsets (0, 0, −5, −7), the arrangement and every invented time are example values chosen for this kit and labeled as examples. The claim that a higher pitch plays a sample faster and so shortens it is how many samplers work, stated as "many samplers" and "if the sampler plays it faster", not as a fact about Quick Sampler.
- **No existing music.** Nothing is notated or printed. No songs, artists or recordings are named. Students record their own voice and are told not to sample commercial recordings.
- **Art:** the cover, previews and deck use the owner's pre-made art (`art/hero.png`: a studio microphone, a laptop showing an abstract waveform, monitors, headphones, an audio interface and a pad controller, no screen text), copied whole from `tpt/_art/next/winter-vocal-chop/`; logged in `tpt/_art/USAGE.md`.
