What this is ·

A 3-class-period radio ident project for high school music technology. Every student makes a short ident of 15 to 30 seconds for an invented station: a voice, an original music bed and sound effects, recorded, cleaned up with EQ and compression, and mixed with the music ducked under the words. Students plan the timing with simple arithmetic, present the result and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Look at the tempo table, scripts, signal chain and dB ladder (pages 11–14) and the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–26), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–8), Class 2 (slides 9–11) and Class 3 (slides 12–16). The slide about the three classes carries the badge for all three. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

OVERVIEW
Radio ident project
The project in one line

Holiday Radio Ident · Voice, music bed and effects · Logic Pro
One invented station, twenty seconds, a voice that is always clear.

What students make ·

An ident of 15 to 30 seconds, built in Logic Pro: a voice that reads a script of about 24 to 30 words, an original music bed that fills 8, 10 or 12 bars, and at least two sound effects such as a sleigh-bell-like shaker, wind or a door chime. The voice is recorded with a pop filter and levels that peak around −12 to −6 dBFS, cleaned up with a high-pass filter and a gentle compressor, and the music is ducked under it. Students hand in a bounced file and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the ident and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a voice spot is made. Students learn to plan a short piece with timing arithmetic, to fit a script to a time, to record a voice with sensible levels, to shape it with a high-pass filter and a compressor, and to lower music under words with sidechain compression or automation, all in Logic Pro.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Plan a 20-second ident: use one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds and tempo = 12 × bars to choose a tempo, and place the voice, music and effects on a cue sheet
  • Write a script that fits its time: about 150 words per minute is 2.5 words per second, so a 12-second read is about 30 words
  • Record a clean voice: microphone distance, a pop filter and levels that peak around −12 to −6 dBFS, and read the dB ladder
  • Process the voice with a high-pass filter and gentle compression, then duck the music under it by about 6 to 12 dB, with sidechain compression or automation

About the theme ·

A radio ident is the short sound logo and spot that a station plays between songs: a voice says the station’s name and a promise, a music bed carries it, and a few sound effects set the scene. This project is a winter-season station, but it suits any class: students may invent a station for any season. Every station name in this kit is invented, and none uses call letters.

The skills here are the ones every voice track needs: record cleanly, shape the voice with EQ and compression, and lower the music under the words. They carry over to podcasts, announcements, video and school broadcasts.

SETUP

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones.
  • A microphone and a pop filter, shared if you must. A quiet corner with soft surfaces beats a loud room. A laptop’s built-in microphone will do for practice.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav files, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The tempo table, scripts, dB ladder and cue sheet, printed (pages 11–14 and 18), or shown on the projector.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–26).

Before Class 1 ·

Read the tempo table (page 11) so you can answer ‘how many bars is 20 seconds?’ at 96, 120 and 144 BPM.
Test a microphone in Logic Pro on one computer, and read a line while watching the meter (page 14).
Open Logic Pro on a student computer and check which tools your version has: the Loop Browser, Session Players, Channel EQ, Compressor with a Side Chain menu (see page 8).
Read the worked example (pages 9–10).

What to watch ·

Watch for a voice that clips or is much too quiet, a microphone that moves between takes, a bed that is already loud under the words, and a duck that starts late or snaps back. A good listening day: play each ident and ask the class two questions. Could you hear every word? When did you notice the music move? If a word is lost, find it on the cue sheet together. This kit names no songs or artists.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: what does this setting change? Could you hear every word?
  • Use only loops and sounds that come with Logic, and no existing songs, jingles or station names. Students invent a station and do not use real call letters.
  • A student who is stuck can build the voice, a short bed and the final chime only: those three already make an ident.

The voice ·

The voice can be the student’s own, a classmate’s with permission, or a read of the script recorded by anyone who agrees. Nobody has to record themselves. Use only sounds students made or have the right to use.

Keep the volume moderate

Students listen to their own voice over and over in headphones. Tell them to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a voice spot is made. Students learn to plan a short piece with timing arithmetic, to fit a script to a time, to record a voice with sensible levels, to shape it with a high-pass filter and a compressor, and to lower music under words with sidechain compression or automation, all in Logic Pro.

Common student struggles ·

  • The voice sounds boomy or hissy: check the distance (10 to 20 cm), keep it steady and look at the high-pass filter before anything else (page 16).
  • The recording clips or is very quiet: lower or raise the input gain at the interface, not the track fader, and read the peak against the dB ladder (page 14).
  • The music still covers the words: deepen the duck by a few dB, check that the ramp starts half a second early, and listen again (page 17).
  • The ident runs long: count the words (about 2.5 per second), then check the bars and the cue sheet (pages 12 and 18).

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Keep the tempo at 120 BPM, where 20 seconds is exactly 10 bars and every cue lands on a beat (page 18); use one of the three script templates, and duck the music with volume automation only.

More Advanced: Build a 15-second and a 30-second version of the same ident (page 11), or duck with sidechain compression and then compare it with an automated duck. Or add a second voice, a tagline that comes back at the end.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • A voice that sounds boomy or hissy: the EQ and compression page (page 16) lists the starting points, and the signal chain (page 13) covers distance and the pop filter.
  • Levels: the dB ladder (page 14) gives the amplitude at each level, and the worked example (pages 9–10) shows a full set of levels.
  • Music that covers the words: the ducking page (page 17) shows both methods with depth and ramp numbers.
  • An ident that runs long: the script page (page 12) gives word counts and read times, and the cue sheet (page 18) the seconds, bars and beats.

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 23). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Voice & Recording (rubric row 1): listen for pops and hiss, check the peak on the meter against the dB ladder and count the script words (pages 14 and 12).
  • Music Bed & Effects (rubric row 2): check that the bed is original, that its length matches the bars and that each effect sits on a cue (page 15).
  • EQ & Compression (rubric row 3): open the plug-ins, find the high-pass filter, and ask the student to say what each change did (page 16).
  • Ducking & Mix (rubric row 4): play the ident with the voice solo and with it mixed, and look at the duck depth and ramp (pages 17 and 19).
  • Plan & Reflection (rubric row 5): the tempo table work, the cue sheet, the feedback questions and the Reflection page (page 26) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 26): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Plan & Reflection row of the rubric.

Holiday Radio IdentLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Holiday Radio Ident is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

Project and tempoFile › New…, then an Audio track to record the voice and a Software Instrument track for the bed. Set the project tempo in the control bar to 96, 120 or 144 BPM and keep the time signature at 4/4. The timing arithmetic only works at that tempo, so set it first. Set a cycle from bar 1 to the bar after your last bar to hear the whole ident.
Record the voiceOn the Audio track, choose the microphone or interface as the input and click the record-enable button (R). Watch the track’s level meter while you read the loudest line: set the gain on the interface, not the fader, so the peaks land around −12 to −6 dBFS. Keep a pop filter in front of the microphone, and re-record a take that clips.
Music bedOpen the Loop Browser (the loop icon in the toolbar) and search for drums, bass, keys or pads in a winter mood. Loops follow the project tempo. Or add a Session Player such as the Drummer, if your version has them, or draw a simple pattern in the Piano Roll on an Alchemy or Retro Synth patch. Make the bed as long as your bars: 8, 10 or 12.
Sound effectsSearch the Loop Browser or the patch browser for shaker, jingle, bell, wind or chime sounds, if your version has them, or build a wind sound from a noise patch. Place each effect on a beat of your cue sheet and keep it short. Sleigh-bell-like shakers sit well on the beat; a chime works as the final hit.
EQ for the voiceOpen Channel EQ on the voice track. Switch on its lowest band as a high-pass (low cut) filter if your version offers one and set it near 80 Hz. For presence, make a small boost with one band and listen; start with small moves, and remove the boost if the voice sounds harsh.
Compression for the voiceInsert Compressor on the voice track. Set a ratio of about 3:1 or 4:1, then lower the threshold until the gain reduction meter shows about 3 to 6 dB on the loudest words. Use the make-up gain, if your version has it, to bring the level back. Choose settings by ear; this kit names no presets.
Ducking with sidechainInsert Compressor on the music track. In the plug-in header, open the Side Chain menu (if your version has it) and choose the voice track. Lower the threshold until the music drops about 6 to 12 dB while the voice speaks, and set the release so it rises smoothly.
Ducking with automationPress A to show automation on the music track and choose Volume. Click points to draw the music down about half a second before the voice and back up half a second after it. Draw ramps, not steps, so the move is not heard.
Mixer and bouncePress X for the Mixer and set levels and pan. Keep the master peak below your ceiling. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV, for the whole ident. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented plan on paper: a 20-second ident for Snowline Radio at 120 BPM. No audio is included. Student projects will differ. Page 1 of 2: the plan, the script and the cue sheet.

1 · A weak plan

I will make a radio ident with a voice and music, and it will sound good.

It names no tempo, no length in bars, no script and no times.

2 · A clear plan

My ident is for Snowline Radio at 120 BPM in 4/4. One bar lasts 240 ÷ 120 = 2 seconds, so 20 seconds is exactly 10 bars. The music bed starts on bar 1. The voice reads a 24-word script in about 9.6 seconds, from 4 seconds (bar 3 beat 1) to 16 seconds (bar 9 beat 1), so 12 seconds are available. The music starts to fall at 3.5 seconds (bar 2 beat 4), is 8 dB lower when the voice comes in, and is back at full level at 16.5 seconds (bar 9 beat 2). A chime and the final hit land at 18 seconds (bar 10 beat 1), and the ident ends at 20 seconds.

3 · The script (template A) ·

This is Snowline Radio. Frost on the windows, snow on the roof, warm music inside. Stay cozy with us all winter long. Snowline Radio.

24 words ÷ 2.5 words per second = 9.6 seconds; the window is 16 − 4 = 12 seconds, so there are 2.4 seconds for pauses.

The same ident, page 2 of 2: the timeline, the voice processing, then the moments to point out.

6 · The timeline: 10 bars, 5 layers ·

7 · The voice processing

High-pass at 80 Hz (−3 dB at the corner). A small example boost of +2 dB near 3 kHz, added by ear and kept because the consonants were clearer. Compressor at 4:1 with the threshold at −17 dB. The loudest words peak at −9 dBFS, which is 8 dB over the threshold, so the gain reduction is 8 × (1 − 1/4) = 6 dB and the peak leaves the compressor at −15 dBFS before make-up gain.

8 · The duck

Method: volume automation on the music track. The music starts to fall at 3.5 s (bar 2 beat 4), is 8 dB lower at 4 s, and rises from 16 s (bar 9 beat 1) to full level at 16.5 s (bar 9 beat 2). The ramps last 0.5 second. The same duck with sidechain compression would be set by the threshold until the music drops about 8 dB under the voice.

9 · The effects

The shaker plays on every beat of bars 1 and 2 and again on bar 9; the wind fades in over bars 1 and 2 and out under the voice; the music falls 8 dB over half a second before the voice; the chime is 3 dB under the final hit.

10 · Three feedback questions (invented)

1. Could you hear every word? Which word was hardest to hear, if any? 2. When did you first notice the music move, and did it feel smooth? 3. Does the final chime feel like an ending?

11 · Moments to point out in class
  • The music is the loudest at the start and the end and quietest under the voice, so the voice always leads.
  • At 120 BPM every cue falls on a beat, so the class can clap along with the duck and hear exactly when it begins.

Step 1: plan the ident. In 4/4 there are 4 beats in a bar. These formulas turn a tempo into seconds and bars. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas (4/4)

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM

Seconds per bar = 240 ÷ BPM

Bars in 20 seconds = 20 ÷ (240 ÷ BPM) = BPM ÷ 12

Tempo = 12 × bars, when 20 seconds is a whole number of bars

A position is at ((bar − 1) × 4 + (beat − 1)) × 60 ÷ BPM seconds

Example: 120 BPM: 240 ÷ 120 = 2 s per bar, 20 ÷ 2 = 10 bars, and 120 ÷ 12 = 10 too.

Rounding rules

Seconds per beat and per bar to 3 decimals, bars to 4 decimals in the first table and to 2 in the second; a 5 rounds up. So 240 ÷ 144 = 1.667 seconds per bar (the exact value is 5/3), and 20 ÷ (5/3) is exactly 12 bars.

Step 2: write and record the voice, continued. Follow the sound from your mouth to the mix. Each box is one place where you can make the sound better or worse, and the order matters: the high-pass filter and the compressor come after the recording.

Microphone technique
I sit 10 to 20 cm from the microphone, about the width of my hand, and stay at that distance for every take.
A pop filter sits between my mouth and the microphone, so the P and B sounds do not pop.
I speak across the front of the microphone, not into a corner, in a quiet room with soft surfaces.
I wear headphones so the music and my voice do not leak back into the microphone.
Gain staging
I set the input gain on the interface while I read the loudest line of the script.
The loudest words peak around −12 to −6 dBFS on the track meter, and never reach 0 dBFS.
A take that clips is deleted and recorded again; turning it down later does not fix it.
A quieter recording is safe: the next page explains why.
Why 10 to 20 cm

Close to the microphone the voice gets boomy, a change called the proximity effect with many microphones; far away the room gets in. A steady distance keeps the tone and the level the same from word to word. Nobody has measured your room, so try two distances and listen.

Step 2: write and record the voice, continued. In a digital recording 0 dBFS is the highest level there is, so every real level is a negative number. The amplitude (how far the wave swings, as a share of full scale) is 10 raised to the power of dB ÷ 20. Aim for the loudest words to peak around −12 to −6 dBFS.

A bed with a shape

Open (first 4 seconds): the bed at full level, so listeners hear the station. Under the voice (4 to 16 seconds): ducked and simpler. Out (16 to 20 seconds): back to full for the final hit and chime. Fewer layers under the voice leave room for the words.

Step 4: clean up the voice. Put Channel EQ and then a compressor on the voice track. The numbers on this page are starting points, not rules. Change one thing at a time, start with small moves, and let your ears decide.

EQ: remove rumble first ·

Compression: even it out ·

Starting points for voice
  • High-pass: switch it on near 80 Hz. It removes rumble and breath thumps below the voice. Raise it a little only if the voice still sounds full.
  • Presence: if the words are hard to follow, try one small boost, such as 1 to 2 dB, and listen. Take it back if the voice gets harsh.
  • Ratio: about 3:1 or 4:1. Guides give anything from 2:1 to 5:1.
  • Threshold: lower it until the loudest words show about 3 to 6 dB of gain reduction. About 4 dB or less is gentle.

Step 5: duck the music. Ducking lowers the music while the voice speaks and lets it rise again afterward, so every word stays clear. Start with 6 to 12 dB of reduction and a ramp of about half a second, then adjust by ear. Two ways to do it are below.

Way 1 · Sidechain compression
Put a Compressor on the music track.
In the plug-in header, open the Side Chain menu (if your version has it) and choose the voice track.
Lower the threshold until the music drops about 6 to 12 dB while the voice speaks.
Set the release so the music rises smoothly after the last word.

It reacts by itself if you change the voice, but its settings need careful listening.

Way 2 · Volume automation
Show automation on the music track and choose Volume.
Click a point half a second before the voice, and another where the music is 8 dB lower.
Click a point where the voice ends, and one half a second later back at full level.
Check the ramps: a straight, gentle slope, never a step.

You see and control the exact timing, but you redraw it if the voice moves.

Step 5: duck the music. The cue sheet puts every event on the clock and on the grid. The voice window is 12 seconds, from 4 s to 16 s. The duck starts half a second before the voice. The table gives the bar and beat of every cue at the three tempos.

How to find a cue at your tempo

Beats from the start = second × BPM ÷ 60. Bar = 1 + the whole bars in (beats ÷ 4). Beat = 1 + the beats left over.

Example: 96 BPM, second 4: 4 × 96 ÷ 60 = 6.4 beats, which is 1 whole bar and 2.4 beats, so bar 2, beat 3.4. A beat of 3.4 is 40% of the way from beat 3 to beat 4.

Holiday Radio Ident

Music Technology | Radio Ident Project | 3 class periods

Overview ·

Make a radio ident in Logic Pro, 15 to 30 seconds long, for an invented winter station. It is built from a voice that reads a short original script, a music bed that is an original loop or pattern built from Logic’s own sounds, and sound effects such as sleigh-bell-like shakers, wind or a door chime. Students plan the length with timing math (tempo = 12 × bars for a 20-second ident, so 96, 120 and 144 BPM give 8, 10 and 12 bars), record the voice with good microphone technique and safe levels, clean it up with EQ and compression, and duck the music under the voice, either with sidechain compression or with automation. Nobody needs to read music, and no song is used.

Goals ·

  • Plan a 20-second ident: use one bar = 240 ÷ BPM seconds and tempo = 12 × bars to choose a tempo, and place the voice, music and effects on a cue sheet
  • Write a script that fits its time: about 150 words per minute is 2.5 words per second, so a 12-second read is about 30 words
  • Record a clean voice: microphone distance, a pop filter and levels that peak around −12 to −6 dBFS, and read the dB ladder
  • Process the voice with a high-pass filter and gentle compression, then duck the music under it by about 6 to 12 dB, with sidechain compression or automation

Key Vocabulary ·

IdentA short sound logo or spot, usually 5 to 30 seconds, that tells listeners which station they are hearing

Tips for Success ·

  • Record the voice first and build the music around it. The bed has to leave room for the words.
  • Say the script out loud twice before you record. Time yourself: the read should be shorter than the voice window, so there is room to breathe.
  • Start with small moves on the EQ, the compressor and the duck. Change one thing at a time and listen after each change.
  • Save a new version of your project at the end of each class, so you can always go back.
