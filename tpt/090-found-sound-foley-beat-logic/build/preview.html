The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a tempo and at least 6 clean, safely recorded sounds, so that Class 2 is all trimming, roles and pattern. Check the take logs before they go.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the plan, the recording, the trimming and the roles; Class 2 is the pattern, the swing, the arrangement, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the arrangement and the mix, and Class 4 on presentations and reflection.

Step 4: build the pattern. A pattern is a set of hits that repeats. Each lane is one of your sounds and each column is one 16th-note step, so a bar has 16 steps and the 4-bar pattern has 64. Different sounds can share a step, so they play together. Play your own pattern, then draw it on the blank grid.

Reading the grid

A hit on grid step s of bar b starts at ((b − 1) × 16 + (s − 1)) × 15 ÷ BPM seconds. At 100 BPM, bar 2, grid step 14 (the tube tap) is at (16 + 13) × 0.15 = 4.35 s.

The box tap plays on grid steps 1, 7 and 11 and the tape pull on 5 and 13, which are beats 2 and 4. Velocity sets how hard each hit is, if your version shows one for each step.

Step 6: layer, arrange and mix. An arrangement puts your sounds into sections. This one is 16 bars, four sections of 4: an intro, a build, the groove and an outro. Each of your sounds is a layer, and this model has 6 layers. Sounds join and leave so the listener hears the beat grow and then settle.
