What this is ·

A 4-class-period remix project for high school music technology. Each student chooses one of three public-domain carols, enters its melody into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, and flips it into a genre of their choice: new tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds around the same tune. The remix is 60 to 90 seconds. Students plan it, build and mix it, present it and reflect on it. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Decide which carols to offer (page 9) and check the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–25), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–9), Class 3 (slides 10–11) and Class 4 (slides 12–15). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, recordings, lyrics, artists or albums are named or included. The three melodies are public domain and appear only as note charts. Students make every sound in their remixes themselves, from the instruments and drum kits in Logic.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: see the variations on page 6: one looped section on Jingle Bells, or a mode flip with a new melody rhythm.
  • Carol choice: Jingle Bells and Deck the Halls are winter songs; Silent Night is a Christmas carol. Offer the ones that suit your school, or a public-domain melody you approve.
  • Feedback: students write three questions and answer classmates’ on a class board or on paper.
  • Grading: the rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 22).

OVERVIEW
Remix project
The project in one line

Carol Flip · Remix · Logic Pro
Take a carol everyone knows and flip it into any genre.

What students make ·

One original remix, 60 to 90 seconds long, of a public-domain carol. The melody is entered from a piano-roll chart and stays recognizable; the tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds belong to the student’s chosen genre. It has at least four layers and an intro, melody sections and an outro, is mixed with volume, pan and EQ, and uses an effect that suits the genre. Students hand in a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans and a reflection, then play the remix and take questions.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a genre is built. Students learn that tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds, not only the notes of a melody, make a style, and they practice entering and editing notes in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Change a tune’s genre by changing tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds, not only the speed
  • Keep a melody recognizable while everything around it is new
  • Plan, build and explain a 60 to 90 second arrangement

SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The three charts, printed (pages 12, 13 and 14), or shown on the projector.
  • The genre cards, printed (pages 15–16).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–25).
  • A MIDI keyboard is optional: every melody can be drawn with the mouse.

Before Class 1 ·

Decide which carols you will offer (page 9).
Open Logic Pro on a student computer: can you make a track, open the Piano Roll and hear playback through headphones?
Decide how students will give each other feedback (a class board, or paper).
Read the worked example (pages 10–11).

What to watch ·

Students enter the notes but get a wrong one, the flip is only a speed change, the melody is buried, or a genre is a name and nothing more. Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished flips without saying the title and let the class name the carol. A tune that people name quickly is a flip that worked.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: which of your three flips is this? Can you hear the carol?
  • On Class 1, say each genre’s tempo range aloud and tap it: 70 for lo-fi, 128 for house.
  • A student who is stuck on a genre can start from a Drummer track and flip the rest.

Choosing carols ·

A respectful choice

Jingle Bells and Deck the Halls are winter songs; Silent Night is a Christmas carol. Offer the ones that fit your school and let students choose. If a student prefers another melody, a public-domain one you approve (first published in the United States in 1930 or earlier) works with the same plan, and the blank piano roll on page 17 is there for their own chart.

Keep the volume moderate

Bass-heavy genres and loud peaks are tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches how a genre is built. Students learn that tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds, not only the notes of a melody, make a style, and they practice entering and editing notes in Logic Pro’s Piano Roll.

Common student struggles ·

  • The notes go in but the tune sounds wrong: check the grid setting and the note lengths against the chart, bar by bar (Logic quick reference, page 8).
  • The flip is only a speed change: ask which three things changed besides the tempo (the Flip plan, page 17).
  • The melody gets buried under drums and bass: turn the backing down or EQ the melody up, so the carol stays the star.
  • A genre name with no genre craft: use the genre cards (pages 15–16) to check the tempo, drums and sounds.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Use Jingle Bells, loop one 4-bar section, and start from a drum pattern on a genre card or a Drummer track.

More Advanced: Flip the mode (major to minor) and the rhythm of the melody, then add a countermelody or a key change in the last section. Silent Night is the hardest chart: it is in 6/8.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Wrong notes: the chart pages (pages 12, 13 and 14) list every note by bar, and the Logic quick reference (page 8) shows the grid.
  • Only a speed change: the Flip plan (page 17) asks for three flips, and slide 8 shows one.
  • A buried melody: the mix checks on the Arrangement plan (page 18) and slide 11.
  • A genre in name only: the genre cards (pages 15–16) and slide 9.

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 22). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Melody Accuracy (rubric row 1): open the project and compare the Piano Roll with the chart, bar by bar. A planned change such as an octave or a minor mode is correct, not an error.
  • The Flip and Genre Craft (rubric rows 2 and 3): ask which three things changed, then check tempo, drums and sounds against the genre card.
  • Arrangement and Mix (rubric row 4): compare the project with the Arrangement plan (page 18). Is there an intro, melody sections and an outro, and can you hear the melody?
  • Plan and Reflection (rubric row 5): the Flip plan (page 17), the questions on the feedback planner and the Reflection page (page 25) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 25): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Plan & Reflection row of the rubric.

Notes / observations ·

Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished flips without saying the title and let the class name the carol. A tune that people name quickly is a flip that worked.

Carol FlipLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Carol Flip is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add a Software Instrument track with Track › New Tracks…. Use one track for the melody and more for bass, chords and sounds.
Tempo and time signatureType the tempo in the control bar. Silent Night is in 6/8: set the time signature before you enter notes (the control bar, or the Signature track in Global Tracks).
Make a region, open the Piano RollWith the Pencil tool, click on the track where bar 1 starts to make an empty MIDI region, then double-click it to open the Piano Roll editor.
Draw notes from the chartPick a grid value in the Piano Roll’s Snap menu (for example, 1/16 note) so notes land on the chart’s sixteenth-note grid, click with the Pencil to add a note and drag its right edge to change its length. A MIDI keyboard is optional.
Fix wrong notesSelect a note and drag it up or down a row, or press delete. To lower every E to Eb, select the E notes together and move them down one row.
Timing and swingIn the Region inspector, use Quantize to tighten notes and its swing setting to make a straight groove bounce, as in lo-fi or reggae.
Transpose and octavesSelect a region and change Transpose in the Region inspector to move a whole melody up or down; 12 steps is one octave.
DrumsMake a Drummer track and pick a style, or make a Drum Machine Designer track and open the Step Sequencer: click the 16 steps for each drum, as on the genre cards.
Bass, chords and soundsBrowse the Library (press Y) for instruments, or open Alchemy, Retro Synth or Vintage Electric Piano. Session Players (Bass Player, Keyboard Player) can follow a Chord Track in Global Tracks.
Effects and mixingAdd plug-ins in a channel strip’s Audio FX slots: Channel EQ, Compressor, Bitcrusher, Tape Delay, ChromaVerb. Press X for the Mixer, and use each track’s volume and Pan.
AutomationPress A to show automation. Pick a parameter, such as a filter cutoff, volume or reverb send, and draw points so the sound moves.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

Where the tunes come from ·

  • Jingle Bells: First published in Boston in 1857 as “One Horse Open Sleigh”.
  • Deck the Halls: A centuries-old Welsh winter melody, in print long before 1930. The English words (1862) are not used.
  • Silent Night: Composed in 1818; the melody only. The words are not used.

The public-domain rule ·

In 2026 a melody is in the public domain in the United States when it was first published in 1930 or earlier (1931 from 1 January 2027). If a student suggests another carol or winter song, check its first publication date before you approve it, and never use a recording, a film or pop version, or its lyrics unless they are public domain too.

Picking a tune for a student ·

  • First time in the Piano Roll: Jingle Bells. One repeated note, then a few skips.
  • Likes to loop and layer: Deck the Halls. Four bars repeat well under any groove, and there is a blank piano roll to write an answer phrase.
  • Wants a challenge, or a slow, gentle genre: Silent Night, in 6/8.
Reading the charts

Pitch names run down the left; middle C is C4. Bold lines are bars, grey lines are beats. A note’s length is the name beside it: 16th, eighth, dotted eighth, quarter, dotted quarter, half, dotted half or whole. Chords are one idea that fits, not the only one.

Why 6/8 is harder

Silent Night has six eighth notes in a bar, felt as two groups of three. Students set 6/8 in Logic first. Offer it to students who want a challenge or a slow, gentle genre.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented plan on paper: Jingle Bells, the 8-bar chorus, flipped to C minor as house music at 128 BPM. No audio is included. Student projects will differ. Page 1 of 2: the plan and the flip.

1 · A weak plan

I will make Jingle Bells into a cool dance song.

It names no tempo, no carol part and nothing that changes.

2 · A clear plan

My carol is Jingle Bells, the 8-bar chorus. My genre is house at 128 BPM. I flip five things: the mode (every E becomes Eb, so it is in C minor), the tempo (128 BPM), the drums (a four-on-the-floor kick), the bass (short synth notes between the kicks) and the sounds (a bright synth lead and a pad). The melody keeps its rhythm, so people will still know it. It is 40 bars, 75 seconds: intro, melody, break, melody an octave higher, outro.

3 · Five flips, and what stays ·

ModeC major to C minor: every E becomes Eb (the only pitch that changes)
Tempofrom a bouncy walking pace to 128 BPM
Drumsa four-on-the-floor kick, claps on 2 and 4, offbeat hats
Bassshort synth notes on the chord roots, between the kicks
Soundsa bright synth lead and a pad instead of bells
StaysThe order of the notes and every length: the melody keeps its rhythm, so the carol is still the carol.

Carol FlipGenre cards · 1 of 2
STUDENT
Classes 1–2

A genre is a bundle of choices. Each card gives the tempo range, a one-bar drum pattern (16 steps, 4 to a beat; shaded steps are the beats), a bass idea, chords in a key that works with the carol charts, and sounds to try. Copy a pattern into the Step Sequencer, or use a Drummer track. Page 16 has two more cards and a blank one.

Lo-fi hip-hop · 70–90 BPM · laid-back, with swing

Drums. Kick on beat 1 and just after beat 3; snare on beats 2 and 4; hats on every eighth note. Add swing in Logic’s quantize settings.

Bass. Soft sub or electric bass: long root notes.

Chords. Cmaj7 – Am7 – Dm7 – G7 (C major)

Sounds. An electric piano with a low-pass filter, a little Bitcrusher, a quiet Tape Delay.

Try. Play the melody an octave lower, a hair behind the beat.

House · 120–128 BPM · driving, four on the floor

Drums. Kick on every beat; claps on beats 2 and 4; hats on the offbeats (the “and” of each beat).

Bass. Short synth notes on the offbeats, between the kicks.

Chords. Am – F – C – G (A minor or C major)

Sounds. Bright synth chords or stabs (Alchemy or Retro Synth), a lead for the melody, a filter-sweep riser.

Try. Use the Compressor’s side chain so the pad ducks on every kick, and cut the kick for a break.

Trap · 130–150 BPM · half-time: slow and heavy

Drums. Snare or clap only on beat 3, halfway through the bar; kick off the beat; busy hats, with a quick roll at the end.

Bass. 808-style: long, low sine-wave notes (a sub bass in Alchemy or Retro Synth).

Chords. Cm – Ab – Eb – Bb (C minor)

Sounds. A dark pad, a bell or pluck for the melody, crisp hats.

Try. Play the melody half as fast, each note twice as long, so it floats over the beat.

Reggae · 70–80 BPM · relaxed, with space

Drums. One drop: kick and snare (or rim) together on beat 3 only, nothing on beat 1; hats on every eighth.

Bass. Deep and melodic, with plenty of space between notes.

Chords. C – F – G – C (C major)

Sounds. Short chops of keys or guitar-style chords on the offbeats, an organ, a little tape echo.

Try. Leave a gap after every bass note, and sit the melody slightly behind the beat.

Pop-rock · 100–130 BPM · steady and punchy

Drums. Kick on beats 1 and 3, snare on beats 2 and 4, hats on every eighth. A Drummer track set to a rock style plays this for you.

Bass. Root notes in steady eighth notes.

Chords. C – G – Am – F (C major)

Sounds. An organ or piano for the chords, a clear lead for the melody, a touch of reverb.

Try. Hold the drums back for the intro and bring them in with a fill.

Carol Flip

Music Technology | Remix Project | 4 class periods

Overview ·

Take a carol everyone knows and flip it. Choose one of three public-domain melodies, enter it into Logic Pro from a piano-roll note chart, and remix it in any genre you like: change the tempo, the drums, the bass, the chords, the sounds and, if you want, the mode or the rhythm of the tune. Your finished remix is 60 to 90 seconds long, and people should still be able to name the carol.

Goals ·

  • Enter a melody into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from a note chart, with the right pitches and lengths
  • Change a tune’s genre by changing tempo, drums, bass, chords and sounds, not only the speed
  • Keep a melody recognizable while everything around it is new
  • Plan, build and explain a 60 to 90 second arrangement

Tips for Success ·

  • Keep the melody the star. If a listener cannot name the carol in the first ten seconds, simplify what is around it.
  • Change the feel before you change the notes: tempo, drums and bass flip a tune faster than new notes do.
  • One minute goes fast. Add one layer at a time and let each one be heard before you add the next.
  • Save a new version of your project before each big experiment, so you can always go back.
