Teacher Notes

Treble Clef Note-Name Task Cards, Winter Edition · grades 2–5

What it is

72 winter task cards for reading treble clef note names, in three levels, with a student reference page, recording sheets, three worksheets and full answer keys. Snowflakes and mittens, nothing tied to one celebration.

The three levels

  • Level A (cards A1–A24): one note on the staff, E to F. Three answer choices, so they work as clip cards.
  • Level B (B1–B24): three notes per card, adding D below and G above the staff.
  • Level C (C1–C24): name 3–7 notes to spell a word, adding low C and high A on ledger lines.

Rough guide: Level A for grade 2 or first treble lessons, B for grades 3–4, C for grades 4–5 or fast finishers.

Ways to use them

  • Scoot / around the room: tape cards around the room; students write answers on their recording sheet.
  • Clip cards (Level A): students clip a clothespin on the answer. Mark the back with a dot to self-check.
  • Centers, partners, early finishers, sub days.
  • Quick check: show one card under a document camera as a warm-up or exit ticket.

Prep

Print the card pages on card stock and cut on the outlines (8 per page). Laminate if you like. Print the recording sheets and worksheets on plain paper. Everything prints well in black and white.

About the notation

Every card shows a treble clef and quarter notes engraved with a professional music font. Stems follow the usual rule: below the middle line, stems go up; on or above it, stems go down. Each note’s position was checked by computer against its answer, and every answer key comes from the same checked data.

Standards

Supports reading melodic patterns in standard notation (National Core Arts Standards, Music, MU:Pr4.2, grades 2–5).

Memory aids

Lines: E G B D F (“Every Good Bird Does Fly”). Spaces: F A C E. Use any saying your students already know.

All cards, words, art and layouts are original. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

REFERENCE
Keep it handy
Keep this page next to the task cards. Every note sits on a line (the line runs through it) or in a space (between two lines).
The treble staff, low C to high A ·
Line notes ·

Bottom to top: E G B D F
“Every Good Bird Does Fly”

Space notes ·

Bottom to top: F A C E
The spaces spell “FACE”, like a face in the snow.

Just outside the staff ·

D hangs under the bottom line and G sits on top. Low C and high A sit on their own short line, a ledger line.

Tips ·
  • The treble clef curls around the G line (line 2). It is also called the G clef.
  • Count up or down from a note you know. Each step from a line to the next space is the next letter.
  • The music alphabet only uses A B C D E F G, then starts again at A.
