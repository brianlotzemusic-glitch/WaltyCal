What this is ·

A complete 6-day scoring-to-picture project for high school music technology. Students score a one-minute video of a walk through the woods at night in Logic Pro: 32 measures in two minor keys, with reverb and delay on shared buses, built from the Logic library, their own microphone recordings and free sound effects. They present their score with the video.

This is Unit 6 of my Digital Music Production 1 course, the first open-ended project of the year. It works just as well on its own, and it fits October as well as November.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the overview and pacing (pages 3–6) and the teacher notes (pages 10–13).
  2. Find your video (box on the right) and check the setup list (page 4) on one student computer.
  3. Practice the bus demo once (script on page 12, Logic steps on pages 20–21).
  4. Cue up the two listening examples on YouTube or Spotify: the Halloween theme (John Carpenter) and the Stranger Things theme (Kyle Dixon & Michael Stein). No audio is included.
  5. Print or post the student pages (pages 14–28).

The intro deck ·

You present it on Day 1 (slides 1–9: hook, what makes music spooky, dry and wet, what a bus is, the reverb and delay bus in Logic) and Day 2 (slides 10–16: two minor keys, a worked score, the project, steps, deliverables, rubric). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

Spooky TunesUnit overview
OVERVIEW
DMP1 · Unit 6

What students make ·

32 measures of atmospheric music scored to a one-minute video of a walk through the woods at night, in two different minor keys, with reverb and delay routed through shared buses.

What students learn ·

Minor keys feel different from major keys, and now they know why. Half steps create dissonance. Reverb creates space and distance. Pitch bending creates unease. Delay creates echo and mystery. Music can tell a story without words. Less can be more: silence and space are creative choices.

Why it lands here ·

This is the first truly open-ended creative project, and it lands after students have chord vocabulary, melodic instincts, form awareness and the beginning of mixing understanding. The video anchor removes the ‘what should I make?’ anxiety while still demanding genuine creative decisions.

Mixing concept: reverb & delay via bus ·

Students need reverb to solve a creative problem: how do I make this sound scary? The bus/send workflow is taught as the right way to use time-based effects: ‘Put the reverb on a bus, not directly on the track. That way you control how much of it you add.’

A note from me

This project gives students permission to be weird. Some of your most musically undeveloped students will surprise you, because there is no wrong answer, only more or less effective choices. Celebrate the strange ones.

Goals ·

  • Understand what a bus is and why it exists
  • Set up a reverb bus and a delay bus in Logic using aux channels and sends
  • Use reverb and delay to create a sense of space, distance, and atmosphere
  • Find, record, and import sounds from multiple sources: the Logic library, your own microphone recordings, and a free sound-effects site
  • Use two different minor keys to shape the mood, and change key at a moment that matters
  • Compose music that serves a visual, making deliberate decisions about what happens when in the video
  • Score a one-minute video with no other structural restrictions: let the video drive the decisions

Spooky TunesLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher

Everything students need in Logic for this unit, in one table. Students get the step-by-step version of the bus setup on the How to Bus pages (19–22).

Import the videoFile › Movie › Open Movie. The movie plays in a floating window and a Movie track appears. Make sure the movie starts at bar 1. You can skip importing the movie’s own audio.
TempoDouble-click the tempo in the control bar and type 128 (32 measures = 60 seconds). Other lengths: BPM = 7680 ÷ seconds.
MarkersShow the global tracks (G) to see the Marker track. Move the playhead to a moment and create a marker with Option-' (apostrophe) or the + in the Marker track. Double-click a marker to rename it.
Record a soundCreate an audio track, choose your interface’s input, record-enable the track and press R. Keep the level out of the red.
Import a downloadDrag the MP3 from Finder into the Tracks area. Logic makes a new audio track.
Reverb busOn a track’s channel strip, click an empty Send slot › Bus › Bus 1. Logic creates an Aux. Put Space Designer on the Aux; Dry 0%, Wet 100%.
Delay busSame steps with Bus 2 and Tape Delay (Dry 0%, Wet 100%).
Send levelDrag the small knob in the Send slot. Reverb: start around –15 dB. Delay: start around –20 to –25 dB.
Pitch bendUse the pitch-bend wheel or joystick on a MIDI keyboard while you record a software instrument part.
AutomationPress A to show automation. Choose the send level in the track’s automation menu and draw points to swell the reverb in or out.
MixerPress X. Aux channels sit to the right of the track channels.
ExportFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx project and choose Compress. Optional for presenting: File › Movie › Export Audio to Movie.
Tempo math

32 measures × 4 beats = 128 beats. At 128 BPM that is exactly 60 seconds: 1 measure = 1.875 seconds and every 4 measures = 7.5 seconds. For a 64-second clip use 120 BPM; for 75 seconds, about 102 BPM.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 4

What this unit does ·

This is the first open-ended project in DMP1. After four units of structured constraint, students get their first taste of genuine creative freedom. The spooky theme and the fixed video brief give just enough structure to prevent paralysis (students know what they are making and roughly how long it needs to be), but there are no chord-progression or song-form requirements. The brief is: make something that sounds like this video looks.

The primary technical goal is bussing. This is the first formal introduction to routing in Logic and students consistently find it confusing. The unit gives you 6 days partly because the effects processing is worth exploring, but also because some students will need multiple attempts to get the bus routing right before they can focus on the creative work.

The combination of bussing, an open brief and video scoring makes this unit pedagogically rich. Students who have been cautious and mechanical in previous units often surprise themselves here. The absence of rules removes the fear of being wrong.

The two minor keys ·

The one musical rule is two different minor keys: key 1 for measures 1–16 and key 2 for 17–32, with the change placed on a moment in the video. It gives students a harmonic home without taking away the freedom, and it turns a key change into a storytelling decision. Atonal sound-effect layers sit on top. If your students have not met minor keys yet, the Minor Key Map (page 23) teaches the minor scale and its first chord, and the reference card (page 24) gives eight keys ready to use.

How this kit differs from my classroom version
  • No video is included: you supply a one-minute clip (page 4).
  • The two-minor-keys rule, the 32-measure length and the written reflection come from my course restructure; the rubric’s Composition & Pacing row now grades the key change.
  • Equipment and websites are named as examples; any interface, microphone and free sound-effects site works.

Common student struggles ·

Space Designer not set to 100% wet: The single most common error. If Space Designer is outputting any dry signal, students will hear double: the dry signal from the track AND the dry signal from the bus. The bus should be 100% wet. Walk around on Day 2 and check every student’s Space Designer settings before anything else.

Wrong bus number on the Aux input: Students create an Aux channel by hand but forget to set its input to a bus, or set it to the wrong bus number. Show them explicitly: the Aux input must match the bus number on the send. Bus 1 sends to Bus 1. If they do not match, nothing happens. Creating the bus from a track’s Send slot (as the How to Bus pages do) avoids most of this.

Not hearing any reverb at all: Usually means the Aux channel fader is all the way down, or the send level is at its lowest. Check both. The send knob needs to be turned up and the Aux fader needs to be at a reasonable level.

Blank page paralysis on the creative side: Some students freeze without harmonic or structural constraints. The redirect: download three sounds you find unsettling, import them into Logic, and just play. You do not need a plan yet. The plan emerges from the sounds.

Everything drenched in reverb equally: Students discover reverb and apply maximum amounts to every track. The result is an undifferentiated wash. Prompt them: some sounds should feel close and dry, some should feel distant and wet. The contrast is what creates space.

The key change nobody hears: Students switch the melody to key 2 at measure 17 but leave the drone or bass on the old root, so nothing seems to change. Have them move the lowest sound to the new root at the same moment, and place that moment on a cut or a shift in the video.

Day 1: the bus demo (20 minutes) ·

This is teacher-led. Students watch first, then replicate. Here is a suggested sequence.

  1. 1
    Open a new Logic project. Add 3 or 4 audio or instrument tracks with different sounds playing.
  2. 2
    Play the project. Ask: what if I want all of these to sound like they are in the same room? I could add reverb to each track individually, but then I have three separate reverbs to manage. Show this briefly: add Space Designer to one track, then copy it to another. Ask: what is the problem here? (Any change to one reverb does not affect the other.)
  3. 3
    Delete the individual reverbs. Open the Mixer (X). On the first track, click an empty Send slot and choose Bus 1: Logic creates an Aux channel. Rename it Reverb Bus.
  4. 4
    Check that the Aux input reads Bus 1. Add Space Designer to the Aux insert. Set it to 100% wet (Dry 0%).
  5. 5
    Turn that track’s send up. Play the project. Ask: what do you hear? What changed?
  6. 6
    Add sends to the other tracks at different levels. Ask: what happens when one track has a high send and another has a low send? (Different apparent distance.) This is the moment the concept lands.
  7. 7
    Ask: what would happen if I wanted a delay too? Walk through setting up Bus 2 with Tape Delay. The second setup goes faster: students now understand the pattern.
  8. 8
    Open the project on a student computer and ask a student to set up the reverb bus from scratch while the class watches. This is the most effective check for understanding.

About the video ·

Before playing it for students, watch your clip yourself and identify 3–4 moments worth noting: a visual cut, a shift in lighting, a moment of stillness. These are the compositional opportunities you will point students toward during working days.

Play the video for the full class at the start of Day 1 and again at the start of Day 2. Watch it once without comment, then watch it again and ask: what moments stood out? What did you expect to hear? This primes students to think cinematically before they open Logic.

Suggested variations ·

More accessible: Pre-build the reverb and delay buses in a Logic template and distribute it. Students open the template and focus on sound collection and composition rather than routing. The How to Bus pages become a reference for understanding what is already built rather than a setup guide. You can also allow one key for the whole piece.

More advanced: Challenge students to automate the send level on at least one track so the reverb swells in or out at a specific moment in the video. Introduce the idea of a wet/dry blend that changes over time as a compositional tool. Or ask for a half-step key change (A minor to B-flat minor) at the scariest moment.

Grading tips ·

  • Each category is scored 2–5, for a total out of 25. Students self-score first; you score in the second column (page 28).
  • Watch once with the video for Composition & Pacing (does the key change land on a moment?), once eyes-closed for Sound Design and Reverb & Delay (near and far), then open the Mixer to check both buses, 100% wet, and the sends.
  • The reflection is the quickest evidence of intention: two named techniques with a reason each.

Resources ·

  • The intro deck (.pptx and PDF), included.
  • Listening: the Halloween theme (John Carpenter) and the Stranger Things theme (Kyle Dixon & Michael Stein), from your own YouTube or Spotify access. A search for spooky hip-hop instrumentals or horror film scores gives more.
  • A free sound-effects site you approve, and a short Logic “bus and send” tutorial video if you like one.

The one thing to remember about buses

Your original track still plays dry. The bus adds the wet (effected) signal on top. The send knob on your track controls how much signal goes to the bus.

Space Designer on the bus must be set to 100% wet. If it is outputting any dry signal, you will hear everything doubled.

Project sheet

Digital Music Production 1 · Unit 6 · Open-Ended · 6 Days · Reverb & Delay via Bus

Overview ·

This is your first open-ended project. There is no chord progression required. No song form required. No right answer. Your brief is a one-minute video of a walk through the woods at night, and your job is to score it. The music should make the video feel the way it looks. Use any sounds you can find or record. Experiment. Make something unsettling. Your one musical rule: two different minor keys, one for each half.

The new technical skill in this unit is bussing: routing reverb and delay through shared aux channels so multiple tracks share the same effect. Your teacher will demo this on Day 1. The How to Bus pages walk you through the setup step by step.

Goals ·

  • Use reverb and delay to create a sense of space, distance, and atmosphere
  • Find, record, and import sounds from multiple sources: the Logic library, your own microphone recordings, and a free sound-effects site

Composing to picture ·

Scoring to a video is different from writing music for its own sake. The video tells you what to do and when. Before you build anything, watch the video twice and take notes.

Watch before you buildPlay the video twice without touching Logic. What moments stand out? Where does the energy change? What would a sound do to that moment? Write notes on your cue sheet before you open a single plugin.
Mark your momentsAdd markers in Logic at the timestamps where you want something to happen. A footstep. A sudden silence. A swell. Having the map in advance stops you from improvising past the moments that matter.
Silence is a toolIf nothing is playing for a moment and then a sound enters, the entry feels enormous. Silence is not dead air: it is tension. Use it deliberately.
Let the video breatheYou do not need to fill every second with sound. Sparse music over a quiet visual can be more unsettling than a wall of noise. Less is often more here.
Match the energyIf the video gets more intense, your music should respond. Build toward moments of tension and release from them. A swell of wet reverb signals that something is coming. Save your key change for a moment that deserves it.

Tips for success ·

  • Set up the buses first. Do not start collecting sounds until both buses are working. If you build 10 tracks and then realize your bus routing is wrong, you have to fix everything.
  • Record something weird. The microphone is there: use it. A recording of your own voice processed beyond recognition is more interesting than any preset.
  • Half steps are spooky. Try two notes a half step apart, or slide between them with the pitch-bend wheel.
  • Atonal does not mean random. Your sound-effect layers do not need a key, but they should feel like they belong together. Choose sounds for texture and tension, not just because they sound unpleasant.
  • Watch the video with your music at least once per working day. It is easy to lose track of whether you are actually serving the picture or just making a piece of music that happens to be playing at the same time.
  • Keep the How to Bus pages open. If your routing stops working, the troubleshooting table will fix almost every common problem.

1 of 4 · What a bus is

Keep these pages open next to Logic while you work.

What is a bus, and why does it exist? ·

A bus is a shared channel that multiple tracks can send their audio to. Instead of putting a reverb plugin on every single track individually, you create one reverb bus and send as many tracks as you want to it. The reverb lives in one place. Everything that sends to it gets the same reverb. This matters for three reasons:

  • Consistency: every track that sends to the bus sounds like it exists in the same space.
  • Control: one fader on the bus controls how much reverb everything gets at once.
  • Efficiency: one plugin instead of ten. Less CPU. Less mess.
The send / return relationship

Your original track still plays dry: you always hear the unaffected signal. The send knob controls how much of that signal gets routed to the bus. The bus adds wet signal (the effect) on top. Turn the send up: more reverb. Turn it down: less. The original track is unchanged.

2 of 4 · Reverb

Part 1 · Set up your reverb bus ·

Start from the track you want to add reverb to, not from a blank aux channel. Logic creates the bus automatically when you make the send.

  1. 1
    Open the channel strip for your trackIn the Mixer (X) or the inspector on the left, find the channel strip for the track you want to add reverb to. Look for the Sends section: it sits below the insert plugin slots.
    If you cannot see Sends, widen the inspector or the Mixer so the Sends slots show.
  2. 2
    Click an empty Send slot and choose a busIn the Sends section, click one of the empty slots. A menu appears. Go to Bus and choose Bus 1 (any bus that shows no name next to it is empty).
    Watch what happens: Logic automatically creates an Aux channel strip routed to that bus. You do not need to create it manually.
  3. 3
    Raise the Send levelA small knob now appears in the send slot. At its lowest, nothing is going to the bus. Click and drag it upward. Start around –15 dB.
    This is the most critical step. A send knob at its lowest sends nothing. Nothing will happen until you raise it.
  4. 4
    Add Space Designer to the Aux channelFind the Aux channel Logic just created in the Mixer. Click its first empty Insert slot and choose Reverb › Space Designer. For Spooky Tunes, try a large hall, cathedral or other large-space preset.
    The Aux channel may appear at the far right of the Mixer. Scroll if you cannot find it.
  5. 5
    Set Space Designer to 100% wetInside Space Designer, set the Dry signal to 0% and the Wet signal to 100%. The bus outputs only the reverb. The dry signal always comes from the original track.
    This is the most common mistake. If the plugin is partially dry, you hear the sound doubled. Always 100% wet on a bus plugin.
  6. 6
    Name the Aux channelDouble-click the name at the bottom of the Aux channel strip and type REVERB BUS.
    You only set this up once. Every other track that needs reverb just needs a send pointing to the same bus number.
  7. 7
    Send more tracks to the same busSelect another track. Click an empty Send slot. Choose the same bus number as before. Raise the send level. That track now shares the reverb.
    Do not pick a new bus number for each track. All reverb sends point to the same bus.

3 of 4 · Delay

Part 2 · Set up your delay bus ·

Same process as the reverb bus: start from the track, click a Send slot, choose an empty bus, and Logic creates the Aux. Do this after your reverb bus is confirmed working.

  1. 1
    Click a Send slot and choose a new busOn any track you want delay on, click an empty Send slot. Choose Bus › Bus 2 (or whichever bus is still empty, not the one used for reverb). Logic creates a second Aux channel.
    You can send a track to both the reverb bus and the delay bus at the same time by adding two separate sends.
  2. 2
    Raise the Send levelDrag the send knob up. Start lower than your reverb send: delay is a more noticeable effect. Around –20 to –25 dB is a good starting point.
    Delay is easy to overdo. Less is almost always better.
  3. 3
    Add Tape Delay to the Aux channelFind the new Aux channel in the Mixer. Click its first Insert slot and choose Delay › Tape Delay. Set the plugin to 100% wet (Dry at 0%): same rule as the reverb bus.
    Try Echo for a cleaner sound or Stereo Delay for a wider effect.
  4. 4
    Name the Aux channelDouble-click the channel name and type DELAY BUS.
    You now have two clearly named effect buses.
  5. 5
    Adjust the delay timeIn Tape Delay, the delay time controls the gap between the original and its echo. For atmosphere, try longer times (600–900 ms) with higher feedback. For subtle movement, try shorter times (100–250 ms) with low feedback.
    Turn off tempo sync if you want the delay to float freely. Free-running delay often feels more unsettling.
  6. 6
    Balance reverb and delay togetherPlay the full project and listen to both effects. Reverb creates space. Delay creates movement. Use the fader on each Aux to balance the total effect amount. You can even send the delay bus to the reverb bus by adding a send on the delay’s Aux channel.
    A common approach: reverb on most things, delay on just one or two specific elements. Selective delay draws the ear.

Troubleshooting ·

ProblemFix
I hear reverb, but the whole track sounds wet, with no clear dry soundSpace Designer is not set to 100% wet. Open it and set Dry to 0%, Wet to 100%.
I turned up the send but I can’t hear any reverbCheck that the Aux input is set to the same bus number as the send (Bus 1 for reverb, Bus 2 for delay). Also check that the Aux fader is turned up.
Everything sounds like it’s drowning in reverbYour send levels are too high, or your Space Designer decay is too long. Pull the send knobs down (try –20 dB) and shorten the reverb tail. Or… did you put reverb on the Stereo Out by accident?
The delay is syncing to tempo in a weird wayTape Delay has a tempo sync option. Turn it off and set the time manually in milliseconds if you want free-running delay.
I put reverb directly on a track instead of using the busDelete the plugin from the track’s insert, set up the bus as above, and use a send instead.

Using reverb & delay for atmosphere ·

Heavy reverb on a quiet soundA faint, distant sound buried in reverb feels enormous, like something very far away in a large, empty space. Try a single low note with a long reverb tail.
Delay on a single hitOne sharp sound with a long delay feels like an echo in an empty corridor. High feedback makes it cascade and fade. Low feedback gives one clean echo.
Reverb on a recordingRecord something yourself (your voice, a tap on a surface, a door creak) and send it to the reverb bus. Real recordings with heavy processing sound genuinely unsettling.
Different send levelsNot everything needs the same amount of reverb. A close, dry sound next to a distant, wet sound creates depth and a sense of physical space.
Automate the send levelTurn the send up gradually over 8 measures and the reverb swells in. Pull it out suddenly and the space collapses. Both are effective in different moments of the video.
