## 2. Description

```
Chord progressions for high school music technology: students hear I-IV-V-I and describe how chords feel before they learn a single name, then build major and minor chords, label them with Roman numerals and lay a chord layer over a bassline in Logic Pro. This 5-day ear-first unit is Unit 3 of my Digital Music Production 1 course; the steps are written for Logic Pro, while the music theory (chords, Roman numerals, C major and A minor) carries over to GarageBand, Ableton Live and BandLab.

WHAT'S INCLUDED (ZIP: 35-page PDF + 21-slide intro deck as .pptx and PDF)
• How to use, unit overview, setup page with a 10-minute recipe for the bassline Logic file, and a one-page Logic quick reference (4 pages)
• 5-day pacing guide and minute-by-minute day plans, with the deck slides for each block and a chord spell-check strip (4 pages)
• Teacher notes: the ear-first opening activity, how to frame Roman numerals, the Reference Card, the balance mini-lesson, six common student struggles, variations and grading tips (4 pages)
• Student pages: three classroom posts, a Chord Feel Log for the ear day, the Scary Stuff packet (keyboard, scales, chords, Roman numerals), the KCSRN worksheet (keys, chords, scales and Roman numerals), Reference Cards for C major and A minor plus a blank card, a reference sheet, a 2-page project sheet with the chord function table, vocabulary (12 terms), submission checklist and the /15 rubric (18 pages)
• Answer keys for the packet, KCSRN and Feel Log, and a worked example in both keys with a piano-roll diagram (3 pages)
• Teacher-presented intro deck, 21 slides with speaker notes on every slide: a pop-culture hook, a plain-English definition of a chord, guided listening questions, a chord built in the Logic piano roll, a worked example over the bassline, the project steps, deliverables and rubric

THE FIVE DAYS
• Day 1, Chords by ear: one note, then chords, then I-IV-V-I and a real song; students play seven chords and describe them in their own words. No Roman numerals yet
• Day 2, The keyboard and scales: note names, half steps and whole steps, C major and A natural minor
• Day 3, Build chords: the R-4-3, R-3-4 and R-3-3 formulas, then chords in the piano roll
• Day 4, Roman numerals: name what you already heard, fill in the KCSRN worksheet and Reference Card, build four chords over the bassline
• Day 5, Balance, finish and present: turn the chords down under the bass, submit, and explain the chords in Roman numeral language

WHAT STUDENTS LEARN
• What chords feel like: the I is home, the V is homesick, the vi is tender
• Major sounds bright, minor sounds dark; chords are notes from the scale stacked together
• Roman numerals in C major and A minor, white keys only, as names for feelings
• Balance: the background steps back so the listener can follow the main part

GOOD TO KNOW
• Requires Logic Pro on a Mac. A MIDI keyboard helps (Musical Typing works too). The Logic file is not included; the setup page shows how to make the 4-measure bassline file in about 10 minutes
• Built for about 53-minute periods, grades 9-12; no music reading needed. Every chord, scale and answer was checked by computer against the reference cards
• The deck uses the free Oswald and Source Sans 3 fonts (built into Google Slides); install them for PowerPoint or Keynote, or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look
• Songs are named by title and artist only (Twist and Shout and Let It Be, The Beatles). Play them from your own YouTube or Spotify access. No audio, video, lyrics or album art are included
• Unit 4, Bass & Melody Writing, picks up where this unit ends and is sold separately
• Every student page prints well in black and white
```
