Teacher Guide

Color-Coded Carol Play-Alongs for Tuned Tubes, Bells & Xylophone · grades 1–5

What you need

  • One C-to-C set of 8 color-coded tuned percussion tubes, or 8 resonator bells, a color-coded xylophone, metallophone or handbells (stick a matching color dot on each bar).
  • Each page uses only the white notes C D E F G A B and high C, so one set plays all five carols. No sharps or flats, so no extra tubes.

How to use a page

  1. Look. Match the colors on the staff and strip to the colors of your tubes. Every block also has its note letter, so the page works in black and white.
  2. Say it. Read the colors aloud in rhythm while tapping the beat (the small numbers under the strip).
  3. Play it. Tap tubes on a knee, palm or the floor, or strike bells and bars. Start slowly.
  4. Build it. Easy version first, then the full version, then the group parts.

Which version?

  • Easy (grades 1–2): the first part of each carol with a simplified rhythm: only quarter, half and whole notes.
  • Full (grades 3–5): the whole melody with eighths, dotted rhythms and (in one carol) sixteenth notes, shown on the staff and as blocks.
  • Parts (all grades): a score for 2–3 groups. Each group owns some colors and plays only those blocks.

Groups of players

Divide the class into 2 or 3 groups and give each group a share of the colors. The suggested split is printed on each parts page. With 2 groups, join groups 2 and 3. If some students share a set, give each student two tubes so every child has a job.

Tempo

Start at the slower number and move up when the class can play without stopping. Numbers are beats (quarter notes) per minute. Count in two full bars out loud.

Extensions

  • One student conducts by pointing at the strip.
  • Play the melody on bells while others hold the beat on tubes.
  • Cover one group’s row and ask the class to play it from memory.
  • Use the blank strips on page 19 to write a short tune.

The longest tube is the lowest note. C+ is the short high C tube (red, like C). If your set has only the 8 diatonic tubes, you have everything you need.

The same notes on the staff

C is on the first ledger line below the staff, D is the space under the bottom line, E is the bottom line, F the first space, G the second line, A the second space, B the middle line and high C the third space.

How to read a strip

  • One colored block = one note. The block’s letter tells you which note.
  • Block length = note length: a wide block is a long note.
  • Space with no block = silence. The small numbers are the beats. A black bar joining two blocks is a tie: play it once and hold.
  • On the parts pages each row is one group: play only your own row.

Bells and xylophones

  • Put a color dot or tape on each bar: C red, D orange, E yellow, F green, G turquoise, A blue, B purple, high C red.
  • Resonator bells and handbells: hand each child the colors for their part.
  • Notes sounding an octave higher or lower than written do not change the colors.
