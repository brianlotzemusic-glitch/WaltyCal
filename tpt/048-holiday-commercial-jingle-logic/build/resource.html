What this is ·

A 4-class-period commercial music project for high school music technology. Each student is a jingle writer. A client, an invented product with a brief, needs a 30-second ad: students plan it in bars, pick a tempo, key and chord loop, build the music in Logic Pro, write and record an 8 to 12 word voice-over, duck the music under it, and bounce exactly 30 seconds. A classmate acts as the client and gives feedback. It is an original Hudson Beat project.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Look through the client briefs (page 11) and the worked example (pages 9–10), and check the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–27), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–6), Class 2 (slides 7–8), Class 3 (slides 9–11) and Class 4 (slides 12–15). Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, recordings, loops, lyrics, jingles, artists or brands are named or included, and no melody is supplied. Students write every tune and every word themselves, from the instruments, samplers and plug-ins in Logic Pro. Every client, product and tagline is invented.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: see the variations on page 6.
  • Own product: students may invent a product with your approval; it needs a name, an audience, a mood, a tagline and two sounds.
  • No microphone? The Mac’s built-in microphone works; see the setup page (4).
  • Grading: the rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 23).

Commercial JingleProject overview
OVERVIEW
Commercial music project
The project in one line

Commercial Jingle · Commercial music · Logic Pro
Write a 30-second ad for an invented product, with a voice-over.

What students make ·

One original jingle of exactly 30 seconds for one of four invented clients. It has a chord loop in a chosen key and tempo, at least four layers (made with Drummer or a Session Player and at least one of Alchemy, Retro Synth or Quick Sampler), both must-include sounds, an 8 to 12 word voice-over that ends with the tagline, and a mix in which the music ducks under the voice. Students hand in a bounced .wav or .mp3 and the zipped .logicx project, plus their plans, a client feedback sheet and a reflection.

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches commercial music as a design problem with a client. Students learn to plan music to a clock (bars, beats and seconds), build a loop from a chord progression, record and process a voice, and balance music and speech with ducking, then take a client’s feedback and improve their work.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Plan a 30-second jingle in bars and seconds from a client brief, and prove that the numbers add up
  • Build a chord loop and a short arrangement (intro, verse, hook, tag) with Logic Pro tools
  • Write, time, record and mix an 8 to 12 word voice-over so the voice sits clearly over the music
  • Export a file of exactly 30 seconds and improve it with a client’s feedback

Students should know how to open a project, add a software instrument and an audio track and use the mixer in Logic. Nobody needs to read music: Drummer and the Session Players can play the backing, and the chord tables name every chord.

Commercial JingleSetup and what to watch
SETUP
Teacher

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Headphones matter: they keep the music out of the voice recording.
  • A microphone for the voice-over. The Mac’s built-in microphone works; a USB microphone is better. A quiet corner helps more than a better microphone.
  • A place to hand in the .wav or .mp3, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The client briefs, printed (page 11), or shown on the projector.
  • The recipes, script, voice, ducking and bounce pages, printed (pages 14–19).
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–27).

Before Class 1 ·

Decide which briefs you will offer (page 11).
Open Logic Pro on a student computer: can you add a Drummer track, an Audio track and a Quick Sampler track, and hear them through headphones?
Test the microphone: record two words, play them back, and find a quiet place for recording (a practice room, a closet or a corner).
Read the worked example (pages 9–10).

What to watch ·

Students build a jingle that is 28 or 33 seconds long, write a script too long to say clearly, bury the voice under the music, or crush the mix.

Try this: Pairs well with a listening day: play the finished jingles without saying the product and ask the class what is being sold, who it is for and what the tagline was. A jingle the class can repeat after one listen is a jingle that works.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: what does this layer do? Where does the voice start? What does the client want to remember?
  • Recording needs quiet. Stagger it: half the class records while the other half edits, then swap.
  • A student who is stuck can start from a Drummer track and a Keyboard Player and change only the chords and the hook.

Originality and voices ·

Everything is original

Students write their own tune and words. No real jingle, song, slogan or brand is used or imitated, and every product in this kit is invented. Students record their own voice or a classmate who agrees; nobody imitates a real person. The season is winter and secular: no carols or holiday songs.

Keep the volume moderate

Voices and loud hooks are tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

Commercial JingleTeacher notes · the big idea
TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

Teaches commercial music as a design problem with a client. Students learn to plan music to a clock (bars, beats and seconds), build a loop from a chord progression, record and process a voice, and balance music and speech with ducking, then take a client’s feedback and improve their work.

Common student struggles ·

  • The music does not add up to 30 seconds: start from the tempo table (page 13) and use bars = BPM ÷ 8, then set the locators on the bar after the last one (page 19).
  • The voice is lost under the music: duck the music (page 18), thin the layers while the voice speaks, and make sure the voice has a high-pass filter and a Compressor (page 17).
  • The script is too long to say clearly: count words and syllables (page 16). More than 12 words, or more than about 8 syllables in one 2-second bar, will rush.
  • The chord loop sounds wrong: check the Roman numerals and key on page 12 (a capital numeral is a major chord, a small one is minor), and make sure the Session Players follow the same chords.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Offer brief C at 120 BPM (15 bars) with the I–V–vi–IV loop. Students use Drummer and Keyboard Player for the whole backing, write a script of 8 words, and duck by automation (volume points) instead of sidechain. Give a script starter if needed.

More Advanced: Ask for two versions: the full 30-second ad and a 15-second cut-down at the same tempo, with the tagline kept. Or ask for a sung tagline instead of a spoken one, a key change into the tag, or a second voice. Students can design an audio logo of about 3 seconds from the hook.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Not 30 seconds: the tempo table (page 13) and the end-locator table (page 19).
  • A lost voice: the voice chain (page 17), the ducking page (page 18) and slides 10–11.
  • A script that rushes: the word, syllable and seconds check (page 16) and slide 9.
  • A wrong-sounding loop: the Roman numeral tables (page 12) and slide 8.

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 23). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Brief & Concept (rubric row 1): read the brief card (page 11) and listen for the tagline, the product and both must-include sounds.
  • Music & Structure (rubric row 2): compare the structure plan (page 13) and the layer map (page 15) with the project: do the bars add up to 30 seconds, and do the sections enter where planned?
  • Voice-over & Script (rubric row 3): check the script has 8 to 12 words and the tagline (page 16), then listen for clear words, a clean recording and no harsh S sounds.
  • Mix & Export (rubric row 4): listen for ducking, check nothing clips and check the file length in Finder. Feedback & Reflection (rubric row 5): the client feedback sheet (page 25) and the Reflection page (page 27) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 27): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. Use it as evidence for the Feedback & Reflection row of the rubric.

Notes / observations ·

Commercial JingleLogic quick reference
SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a jingle is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 to 3 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks… (Software Instrument, Audio, Drummer). Name every track so your layer map matches (page 15).
Tempo and the rulerType the tempo in the control bar (double-click it) and keep 4/4. Bars = BPM ÷ 8 for 30 seconds (page 13). The position display can show bars and beats or minutes and seconds.
Chord TrackShow the global tracks (G) and add the Chord Track, if your version has it, then enter your four chords. Session Players and some loops follow it. Without it, play the chords yourself or draw them in the Piano Roll.
Drummer and Session PlayersAdd a Drummer track, choose a drummer whose style fits the mood and move the puck on its XY pad: right is louder, up is busier. If your version has Session Players, add a Bass Player and a Keyboard Player (page 14).
Alchemy and Retro SynthClick the instrument slot on a channel strip and choose Alchemy or Retro Synth. Browse the Library (Y) for a pluck, bell or bass as a starting point and change the filter and effects.
Quick SamplerChoose Quick Sampler as the instrument and use its record function to capture a sound from your microphone, such as a mug clink or a snap. Shorten it, set its pitch and play it from the keyboard or draw notes.
Loop BrowserPress O to open it. Search by mood or instrument, and drag a loop into the project. Loops follow the project tempo; check that the key fits your chords.
Recording the voiceAdd an Audio track, choose your microphone as its input, click the track’s R button to record-enable it and press the Record button. Wear headphones so the music does not leak into the microphone.
Channel EQ, Compressor, DeEsser 2Insert them on the voice track in that order (page 17). Turn the lowest band of Channel EQ into a high-pass filter; DeEsser 2 softens harsh S sounds.
DuckingEither insert Compressor on the music and pick the voice track in its Side Chain menu, or press A and draw volume points on the music (page 18).
BounceTurn Cycle on (C) and set it from bar 1 to the bar after your last one. File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), WAV or MP3. Check the length in Finder (page 19).

An invented plan on paper: a 30-second ad for Fluffwick Snug Wool Socks, for everyone with cold feet, mood cozy and cheerful, tagline “Happy feet all winter”, must include a finger snap and a high pluck. Invented, on paper: no audio is included, and a student’s numbers will differ. Page 1 of 2: the plan and the numbers.

1 · A weak plan

I will make a catchy song for the socks with a voice saying something about them.

It names no tempo, key, loop, sections or script, and nothing says it is 30 seconds.

2 · A clear plan

My client sells Fluffwick Snug Wool Socks to everyone with cold feet, in a cozy and cheerful mood. My jingle is 30 seconds at 120 BPM, so 15 bars of 2 seconds each: intro 2, verse 4, hook 6 and tag 3 bars. The loop is I–V–vi–IV in G (G D Em C), and the tag walks home on C D G. Six layers, 12 spoken words, and the tagline Happy feet all winter lands in bar 13.

A chord progression is a short pattern of chords that repeats. Producers write it with Roman numerals so it works in any key. A capital numeral is a major chord (I, IV, V); a small numeral is a minor chord (ii, vi). In C major, I = C, ii = Dm, iii = Em, IV = F, V = G and vi = Am. The loop I–V–vi–IV in C is C G Am F.

Time at a bar

Time = (bar − 1) × seconds per bar. At 120 BPM, bar 13 starts at (13 − 1) × 2 = 24 s. A beat is 60 ÷ BPM seconds. Use it to place the voice lines and the hook.

Rules of thumb

Keep the intro to 1 or 2 bars: ads are short. Make the hook the biggest section. Say the tagline in the tag or at the end of the hook, and finish on a clear last chord.

Commercial JingleBuild the music · Logic Pro recipes
STUDENT
Class 2

Five recipes, in words. Settings are starting points: change them by ear. Use at least four layers: Drummer or a Session Player, and at least one of Alchemy, Retro Synth or Quick Sampler. Both must-include sounds from your brief go in the music.

Drummer · The groove

Add. Add a Drummer track (the + button in the track list, then choose Drummer) and choose a drummer whose style fits the brief’s mood.

Shape. Drag the puck on the XY pad: right is louder, up is busier. Keep it low in the verse and high in the hook.

Trim. Turn off parts you do not need (such as the hi-hat) so the voice has room, and draw a short fill into the hook.

Session Players · Chords and bass

Chord Track. Show the global tracks (G), add the Chord Track if your version has it, and type your four chords bar by bar.

Players. If your version has Session Players, add a Bass Player and a Keyboard Player; they follow the Chord Track. If not, draw chords in the Piano Roll with a Software Instrument.

Keep it simple. Roots of the chords are enough for bass. Choose a soft keyboard sound so the voice can sit on top.

Alchemy or Retro Synth · The hook

Sound. Pick a pluck, bell or bright lead from the Library (Y), then change its filter and release by ear. Retro Synth is a simpler choice for a bass or lead.

Tune. Write a hook of three to five notes from your key. Repeat it. A hook people can hum is better than a clever one.

Room. Leave the hook out, or play it quietly, when the voice speaks.

Quick Sampler · A must-include sound

Record. Choose Quick Sampler and use its record function to capture your own sound: a mug clink, a stomp, a crunch or a snap. Nothing is supplied.

Shape. Trim the start, set the pitch and add a high-pass filter if it sounds boomy. Play it from the keyboard.

Place. Put it on a beat you can hear every time, such as beats 2 and 4, and use it in the hook.

Loop Browser · Fills, risers and stings

Search. Press O. Search by mood or by words such as riser, sparkle or whoosh. Click a loop to preview it at your tempo.

Use a little. Drag in one riser before the hook and one sting for the last bar. Check that any loop with pitch fits your key.

Rights. Use only the Apple Loops that come with Logic, and do not copy a loop that sounds like a real jingle.

Three habits of a good jingle writer

Simple: a loop, a groove and one hook are enough. Repeat: a hook the listener hears three times will stay. Leave room: the voice is the most important sound, so keep the middle of the mix free when it speaks.

A line fits when…

seconds needed = words ÷ 2 (slow) and also syllables ÷ 3, and the larger of the two must be no more than the seconds you give the line. Start time = (bar − 1) × seconds per bar + (beat − 1) × seconds per beat.
Example: “Happy feet all winter.” has 4 words and 6 syllables, starts at bar 13, beat 1 = 24 s and takes 1.6 s at 2.5 words a second, so it ends at 25.6 s.

Record it
  1. Put on headphones. Add an Audio track, choose the microphone as its input and click its R button.
  2. Sit 6 to 8 inches from the microphone, a little to one side so “p” and “b” do not pop. Use a pop screen if you have one.
  3. Say the line out loud, then set the level so the loudest word reaches about the top of the meter’s green or yellow, never red.
  4. Play the music quietly in your headphones and record at least three takes of the whole script.
  5. Choose the best take of each line. Trim the silence around it and line it up with the bar you planned.
Clean it up
  • Channel EQ: turn the lowest band into a high-pass filter near 80 Hz to remove rumble and breath.
  • Compressor: start with a ratio around 3:1 and lower the threshold until the loudest words are turned down by a few dB.
  • DeEsser 2: if “s” and “t” sound sharp, set it near the harsh frequency (often 5 to 8 kHz) and raise the amount until they soften.
  • Reverb: add only a trace, so the voice stays close and clear.

Step 6: duck the music. Ducking turns the music down while the voice speaks, then back up. About 4 to 6 dB is enough. The picture is the worked example: the colored lines are the voice, and the line below is the music level.

Method A · sidechain
  1. Group the music tracks (select them and make a Track Stack) so one plug-in controls all of them.
  2. Insert Compressor on the stack. In its Side Chain menu (top right of the plug-in window) choose the voice track.
  3. Play a voice line and lower the threshold until the music dips by about 4 to 6 dB.
  4. Set a short attack and a release of about half a second so it returns smoothly.
Method B · automation
  1. Press A to show automation on the music tracks or stack, and choose Volume.
  2. Click to add four points around each voice line: the level half a beat before it, the dip when it starts, the end of the line and back up one beat later.
  3. Drag the two middle points down 4 to 6 dB.
  4. Play it and smooth the points until you cannot hear the dip, only the clear voice.

Commercial Jingle

Music Technology | Commercial Music Project | 4 class periods

Overview ·

You are a jingle writer. A client has hired you to make a 30-second commercial for a product that does not exist, and your music and voice-over must sell it. You choose one of four client briefs, plan the 30 seconds in bars, pick a tempo, key and chord loop, and build the music in Logic Pro with Drummer, Session Players, Alchemy or Retro Synth, Quick Sampler and library loops. Then you write and record an 8 to 12 word voice-over, mix it so the voice sits over the music, and export exactly 30 seconds.

Goals ·

  • Plan a 30-second jingle in bars and seconds from a client brief, and prove that the numbers add up
  • Build a chord loop and a short arrangement (intro, verse, hook, tag) with Logic Pro tools
  • Write, time, record and mix an 8 to 12 word voice-over so the voice sits clearly over the music
  • Export a file of exactly 30 seconds and improve it with a client’s feedback

Key Vocabulary ·

TaglineThe short phrase a client wants the listener to remember, such as the line that ends the ad
Voice-overSpeech recorded to play over music, spoken by someone who is not seen
DuckingTurning the music down while the voice speaks, then back up, so every word can be heard

Tips for Success ·

  • A jingle is short. Say the product name and the tagline clearly, and make the hook something a listener can hum after one play.
  • Keep the music simple under the voice. Leave small gaps in the hook for the words, and let the music say more when the voice rests.
  • Record at least three takes of the whole script and keep the best one. Speak with a smile; listeners can hear it.
  • Save a new version of your project before each big change, so you can always go back.
