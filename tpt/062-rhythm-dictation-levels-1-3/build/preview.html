About Rhythm Dictation

Hear it, say it, write it

In rhythm dictation you listen to a rhythm and write it in notation. Every exercise has blank bars with the barlines and the time signature already printed. Follow the same five steps each time.

1

Listen. Your teacher performs the rhythm. Keep a steady beat and notice the time signature and the number of bars.

2

Echo. Clap or say the rhythm back with the syllables so you know what you heard.

3

Audiate. Hear the rhythm silently in your head while your finger keeps the beat. This is called audiating, and it is the step that makes writing easier.

4

Write. Write the notes and rests bar by bar. Make every bar add up to the time signature.

5

Check. Clap what you wrote, count each bar and compare it with the answer key.

Worked example

You hear: ta ti-ti ta shh.

Add it up: ta is 1 beat, ti-ti is 1 beat, ta is 1 beat and shh (a quarter rest) is 1 beat. That makes 4 beats, which fills one bar of 4/4.

Your turn

Your teacher performs a two-bar rhythm in 4/4. Listen, echo, audiate, then write it here.

Remember

  • The top number of a time signature tells you how many beats fill a bar.
  • The bottom number names the note that gets one beat.
  • A rest is silence, but it still counts and it still needs to be written.
