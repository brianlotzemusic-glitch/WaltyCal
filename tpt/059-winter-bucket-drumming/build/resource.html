Teacher Notes

Winter Bucket Drumming · 6 grooves · 18 parts · grades 3–8

What this is. Six original four-bar bucket-drumming grooves in 4/4. Every groove is written as three parts that layer: Part 1 Bass is low and steady, Part 2 Groove is the main pattern, and Part 3 Sparkle adds rim clicks and rests. Students say each rhythm with syllables, pat it, and then play it on buckets, adding one part at a time until an ensemble plays all three together. Grooves 1 to 4 use quarter notes, quarter rests, half notes and eighth notes. Grooves 5 and 6 add sixteenth notes to Part 2. All the rhythms are original, so no tune is used.

Getting ready. You need a plastic bucket for each student or each pair, sticks or just hands, and a flat floor with room to spread out. Read the safety and setup page (page 3) first, then teach the notation key (pages 4–5). Each groove has its own chart on pages 8–13 with the three parts stacked, so the bars line up. The layering activity (page 6) and the sample score (page 7) show how to build a groove one part at a time. Ensembles of 3 or 4 students fill in the ensemble planner (page 14), and the rubric and score sheet (page 15) records what each student can play.

Suggested pacing: 4 sessions

Differentiation

  • Grades 3–5. Begin each session by saying and patting the parts on knees before any buckets come out. Use Parts 1 and 2 for Grooves 1 to 4, and add Part 3 once the class keeps a steady beat. Leave out the accents at first, play with hands instead of sticks, and keep the tempo slow. Grooves 5 and 6 are a stretch goal for this age.
  • Grades 6–8. Play all three parts from the start and rotate so every student plays every part. Add the sixteenth notes in Grooves 5 and 6, and ask ensembles to play from memory after two passes. Challenge them to change the dynamics, for example soft for the first pass and loud for the last.

Assessment

The rubric and score sheet (page 15) has two parts. Part A counts how many of the 18 parts (6 grooves with three parts each) a student plays correctly. Part B rates four skills on a 3-point scale for a total of 12 points. Listen to each ensemble during Session 4 and score what you hear.

Safety and Setup

Care of the buckets, protecting hearing, and where to play

Bucket drums get loud quickly, so agree on the rules before the first bucket is handed out.

Clean, safe buckets

Use clean plastic buckets with no cracks and no sharp edges. Wash and dry them before the first session, and never use a bucket that held paint or chemicals. Run a hand around each rim and tape over any rough spot. Plastic buckets are quieter than metal ones. A snap-on lid gives a clearer click on the rim. Take off or tape down any metal handle so it does not rattle.

Protect hearing

Loud sounds can damage hearing. Keep the volume low enough that students can still talk to each other between rounds. Agree on a stop signal, such as a raised hand, and stop at once when you see it. Play short rounds of one or two minutes and rest between them. Never hold a bucket near an ear. If the room is too loud, play more softly or use a larger space.

Sticks or hands

Smooth wooden or plastic sticks work well. Hands are a good choice for younger students and for a quiet start. Sticks only touch the bucket: they are never swung toward people or other buckets, and they go on the floor whenever the teacher is talking. Leave a stick length of space between ensembles.

Setting up

Turn each bucket upside down on a flat floor or a mat so it cannot tip. Sit on a chair or the floor with the bucket in front of you, and never set a bucket on a desk. Put the Bass bucket, the Groove bucket and the Sparkle bucket of an ensemble close together so the players can see and hear each other.

Where to play on the bucket

Center

A low, round sound. Part 1 Bass plays in the center of the bucket.

Between center and edge

A middle sound. Part 2 Groove plays here, halfway between the center and the edge.

Rim click

A light click, written with an x notehead. Part 3 Sparkle taps the rim with the tip of a stick or a fingertip.

Quick rules for students

  • Play only when the Conductor or the teacher has counted in "1 2 3 4".
  • Stop playing at once on the stop signal, and put sticks down on the floor.
  • Play with a relaxed hand and let the stick bounce. Do not hit hard.
  • Listen to the other parts. If you cannot hear them, play more softly.
  • At the end, wipe the buckets, stack them, and count the sticks before anyone leaves.

Neutral clef

Two short bars: this staff is for rhythm, so there are no pitches and every note sits on the one line.

Time signature 4/4

Four beats in each bar, and a quarter note gets one beat.

Barline

Marks the end of a bar. Every bar in this resource has four beats.

Final barline

A thin and a thick line: the groove ends here.

Hand letters

R is the right hand and L is the left hand. A letter sits under every note. Hands alternate R, L, R, L from the first note of bar 1 to the end of bar 4, and a rest does not change whose turn it is. No letter sits under a rest.

Stems and beams

On this one-line staff every stem points up and sits on the right side of its notehead. One beam joins eighth notes and two beams join sixteenth notes. The say-it line under the letters gives the syllables for each beat.

Winter Bucket DrummingLayering activity
ACTIVITY
Build it one part at a time

Every groove has three parts that fit together like layers. Part 1 Bass is the low, steady base. Part 2 Groove is the main pattern. Part 3 Sparkle puts rim clicks and rests on top. All three parts are in 4/4 and all have 4 bars, so they line up beat for beat. The sample score (page 7) shows how.

Four steps

1

Say it and pat it. The whole class says Part 1 from its say-it line while patting knees in the order of the hand letters. Then do the same with Part 2 and with Part 3, one part at a time.

2

Bass starts. Each ensemble counts in "1 2 3 4" and the Bass player plays Part 1 alone for two passes through the four bars.

3

Groove joins. The Bass player keeps going. The Groove player comes in on beat 1 of the next pass. Play two passes with two parts.

4

Sparkle joins. The Sparkle player comes in on beat 1 of the next pass. Play two passes with all three parts, then everyone stops together at the end of bar 4.

Build map

WhenWho playsLengthBars
Step 2 (Bass starts)Part 1 Bass2 passes8 bars
Step 3 (Groove joins)Part 1 Bass and Part 2 Groove2 passes8 bars
Step 4 (Sparkle joins)Parts 1, 2 and 32 passes8 bars

One pass is the four bars of the groove. Steps 2 to 4 are 6 passes, or 24 bars. At about 70 beats per minute that takes about 80 seconds.

Ensembles of 3 or 4

Put students in ensembles of 3 or 4. Each ensemble has a Bass player, a Groove player and a Sparkle player. In an ensemble of 4 the fourth student is the Conductor, who counts in "1 2 3 4", keeps the beat softly on a knee and gives the stop signal. In an ensemble of 3 the Bass player counts in.

Swap and repeat

After each build, rotate roles so every student plays every part. An ensemble of 4 takes 4 rounds, and an ensemble of 3 takes 3. Use the ensemble planner (page 14) to record who played what. If the layers fall apart, go back one step. Quiet and steady beats loud and fast.

Cues that help

The player who is about to enter gives a clear nod on beat 4 of the last bar of the pass before. Count in out loud for the first pass, and count silently in your head for the passes after that.

SCORE
Groove 1 stacked

A score stacks the parts so that notes that sound at the same moment sit straight above one another. Read left to right to follow the time, and read straight down to see what everyone plays at once. The bars, the counts and the beats line up in all three parts. This sample is Groove 1, Snow Boots.

Read straight down

  • Bar 1, beat 1: Bass plays, Groove plays and Sparkle rests.
  • Bar 1, beat 2: Bass plays, Groove plays and Sparkle plays.
  • Bar 4, beat 3: Bass plays, Groove plays and Sparkle rests.

Try it

Point at the score and follow one beat at a time while the teacher counts "1 2 3 4" slowly. When one part rests, listen for the parts that are still playing.
