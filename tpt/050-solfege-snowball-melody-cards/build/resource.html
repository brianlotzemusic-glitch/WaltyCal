Teacher Notes

Solfege Snowball Melody Cards · 32 patterns in 4 sets · grades 2–4

How to use

  • Each card is a four-bar pattern in 4/4 on the treble staff in the key of C, with the solfege syllable printed under every note.
  • The notes are do, re, mi, sol and la (C, D, E, G and A). C is do, so this is movable-do solfege with do on C.
  • Rhythms use quarter notes, pairs of eighth notes, half notes and quarter rests. Say them as ta, ti-ti, ta-a and shh.
  • All 32 patterns are original. They were written for this set and are not tunes from songs.
  • Hand signs are optional and described in words on page 3. Use the ones your school already uses.

Singing range

  • Every pattern stays between C4 (middle C) and A4, a range of a sixth. Sets A and B stay between E4 and A4.
  • Give the class the starting pitch for do on a keyboard or bells, then sing do, mi, sol to set the key.
  • If low C is hard for your singers, start do a step higher on D4. The patterns still work in that key.

Set A uses sol and mi only. Set B adds la. Set C adds do and re. Set D uses all five notes. Page 12 is a tracker. Pages 13–14 are the build-a-melody mats and page 15 holds the rhythm slot cards to cut out. Pages 16–18 are the answer key and sample mats.

Pacing

  • Plan one set every two weeks, about 10 minutes a lesson: two or three cards a day, or one card a day as a warm-up.
  • Weeks 1 and 2: Set A. Weeks 3 and 4: Set B. Weeks 5 and 6: Set C. Weeks 7 and 8: Set D.
  • Use the mats after Set B (mat 1) and after Set D (mat 2), or any time for early finishers.

Read and sing in 4 steps

1Clap

Clap the four bars. Count 1 2 3 4 as you go.

2Say

Say the rhythm words (ta, ti-ti, ta-a, shh) in time.

3Sing slowly

Sing the syllables slowly, with hand signs if you like.

4Sing again

Fold the card along the dashed line and sing from the notes alone.

Good to know

Each pattern has exactly one answer, printed in the key on pages 16 and 17. No standards codes are listed: match the skills to your own curriculum. No songs, lyrics or copyrighted characters are used.

Student Reference

Keep this page beside you while you read and sing.

The five snowball notes

The letters show where each note lives on the staff. C is do, the lowest note, and it sits just below the staff on its own short line.

Hand signs (optional)

NoteHand sign
doA closed fist, held at about waist height.
reA flat hand slanting upward, fingers pointing out and slightly up.
miA flat hand held level, palm down, at about chest height.
solA relaxed open hand, palm facing in, fingers pointing forward, at about shoulder height.
laA relaxed hand drooping from the wrist, fingers pointing down, at about eye height.

Rhythm words

SymbolNameSayLength
quarter noteta1 beat
two eighth notesti-ti1 beat together
half noteta-a2 beats
quarter restshh1 beat of silence

Snow tip: a melody moves like a snowball. It can roll in small steps (mi to sol) or leap a little farther (do to mi). Watch the noteheads rise and fall as you sing.

Solfege Snowball Melody CardsRhythm slot cards
CUT OUT
12 rhythm bars
Cut along the dashed lines. Each card is one bar of 4/4. Pick four cards for your mat, put them in the order you like, and glue each one in a box. The notes line up with the staff below. You can use the same card twice if you cut out a second copy.

Beats: ta is 1, ti-ti is 1 (two eighth notes), ta-a is 2 and shh is a 1 beat rest. Every card adds up to 4 beats.

Sample Mats and Rhythm Bank

One way to build each mat. Many other melodies work too.

A melody is correct when each bar adds up to 4 beats and every note sits in the right place. Do is on the short line below the staff, re is in the space under the bottom line, mi is on the bottom line, sol is on the second line, and la is in the second space of the staff.
