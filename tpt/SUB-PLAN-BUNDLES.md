# Monthly Music Sub Plan Bundles (owner order, 23 Nov 2026)

Owner: "Let's prioritize sub plan bundles that don't require technology or for the teacher to have music skills. Let's make a bundle for
each month that highlights different holidays, important events or seasons."

Why: sub plans are a proven music category (17 of the top 288 products; "Elementary Music Sub Plans for the Non-Music Sub, Set 1" has 876
reviews at $15; the owner's own two sub-plan listings drew the most views of any classroom product). A teacher buys when she is sick or
planning ahead, and needs the lesson to work with a sub who knows no music.

## What one monthly bundle is
One product (PDF, US Letter, about 32-40 pages), the next version of `tpt/007-music-sub-plans-k5` (read its `gen.py`, UPLOAD.md, and look at
its pages first: same structure, same quality bar), themed to one month:
- **5 sub days**, each a 30-45 minute class, usable in any order, each with the month's theme.
- For each day: a one-page **sub plan** (timed steps, exact words to say, what to hand out, how to handle the first minutes), and **two
  student sheets**: one K-2 and one 3-5 (10 sheets in all), plus a **closing game** from a shared game bank.
- Front matter: cover, **Sub Quick Start** (the 3 things to do in the first 5 minutes), **Welcome sheet** the teacher fills in (classes,
  procedures, where things are), days at a glance, a **big chart** for any chant or word pattern, **Early Finishers** page, **answer keys**,
  **Sub report form** (notes back to the teacher), terms of use and credits, and the **Thank you and please rate this** page.
- A month note: one short teacher-facing paragraph on why this month matters (the holiday, event or season), in plain facts.

## Hard rules (the whole point of the product)
1. **No technology.** Nothing needs a computer, projector, speaker, recording, QR code, video link or website. Paper, pencils, crayons,
   scissors optional, hands and voices only.
2. **No music skills.** The sub never reads notation, sings, plays, or names a pitch. Rhythm is taught through speech rhythm (words and
   syllables, "clap the syllables of each word"), body percussion pictures, and games with words on the page. If notation appears on a
   student sheet, the sub's page says exactly what to say and the key is printed.
3. **Everything is original** or public domain: no popular songs, no lyrics, no copyrighted characters or speeches (do NOT quote copyrighted
   speeches or songs; state facts in your own words). Public-domain music facts (composer born before 1900, pieces published 1930 or
   earlier) are fine. Facts are checkable and accurate; write the checking source in QA.md.
4. **Respectful and accurate** for cultural and religious holidays and heritage months; secular and inclusive; public-school safe.
5. Each day works for a mixed class: K-2 and 3-5 sheets differ in reading level and task, not topic.

## The five-day recipe (fill every month the same way)
- **Day 1, Read and Respond:** a short original reading about the month's topic (K-2: 6-8 simple sentences the sub reads aloud; 3-5: about
  200 words students read), with questions and a draw-it task. Answers on the key.
- **Day 2, Rhythm Words:** speech rhythm with the month's vocabulary (clap the syllables, say the word, color the claps; 3-5 count
  syllables, build a four-beat chant, write it). No notation required of the sub.
- **Day 3, Draw and Design:** design an instrument, a poster or a scene that connects the month's theme to music (K-2 draw and label; 3-5
  draw, label and write two sentences).
- **Day 4, Move and Freeze:** a no-music movement game (the sub taps a pencil or claps; students move, freeze, copy), with picture cards to
  cut out or just point at, and a short reflection sheet.
- **Day 5, Word Games:** a themed music word search and a decode-the-message puzzle or crossword, with keys (K-2: match and trace).
Vary the activity names and art each month so the 12 bundles are not clones; keep the structure.
