The one rule for Class 2

Read every proposal before the student starts building. A one-sentence proposal goes back with a question (what will it sound like? which tools?), not a grade. The Proposal planner (page 13) has the frame.

Classes 3–5

Working days. Class 5 has no new slide. Circulate and use this project as a diagnostic: note who manages time, who needs structure and who is ready for more (my closing note).

Free PlayWorked example · an invented piece
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Late Bus Home”, described on paper: no audio is included, so the numbers are easy to check. Students’ projects will differ.

1 · A vague proposal

I will make a chill beat.

Nobody can say what it sounds like or which tools it needs.

2 · A clear proposal (concept, tools, sound)

I am making “Late Bus Home”, a mellow instrumental about the ride home after a long day, about two and a half minutes long. I will start with a slow drum groove and a soft bass, add warm chords on a keyboard sound, and write a simple melody for the choruses. In Logic I will use drum sounds and software instruments, EQ to keep the bass from covering the chords, and panning to spread the parts out. It will sound relaxed and a little sleepy at 96 beats per minute, and it should get fuller each time the chorus comes back.

3 · The arrangement map: 2:30 ·

4 · Checking the numbers, and why it works

96 beats per minute is 0.625 s a beat, so a bar of 4/4 is 2.5 s. Sections: 4 + 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 = 60 bars, and 60 × 2.5 s = 150 s = 2:30, as the proposal says. Every section has a job and something changes each time: layers enter, the melody arrives in the choruses, a riser leads into each chorus and the break strips the track back.

5 · A mix plan: EQ, panning and volume on every track ·

TrackVolumePanningEQ
Drumssteady, under the melody in the chorusescentercut a little of the muddy low-mids
Bassjust under the drumscenterroll off the highs so it stays out of the chords’ way

6 · Three Padlet questions (invented): 1. Where did you first hear the track get fuller? 2. Which part would you turn up or down, and why? 3. Does the title match the way the track feels?

Requirements ·

Length is up to you — but your track should feel complete, not unfinished
Use a variety of tracks and instruments appropriate to your chosen style
Apply basic mixing: EQ, panning, and volume balancing on all tracks
Write a project proposal paragraph (submitted via your class page)

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Explore — Open Logic, experiment with sounds, and brainstorm ideas (Idea sketchpad: page 12)
  2. 2
    Propose — Write a paragraph describing your concept, tools, and what the final product will sound like. Submit via your class page (Proposal planner: page 13)
  3. 3
    Build — Lay down your foundation (drums, chords, bass) and begin shaping your arrangement (Work plan: page 14)
  4. 4
    Develop — Add layers, melodies, FX, and transitions. Refine your sound and structure
  5. 5
    Mix — Balance your levels, apply EQ and panning, and clean up your project
  6. 6
    Finalize — Bounce your final track and prepare your presentation
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.
