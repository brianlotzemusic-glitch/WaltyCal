What this is ·

A 7-class-period open-ended project for high school music technology. Each student picks any style, technique or idea, writes a proposal paragraph, builds the track in Logic, mixes it, bounces it and presents it, with Padlet questions for feedback. It is the project from my Digital Music Production 2 course (focus: creative freedom), and the projects in that course can be taught in any order. My teacher notes call it a diagnostic project as much as a creative one.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and my teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Set up the Presentation Padlet and the submission spot (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 12–21), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–10), Class 3 (slides 11–12), Class 4 (slides 13–14), Class 6 (slides 15–17) and Class 7 (slides 18–19). Class 5 is a working day with no new slide. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

No audio included

No audio, video, songs or examples are included. Students make every sound in their tracks themselves, so everything in their projects is their own. For the Class 1 hook, you only ask questions aloud.

Make it fit your class ·

  • Easier or harder: my variations on page 6: a short list of prompts to choose from (page 10 has twelve), or a minimum length, original MIDI tracks only, or an artist statement.
  • Feedback board: my sheets use Padlet for the three questions; any class board works.
  • Any style: the project is open, so the pages never name a genre; students fill in their own.
  • Grading: my rubric, /25, the average of the student’s and your score (page 18).
In one sentence

Students pick something they care about, write a plan, follow it through the whole production cycle and play the result for the class.

Explore any style or technique of your choice. Find your artistic voice and set goals for the semester.

What students make ·

One finished track in a style of the student’s choosing, as long as it feels complete and uses a variety of tracks and instruments, with EQ, panning and volume balanced on every track. They hand in a proposal paragraph, a bounced .mp3 or .wav and the zipped .logicx project, plus a Padlet post with three feedback questions, then play the track and present it.

What this project reinforces ·

This is a diagnostic project as much as a creative one. It lets you see where each student is — their comfort with Logic, their creative instincts, and their ability to manage time independently. It also establishes the proposal and Padlet Q&A workflow they’ll use all semester.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Explore Logic Pro creatively with full artistic freedom
  • Develop project management skills through proposals and documentation
  • Practice the full production cycle: idea composition mix presentation
  • Build confidence in making creative decisions independently

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones. Speakers help for the final listen.
  • A place to submit the proposal paragraph, an .mp3 or .wav and a zipped .logicx project (your class page).
  • The Presentation Padlet, set up before Class 1 (my resource: this is yours to set up; none is included).
  • A rule about Apple Loops. My sheets do not forbid them, and my Harder variation asks for none. Decide before Class 1.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 12–21).

Before Class 1 ·

Set up the Presentation Padlet in your class page before Class 1.
Check that a student computer opens Logic and plays back through headphones.
Decide how students will choose: free choice, or a short list from page 10 (my Easier variation).
Print the student pages; set up the submission spot for the proposal.

What to watch (my closing note) ·

This is a diagnostic project. Use it to calibrate your expectations for the group: note who manages time well, who needs more structure, and who might be ready for bigger challenges.

Kit suggestions, not part of my sheets
  • While students work, ask three questions: What does it sound like so far? What is the next thing you will add? What would you cut if you ran out of time?
  • The Work plan (page 14) shows time management in the students’ own words.
  • Keep a running list of who needed a nudge and when.

Music they did not make ·

A kit suggestion

If a student recreates a song they like (my Easier variation), they build their own version in Logic. No one else’s audio goes into the project, and only the student’s own track is bounced and submitted.

Keep the volume moderate

Tell students to start with the headphones low and to check the final mix at a normal level.

Free PlayTeacher notes · the big idea
TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

This is a diagnostic project as much as a creative one. It lets you see where each student is — their comfort with Logic, their creative instincts, and their ability to manage time independently. It also establishes the proposal and Padlet Q&A workflow they’ll use all semester.

Common student struggles ·

  • Analysis paralysis — students freeze because they have too many choices. Encourage them to commit to an idea early and iterate.
  • Weak proposals — some students write one vague sentence. Push them to describe what their track will sound like and which tools they’ll use.
  • Poor time management — students underestimate how long mixing takes. Remind them to leave at least one full class for final mixing and bounce.
  • Students treat the presentation Q&A as an afterthought — remind them that thoughtful questions lead to better feedback.

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Provide a list of 3–5 project prompts they can choose from (e.g., “make a 30-second intro for a podcast,” “recreate a song you like”) to reduce overwhelm.

More Advanced: Require a minimum track length (2+ minutes), a specific number of original MIDI tracks (no Apple Loops), or a written artist statement explaining their creative choices.

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • Analysis paralysis: the Idea starters (page 10) and the Idea sketchpad (page 12).
  • Weak proposals: the Proposal planner (page 13) and the worked example (page 11).
  • Time management: the Work plan (page 14) and slide 14, which protects mix time.
  • Q&A as an afterthought: the presentation planner (page 20) has students write the three questions early.

How to grade ·

  • My rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 18). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.

Suggestions from this kit, not part of my rubric:

  • Creativity & Originality: ask what the student was going for in the proposal, then listen for it. Defaults and clichés are fine as a start; the question is what they changed.
  • Technical Skill: open the project. Which Logic tools did they use, and are EQ, panning and volume set on every track (my third requirement)?
  • Composition & Structure: compare the arrangement with the Work plan (page 14). Does the piece feel complete, with sections that change?
  • Sound Design & Mix Quality: listen on headphones and on speakers (my fourth tip). Ask which part leads in each section.
  • Documentation & Presentation: the proposal, the Work plan, the three Padlet questions and the Reflection page (page 21) are the evidence.

Reflection · added by this kit ·

The rubric mentions reflection, but the project sheet has no reflection task, so this kit adds one (page 21): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. It adds no points and no rubric row; use it as evidence when you score Documentation & Presentation.

SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a Free Play project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 3 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; check the labels on yours.

New project and tracksFile › New…, then add tracks with Track › New Tracks…: a Software Instrument track for MIDI sounds, an Audio track for recordings, a drum track for beats.
Play and record MIDIUse a MIDI keyboard, or turn on Musical Typing (Command-K) to play from the computer keyboard. Arm the track and press Record (R).
Apple LoopsOpen the Loop Browser (O) to find ready-made loops. My Harder variation asks for original MIDI tracks and no Apple Loops, so decide your rule before Class 1.
ArrangementCopy and paste regions to build sections, and name and color them so every section is easy to see. Sketch the whole form first, then fill it in.
Mixer: volume and panningPress X to open the Mixer. Move each track’s fader for volume and its Pan knob to place it left or right.
EQAdd Channel EQ to the channel strip. Cut what you do not need before you boost anything, and check the result with the track soloed and in the full mix.
EffectsInsert effects on the channel strip, for example a reverb or a delay, and keep them subtle until the rest of the mix is balanced.
TransitionsA reverse sound, a riser, a drop out or a filter sweep can join two sections. Draw it with automation or build it from a sound.
AutomationPress A to show automation, choose a parameter such as volume or a filter, and draw changes over time.
Save versionsUse File › Save As… with a numbered name before each big change.
Bounce and zipFile › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

My Easier variation is a list of 3–5 prompts students can choose from, to reduce overwhelm. Twelve prompts you can cut down to a short list. Two are my own examples; the rest are kit suggestions. None is a requirement.

Twelve starter prompts (kit suggestions) ·

Make something for something
  • a 30-second intro for a podcast (my example)
  • a theme for a board game that does not exist yet
  • a background loop for a study session
Make something in a style
  • a track in a style you rarely make
  • a slow beat with a simple melody on top
  • a build that grows for a minute, then drops out
Make something with a rule
  • only three instruments
  • every section is different from the one before
  • a track that could not exist without its first sound
Make something from something you like
  • recreate a song you like (my example): make your own version
  • take what you notice in music you like and use it in a new piece
  • change one choice (tempo, instrument or mood) and see what happens

Free PlayWorked example · an invented piece
EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented piece, “Late Bus Home”, described on paper: no audio is included, so the numbers are easy to check. Students’ projects will differ.

1 · A vague proposal

I will make a chill beat.

Nobody can say what it sounds like or which tools it needs.

2 · A clear proposal (concept, tools, sound)

I am making “Late Bus Home”, a mellow instrumental about the ride home after a long day, about two and a half minutes long. I will start with a slow drum groove and a soft bass, add warm chords on a keyboard sound, and write a simple melody for the choruses. In Logic I will use drum sounds and software instruments, EQ to keep the bass from covering the chords, and panning to spread the parts out. It will sound relaxed and a little sleepy at 96 beats per minute, and it should get fuller each time the chorus comes back.

3 · The arrangement map: 2:30 ·

4 · Checking the numbers, and why it works

96 beats per minute is 0.625 s a beat, so a bar of 4/4 is 2.5 s. Sections: 4 + 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 + 4 + 8 + 4 = 60 bars, and 60 × 2.5 s = 150 s = 2:30, as the proposal says. Every section has a job and something changes each time: layers enter, the melody arrives in the choruses, a riser leads into each chorus and the break strips the track back.

5 · A mix plan: EQ, panning and volume on every track ·

TrackVolumePanningEQ
Drumssteady, under the melody in the chorusescentercut a little of the muddy low-mids
Bassjust under the drumscenterroll off the highs so it stays out of the chords’ way
Chordssofter than the melodya little left and rightcut the lows so the bass has room
Melodythe loudest part in the chorusescenterlift the upper mids so it cuts through
FXvery quietwidecut the lows so the risers stay light

6 · Three Padlet questions (invented): 1. Where did you first hear the track get fuller? 2. Which part would you turn up or down, and why? 3. Does the title match the way the track feels?

Digital Music Production | Open-Ended Project | 7 class periods

Overview ·

This is your chance to make whatever you want. Use Logic Pro to explore any style, technique, or idea that excites you. The only rule: have a plan and follow through. This project is about finding your creative voice and showing that you can manage a project from start to finish.

Goals ·

  • Explore Logic Pro creatively with full artistic freedom
  • Develop project management skills through proposals and documentation
  • Practice the full production cycle: idea composition mix presentation
  • Build confidence in making creative decisions independently

Requirements ·

Length is up to you — but your track should feel complete, not unfinished
Use a variety of tracks and instruments appropriate to your chosen style
Apply basic mixing: EQ, panning, and volume balancing on all tracks

Steps / Process ·

  1. 1
    Explore — Open Logic, experiment with sounds, and brainstorm ideas (Idea sketchpad: page 12)
  2. 2
    Propose — Write a paragraph describing your concept, tools, and what the final product will sound like. Submit via your class page (Proposal planner: page 13)
  3. 3
    Build — Lay down your foundation (drums, chords, bass) and begin shaping your arrangement (Work plan: page 14)
  4. 4
    Develop — Add layers, melodies, FX, and transitions. Refine your sound and structure
  5. 5
    Mix — Balance your levels, apply EQ and panning, and clean up your project
  6. 6
    Finalize — Bounce your final track and prepare your presentation
  7. 7
    Present & Get Feedback — Create your own Padlet post with 3 questions you want classmates to answer about your project. Present to the class. During classmates’ presentations, answer at least 2 of their 3 questions on their Padlet post.

Tips for Success ·

  • Start with a clear idea, but don’t be afraid to let it evolve. Some of the best projects shift direction during the process.
  • Don’t spend 3 days picking sounds. Get something down early and refine as you go.
  • If you get stuck, listen to music that inspires you. Pay attention to what they did and try something similar in your own project.
  • Listen to your track on headphones AND speakers before you call it done.

What a reflection is

A reflection is a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Answer all four prompts in your own words. You choose how.

Written. I will write my answers on this page.
Oral. I will talk my answers through with my teacher. My teacher fills in the box at the bottom.

1  What went well? Name something in your project you are proud of, and say why it worked.

2  What did you struggle with? Say what was hard and what you tried to fix it.

3  What would you do differently next time? Be specific: a step, a choice, a sound or a tool.

4  Look at the rubric (page 18). Which category are you proudest of, and which would you work on first next time?
