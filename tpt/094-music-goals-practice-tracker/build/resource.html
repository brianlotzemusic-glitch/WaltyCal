TEACHER

How to use it in class

  1. Launch (one class period). Read page 3 together and teach the SMART words on page 4. Students write goals on pages 5–6 and a plan on page 7.
  2. Four weeks. Students fill in one log, page 9 (minutes) or page 10 (what I worked on), a little each day. Two minutes at the start or end of class works for a quick check-in.
  3. Weekly reflection. After days 7, 14, 21 and 28, students complete one page from pages 11–14. Five to eight minutes is enough.
  4. Finish. On days 29 and 30 students add up their minutes on page 15, color the chart on page 16 and may take the certificate on page 17.

A log is a reflection tool, not a grade

These pages help students notice what they do and what helps them. They are not scored. Please do not turn minutes into points: that rewards students who have more time, more space or an instrument at home.

If you need a record, note that a student completed a reflection, and compare each student only with their own goals.

Praise effort and choices, such as "You picked a smaller goal and kept going," and not the minute total.

Every student can take part

Not every student practices at home or owns an instrument. Students may log any way I made music: playing, singing, tapping or clapping, listening closely or composing or making a beat, whether at school, at home or at a relative's house. Time in your own class counts too. A student with no music time one day writes a zero or leaves the cell empty, and that is fine.

Goals can be for any kind of music-making. See the non-instrument example on page 4.

Sharing with families

Send home page 3 and the log your class uses. A sentence you can paste into a note: "This tracker helps your child set a music goal and notice what helps. Please ask what they worked on this week. There are no grades and no signatures."

Suggest the question "What did you work on?" instead of "Did you practice?"

Music Goals TrackerSMART goals guide
GOALS
SMART in five letters
NameDateClass

A SMART goal is a goal with five parts. Use the five letters as a checklist for every goal you write.

S
Specific
Say exactly what you will do. Ask: What will I play, sing, tap, listen to or create?
M
Measurable
You can count it or check it. Ask: How will I know when I am done? How many times, minutes or measures?
A
Achievable
It is a stretch, but you can really do it. Ask: Can I do this in the time I have? Who could help me?
R
Relevant
It matters to you and to your music. Ask: Why do I want this? How does it help my music?
T
Time-bound
It has a finish line. Ask: By which day or date will I be done?

A fuzzy goal

"Get better at my music."

You cannot tell what to do or when you are done.

Another SMART goal

By day 30, I will listen to 3 different kinds of music and write one sentence about each.

A SMART goal

"By day 30, I will play or sing the first 8 measures of my class piece without stopping, at the speed my teacher chose. I will make music at least 5 days each week to get ready for our performance."

PLAN
Warm-up, new, review, fun
NameDateClass

A 20-minute plan: 3 + 10 + 4 + 3 = 20

Warm-upGet your hands, breath or voice ready: long notes, easy patterns, slides, steady taps.
New materialLearn something you cannot do yet. Go slowly and work in small pieces.
ReviewGo back to something you already know and make it smoother.
FunPlay, sing, tap or make up anything you enjoy.

No instrument at home? Make a music plan anyway

  • Warm-up: hum or sing a slow pattern, or clap a steady beat.
  • New material: tap a new rhythm, or learn a short melody by ear.
  • Review: sing or tap something you already know.
  • Fun: make up a beat, a melody or a rhythm, or listen to a favorite.

TIPS
8 ideas to try
NameDateClass

These are ideas, not rules. Different tips work for different people. Try one or two, and use your weekly reflection to see what helps.

1
Short and regular is a good place to start

A little music time on most days can feel easier to keep going than one long session. Pick an amount you can really do.

Pick a time and a place

Link music time to something you already do, such as after a snack or before dinner. A steady spot helps you remember.

Warm up first

Begin with something easy so your hands, breath or voice are ready. It also helps you settle in.

Start slowly

Choose one line, one measure or one phrase. Fix the small part, then put it back into the whole.
