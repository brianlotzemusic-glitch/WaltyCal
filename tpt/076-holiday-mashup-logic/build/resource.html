What this is ·

A 3-class-period mashup project for high school music technology. Every student makes a DJ-style mashup of two public-domain carols, “Jingle Bells” and “Deck the Halls”, in Logic Pro: they match the tempo and the key with ratios, percents and semitones, enter both melodies from a note chart, check the match with Smart Tempo and Flex if their version has them, layer the melodies over one groove, arrange 44 bars, mix, present and reflect. It is an original Hudson Beat project, and both melodies are in the public domain.

Quick start ·

  1. Read the pacing guide (page 5) and the teacher notes (pages 6–7).
  2. Look at the tempo page, the key page and the note charts (pages 11–14) and the setup (page 4).
  3. Print the student pages (pages 11–26), one set per student.
  4. Open the intro deck and read the speaker notes for Class 1.

The intro deck ·

Every slide has a class: Class 1 (slides 1–7), Class 2 (slides 8–10) and Class 3 (slides 11–15). The slide about the three classes carries the badge for all three. Every slide has speaker notes.

Fonts: the deck uses Oswald and Source Sans 3 (free on Google Fonts, built into Google Slides). If PowerPoint or Keynote shows a different font, install the two fonts or present the PDF copy, which shows the intended look.

What students make ·

A 44-bar track in Logic Pro: a 4-bar intro, the chorus of “Jingle Bells”, a 4-bar bridge, eight bars of “Deck the Halls”, a 16-bar mashup with both melodies layered over one groove and a 4-bar outro. Both melodies are entered from the note charts in the same key and at the same tempo. Students work out the tempo ratio, a target tempo and each song’s percent change, find the shortest key shift, check the match with Smart Tempo and Flex if their version has them, and hand in a bounced file and the zipped .logicx project, plus their tempo and key pages and a reflection, then play the track and take questions.

Standards ·

Supports the National Core Arts Standards for Music (Music Technology, high school): generating and developing ideas (MU:Cr1.1, MU:Cr2.1), presenting work (MU:Pr6.1) and responding to it (MU:Re7.2).

Goals ·

  • Compare two tempos as a ratio, choose a target tempo, and calculate each song’s percent speed change and the seconds per bar at the target
  • Find the semitone distance between two keys, choose the shorter direction (at most 6 semitones) and name each song’s key after the move
  • Enter two public-domain melodies into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from note charts, and check the match with Smart Tempo and Flex if your version has them
  • Layer both melodies over one groove, arrange 44 bars with a bar-and-time table, then mix, bounce and reflect on the choices

About the two carols ·

A DJ-style mashup plays two songs together, and it works only when they share a tempo and a key. “Jingle Bells” was written by James Lord Pierpont and published in Boston on September 16, 1857, under the title “The One Horse Open Sleigh”; the chorus that people sing today is a later form that is documented on a recording from 1898. “Deck the Halls” is a Welsh melody, commonly dated to the sixteenth century, that belongs to a winter song called “Nos Galan”; its English words, by Thomas Oliphant, date to 1862. All of this is far older than the 1930 cutoff, so both tunes are in the public domain. Only the titles are given: no lyrics are printed, and nobody needs to sing.

SETUP

You need ·

  • A Mac with Logic Pro for each student, and headphones.
  • A place to hand in the .mp3 or .wav files, the zipped .logicx and the reflection.
  • The tempo page, key page and note charts, printed (pages 11–14), or shown on the projector.
  • Printed student pages, one set each (pages 11–26).
  • A MIDI keyboard is optional: the notes can be drawn in the Piano Roll. Nobody has to read music, only the note chart.

Before Class 1 ·

Read the note charts (pages 13–14) and enter the first four bars of one melody in Logic so you know how long it takes.
Open Logic Pro on a student computer and check which tools your version has: Smart Tempo, Flex Time, Flex Pitch, Alchemy, Drum Machine Designer, Session Players (see page 8).
Check that you can add a marker and change the project tempo.
Read the worked example (pages 9–10).

What to watch ·

Watch for percent changes with the wrong sign, key shifts that go the long way round, melodies entered an octave off, a Flex stretch that is too big and two melodies that are still in different keys when they are layered. A good listening day: play the two carols one after the other with no matching, then play a student’s matched version, and ask the class what changed. Ask which was harder to fix, the tempo or the key. This kit names no recordings or artists, and both melodies are public domain.

Ideas for the room
  • Ask while students work: which carol changed more, and by what percent? Which way did you move the key?
  • Say the theme plainly: two songs fit together when they share a tempo and a key, and both can be matched with arithmetic.
  • A student who is stuck can use the mean tempo and the key of F, as in the worked example: that already works.

The tunes ·

Both tunes are in the public domain. The words are not printed, so students work from the melodies alone. If a student wants to use another carol, check its first publication date: in 2026 it must be 1930 or earlier.

Keep the volume moderate

Two melodies layered over a groove can be tiring in headphones. Tell students to start low and check the final mix at a normal level.

TEACHER NOTES
1 of 2

What this project reinforces ·

This project teaches why two songs do not fit together by default and what it takes to make them fit. Students learn to compare tempos as a ratio, to choose a target tempo and turn it into a percent change and seconds per bar, to count semitones between keys with the circle of fifths and choose the shorter move, and to check the result by ear, with a simple clash test and with Logic Pro’s Smart Tempo and Flex tools if their version has them.

Common student struggles ·

  • The percent change comes out backwards: it is (target ÷ original − 1) × 100, so a song that has to speed up is positive and one that has to slow down is negative (page 11).
  • The key shift goes the long way round: count both directions on the circle of fifths or the table, and take the one that is 6 semitones or fewer (page 12).
  • The melody is entered in the wrong octave: Logic calls middle C “C3” by default and the chart calls it C4, so subtract one from the chart’s octave number (page 13).
  • Flex Time makes the audio sound strange, or the tool is missing in their version: keep the stretch small by meeting in the middle, and remember that MIDI melodies need neither tool (page 15).

Suggested variations ·

Easier: Let students choose one carol’s tempo as the target, so that only one song changes (page 11), enter both melodies in F major, and skip the Smart Tempo and Flex step (page 15). Ask for 2 questions at the presentation.

More Advanced: Add a third carol from a score you may legally use and find its ratio and shift against the other two, or put a key change into the mashup section (page 12), or write a second chord loop for the mashup and compare the two by ear and with the clash check (page 16).

Where this kit helps with each struggle
  • The percent change: the tempo page (page 11) gives the formula, a worked pair and a table for six tempo pairs.
  • The shift: the key page (page 12) counts the semitones both ways round for six key pairs and draws the circle of fifths.
  • The octave: the note charts (pages 13–14) list every note in both keys, and the Logic quick reference (page 8) explains Logic’s octave names.
  • Flex: the Smart Tempo and Flex page (page 15) gives the audio lengths to expect, so students can tell a good stretch from a bad one.

How to grade ·

  • The rubric: five categories, each scored 1–5, /25 (page 23). Students score themselves first; the final grade is the average of their score and yours.
  • Tempo Match (rubric row 1): check the ratio, the target tempo and both percent changes against the tempo page (page 11).
  • Key Match (rubric row 2): check that the shift is the shorter direction and that the new key names are right (page 12).
  • Melodies & Layering (rubric row 3): play each melody alone against the chart, then the layered section (pages 13–14 and 16).
  • Arrangement & Mix (rubric row 4): check the markers against the map, listen for both melodies over the groove and check the meter for clipping (pages 18 and 19).
  • Plan & Reflection (rubric row 5): the tempo and key pages, the feedback questions and the Reflection page (page 26) are the evidence.

Reflection ·

Every student reflects (page 26): a written or oral conversation about how you did on the project and what you would do differently next time. Students write it or talk it through with you; either way they answer the four prompts. Allow about 10 minutes after the presentations. SETUP
Teacher + student

Where to find the tools a mashup project is likely to use. Demonstrate what your class needs on Classes 1 and 2 and then let students work. Names are from current Logic Pro versions; labels move between versions, so check yours.

Project and tempoFile › New…, then a Software Instrument track to start. Set the project tempo in the control bar to your target tempo and keep the time signature at 4/4. Smart Tempo, if your version has it, can detect the tempo of a recording; for the MIDI melodies in this project set the tempo yourself so that the bar arithmetic on the tempo page holds.
Enter the melodiesOpen the Piano Roll and draw the notes with the pencil tool on a grid of 1/16 or 1/8, one melody per track. Pitch names run down the left. Logic names middle C “C3” by default; the chart calls it C4, so subtract one from the chart’s octave number.
Move a melody to the target keyTo move a MIDI region by semitones, look for Transpose in the region inspector, or select its notes in the Piano Roll and move them up or down by the number of semitones from the key page. If your version labels it differently, use the Piano Roll. The charts list every note in both keys, so you can check the result.
Smart TempoSmart Tempo, if your version has it, analyzes the tempo of a recording that you import or record, and you choose whether the project follows the recording or the recording follows the project. Look in the project settings, the tempo display and the region inspector for its options, since the places vary between versions. A detected tempo that is half or double what you expect means the tempos are in a 2:1 ratio.
Flex TimeFlex Time, if your version has it, stretches an audio region so that it follows a new tempo without changing its pitch. Look in the region or track inspector for the Flex and Follow settings and turn them on for the audio region, then change the project tempo to your target and compare the region’s length with the numbers on the Smart Tempo and Flex page. Small stretches sound natural; large ones can sound artificial.
Flex PitchFlex Pitch, if your version has it, edits the pitch of a single-line audio recording without changing its length: look in the region or track inspector for the Flex settings and choose Flex Pitch as the mode. It is made for fine pitch corrections; to move a whole MIDI melody by semitones, use Transpose instead.
Drums, bass and chordsUse Drum Machine Designer with a kick, a snare or clap and a hat on three pads. In the Piano Roll set the grid to 1/16 and click in the hits from your groove card. Use Alchemy or Retro Synth for a bass and a pad, and keep the bass to one note at a time in a low octave. Session Players, if your version has them, can play chords and a groove once you choose the chords yourself; they are optional.
Sections with markersShow the Marker global track (the Global Tracks controls or the View menu). Add a marker with the plus (+) button in that track, or with a Create Marker key command if your version has one, then drag or type it to the bars on your arrangement map. Name them Intro, Jingle Bells, Bridge, Deck the Halls, Mashup and Outro.
Mixer, automation and bouncePress X for the Mixer and set levels and pan. Press A to show automation. Keep both melodies clear above the groove, and keep the master out of the red. Then File › Bounce › Project or Section (Command-B), MP3 or WAV, for the whole track. In Finder, Control-click the .logicx and choose Compress.

EXAMPLE
Teacher

An invented plan on paper: a mashup at 108 BPM in F major. No audio is included. Student projects will differ. Page 1 of 2: the plan and the numbers.

1 · A weak plan

I will speed up one song and slow the other and hope that they match.

It names no tempo, no ratio, no percent, no key and no checks.

2 · A clear plan

My mashup joins Jingle Bells (typical class tempo 120 BPM, notated in G major) and Deck the Halls (96 BPM, notated in F major), both in 4/4. The tempos are in the ratio 120 : 96 = 5 : 4, so neither is half or double the other. I chose the mean, (120 + 96) ÷ 2 = 108 BPM. Jingle Bells changes by (108 ÷ 120 − 1) × 100 = −10% and Deck the Halls by (108 ÷ 96 − 1) × 100 = +12.5%. One bar lasts 240 ÷ 108 = 2.222 seconds, so my 44-bar track lasts 97.78 seconds. The keys are 2 semitones apart: G down to F is −2 and F up to G is +10, so the shorter move is −2. Jingle Bells goes down to F major (its relative minor goes from E minor to D minor) and Deck the Halls stays in F major. I layer the Jingle Bells chorus over Deck the Halls twice, and with the −2 shift the beat check finds 45 smooth, 14 mild and 5 hard pairs out of 64, the fewest hard clashes of any shift.

Why these choices

The mean tempo asks each song for a change of about a tenth, and the two audio stretches are equal and opposite (+11.11% and −11.11%), so neither song is bent far. F major keeps Deck the Halls exactly as notated and asks only Jingle Bells to move, 2 semitones down. The beat check backs the choice up: no other shift has fewer hard clashes (page 16).

Try it yourself

Change the target key to G major: Jingle Bells stays, Deck the Halls moves +2. Run the beat check on page 16 again: the counts are 45 smooth, 14 mild and 5 hard, the same as before. Why?

Step 1: match the tempo. Published editions mark very different tempos (Jingle Bells 80 to 144 BPM and Deck the Halls 96 to 160 BPM in the files read), so this page uses a typical class tempo for each: 120 BPM for Jingle Bells and 96 BPM for Deck the Halls. Both charts are in 4/4. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM · Seconds per bar in 4/4 = 240 ÷ BPM (3/4: 180 ÷ BPM; 2/4: 120 ÷ BPM)

Ratio = faster ÷ slower, reduced: 120 ÷ 96 = 5 ÷ 4. A 2 : 1 ratio is double-time or half-time.

Percent speed change = (target ÷ original − 1) × 100. Positive: speed up. Negative: slow down.

Target: one song’s tempo, or the mean. Track = 44 bars × seconds per bar.

Rounding

Seconds per bar to 3 decimals (240 ÷ 108 = 2.222) and the track to 2 (97.78); a 5 rounds up. Percents are exact or to 2 decimals. Both carols have the same tempo after the match, so they share one bar length.

Step 2: match the key. The 4/4 edition of Jingle Bells charted here is in G major and Deck the Halls is in F major in two of the three editions compared (an adaptation of the 1857 edition of Jingle Bells is in A-flat major, and one Deck the Halls edition is in E-flat major, so check the key of any score you use). To layer the songs, move one or both so that they share a key. # means sharp and b means flat.

The rules

Semitones up from key A to key B = (B − A) mod 12, counting each key as a number of semitones above C (C = 0, C# = 1 … B = 11).

Shorter move: if the count up is more than 6, subtract 12 (10 up is 2 down). Never move more than 6 semitones. A count of exactly 6 is a tie.

The circle counts how related two keys are: each step clockwise adds a sharp (G has 1, D has 2) and each step counterclockwise adds a flat (F has 1, Bb has 2). It is a different count from the semitones.

Relative minor = 3 semitones below the major key: G major and E minor share a key signature, and so do F major and D minor.

The arc on the circle

G and F are two steps apart on the circle, through C. Two steps down is G to C to F, and it is also 2 semitones down from G to F.

Step 4: check with Smart Tempo and Flex. A MIDI melody follows the project tempo by itself, but a recording has a fixed length. These tools are for audio, and each is there only if your version has it; look in the region or track inspector for the settings. Smart Tempo works out the tempo of a recording. Flex Time stretches a recording so that it follows a new tempo without changing its pitch. Flex Pitch edits the pitch of a single-line recording without changing its length.

Speed and length move in opposite directions: a song that speeds up by 12.5% gets 11.11% shorter, because the length changes by (original ÷ target − 1) × 100. At the mean tempo both 8-bar melodies last the same 17.78 seconds, so they end on the same bar line.

Speeding up a recording with no stretch raises its pitch too. Moving to the mean tempo this way would push Jingle Bells down by 1.82 semitones and Deck the Halls up by 2.04, which is not what either key needs. Flex Time changes the length alone and a transposition changes the pitch alone, so you can set each by its own number. 100 cents make 1 semitone.

Step 5: layer and build the groove, continued. Each card is a one-bar drum pattern (16 steps, 4 to a beat; shaded steps are the beats). Click it into a Drum Machine Designer track (or draw it in the Piano Roll with the grid on 1/16) and repeat it. Both melodies share one groove, so the groove is where the mashup is glued together.

Steady groove · 108 BPM feels like 108

Drums. Kick on beat 1, the “and” of 2 and beat 3; snare on beats 2 and 4; hat on every eighth note.

Bass and chords. Bass plays the chord root on beat 1 and again on the eighths. Keys or guitar play each chord once or twice a bar.

Try. Add a snare hit on the “and” of 4 in the last bar of every four to push into the next phrase.

Half-time groove · 108 BPM feels like 54

Drums. Kick on beat 1 and the “and” of 3; one snare hit on beat 3; hat on every eighth note. The backbeat lands half as often, so it feels half as fast.

Bass and chords. Bass holds each chord root for half a bar. Pads or keys play the chord for the whole bar.

Try. Use it under the Deck the Halls section, or under the intro, then switch to the steady groove for the mashup.

Half-time and double-time

A 2 : 1 tempo ratio is a half-time or double-time match. Putting the snare on beat 3 instead of beats 2 and 4 makes a groove feel half as fast: at 108 BPM it feels like 54 BPM, and the steady groove at 54 BPM would feel like 108. This is a way to match two songs a 2 : 1 apart with no stretch at all, and a way to make the intro and the second carol feel different from the mashup.

Holiday Mashup

Music Technology | Mashup Project | 3 class periods

Overview ·

Make a DJ-style mashup in Logic Pro from two public-domain carols, “Jingle Bells” and “Deck the Halls”. Work out the ratio of their tempos, choose a target tempo and calculate each song’s percent speed change and the seconds per bar. Find the shortest transposition between their keys and name each song’s new key. Enter both melodies from a note chart (16 bars of 4/4 each), check the match with Smart Tempo and Flex Time or Flex Pitch if your version has them, and layer the two melodies over one groove. Arrange 44 bars in six sections: an intro, each carol, a bridge, the mashup and an outro. Nobody needs to compose a melody.

Goals ·

  • Compare two tempos as a ratio, choose a target tempo, and calculate each song’s percent speed change and the seconds per bar at the target
  • Find the semitone distance between two keys, choose the shorter direction (at most 6 semitones) and name each song’s key after the move
  • Enter two public-domain melodies into Logic Pro’s Piano Roll from note charts, and check the match with Smart Tempo and Flex if your version has them
  • Layer both melodies over one groove, arrange 44 bars with a bar-and-time table, then mix, bounce and reflect on the choices

Key Vocabulary ·

MashupA track that plays parts of two songs together or back to back. It works when the two songs share a tempo and a key
Tempo matchMaking two songs run at the same speed: compare the BPM numbers as a ratio, choose a target tempo and change each song by a percent
SemitoneThe smallest step on a keyboard, from one key to the very next. Twelve semitones make an octave; moving every note by the same number of semitones is transposing
Time stretch (Flex Time)Making an audio recording longer or shorter without changing its pitch, so that it can follow a new tempo
Smart TempoA Logic Pro feature, if your version has it, that listens to a recording and works out its tempo

Tips for Success ·

  • Match the tempo and the key before you layer anything: two melodies in different keys cannot be rescued by the mix.
  • Move the song that needs the smaller change, or meet in the middle: the tempo page shows both choices side by side.
  • Listen to each melody alone at the target tempo before you layer them.
  • Save a new version of your project at the end of each class, so you can always go back.
