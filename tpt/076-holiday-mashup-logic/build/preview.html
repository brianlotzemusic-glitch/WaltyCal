The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a target tempo and a target key from the tempo and key pages, with both percent changes and both shifts written down, so that Class 2 is all melodies and layering. Ask a few students to say their numbers aloud before they go.

With two or four periods

Two classes: Class 1 is the tempo, the key and the melodies; Class 2 is the check, the layering, the arrangement, the mix and short presentations. Four classes: keep Classes 1 and 2, then spend Class 3 on the arrangement and the mix, and Class 4 on presentations and reflection.

Step 1: match the tempo. Published editions mark very different tempos (Jingle Bells 80 to 144 BPM and Deck the Halls 96 to 160 BPM in the files read), so this page uses a typical class tempo for each: 120 BPM for Jingle Bells and 96 BPM for Deck the Halls. Both charts are in 4/4. Every number below is computed, so you can check your own arithmetic against it.

The formulas

Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ BPM · Seconds per bar in 4/4 = 240 ÷ BPM (3/4: 180 ÷ BPM; 2/4: 120 ÷ BPM)

Ratio = faster ÷ slower, reduced: 120 ÷ 96 = 5 ÷ 4. A 2 : 1 ratio is double-time or half-time.

Percent speed change = (target ÷ original − 1) × 100. Positive: speed up. Negative: slow down.

Target: one song’s tempo, or the mean. Track = 44 bars × seconds per bar.

Rounding

Seconds per bar to 3 decimals (240 ÷ 108 = 2.222) and the track to 2 (97.78); a 5 rounds up. Percents are exact or to 2 decimals. Both carols have the same tempo after the match, so they share one bar length.

Step 2: match the key. The 4/4 edition of Jingle Bells charted here is in G major and Deck the Halls is in F major in two of the three editions compared (an adaptation of the 1857 edition of Jingle Bells is in A-flat major, and one Deck the Halls edition is in E-flat major, so check the key of any score you use). To layer the songs, move one or both so that they share a key. # means sharp and b means flat.

The rules

Semitones up from key A to key B = (B − A) mod 12, counting each key as a number of semitones above C (C = 0, C# = 1 … B = 11).

Shorter move: if the count up is more than 6, subtract 12 (10 up is 2 down). Never move more than 6 semitones. A count of exactly 6 is a tie.

The circle counts how related two keys are: each step clockwise adds a sharp (G has 1, D has 2) and each step counterclockwise adds a flat (F has 1, Bb has 2). It is a different count from the semitones.

Relative minor = 3 semitones below the major key: G major and E minor share a key signature, and so do F major and D minor.

The arc on the circle

G and F are two steps apart on the circle, through C. Two steps down is G to C to F, and it is also 2 semitones down from G to F.
