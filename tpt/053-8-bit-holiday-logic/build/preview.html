The one thing to do on Class 1

Make sure every student leaves with a tempo from 120 to 150 BPM, a project in 4/4 and the first five bars of the melody entered. If the tempo is only a guess, tap it: tap a steady beat for ten seconds and count the taps.

If you only have three classes

Merge Classes 2 and 3: build the arpeggios, bass and drum on Class 2, then arrange and mix on Class 3 and use Class 4 for bouncing, presentations and reflection.

About this chart

Chart bar 1 is the pickup bar: three beats of rest, then one note on beat 4. Chart bar 2 is the first full bar of the tune, and the verse ends on a long G in chart bar 9. Logic calls middle C “C3”, so subtract one from the octave numbers here when you read your Piano Roll. Chords are one idea that fits; try others by ear. Step 3 of the project sheet: enter the melody on your square-wave lead.

Up

Arpeggiator: Note Order: Up · Rate 1/16

C chord, one bar (16 steps): C E G C E G C E G C E G C E G C

Sounds like: Climbs through the chord and starts again. The classic, driving game sound.

Up & Down

Arpeggiator: Note Order: Up & Down · Rate 1/16

C chord, one bar (16 steps): C E G E C E G E C E G E C E G E

Sounds like: Climbs, then falls, so the top and bottom notes are played once per turn. It bounces.

Down

Arpeggiator: Note Order: Down · Rate 1/16

C chord, one bar (16 steps): G E C G E C G E C G E C G E C G

Sounds like: Falls through the chord. It feels calmer and sounds good under a held lead note.

Up, two octaves

Arpeggiator: Note Order: Up · Octave Range 2 · Rate 1/16

C chord, one bar (16 steps): C E G C E G C E G C E G C E G C

Sounds like: Climbs through the chord, then through the same notes an octave higher, then repeats. It sparkles.

Try: Up in the chorus and Up & Down in the verse. Some versions of the Arpeggiator repeat the top and bottom note in Up & Down; if yours does, the idea is the same. The numerals show each chord’s place in the key: I, IV and V are major, ii, iii and vi are minor, and V7 adds a seventh to V.
