Teacher Notes

Winter Recorder Songbook · 8 tunes on three notes · grades 3–5 · soprano recorder

What this is. A first songbook for beginner soprano recorder. Every tune uses only the notes B, A and G, so students can play real tunes after learning three fingerings. There are 8 tunes (2 public-domain tunes and 6 originals), with fingering charts, a practice checklist, a tune tracker, two quizzes and an answer key.

Written pitches. All notes are written pitches on the treble staff: B4, A4 and G4. A soprano recorder sounds one octave higher than written, so students will hear B5, A5 and G5. The fingerings are baroque (English) fingerings. The fingering charts are on page 3.

Rhythms. Tunes 1 to 6 use whole notes, half notes, quarter notes and quarter rests. Eighth notes appear only in tunes 7 and 8. Tunes are in 4/4, except tune 4, which is in 3/4.

Four-week pacing

WhenFocusPages
Week 1Posture, breath and the soft "too" (page 3). Teach B, then A, then G with the fingering charts. Tunes 1 and 2.pp. 4
Week 2Steady beat and quarter rests. Tune 3 (Au Clair de la Lune) and tune 4 in 3/4.pp. 5
Week 3Write the note names for tunes 5 and 6. Breathe only on the rests and half notes.pp. 6
Week 4Eighth notes in tunes 7 and 8. Finish the tracker, then give the quizzes.pp. 7, 10–11

Assessment ideas

  • Play and pass. Each student plays one tune for you. Use the tune tracker on page 9 to record names, fingerings, steady beat and a full performance.
  • Fingering check. Hold up a note name and watch students show the fingering without playing.
  • Written check. The two quizzes (pages 10–11) cover note names, fingerings, drawing notes and steps versus skips. The key is on page 12.
  • Self-check. Students tick the practice checklist on page 8 each day and bring it back weekly.

Hygiene note

Each student uses only their own recorder and never shares it. Have students start with a clean mouthpiece, and store each recorder in a labeled bag. Follow your school or district guidance on cleaning.

Standards

The tunes and checklists support the Performing process of the National Core Arts Standards. Please match them to your own curriculum.

Get ready: follow these three steps before you play.

1Posture

Sit or stand tall with both feet flat. Hold the recorder out in front, tilted slightly down.

2Breath

Take a calm breath, as if you are fogging a window. Blow a gentle, steady stream of air.

3Tonguing

Start each note by touching your tongue behind your top teeth and saying a soft "too".

Fingering charts

Black circles are covered holes. White circles are open holes. Cover each hole with the soft pad of your finger so no air leaks. Holes 6 and 7 are double holes. Move from B to A by adding finger 2, and from A to G by adding finger 3.

B

Cover: thumb and hole 1.
Left hand: thumb + 1. Right hand: all holes open.

A

Cover: thumb, hole 1 and hole 2.
Left hand: thumb + 1 + 2. Right hand: all holes open.

G

Cover: thumb, hole 1, hole 2 and hole 3.
Left hand: thumb + 1 + 2 + 3. Right hand: all holes open.

3Au Clair de la LunePublic domain
4/4 · Tempo: about 72 beats per minute · Notes: B, A, G. Note names are shown.
Tonguing: Repeat the same note with a fresh "too" each time, with a little space between notes.

Tonguing: Count 1-2-3 and tongue the first beat of every bar a little more clearly.
Breath: Breathe on the quarter rests at the end of bars 4 and 8.

7Hot Cross BunsPublic domain
4/4 · Tempo: about 72 beats per minute · Notes: B, A, G. Write the note names (answers on page 12).
Tonguing: Each pair of eighth notes is two quick "too-too" taps in the time of one beat. Keep them light and even.
Breath: Breathe before bar 3 and again before bar 4.
Traditional English street cry. In print by 1733. Public domain.

4/4 · Tempo: about 72 beats per minute · Notes: B, A, G. Write the note names (answers on page 12).
Tonguing: Eighth notes are quick and light: think "too-too" for each pair, then settle back to "too".
Breath: Breathe after bars 2, 4 and 6, on the half note or the rest.
